# Alberta () — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: CAD (tables in C$ millions unless stated; ~USD alongside at USD/CAD 1.40) · Results released 26 Oct 2026 (AiGC Market Performance Report – September 2026 edition; covers 13 Jul–30 Sep 2026)**

> Alberta's first 80 days as Canada's second open iGaming market produced C$2,885m (~$2,061m) of wagers and C$122.1m (~$87.2m) of non-adjusted GGR, with September alone at C$56.6m (~$40.4m) — a run-rate (~C$680m annualised) already ~1.7x the GGR implied by the government's own C$76m year-one revenue forecast. It is a casino-led market (78% of NAGGR) run on an Ontario-style conduct-and-manage model with a ~22% effective public take, cheap licences and the strictest day-one advertising rules in Canada; the swing factors from here are the Premier's threatened advertising clampdown, enforcement against the ~70% offshore base, and how 50 approved sites split a ~5m-person province.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market structure.** Alberta opened Canada's second competitive iGaming market on 13 Jul 2026 under the iGaming Alberta Act (SA 2025, Bill 48) and amended Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act. Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis (AGLC) is regulator and standard-setter; the new Crown corporation Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC) 'conducts and manages' the market and contracts private operators — a direct analogue of Ontario's AGCO/iGO split. Minimum age is 18 (Ontario: 19).
- **Size (primary, unaudited).** AiGC's September Market Performance Report shows 13 Jul–30 Sep wagers of C$2,885.1m (~$2,061m), winnings C$2,762.9m (~$1,974m) and non-adjusted GGR (NAGGR = wagers less winnings, before promotional costs) of C$122.1m (~$87.2m); blended hold 4.23%. Monthly NAGGR: Jul (19 days) C$22.2m → Aug C$43.3m → Sep C$56.6m (+30.7% MoM) on wagers of C$542.9m → C$1,064.8m → C$1,277.4m (+20.0%).
- **Run-rate vs forecast.** September annualises at ~C$680m (~$485m) NAGGR before further operator additions — ~1.7x the ~C$390m GGR implied by Minister Dale Nally's C$76m (~$54m) year-one government revenue forecast, and inside the C$850m–C$1.2bn year-one range third parties attribute to H2 Gambling Capital (two conflicting figures; neither read at source). Our estimate for the first 12 months is C$740–810m NAGGR (3–5% monthly growth) — see forecast.
- **Online vs retail.** AiGC reports online only. The legacy AGLC estate (≈30 casinos' slots, VLTs, lottery, bingo and the state site Play Alberta) booked FY2024-25 gaming net sales of C$2,133.7m (~$1,524m) and gaming net income of C$1,486.9m (~$1,062m). Play Alberta's FY2024-25 net sales were C$269.9m (~$193m) and government estimated it held only 23–32% of provincial online play, i.e. ~70% of iGaming was offshore pre-launch. On September's run-rate, regulated online NAGGR is now ~C$680m/yr vs Play Alberta's C$270m — roughly a 2.5x step-up in onshore online revenue.
- **Product mix.** Casino (RNG slots, tables, poker, keno) delivered 78.2% of Q3 NAGGR (C$95.5m, ~$68.2m), Sports & Event Betting 16.4% (C$20.1m, ~$14.4m) and Live Casino 5.4% (C$6.6m, ~$4.7m). September split: 75.6% / 19.5% / 4.9%. Fantasy Sports and Peer-to-Peer (poker/exchange) are eligible product categories but reported no activity in Q3.
- **Sports betting.** Q3 handle C$325.8m (~$233m), NAGGR C$20.1m, hold 6.2%. September handle C$152.7m (+36% MoM) and NAGGR C$11.1m (+114%) at a 7.3% hold as NFL/CFL/college football returned; August hold was only 4.6%. Betting is 11–12% of wagers but 16–20% of NAGGR — higher margin, far lower turnover than casino.
- **Players.** Active player accounts rose 226,959 (Jul) → 314,091 → 388,585 (Sep); ARPPA C$98 → C$138 → C$146 (~$104); wagers per account ~C$3,300/month. Accounts are not unique players (multi-operator sign-ups), so penetration cannot be computed from this series.
- **Licensing regime.** Two-step entry: (1) AGLC registration per distinct iGaming site (due diligence, compliance attestations, integration with the centralised self-exclusion system), then (2) a commercial operating agreement with AiGC; betting starts only once AiGC notifies. Fees are fixed and non-refundable: C$50,000 (~$36k) one-time application (includes due-diligence costs) and C$150,000 (~$107k) annual registration per site; platform/critical-system suppliers C$15,000/yr (~$11k); other suppliers (e-wallets, oddsmakers, integrity monitors, test labs) C$3,000/yr (~$2k). Standards sit in AGLC's Standards and Requirements for Internet Gaming (SRIG, ~85 pp, Jan 2026; revised 18 Jun 2026).
- **Fiscal regime (revenue share, not a tax statute).** Per Alberta.ca: 3% of gross gaming revenue is allocated first to First Nations initiatives (2%) and social-responsibility/problem-gambling funding (1%); the remaining 'net iGaming revenue' is split 80% operator / 20% province. Effective public take ≈22.4% of GGR (our estimate; the precise definition of 'net' — e.g. treatment of promotional credits and GST — is not published). Base is revenue, not turnover; no player withholding (recreational winnings untaxed in Canada).
- **Public take to date (estimate).** Applying the published formula to NAGGR gives ~C$27.4m (~$19.6m) for Q3 — province C$23.7m, First Nations C$2.4m, RG C$1.2m — and ~C$12.7m in September alone (~C$152m annualised vs C$76m budgeted for year one). Treat as an upper bound: if the agreements compute the share on promo-adjusted revenue the take is lower.
- **Operator landscape.** 22 sites went live on day one from 50 registered operators; active operators (with agreements and live sites) were 16/20/24 in Jul/Aug/Sep. At the 13 Oct 2026 grace-period close AiGC listed 29 operators and 50 sites, including bet365 (casino and sportsbook), FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, BetRivers, Bally Bet, BetVictor, BET99, Stake (Stake Canada Ltd), Betway, JackpotCity, PlayOJO, TonyBet, PlayBetNova (Delasport) and AGLC's Play Alberta. AiGC CEO Dan Keene guides to 60+ sites in 2027. Operator-level shares are not disclosed (n/a).
- **Channelisation and enforcement.** AiGC's stated goals: 70% of online activity on regulated sites after year one and 75% by year two (from ~30% pre-launch). The three-month transition ended 13 Oct 2026; unregistered sites must stop taking Alberta wagers and AGLC says it will monitor and act. Enforcement tools (payment/ISP blocking, affiliate liability) are not publicly specified — this is the key swing factor for the 70% target.
- **Advertising — strictest in Canada, and tightening.** SRIG (18 Jun 2026) bans public advertising of inducements, bonuses and credits (permitted only on the operator's own site or via opted-in direct marketing); bars athletes except for responsible-gambling messaging; prohibits content appealing to minors (cartoons, influencers, celebrities), billboards near schools, 'excessive play' or financial-security claims; land-based casinos may not promote iGaming inducements. Premier Danielle Smith said on 25 Oct 2026 that Albertans are complaining about ad saturation and rule changes may follow — a live regulatory risk for Q1–Q2 2027.
- **Responsible gambling.** A centralised self-exclusion system covering all iGaming sites, all land-based venues or all gambling was live from day one (Ontario still lacks this); 1% of GGR is earmarked for RG and treatment; the Premier has said operators should fund addiction treatment. AML/KYC runs to FINTRAC standards with sanctions screening, account-sharing detection and continuous geolocation.
- **Payments.** AiGC publishes no deposit-method data. Payment providers and e-wallets must register with AGLC as 'supplier–other' (C$3,000/yr). Canadian regulated iGaming is Interac e-Transfer-led with cards and wallets secondary [industry norm, not quantified by the regulator]; the SRIG position on credit-card and crypto deposits was not verified in this research.
- **Governance and politics.** Lead ministry is Service Alberta and Red Tape Reduction (Minister Nally). AGLC is simultaneously regulator and operator of Play Alberta — a conflict flagged by trade press — and a bill tabled in May 2026 could enable a sale of the Play Alberta platform/customer data. Budget 2026 assumes AGLC gaming and lottery net income of C$1,577m (~$1,126m) in 2026-27 rising to C$1,635m by 2028-29 and total AGLC net income ~C$2.4bn/yr — land-based cannibalisation by online is not visibly modelled.
- **Benchmark.** Ontario (≈3x Alberta's population) generated FY2024-25 wagers of C$82.7bn and GGR of C$3.2bn (iGO) and >C$4bn GGR in calendar 2025 on C$98.3bn wagers (+34% YoY). Alberta's September NAGGR equals roughly 17% of Ontario's monthly GGR on ~1/3 of the population — consistent with a market in month three of channelisation, with headroom as the roster fills.
- **Read-through for an operator.** Fundamentals are attractive: casino-led mix, 18+ age, high-income province, ~22% effective take, negligible fee drag (C$150k ≈ 0.5% of an average operator's annualised Sep NAGGR), Ontario-compatible compliance stack and transparent monthly data. Constraints are commercial and political: 50 sites chasing ~5m people, promotions pushed only through owned channels (so NAGGR flatters net revenue), an imminent advertising review, undefined 'net revenue' base, and no operator-level data to track share. Late entrants need differentiated product (live, P2P/poker once liquidity rules settle) or Ontario scale; sports-only models face thin, volatile economics.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Sports & event betting

*A 16% minority product with volatile hold (4.6%→7.3%) — September's C$11.1m already exceeds Play Alberta's entire FY2024-25 sports take.*

### Q3 handle and hold

**Licensed books took C$325.8m (~$232.7m) handle and C$20.1m (~$14.4m) NAGGR over 80 days, a volatile 6.2% blended hold.**

- July (19 days, final World Cup week): C$60.9m handle, C$3.8m NAGGR, 6.2% hold.
- August, baseball-heavy and low-margin: C$112.2m handle, C$5.2m NAGGR, 4.6% hold.
- September with NFL, CFL and college football: C$152.7m handle (+36% MoM), C$11.1m NAGGR (+114%), 7.3% hold.
- Betting's NAGGR share swung 17.3% → 12.1% → 19.5% while its wager share stayed 11–12%.
- Vertical earns roughly 1.7x casino's margin per dollar staked but on a fraction of the turnover.

### Hold outlook

**The 6.2% hold trails the 8–10% parlay-heavy US books print; we expect Q4 to normalise toward 7–8%.**

- Low hold points to a straight-bet/moneyline-weighted early mix and aggressive launch pricing.
- Inducements may only be marketed on-site or via opted-in CRM, so boosts and bet credits are spent inside apps.
- AiGC's 'non-adjusted' metric does not strip out promotions.
- Q4 catalysts: NHL (Oilers, Flames), NFL mid-season and the Grey Cup in Calgary on 15 Nov 2026.
- Betting's NAGGR share should oscillate between ~15% in quiet months and ~20% in football months.

### Eligible category and baseline

**September's C$11.1m alone exceeded Play Alberta's entire FY2024-25 sports net sales of C$8.0m (~$5.7m), 3% of its total.**

- Category covers traditional sports, esports and novelty markets with single-event, live, pool and parlay bets.
- Single-event wagering has been lawful in Canada since the 2021 Criminal Code amendment.
- Peer-to-peer sports betting (exchanges) is a separate eligible category with no Q3 activity — a white space.
- Baseline underlines how much sports value was offshore before launch.

### Demographics and acquisition

**An 18+ age floor (Ontario is 19+) widens the young-male cohort, but acquisition is constrained and share will likely concentrate.**

- Athletes may appear only in responsible-gambling messaging; public bonus advertising is banned.
- Licensees are exposed if affiliates also promote unlicensed sites.
- Growth leans on brand, media and league partnerships (bet365 is a CFL partner in Ontario and Alberta), sponsorship and CRM.
- Operator-level handle shares are not published (n/a).
- Mature-market precedent suggests FanDuel/DraftKings/bet365/BetMGM/theScore concentrate share, leaving sports-only challengers thin, volatile economics unless they cross-sell casino.

### Regulatory watch items

**Any advertising tightening is likely to land hardest on sportsbook marketing; prediction-market treatment is unaddressed.**

- The Premier's 25 Oct comments about ad saturation were anchored in sports broadcasts.
- US-style event-contract/prediction-market products are not an Eligible iGame under the Act.
- Whether AGLC treats such offers to Albertans as unlicensed gambling has not been addressed in any source reviewed.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Online casino & live casino

*Casino is where the value sits — 78% of NAGGR at a steady ~4.2% hold; live dealer is under-indexed at 5% and shrinking in share.*

### RNG casino performance

**RNG casino took C$2,275.2m (~$1,625m) of wagers and C$95.5m (~$68.2m) NAGGR, a 4.20% hold — the market's profit pool.**

- Monthly wagers: C$422.4m → C$849.6m (+101%) → C$1,003.2m (+18%).
- Monthly NAGGR: C$17.0m → C$35.7m → C$42.8m (+19.9% in September).
- Hold edged up 4.02% → 4.20% → 4.27%, consistent with a slots-dominant mix recycling balances at 4–5% of turnover.
- Casino's NAGGR share was 76.4% in July, peaked at 82.3% in August when sports hold collapsed, settled at 75.6% in September.
- Category covers slots, progressive jackpots, tables, poker and keno.

### Live casino

**Live casino made C$284.1m (~$203m) wagers and C$6.6m (~$4.7m) NAGGR at a stable 2.3% hold but is under-indexed at 5%.**

- Share of market NAGGR fell 6.3% → 5.6% → 4.9% as RNG-centric brands came online.
- Mature online-casino markets give live dealer a materially larger slice of casino GGR than 5%, so Alberta looks under-penetrated.
- Opening for live-led brands and studios licensing content via AGLC-registered platform suppliers (C$15,000/yr) and test labs (C$3,000/yr).

### Channelisation vs Play Alberta

**September casino-plus-live NAGGR of C$45.6m annualises to ~C$547m, roughly 2.5x the state site's annual casino take within three months.**

- Play Alberta FY2024-25 casino net sales were C$197.3m (~$141m, 73% of its C$269.9m), live dealer C$21.4m, instants C$27.8m.
- Government pre-launch estimate: only 23–32% of online play was regulated.
- Offshore volume is moving onshore quickly, albeit with some demand creation from 24 brands marketing simultaneously.

### Operator unit economics

**Public take of ~22.4% of GGR (estimate) plus GST and C$150,000 per site; promo-adjusted revenue likely 20–35% below NAGGR in year one.**

- Launch-phase promotional intensity is invisible in NAGGR (analyst estimate on the 20–35% gap).
- Blended ARPPA of C$146 (~$104) in September is dragged down by low-value sports accounts; casino ARPPA is likely higher.
- Poker sits inside the casino category and is confined to Alberta liquidity unless an inter-jurisdictional pooling framework emerges.
- Supreme Court of Canada heard Ontario's international-liquidity reference in October 2026; outcome pending, details not verified here.

### Vertical risks

**Risks are regulatory rather than demand-side and do not change the conclusion that casino, not sports, is Alberta's profit pool.**

- Mandatory RG tools and centralised self-exclusion.
- Prohibition on marketing that 'promotes excessive play' or implies skill affects outcomes.
- Game-certification throughput via accredited test facilities.
- Political temperature around gambling harm; the Premier wants operators to fund treatment.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Legacy market: Play Alberta, land-based & lottery

*A C$1.5bn-net-income AGLC estate now competes with — and may be cannibalised by — the open online market; AGLC's dual role and a possible Play Alberta sale are governance overhangs.*

### AGLC estate

**AGLC FY2024-25 gaming net sales were C$2,133.7m (~$1,524m) and gaming net income C$1,486.9m (~$1,062m), flat on FY2023-24 (~C$1,486m).**

- Slots, online and lottery grew while VLT net sales declined; the province-wide VLT fleet was replaced.
- Estate: slots in roughly 30 casinos, a provincial VLT network, lottery via Western Canada Lottery Corporation, bingo, raffles, pull-tickets, PlayAlberta.ca.
- Budget 2026 projects AGLC gaming and lottery net income of C$1,577m (~$1,126m) in 2026-27 rising to C$1,635m by 2028-29.
- Total AGLC net income (gaming, liquor, cannabis) steady at ~C$2.4bn (~$1.7bn) a year.

### Play Alberta

**Play Alberta grew to C$269.9m (~$193m) net sales in FY2024-25 (+15%) yet captured only ~23–32% of provincial online gambling.**

- Net sales rose from C$179m in FY2022-23 to C$235m in FY2023-24.
- FY2024-25 mix: casino C$197.3m (73.1%), instants C$27.8m, live dealer C$21.4m, lottery C$15.5m, sports C$8.0m.
- The 23–32% capture is the origin of the '70% unregulated' figure.
- Play Alberta continues as AGLC's own brand and appears in AiGC's registered-site directory.
- Whether its volumes are consolidated into AiGC's 'operators with an active operating agreement' series is not stated and flagged [unverified].

### Governance overhangs

**AGLC regulates the open market while operating a competing brand, and May 2026 legislation would permit a Play Alberta sale.**

- Ontario avoided this conflict by separating AGCO and iGO from OLG.
- Sale of Play Alberta's platform and customer data could create a privileged, pre-channelised asset for an acquirer.
- Land-based licensees cannot offer or advertise iGaming inducements or link the Winner's Edge retail loyalty scheme to online play.
- This protects the retail estate but blunts omnichannel strategies for Indigenous Gaming Partners, Pure Canadian Gaming and Great Canadian.

### Cannibalisation and First Nations

**Cannibalisation of slots and VLTs by online casino is the unmodelled fiscal risk with online NAGGR already running ~C$680m a year.**

- Budget 2026 assumes flat-to-rising land-based net income.
- The 2% of GGR earmarked for First Nations initiatives is separate from the existing land-based First Nations Development Fund arrangements.
- Allocation mechanics have not been published; Indigenous-owned operators or partnerships may seek a role — a watch item.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, revenue share & fees

*Ontario's template with a 3% top-line carve-out: ~22% effective public take, C$150k/site/year, 18+, and a 'net revenue' base that is not publicly defined.*

### Legal architecture

**AiGC is the legal operator of every 'Eligible iGame' and private brands are contracted suppliers, mirroring iGaming Ontario.**

- Framework: iGaming Alberta Act (SA 2025; Bill 48, passed spring 2025, in force January 2026) plus Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act amendments (RSA 2000).
- Section 207 of the Criminal Code reserves conduct and management of lottery schemes to provinces.
- AGLC registers operators and suppliers and sets the ~85-page SRIG (published 14 Jan 2026, revised 18 Jun 2026 to absorb advertising rules).
- Enforcement runs to fines up to loss of registration.

### Entry process and fees

**Entry is a three-part AGLC application plus negotiated AiGC agreement; fees are C$50,000 one-time and C$150,000 per site annually.**

- Application: corporate and personal due diligence, compliance/attestation package, technical integration with the centralised Self-Exclusion Program.
- Registration opened 13 Jan 2026; launch 13 Jul 2026; grey-market transition ended 13 Oct 2026.
- C$50,000 (~$36k) non-refundable operator application fee includes due-diligence costs; C$150,000 (~$107k) annual registration per site, so multi-skin groups pay per brand.
- Platform providers and critical gaming systems pay C$15,000 (~$11k) a year; 'other' suppliers C$3,000 (~$2k); no supplier application fee.
- No local-ownership, local-server or local-partner requirements in any source reviewed; registration is annual.

### Public take

**3% of GGR is carved off first, then 'net iGaming revenue' splits 80/20 — an effective ~22.4% public take (our estimate).**

- Carve-out: 2% to First Nations initiatives, 1% to social-responsibility and problem-gambling programmes.
- Take is embedded in AiGC agreements, not levied by a tax statute.
- Unverified: whether 'net' is struck after promotional credits/bonuses (AiGC's metric is explicitly 'non-adjusted').
- Unverified: treatment of federal GST on gaming supplies.
- Players face no withholding; recreational gambling winnings are not taxed in Canada.

### Ontario comparison

**Alberta matches Ontario's 20% headline but adds the 3% carve-out, higher per-site fee, age 18 and stricter day-one rules.**

- Ontario's AGCO fee is lower per site [not re-verified here].
- Alberta launched with centralised self-exclusion and inducement/athlete advertising limits; Ontario added athlete restrictions in 2024 and lacks centralised exclusion.
- Ontario's first year ended with 45 operators and 76 sites; Alberta has 29 operators and 50 sites after three months.
- AiGC guides to 60+ sites in 2027.

### Fiscal forecast

**Government's C$76m (~$54m) year-one take implies ~C$390m GGR and looks conservative by roughly half.**

- Applying the formula to Q3 NAGGR yields ~C$27.4m (~$19.6m) in 80 days and ~C$12.7m in September (~C$152m annualised).
- This mirrors Ontario's early under-forecasting.
- Licence cost is immaterial: C$150k is ~0.5% of annualised September NAGGR of an average-sized licensee.
- Binding items are the revenue-share base, GST, promotional intensity and marketing rules.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Advertising, responsible gambling, payments & enforcement

*Day-one rules are the tightest in Canada and the Premier has signalled they may tighten further; enforcement against the offshore 70% is unproven.*

### Advertising rules

**The 18 Jun 2026 SRIG update, summarised by AiGC on 4 Aug 2026, sets Canada's tightest day-one advertising rulebook.**

- No marketing of inducements, bonuses or credits except on the operator's own registered site or to players with express consent.
- No targeting minors, self-excluded or vulnerable people; no cartoons, influencers or celebrities appealing to minors; no billboards near schools or youth venues.
- Athletes only in responsible-gambling messaging; no claims of financial security, skill altering RNG outcomes, chasing losses or 'excessive play'.
- Licensees accountable for affiliates, including those promoting unlicensed sites; land-based licensees may not offer or advertise iGaming inducements.
- Non-compliance carries fines or loss of registration.

### Political direction

**The Premier has signalled further restriction; operators should front-load brand investment and budget for a rule change in H1 2027.**

- 26 Jul 2026: Premier Danielle Smith said gambling websites should pay for addiction treatment.
- 25 Oct 2026: she said Albertans complained of being 'bombarded' by gambling ads and rule changes may be coming.
- Plausible instruments: volume or watershed limits, sports-broadcast caps, mandatory RG-message share, or an RG levy above the current 1% of GGR.

> “bombarded” — Premier Danielle Smith

### Responsible gambling and KYC

**RG architecture is comparatively strong, led by centralised self-exclusion live at launch and 1% of GGR funding treatment.**

- Self-exclusion blocks all iGaming sites, all land-based venues, or all gambling in one step; it is a condition of registration.
- 1% of GGR ≈ C$1.2m from Q3 and ≈ C$0.6m in September alone, on our estimate.
- Limit-setting and RG tools mandated by SRIG; minimum age 18.
- KYC/AML to FINTRAC standards with sanctions screening, account-sharing detection and continuous geolocation monitoring.

### Payments

**AiGC publishes no deposit-method or transaction data (n/a), but processors must register as 'supplier–other' at C$3,000/yr.**

- Supplier registration gives the regulator a lever over the payment rails.
- Canadian regulated iGaming is Interac e-Transfer-led with cards, Apple/Google Pay and Paysafe-type wallets secondary [industry norm; not quantified].
- SRIG's position on credit-card deposits and crypto (Stake entered via Stake Canada Ltd) was not verified and should be confirmed.

### Enforcement

**Enforcement against the offshore 70% is unproven; targets are achievable only if AGLC acts against named holdouts within two quarters.**

- Transition closed 13 Oct 2026; unregistered sites must stop accepting Alberta wagers.
- AGLC says it will monitor and act; AiGC's CEO says the corporation 'has levers', but no toolkit is published.
- Possible tools: payment blocking, ISP blocking, affiliate or ad-platform liability, legal action.
- Ontario suggests channelisation stalls at 80–85% without active payment and advertising enforcement; Alberta targets 70%/75%.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale and ramp: NAGGR of C$122.1m (~$87.2m) in 80 days with September at C$56.6m (+30.7% MoM) annualising ~C$680m — about 1.7x the GGR implied by government's C$76m year-one take — before the roster has filled (24 active of 29 approved operators) and before NHL/NFL peak months. _(structural)_
- Casino-led profit pool: 78% of NAGGR from RNG casino at a steady ~4.2% hold, an 18+ age floor and a high-income province give higher-LTV, lower-volatility economics than sports-led markets; live casino is under-indexed at 5% and a clear gap for specialists. _(structural)_
- Low fiscal drag: ~22.4% effective public take (20% of net + 3% GGR carve-out, est.), C$150,000/site/year (~0.5% of an average licensee's annualised NAGGR), no supplier application fees, no local-ownership or server rules, no player withholding — cheaper than Pennsylvania/Michigan-style iCasino rates and most European multi-tier regimes. _(structural)_
- Channelisation runway: ~70% of online play was offshore pre-launch; AiGC targets 70% regulated after year one and 75% by year two, the grace period closed 13 Oct 2026 and affiliates/payment providers sit inside AGLC's registration perimeter — tailwinds for licensed brands if enforcement is visible. _(early)_
- Product white space: Fantasy Sports and Peer-to-Peer (exchange betting, poker) are already eligible categories with zero reported activity, and live dealer's share is falling as RNG brands launch — first movers can own these verticals. _(early)_
- Ontario-compatible compliance stack and transparent monthly regulator data (Excel, third week of each month) cut entry cost for operators already live in Ontario; the 22-sites-on-day-one launch shows AGLC/AiGC can process registrations at pace. _(structural)_
- Seasonal lift ahead: NFL mid-season, the NHL (Oilers, Flames) and the Grey Cup in Calgary on 15 Nov 2026 should lift Q4 sportsbook handle and push hold toward 7–8% from Q3's 6.2%. _(one-off)_

**Lowlights**

- Advertising clampdown risk: Premier Smith (25 Oct 2026) said Albertans are complaining about ad saturation and rules may change; with inducement ads already banned, the next step is volume/watershed or sports-broadcast limits — hitting late entrants' ability to build brand. _(governance)_
- Crowded field and hidden promo burn: 50 approved sites for a ~5m-person province; because bonuses can only be pushed on-site/CRM, acquisition costs are invisible in AiGC's 'non-adjusted' GGR, so operator net revenue is likely 20–35% below reported NAGGR in year one (estimate). _(structural)_
- Regulator-operator conflict: AGLC both regulates the market and runs the competing Play Alberta brand, and May 2026 legislation could enable a sale of Play Alberta's platform/customer data — a privileged, pre-channelised asset for whoever buys it. _(governance)_
- Opaque revenue-share base: 'net iGaming revenue' is not publicly defined (promo deductibility, GST treatment unknown) and the 3% GGR carve-out is a top-line levy regardless of margin; the government's own C$76m forecast is already stale, inviting a re-cut of the split if the take overshoots. _(watch)_
- Sports economics are thin and volatile: betting is only 16% of NAGGR with hold swinging 4.6% (Aug) to 7.3% (Sep); sports-first entrants without casino cross-sell face marginal unit economics under a ~22% take and tight marketing rules. _(mixed)_
- Data limitations: figures are unaudited, NAGGR is pre-promotion, 'active accounts' are not unique players and no operator-level data is published — share tracking and penetration analysis are impossible from regulator data alone. _(structural)_
- Legal and federal overhangs: the Supreme Court of Canada's pending ruling on Ontario's international liquidity-pooling reference governs whether P2P poker/DFS can pool beyond Alberta; US-style prediction-market contracts are outside the Act with no stated AGLC position; and the Premier's push for operators to fund addiction treatment could translate into a higher RG levy. _(watch)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The 'guidance bridge' here is fiscal: Minister Nally's year-one forecast of C$76m (~$54m) in provincial proceeds implied ~C$390m of first-year GGR, whereas the regulator's first three months printed C$122.1m of NAGGR with September at C$56.6m — a run-rate that, through the published 3%-then-20% formula, yields ~C$12.7m of public take per month (~C$152m annualised). Third-party estimates attributed to H2 Gambling Capital (C$850m to C$1.2bn year-one GGR; conflicting, unverified at source) bracket the upside. Our own 12-month estimate (13 Jul 2026–12 Jul 2027) is C$740–810m of NAGGR assuming 3–5% monthly growth as the roster expands from 24 to ~40 live sites and offshore volume channelises, implying public take of ~C$165–180m — more than double budget.

- **Gov't year-one GGR implied by C$76m take:** ~C$390m (~$279m) (baseline)
- **Q3-26 actual NAGGR (80 days):** C$122.1m (~$87.2m) (31% of implied FY1 GGR in 22% of the year)
- **Sep-26 run-rate annualised:** ~C$680m (~$485m) (+74% vs implied FY1)
- **Our FY1 estimate (3–5% monthly growth):** C$740–810m (~$530–580m) NAGGR; public take ~C$165–180m (est.; +90–110% vs implied FY1)

**Revenue:** we expect monthly NAGGR of C$75–90m by mid-2027 (estimate), with casino holding 75–80% of the mix, sports oscillating 15–20% by season and live casino recovering toward 7–8% as specialists launch; active accounts should pass 500,000 by Q1 2027 but ARPPA will plateau near C$150–170 as lower-value sports accounts dilute the base. **Margins:** operator net revenue will trail NAGGR by 20–35% in year one on launch promotions, then converge as the inducement-ad ban caps above-the-line spend; the ~22% effective take, GST and C$150k/site leave mid-teens EBITDA margins achievable only for top-eight-share operators — expect exits or skin consolidation among the 50 approved sites from H2 2027. **Rules:** the direction of travel is tighter, not looser — we assign a high probability to new advertising volume/placement limits in H1 2027, a meaningful probability of a higher RG levy or treatment-funding obligation, and a decision on Play Alberta's future (sale or repositioning) within 12 months; the revenue split itself is unlikely to be reopened before the first annual AiGC report, but an over-delivering take versus budget raises the risk. **Enforcement** against named offshore holdouts in the next two quarters is the single biggest determinant of whether the 70%/75% channelisation targets — and the upside half of our range — are met.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- October 2026 Market Performance Report (due third week of November): first full month after the 13 Oct grace-period close — look for operator count moving from 24 toward 29+, sports share with NFL/NHL, and whether Fantasy/P2P appear.
- Premier Smith's advertising review: any AGLC bulletin or SRIG amendment on ad volume, watershed or sports-broadcast limits (H1 2027 risk window) and any change to the 1% RG allocation.
- AGLC enforcement actions against unregistered sites, payment processors or affiliates — the credibility test for 70%/75% channelisation.
- Play Alberta: progress of the May 2026 bill enabling a sale of the platform/customer data, and whether AiGC clarifies if Play Alberta volumes are inside its reported series.
- Supreme Court of Canada decision on Ontario's international liquidity-pooling reference — determines P2P poker/DFS viability in Alberta.
- Alberta Budget 2027 (Feb 2027): first official iGaming revenue line against the C$76m forecast, and any sign of land-based slot/VLT cannibalisation in AGLC's net-income forecast.
- Definition and audit of the revenue-share base (promo deductibility, GST) in AiGC's first annual report; First Nations 2% allocation mechanics.
- Roster additions toward Keene's 60+ sites and early consolidation signals among sub-scale skins.
