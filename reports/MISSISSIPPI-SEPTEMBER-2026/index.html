Mississippi

Released 16 Oct 2026 (MGC September 2026 monthly reports; CDC Gaming same day; Gaming Intelligence 19 Oct 2026) · Currency USD (tables in millions unless stated) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

September 2026 was a good month for a flat market: Mississippi's commercial casinos won $195.6m (+4.5% YoY), driven entirely by the Gulf Coast (+7.7%) and a table-games hold bounce, while slots (+2.4%) merely stabilised and the river markets kept shrinking. The structural story is unchanged — a $2.4bn, 100% land-based, retail-only market whose sports book just posted a $34.0m handle (-12.9%) at a 10.6% hold, taxed at ~12% of GGR, and whose Senate killed statewide mobile wagering (HB 4074) for a third straight session; for an online-led operator the only addressable route in remains a casino partnership option on legislation that has not yet passed.

1Executive summary

  • Headline: GGR $195.6m in September 2026, +4.5% YoY — the strongest monthly growth print of 2026 after a -6.5% August; the regulator-reported base is Sep-25 $187.2m (Mississippi Gaming Commission via CDC Gaming/Gaming Intelligence). Gaming Intelligence rounds the increase to 4%; the arithmetic on MGC figures is +4.5%.
  • Market is 100% land-based retail; online penetration is 0% — statewide mobile wagering is prohibited; the only 'online' activity is geofenced on-premises sportsbook apps. Every dollar in the MGC print is cage/floor revenue at 25–26 commercial casinos (AGA 2025 count: 25) plus three Choctaw tribal casinos that report nothing publicly.
  • Product mix: slots 80%, tables 16%, sports 2% — September: EGD win $157.3m (+2.4%), table win $31.3m (+36.8%), retail sports GGR $3.6m (~-47%). Component reports (device-level, modified accrual) do not sum exactly to the headline AGR (~$3.4m residual), which is a reporting-basis difference, not an 'other' product.
  • Year-to-date 2026 is flat at best: summing the nine MGC monthly prints gives ~$1,814m for Jan–Sep 2026 vs an implied ~$1,828m in 2025 (≈-0.7%, our estimate). Monthly YoY: Jan +1.9%, Feb +5.8%, Mar -5.3%, Apr +0.2%, May -2.6%, Jun -1.5%, Jul -0.9%, Aug -6.5%, Sep +4.5%.
  • Full-year 2025 (AGA State of the States 2026, MGC data): GGR $2.43bn, unchanged — EGD $2.02bn (+0.2%), table games $317.7m (-4.8% vs $333.7m), sports $49.8m (+19.7%); Gulf Coast $1.59bn (+0.5%) ≈ 65% of state; river markets ≈ $0.84bn and falling. Casino tax revenue 2025: $287.9m.
  • Coast carries the state: Coastal region $134.2m (+7.7%; slots $106.4m +4.8%, tables $23.8m +33%) = 69% of September GGR; Northern (Tunica/Lula) $38.7m (+2.1%; slots flat at $30.9m, tables +35.5% to $5.7m); Central (Vicksburg/Natchez/Greenville) $22.7m (-8.1%; slots -5.5% to $20.0m) — the only region down.
  • Sports betting is shrinking in absolute and relative terms: handle $34.0m (-12.9% vs $39.1m), GGR $3.6m vs $6.85m, hold 10.6% vs a 17.5% outlier last September; state/local tax ~$435k vs $753k (-42%). Coastal handle $24.1m (70.9%), Central $6.3m (-26.9%, GGR $835k, -45.7%), Northern ~$3.6m (derived).
  • Cross-border leakage is now measurable: Louisiana's mobile books took $384.5m in September (+4.6%) — 11.3x Mississippi's entire handle — and paid $8.88m in tax vs ~$435k here; Louisiana retail handle fell 39% to $20.6m, confirming that mobile cannibalises retail counters in both states.
  • Tax base is GGR; effective rate ~12%: state levy graduated 4% (first $50k/month), 6% (next $84k), 8% (above $134k) — i.e. 8% marginal for every real casino — plus local gross-revenue levies of up to 3.2% (Biloxi, Coahoma, D'Iberville, Adams County) and per-device fees ($100–$150/device/yr). Sports wagering is taxed at the same state and local rates, plus the 0.25% federal excise on handle (AGA). No separate sports-betting tax schedule exists.
  • Tax take: DOR FY2025 (Jul-24–Jun-25) gaming taxes $290.9m — $155.4m to the State General Fund, $36.0m to the highway bond sinking fund ($3m/month fixed), the balance (~$99m) to local governments. FY2026 ran at ~$23.6m/month through February ($188.6m in 8 months); September 2025 collection month $23.9m. FY2027 (Jul–Sep 2026) collections: n/a — DOR PDF not retrievable this session.
  • Licensing: the Mississippi Gaming Control Act (Miss. Code §75-76) restricts casinos to Gulf Coast (land-based within the coastal zone post-Katrina) and Mississippi River counties; licences are site-based, three-year, with suitability findings by the three-member Commission; DOR also charges an annual fee based on games on the floor ($81,200 + $100 per game above 35). No online, skin or standalone sportsbook licence class exists. Property-level GGR is confidential by law, so operator market shares cannot be published.
  • Operator landscape (from public ownership records, not MGC share data): Coast — MGM (Beau Rivage), Hard Rock, Golden Nugget/Fertitta, Boyd (IP), Caesars (Harrah's Gulf Coast), Penn (Boomtown, Hollywood Gulf Coast), Full House (Silver Slipper), independents (Scarlet Pearl, Palace, Island View, Treasure Bay); Tunica — Cherokee Nation Ent. (Gold Strike), Caesars (Horseshoe), Penn (Hollywood, 1st Jackpot), Boyd (Sam's Town), Foundation (Fitz); river south — Penn (Ameristar), Churchill Downs (Riverwalk), Bally's (Vicksburg), Saratoga (Magnolia Bluffs). Sportsbooks are run via casino partners (e.g. BetMGM, Caesars, FanDuel, DraftKings kiosks/apps on-property) [ownership list unverified in-session].
  • Mobile legislation failed a third straight year: HB 4074 (Mississippi Mobile Sports Wagering Act) passed the House 100–11 on 25 Feb 2026 — one online skin per licensed casino, 22% tax on online GGR, state casino tax cut from 8% to 6% as a sweetener, launch no later than 8 Dec 2026, proceeds earmarked to PERS — then died in Senate Gaming/Finance on 17 Mar 2026 without a hearing. HB 1581 (85–31) died the same way; 2025's HB 1302 died in conference.
  • Sweepstakes/'social casino' ban also stalled: SB 2104 (2026) missed the 3 March crossover deadline, leaving Mississippi with no express statute on dual-currency sweepstakes models [single secondary source; unverified]. The grey market is therefore competing with retail casinos untaxed, which is the strongest argument legislators have for regulating online.
  • Player taxation tightened/aligned: DOR withholds 3% at source on gaming winnings; from 1 Jan 2026 the slot withholding threshold rose from $1,200 to $2,000 (DOR notice re-issued 28 Sep 2026), tracking the federal W-2G change — operationally positive for hand-pay frequency on the floor.
  • Payments reality: cash, TITO and cage/ATM card access; sportsbook apps must be funded and used on-property; there is no regulated online deposit ecosystem, no e-wallet or ACH framework for remote play, and no public MGC rule on cashless wagering that we could verify this session (n/a).
  • Responsible gambling / advertising: minimum age 21; MGC administers a statewide self-exclusion list and patron-dispute process; wagering on high-school sports is prohibited and the MGC may prohibit any wager under Reg. Part 9 Rule 3.11 (PEER). There is no online-specific advertising code because there is no online market; casino and sportsbook marketing is governed by general MGC regulations.
  • Political direction: House leadership (Ways & Means, Rep. Casey Eure) is firmly pro-mobile; the Senate Gaming chair has blocked every bill on cannibalisation and tourism grounds. With Louisiana, Tennessee and Arkansas all mobile, and Alabama the only other dark neighbour, pressure builds each football season — but the Senate veto point, not public opinion, is the binding constraint.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: there is no licensable online product today; the realistic 2027–28 entry is as a B2B platform/skin partner to a casino licensee if an HB 4074-style bill passes (22% tax, casino-tethered, single skin per licence). Retail entry would require acquiring an existing property in a flat market where the Coast grows ~0–2% and river markets decline 1–8% a year. Treat Mississippi as an option, not a core market.

3Deep-dive: Casino floor — slots and table games (retail)

Slots stabilised (+2.4%) and tables bounced (+36.8%) in September, but 2025 was flat and 2026 YTD is ~-0.7%; the floor is a mature, Coast-weighted, zero-growth asset.

September slots and tables

Slots won $157.3m (+2.4% on $153.6m) and tables $31.3m (+36.8%) in September 2026; slots stabilised, tables benefited from hold.

  • Slots are ~80% of statewide GGR; 2025 full-year win of $2.02bn was up just 0.2% after -1.5% in 2024.
  • A +2.4% month is a stabilisation rather than a recovery.
  • Tables were -4.4% in September 2025 and -4.8% for full-year 2025 ($317.7m vs $333.7m).
  • Regional detail (Coastal +33%, Northern +35.5%, Central +125% off a $0.8m base) signals favourable baccarat/blackjack hold across all regions.
  • Base discrepancy: last year's print showed September 2025 tables at $23.9m (~+31%); the +36.8% implies a revised $22.9m base. Analyst quotes regulator's change and flags the gap.

Reconciling to headline GGR

The $195.6m adjusted gross gaming revenue comes from tax-return data, while slot and table win use a modified-accrual basis.

  • Slots + tables + sports = $192.2m, leaving a ~$3.4m residual (last September ~$2.9m); not a hidden product line.
  • 2025 AGA/MGC mix: EGD 83%, tables 13%, sports 2%, residual ~2%.

Year-to-date trend

Nine 2026 monthly prints sum to ~$1,814m versus ~$1,828m for Jan–Sep 2025, roughly -0.7%.

  • Three of nine months up (Jan +1.9%, Feb +5.8%, Sep +4.5%); six down, worst August (-6.5%) and March (-5.3%, lapping $226.2m in March 2025).
  • On this run-rate, calendar 2026 lands at $2.40–2.45bn (analyst estimate), essentially a third consecutive flat year at $2.43bn.

Commercial implication

Organic floor growth is not available; share comes from capex, hotel inventory and the Coast's leisure/convention calendar.

  • The Coast's 12 properties posted their first annual floor-GGR increase in three years in 2025 (+0.5% to $1.59bn), the only growing sub-market.
  • Tunica is in structural decline since Arkansas (Southland) and Tennessee opened alternatives; Central's river properties are losing to Louisiana's mobile product.
  • An entrant buys a ~12% tax, 8%-marginal-state regime on a flat top line; maths only works at distressed multiples or with a sportsbook/online option.

4Deep-dive: Sports wagering — retail-only, casino-tethered

$34.0m handle (-12.9%) at a 10.6% hold produced $3.6m GGR and ~$435k of tax; the book is 2% of GGR, shrinking, and now dwarfed 11x by Louisiana's mobile market next door.

Regime and September results

Handle fell 12.9% to $34.0m at a 10.6% hold, producing $3.6m GGR and ~$435k tax versus $753k a year earlier.

  • Legalised August 2018 under existing Gaming Control Act authority; bets only inside a licensed casino at a counter/kiosk or geofenced on-property app.
  • Handle down from $39.1m; last September's 17.5% hold was the highest of 2025 and flattered the comparison.
  • September is historically the book's best month, so a double-digit handle decline at the seasonal peak is a meaningful signal, not noise.

Regional and cross-border

Central's handle fell 26.9% and GGR 45.7% while Louisiana's mobile handle reached $384.5m, 11.3x Mississippi's.

  • Coast wrote $24.1m (70.9% of handle); Central $6.3m with GGR of $835k (13.3% hold); Northern a derived ~$3.6m.
  • Louisiana's September mobile handle was $384.5m (+4.6%) with $8.88m tax, ~20x Mississippi's sports tax.
  • Louisiana's retail counters fell 39% to $20.6m; Mississippi's retail decline occurs without offsetting mobile revenue.

Product mix and history

2025 retail handle of $379.1m was parlay-heavy and football-led, driving a ~13% blended hold well above mobile-market norms.

  • 2025 split: football 31%, parlay cards 24%, basketball 21%, baseball 10%, other 14% (RG.org tabulation of MGC data).
  • Full-year 2025 sports GGR $49.8m (+19.7%, AGA) on broadly flat handle — a hold story that reversed in September 2026.
  • Lifetime through September 2025: $3.20bn handle, $352.7m revenue.

Rules and addressability

Same state (8%) and local (up to 3.2%+) taxes apply with no promotional deduction; the segment is not addressable for online operators today.

  • 0.25% federal excise on handle; high-school events prohibited; Part 9 Rule 3.11 gives catch-all authority to prohibit any wager.
  • No separate sports-wagering licence, skin licence or B2B platform licence beyond standard vendor/manufacturer approvals.
  • Only value is as the political wedge for HB 4074-style legislation: one statewide skin per casino at a 22% online tax.

5Deep-dive: Regional dynamics — Coast vs river markets and cross-border competition

The Gulf Coast (69% of GGR, +7.7%) is a destination leisure market still growing; Tunica (+2.1%) is stable at a lower base; Central (-8.1%) is being hollowed out by Louisiana mobile and regional supply.

Gulf Coast

Coastal generated $134.2m in September 2026 (+7.7%), 69% of GGR, and is the only sub-market where an acquirer could underwrite growth.

  • Slots $106.4m (+4.8%), tables $23.8m (+33%); full-year 2025 Coast GGR $1.59bn (+0.5%), first annual increase in three years.
  • Drive-in and fly-in resort market anchored by Beau Rivage, Hard Rock, Golden Nugget, IP and Scarlet Pearl; Biloxi sales-tax receipts at record levels.
  • Post-Katrina land-based zoning allows integrated-resort capex that barge-based river markets cannot match.

Northern (Tunica)

Northern produced $38.7m (+2.1%), but the trend is a decline with occasional hold-driven relief.

  • Slots flat at $30.9m, tables $5.7m (+35.5%); +2.1% follows -6.5% in June and -4.0% last September.
  • Tunica was once a $1bn+ market serving Memphis; lost roughly two-thirds to Southland, Tennessee mobile sportsbooks and closures (Resorts, Harrah's Tunica, Roadhouse).
  • Operators (Caesars Horseshoe, Cherokee Nation's Gold Strike, Penn's Hollywood/1st Jackpot, Boyd's Sam's Town, Foundation's Fitz) are managing for cash, not growth.

Central river markets

Central was the only region down at $22.7m (-8.1%), with the sports book collapse the most direct evidence of Louisiana leakage.

  • Slots $20.0m (-5.5%); sports handle -26.9% and sports GGR -45.7%.
  • Competes with Louisiana's land-based casinos and, since January 2022, statewide mobile apps.
  • No destination draw and no lever other than supply rationalisation.

Read for B2Tech

Coast assets alone have pricing power and tourism tailwind but are not for sale cheaply; river assets are cheap for a reason.

  • Coast owners are MGM, Hard Rock, Fertitta, Boyd, Caesars and Penn.
  • Under a casino-tethered mobile model, statewide geofencing would make the partner's region irrelevant to customer acquisition.
  • This is why Coast incumbents are lukewarm and the Senate, whose gaming chair cites cannibalisation of the physical tax base, has blocked it.

6Deep-dive: Licensing, tax and fiscal framework

A site-based casino licence regime with no online class; ~12% effective GGR tax (8% state marginal + ~3.2% local + device fees) yielding ~$290m a year; HB 4074 would have cut the state rate to 6% and taxed mobile at 22%.

Licensing regime

The 1990 Gaming Control Act gives a three-member Commission site-based licensing with no online class; 25 commercial casinos in 2025 per AGA.

  • Casinos permitted only in Gulf Coast counties and Mississippi River counties that voted them in; Coast land-based, river on vessels/barges.
  • Licences are property-specific with suitability findings for owners, key employees and major vendors; manufacturers and distributors separately licensed.
  • 26 casinos in 2024; current MGC count unverified; Choctaw's three IGRA casinos sit outside MGC data.
  • Property-level revenue is statutorily confidential; only regional aggregates published, no public market-share series.

Tax structure

State levy is graduated to 8% above $134,000 monthly GGR, plus ~3.2% local and device fees, about 12% all-in.

  • 4% on first $50,000, 6% on next $84,000, 8% above $134,000; remitted weekly with monthly reconciliation by the 20th.
  • Local levies: Biloxi 3.2% plus $150 per device; Coahoma 3.2% plus $150 per device; D'Iberville and Adams up to 3.2%; Bay St Louis $100 per device; AGA average 3–4%.
  • Biloxi's 3.2% splits 40% city general fund, 20% public safety, 20% city education, 10% county education, 10% county public safety.
  • DOR annual fee $81,200 plus $100 per game above 35; sports pays identical rates, no promotional deduction, plus 0.25% federal excise.
  • 3% withholding on winnings; slot threshold raised from $1,200 to $2,000 on 1 January 2026.

Fiscal yield

DOR FY2025 gaming taxes were $290.9m, with FY2026 tracking a flat ~$285–290m year.

  • FY2025: $155.4m to State General Fund, $36.0m ($3.0m/month) to highway bond sinking fund under 1994 HB 1302, ~$99m to local governments.
  • FY2026 collections $188.6m over July 2025–February 2026 (~$23.6m/month; September 2025 collection month $23.9m); AGA calendar-2025 figure $287.9m.
  • FY2027 (July–September 2026) collections could not be retrieved (n/a).
  • 11.2% ad valorem on September 2026's $195.6m implies ~$21.9m tax accrual (state ~$15.6m, local ~$6.3m), analyst estimate excluding device fees and bond top-up.

HB 4074 mobile package

HB 4074 passed the House 100–11 on 25 February 2026 but died in Senate committee on 17 March, the third consecutive failure.

  • Would have authorised statewide mobile sports wagering through licensed casinos, one platform per licence, taxed at 22%.
  • Would have cut the state casino rate from 8% to 6% as cannibalisation offset, required launch by 8 December 2026, and earmarked proceeds to PERS.
  • Referred to Senate Gaming and Finance on 12 March; HB 1302 (2025) died in conference; HB 1581 (85–31, February 2026) also died.
  • Design signal: casino-tethered, single-skin, 20%+ tax, no standalone online licence — a B2B/skin economics market.

7Deep-dive: Legislative direction, payments and responsible gambling

House pro-mobile, Senate opposed, sweepstakes unregulated: the rules are not moving in 2026, and the 2027 session (January–April) is the next window.

2026 session stalemate

The House passed HB 1581 (85–31) and HB 4074 (100–11) but the Senate Gaming chair declined to hear either before the 3 March crossover.

  • Senate objections centre on protecting the physical casino tax base and Coast tourism employment, and scepticism of 'tens of millions' fiscal projections.
  • The House offer (22% online rate, 2-point casino rate cut, PERS earmark) still failed, showing the veto is positional rather than negotiable on terms.
  • SB 2104 to criminalise dual-currency sweepstakes casinos missed the deadline; sweepstakes operators continue with no state tax or MGC oversight [single secondary source; unverified].
  • In 2025 the sweepstakes ban died when the House attached mobile language; the two issues are now politically linked.
  • The 2027 session (January–April) is the next window.

Enforcement posture

MGC enforcement is conventional casino-regulator policing with no evidence this session of action against offshore, sweepstakes or prediction-market platforms.

  • Investigations and Enforcement divisions police licensees, adjudicate patron disputes, run self-exclusion and take illegal-gaming reports; Part 9 gives discretion over sports wagers.
  • Absence of cease-and-desist activity contrasts with Louisiana, Tennessee and other neighbours [not verified — treat as a watch item].
  • Advertising governed by general MGC rules; no affiliate, bonus or marketing regime to comply with — or to build on.

Payments

Payments are a retail cash economy with no ACH/e-wallet/online card-deposit framework because there is no remote product.

  • Cage, ATM/debit at the cage, ticket-in/ticket-out, and sportsbook apps funded and used on-property within a geofence.
  • Any mobile authorisation would require MGC to write deposit, KYC, geolocation and payment-processor rules from scratch; HB 4074's December 2026 deadline implies compressed rulemaking.
  • Whether MGC has adopted cashless/digital-wallet floor rules could not be verified (n/a).
  • 3% withheld at source on reportable winnings; the 2026 $2,000 threshold reduces hand-pay frequency but not the levy.

Responsible gambling

RG architecture is minimal but present: age 21, statewide self-exclusion and a helpline, with no deposit limits, affordability checks or RG levy.

  • Mississippi Council on Problem and Compulsive Gambling operates the helpline [council details unverified in-session].
  • None of the 2026 mobile bills proposed RG conditions beyond standard self-exclusion and age-verification language.
  • Low-compliance-cost, low-political-cover environment; risk is a future mobile bill imports Senate-side RG conditions as the price of passage.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Legislative optionality on mobile is real and well-defined: the House has passed casino-tethered mobile sports wagering three sessions running (100–11 in 2026) with a known shape — one skin per casino, 22% online tax, statewide geofence. If the Senate blockage lifts in 2027, a B2B platform/skin deal with one of ~25 licensees is the entry route and the terms are already drafted. structural
  • The Coast is a genuine destination market with growth: Coastal GGR $134.2m in September (+7.7%), $1.59bn in 2025 (+0.5%, first increase in three years), ~69% of state GGR, land-based resort zoning and record Biloxi sales-tax receipts — the only Mississippi sub-market where an acquirer can underwrite a top line. structural
  • Low tax and low compliance burden by regulated-market standards: ~12% effective GGR tax (8% state marginal + ~3.2% local), no promotional-deduction fights, no RG levy, no affordability or deposit-limit mandates, and a regulator with discretionary (not list-based) wagering rules. structural
  • Quantified pent-up online demand: Louisiana's mobile books handled $384.5m in September (11.3x Mississippi's $34.0m) and paid $8.88m of tax versus ~$435k here; Central-region sports handle fell 26.9% as bettors crossed the line. Each football season sharpens the fiscal argument for the legislature. structural
  • September itself was a clean beat: GGR +4.5% YoY against a 2026 run-rate of ~-0.7%, with all three product lines on the floor positive (slots +2.4%, tables +36.8%) and two of three regions up — evidence the retail base is stable rather than eroding. one-off
  • Sweepstakes remain unbanned after SB 2104 stalled, so the political cost of inaction is rising; the House has already shown it will trade a sweepstakes ban for mobile authorisation, which is the most plausible grand-bargain path to a regulated online channel. watch

▼ Lowlights

  • No addressable online market and no licence class for one: statewide mobile is prohibited, online casino has never been seriously proposed, and the Senate Gaming chair has killed every House bill without a hearing for three sessions. Any Mississippi plan is contingent on a political outcome outside the operator's control. structural
  • Flat-to-declining top line: GGR $2.43bn in both 2024 and 2025, ~-0.7% YTD 2026 on our sum of monthly prints, with six of nine months down; river markets (31% of GGR) are in structural decline (Central -8.1% in September) and only hold volatility lifts tables. structural
  • Sports betting is small and shrinking: $34.0m September handle (-12.9%) at the seasonal peak, GGR $3.6m (-47%), hold normalising from 17.5% to 10.6%, and the book is only 2% of GGR — retail sports is not a growth product and the 2025 +19.7% GGR gain was a hold artefact. structural
  • Casino-tethered design caps upside: the only mobile model on the table gives each incumbent casino one skin and a 22% tax — nearly double the retail rate — so an entrant pays for access through revenue share to a licensee that may prefer its own brand (BetMGM, Caesars, Penn/ESPN Bet successors). Market access could be scarce and expensive. structural
  • Data opacity: property-level GGR is confidential by statute, the MGC publishes no operator shares, no active-player counts and no promotional spend, and even the headline component reports do not reconcile to the AGR print. Due diligence on any acquisition target relies on seller data. watch
  • Grey-market competition is untaxed and unpoliced: sweepstakes and offshore books serve Mississippi customers with no state action visible this session, eroding retail sports handle and giving a future regulated entrant a customer base already habituated to unregulated terms and bonuses. mixed
  • Table-games base revision: the MGC-reported +36.8% does not reconcile with last year's published $23.9m (implies ~+31%); minor, but it means month-on-month series from secondary sources should be re-based from the MGC archive before modelling. one-off

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. Nothing in the rulebook changed in the period: September's +4.5% GGR print is a floor-stabilisation and hold story, not a regime change, and the legislative bridge from 2025 to 2026 is flat — HB 4074 moved the House from 85–31 (HB 1581) to 100–11 but moved the Senate not at all. The moving parts for 2027 are (i) whether a casino-tethered mobile bill with a 22% online rate and a 2-point state casino tax cut can secure a Senate Gaming hearing, (ii) whether a sweepstakes ban is re-linked to it, and (iii) whether a fourth consecutive year of flat GGR and visible cross-border leakage (Louisiana 11x handle, Central sports -27%) shifts Senate leadership. On the market side the bridge is from $2.43bn (2025) to our estimate of $2.40–2.45bn (2026) — the Coast +0–2%, Tunica -2–4%, Central -5–8%, sports GGR falling back toward $40–45m as hold normalises.

Independent view. Our base case is no regulated online channel in Mississippi before 2028. The Senate veto has survived three sessions, a 100–11 House vote and a tax-cut sweetener; absent a change in Senate Gaming leadership or a budget shock, we assign roughly a one-in-three probability that a casino-tethered mobile sports bill passes in the 2027 session, with online casino not on the agenda at all. If it passes, expect a 22% online tax, single-skin-per-licence structure with ~25 skins in theory and perhaps 8–12 in practice, a 9–12-month MGC rulemaking, and a first-year statewide handle in the $1.0–1.5bn range (estimate, benchmarked at ~30–40% of Louisiana's per-capita run-rate) — material versus today's $0.4bn retail book but small in national terms. Retail GGR stays flat at ~$2.4bn with the Coast's share drifting above 65–67%; leverage on the fiscal side is low (the state's ~$290m take is steady) so there is no revenue pressure forcing the Senate's hand. The sweepstakes question is the swing factor: a sweepstakes ban re-attached to mobile authorisation is the only package that has moved both chambers' interests simultaneously. For B2Tech, the right posture is pre-positioning for a B2B/skin partnership with a Coast or Tunica licensee lacking a national online brand (Island View, Scarlet Pearl, Treasure Bay, Palace, Foundation Gaming, Cherokee's Gold Strike), with zero capital committed until a bill clears Senate committee.
