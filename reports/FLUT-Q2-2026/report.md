# Flutter Entertainment plc (FLUT) — Q2 2026 Analyst Report
**NYSE / LSE · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released 5 Aug 2026**
**Market reaction:** Shares closed -11.5% at $92.91 on 5 Aug 2026 (prior close $104.96; premarket -6.6% to $98.04, intraday low near $92.28), a whisker above the 52-week low of $91.52 and ~70% below the 52-week high of $313.69.

> A headline beat with a hollow core: revenue +3% and adj. EBITDA $508m landed above consensus, but the fourth consecutive guidance cut (FY26 adj. EBITDA to $2.655bn, -7% YoY) is entirely a US decision to spend ~$270m more on generosity in H2, taken on the day the CEO announced his exit. International is doing its job (+4% organic, SEA +18%) while absorbing the UK duty step-up; the US is being re-based for a second time in nine months, leverage is 4.3x and the proof point is now Q4 (US EBITDA ~$500m vs ~$700m previously).

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Headline beat, poor quality.** Q2 revenue $4,326m (+3% reported, +2% CC) and adj. EBITDA $508m (-45%) were ahead of consensus (secondary tallies: revenue c.$4.24-4.26bn, adj. EBITDA c.$485m) and $16m/$15m ahead of the company's own US guidance. But adj. EBITDA margin fell 1,020bps to 11.7%, GAAP operating profit swung to a $(144)m loss (Q2-25: +$389m) and the Group posted a $(296)m net loss vs +$37m. Adj. EPS of $0.49 (-83%) screened as a beat or a miss depending on provider ($0.39-0.55) — consensus was not well formed.
- **Fourth consecutive guidance cut, 100% US.** FY26 Group revenue midpoint cut $395m to $17.91bn (+9% YoY) and adj. EBITDA cut $210m to $2.655bn (-7% YoY, i.e. profit now falls in 2026). International is unchanged at $10.51bn/$2.205bn (Q2 beat offset by FX). US midpoint goes to $7.4bn revenue (from $7.795bn) and $760m adj. EBITDA (from $970m; from $1.05bn in February) — now -18% YoY versus +5% guided in May.
- **The cut is a choice, not a miss.** US EBITDA bridge: +$15m Q2 beat, +$50m market-making, +$45m operating cost savings, -$50m from the confirmed one-week NFL delay (Q3 only), -$270m 'net investment to strengthen the proposition and accelerate sportsbook momentum' (revenue -$385m). H2 promotional spend will run 'closer to 6%' of handle (H2-25: 5.6%) versus a previously assumed decline as the Rewards Club matured. Management has explicitly moved the US objective from margin growth to AMP and ARPU growth into 2027.
- **CEO transition.** Peter Jackson steps down after ~nine years; Dan Taylor (President since May, formerly CEO International) becomes CEO on 1 Oct 2026, with Christian Genetski running FanDuel. This is the third leadership change in two quarters. Continuity of strategy was promised on the call; the new CEO nonetheless owns the November cost-program detail and the 2027 US investment decision.
- **US sportsbook: volume ok, price down.** Handle +2% to $11.96bn on sportsbook AMPs +8%, but net revenue margin -170bps to 8.7%: structural margin +40bps to 14.0% (World Cup SGP penetration), sports results a -70bps YoY swing (in-quarter net revenue ~$21m unfavourable vs $90m favourable in Q2-25), and promo +140bps to 5.4% of handle. Sportsbook revenue -15% to $1,039m; management quantifies the results swing at 6pp of US growth.
- **US iGaming is the bright spot.** Revenue +14% to $577m, iGaming AMPs +14%, direct-casino AMPs +26%; GGR share 27% (28% incl. PokerStars US). High-teens FY26 growth reiterated with H2 > H1 on monthly exclusive content and better-than-expected World Cup cross-sell. Alberta (July) is outpacing the Ontario launch.
- **Market position held, market growth not.** FanDuel remains #1 in US OSB (39% GGR share) and iGaming (27%), both flat on Q1. Management estimates the US OSB market grew only ~5% in H1 and assumes the same in H2 — the Q4-25 churn has not reversed; FanDuel is buying reactivation in a flat-ish market.
- **World Cup engagement.** ~10.5m customers engaged group-wide; FanDuel 2.3m, a third of them reactivations; June and July were FanDuel's highest-ever active months; NBA Finals handle ~+40% per game, actives +25%; a record MLB week in late July. No comparable prior-tournament disclosure (2022 pre-dated US scale), so incremental revenue is not sizeable from the release. Early Q3 trading is 'ahead of expectations' on knockout-stage engagement and slightly favourable results.
- **International solid but tax-hit.** Revenue +10% to $2,643m (+7% CC, +4% organic); adj. EBITDA -19% to $476m (-21% CC, -22% organic), margin -670bps to 18.0%, driven by UK remote gaming duty 21%→40% (effective April; FY26 impact $320m, $85m first-order mitigation) and World Cup marketing. SEA +36% (+18% organic) is the engine; UKI +4%; CEE +23%; Brazil organic -14%; Other regions -38%.
- **Cost transformation phase 2.** $500m gross opex and capex savings by 2029, on top of phase 1 (>$300m by 2027, 'ahead of expectations') and ~$200m of UK-tax mitigation savings in 2027; Snai/Betnacional/PokerStars integration to reach $300m annualised run-rate savings by YE26. Cost to achieve guided below 1:1 with $100m in 2026 SDI and more in 2027-28; detail deferred to Q3 in November. Management frames the savings as offsetting inflation and known tax increases and funding reinvestment — i.e. not a margin-expansion program.
- **Below-the-line noise.** $95m legal loss contingencies (India GST provision $62m post Supreme Court ruling; US sales-and-use tax accrual $33m), interest expense +$52m to $162m (Snai/Betnacional financing and Boyd 5% FanDuel buyout), D&A +$30m to $399m, partly offset by a $40m Fox Option fair-value gain (Q2-25: -$81m loss) and a $171m lower tax charge. NCI swung to a $24m credit. Transaction/restructuring/integration cost guidance raised $200m to ~$500m.
- **Leverage 4.3x (3.7x at Dec-25)** despite net debt falling $111m to $10,480m — the move is entirely the LTM EBITDA denominator (~$2.44bn). Available cash ~$1.6bn (Dec-25: $1.8bn); total debt $11,978m after RCF repayment. Management expects H2 cash generation to cut leverage by YE26; our estimate is ~3.8-3.9x, still above Dec-25 and far from the 2.0-2.5x target. $250m H1 buyback completed 11 May (2.4m shares; $1.37bn cumulative since Q4-24); no new tranche.
- **Cash flow quality is weak.** OCF $363m (+1%) was propped by a $328m 'other liabilities' inflow (UK duty accrual, tax provisions) and a positive player-deposit swing; free cash flow incl. financing capex and ex player funds fell 56% to $125m (H1: $248m, -52%). Interest paid $228m (+$102m). Capex guidance trimmed $40m to $815m and D&A ex acquired intangibles to $730m.
- **Prediction markets re-plumbed.** FanDuel Predicts sports/novelty contracts move from CME to Crypto.com (CME retained for financials; 'modest' economic benefit), with a nationwide 'One App' and portable positions before NFL. H2 Predicts gross revenue will be absorbed by CAC and the investment line is no longer disclosed separately (May guidance carried ~$(300)m). Market-making delivered $6m in Q2 and is guided to ~$50m for FY26 at high margin. Cannibalisation of regulated-state customers described as low single-digit.
- **Regulation and tax.** UK RGD at 40%; North Carolina OSB tax lifted to 23% with 6% on prediction markets; Brazil self-exclusion register slowing the market; India exit removed 2.5m AMPs (17pp drag on Group AMPs, 21pp on International). Management hopes for one new US iGaming state by end-2027 (Virginia furthest advanced); Supreme Court resolution on prediction markets pending.
- **Governance housekeeping.** LSE secondary listing cancellation completed 3 Aug 2026 (NYSE sole listing — the 'LSE' ticker in coverage notes is now historical). Company disclosed an immaterial error in prior constant-currency calculations (regional growth rates restated in the KPI pack). Risk-factor language continues to reference remediation of material weaknesses in ICFR.
- **Phasing into H2.** US: ~20% of FY revenue in Q3 (~$1.5bn) with EBITDA ~breakeven; Q4 US EBITDA ~$500m vs ~$700m before. International: ~24% of FY revenue (~$2.5bn) and ~23% of EBITDA (~$507m) in Q3. Implied Group Q3 adj. EBITDA ~$430m and Q4 ~$1.08bn [derived] — Q4 carries ~41% of the year.
- **Thesis read-through.** The International cash engine is absorbing a permanent UK tax step-up while the US growth engine is re-based for a second time; FY26 Group EBITDA falls YoY for the first time since the FanDuel ramp. The bull case is that ~6% promo intensity with a universal loyalty program converts World Cup reactivations into 2027 AMP/ARPU growth under a new CEO; the bear case is that promo intensity is structural in a ~5%-growth market with prediction markets nibbling at the edge. Shares fell 11.5% to $92.91, a whisker above the 52-week low.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — US (FanDuel)

*Share held at #1 (39% OSB / 27% iGaming) but sportsbook revenue -15% on margin, promo stepped up to 5.4% of handle and heading to ~6%; FY26 US EBITDA guide cut 22% to $760m as a deliberate reinvestment.*

### Share and revenue

**Share held at #1 (39% OSB / 27% iGaming) but segment revenue fell 6% to $1,683m, $16m ahead of guidance.**

- GGR shares 39% OSB and 27% iGaming (28% including PokerStars US, now run inside FanDuel), both unchanged on Q1.
- Revenue -6% to $1,683m (-5% QoQ); AMPs +9% to 3.84m but -10% sequentially as basketball wound down without NFL.
- Derived monthly ARPU ~$146, 14% lower year-on-year though 6% above Q1.
- Adverse sports results swing cost 6 percentage points of growth (Knicks June title run); ex-results sportsbook 'broadly in line' with plan.
- Quarter $16m revenue and $15m EBITDA ahead of guidance; sportsbook now 62% of revenue versus 68% a year ago.

### Sportsbook margin

**Sportsbook revenue dropped 15% to $1,039m as promo rose to 5.4% of handle, heading 'closer to 6%' in H2; the biggest model change.**

- Handle +2% to $11.96bn on sportsbook AMPs +8%; revenue -15% to $1,039m (-9% QoQ); net revenue margin -170bps to 8.7% (Q1: 8.6%).
- Structural margin +40bps to 14.0% (Q1: 13.7%) on Same Game Parlay penetration, keeping 15% (2027) and 16% (long-term) targets credible.
- Results 10bps favourable gross vs 80bps last year; net revenue effect ~$21m unfavourable (Q2-25: ~$90m favourable; Q1-26: ~$33m unfavourable), costing ~$11m EBITDA.
- Promotional spend +140bps to 5.4% of handle (Q1: 4.9%) on Arkansas launch (March) and World Cup; H2 above the 5.6% of H2-25.
- Management-initiated change cuts both ways: can be dialled back, but prior margin trajectory was not deliverable at targeted customer growth.

> “deliberate investment decision” — CFO

### iGaming

**iGaming revenue grew 14% to $577m with high-teens FY26 growth reiterated and H2 stronger than H1.**

- Revenue +14% to $577m (+2% QoQ; H1 $1,141m, +17%) on iGaming AMPs +14%, direct-casino AMPs +26%.
- Slots and direct acquisition drive growth; table games held back by cross-sell from a smaller sportsbook base.
- Monthly exclusive content launches (Love Island: Unlocked, Monopoly Live, Huff N' Puff, Willy Wonka); World Cup cross-sell actives ahead of plan; Q4 cross-sell expected better than last year's execution-impaired Q4.
- Alberta launched in July, 'significantly' outpacing the Ontario launch.
- iGaming carries a higher cost-of-sales rate than sportsbook, so mix shift is a gross-margin headwind even as it stabilises revenue.

### Costs and EBITDA

**Adj. EBITDA fell 70% to $119m, a 7.1% margin, as cost of sales hit 59.1% and S&M jumped 61% to $353m.**

- Cost of sales +510bps to 59.1% of revenue (Q1: 59.2%; Q2-25: 54.0%; H1 59.1% vs 55.7%) on state tax increases (~220bps), generosity, results and iGaming mix.
- Sales and marketing +61% to $353m, 21.0% of revenue (Q2-25: 12.2%; Q1-26: 21.5%); H1 S&M $732m, 21.2% vs 17.2%.
- Technology/R&D +27% to $109m; G&A -9% to $107m on phasing; increase reflects headcount, server/cloud and prediction-market build-out.
- Adj. EBITDA $119m (-70%, flat QoQ), 7.1% margin (-1,520bps); H1 $238m (-58%), 6.9% margin.
- $45m of additional US operating cost savings now in H2 guidance.

### Engagement

**Marquee-event engagement was genuinely strong, but FanDuel is buying share in a market growing only ~5% in H1.**

- 2.3m customers engaged in the World Cup, a third reactivations; SuperSub and BetProtect+ cited as differentiators.
- NBA Finals handle ~40% higher per game year-on-year, actives +25%; June and July highest-active months in FanDuel's history; record MLB week in late July.
- Rewards Club at 70% of base; 82% of surveyed customers say improved experience, over half say lifted activity; universal by NFL kick-off.
- Management estimates US OSB market grew only ~5% in H1, assumes H2 no better; Q4-25 'customer-losing weeks' churn has not reversed.
- ROI case rests on 2027 ARPU rather than 2026 revenue.

### FanDuel Predicts

**Predicts Q2 revenue 'not material'; market-making guided to ~$50m for FY26, credible but early, with disclosure transparency reduced.**

- Sports and novelty contracts migrating from CME to Crypto.com (CME retained for financial contracts), a 'modest' economic positive and a catalogue step-change before NFL.
- Nationwide 'One App' with portable positions launches before the season.
- Predicts investment no longer broken out (May guidance ~$(300)m Predicts EBITDA drag plus ~$(105)m new states); H2 gross revenue absorbed by acquisition spend before 'increasing in 2027'.
- Market-making generated $6m in Q2, guided ~$50m FY26, high-margin and scalable; cannibalisation 'low single-digit'.
- North Carolina lifting OSB tax to 23% and applying 6% to prediction markets; Supreme Court resolution pending; loss of separate disclosure is a step back.

### Guidance

**FY26 US guidance cut to $7.4bn revenue and $760m adj. EBITDA (-18%), with Q4 EBITDA ~$500m versus ~$700m prior plan.**

- Revenue $7.4bn (+6% YoY); ~20% (~$1.5bn) in Q3 with Q3 EBITDA 'approximately breakeven'.
- Bridge from $970m: +$15m Q2 beat, +$50m market-making, +$45m opex savings, -$50m NFL one-week delay (Q3 only), -$270m net investment.
- Implied H2 revenue ~$3.95bn (H1: $3.45bn) and H2 EBITDA ~$522m [derived].
- 'Measured view of current market trends' embedded in investment line, leaving limited explicit cushion for customer-friendly NFL results.
- Management's test is 'a bigger business with a better trajectory' entering 2027; the market's test is Q4 EBITDA against $500m.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — International (UKI, SEA, APAC, CEE, Brazil, Other)

*+10% reported / +4% organic with SEA (+18% organic) carrying the portfolio; adj. EBITDA -19% to $476m as UK duty (21%→40%) and World Cup marketing take 670bps off margin; FY guidance unchanged.*

### Segment overview

**Revenue +10% to $2,643m (+4% organic) but adj. EBITDA -19% to $476m as margin fell 670bps to 18.0%.**

- Revenue +10% (+7% CC, +4% organic; +4% QoQ); Snai and Betnacional contributed ~$146m non-organic revenue.
- AMPs -16% to 10.4m, entirely the 21-percentage-point India exit drag (Junglee 2.5m AMPs in Q2-25); ex-India base grew, +3% sequentially; ARPU ~$84, flat on Q1.
- Adj. EBITDA $476m (-21% CC, -22% organic; -19% QoQ), margin 18.0% (Q1: 23.1%), 'broadly in line with expectations'.
- H1 revenue $5,184m (+18%); H1 adj. EBITDA $1,063m (-4%), 20.5% margin (-470bps).
- Cost of sales +580bps to 51.9% (Q1: 49.0%; H1 50.4% vs 45.1%) on UK duty and Snai's higher cost of sales.

### Sportsbook and iGaming

**Sportsbook +14% to $1,190m with promo at just 3.4% of handle; iGaming +7% to $1,358m but organic growth slowed to +3%.**

- Sportsbook handle +13% to $9.02bn (+8% CC, +3% organic); net revenue margin +10bps to 13.2% (Q1: 11.9%); structural margin -20bps to 16.2%.
- Results 40bps favourable (Q2-25: +30bps), ~$37m revenue and ~$23m EBITDA, reversing Q1's ~$100m/$77m headwind; promo -20bps to 3.4% of handle.
- 3.4% promo intensity versus US 5.4% heading to ~6% is the structural reason International margins are 2.5x US margins.
- iGaming +7% to $1,358m (+6% CC, +3% organic; -2% QoQ); Other revenue +9% to $95m.
- Organic iGaming deceleration from +8% in Q1 attributed to UK generosity recalibration and Snai migration, not demand; June exit rate (Snai AMPs +30%) must show in Q3.

### UK & Ireland

**UKI revenue +4% to $971m; remote gaming duty rose from 21% to 40%, a $320m FY26 EBITDA impact with $85m mitigation.**

- Revenue +3% CC and organic; +8% QoQ; H1 $1,871m, +3%.
- iGaming +7% to $526m (+6% CC) on iGaming AMPs +22%, Flutter Jackpots roll-out and Sky Gaming improvement.
- Sportsbook -2% to $391m on handle -1%; momentum improved as customers adapted to new Sky Bet interface and World Cup started strongly.
- Management leaning on headcount savings rather than marketing cuts; expects 'significant' second-order share gains in H2.
- Share gains plausible given scale but not yet visible in a +3% organic print.

### Southern Europe & Africa

**SEA was the standout at +36% to $896m (+18% organic), with Sisal at an all-time-high Italian share.**

- Revenue +33% CC, +18% organic; -5% QoQ; H1 $1,836m, +66%; includes 8pp benefit from PokerStars migrations.
- Organic iGaming +18% on Sisal Italy and expanded Türkiye offer; organic sportsbook +17% on MyCombo and World Cup.
- Snai migration onto Sisal platform completed in April; brief share dip then strong June recovery (Snai AMPs +30%), unlocking cost synergies.
- Management expects growth rate to persist into H2; Italy now the most important organic growth driver outside US iGaming.

### APAC and CEE

**APAC flat organic at $398m with India closure explaining the CC decline; CEE grew 23% to $170m.**

- APAC -1% to $398m (-9% CC, flat organic; +30% QoQ; H1 $703m, -2%).
- Sportsbet +11% USD (flat CC) on handle +12% as AFL, NBA and soccer offset greyhound and racing softness; mix diluted structural margin.
- CEE +23% to $170m (+21% CC and organic; H1 $330m, +19%); iGaming +16% with share gains in Serbia, Georgia and Armenia.
- CEE sportsbook +59% on handle +21% and 310bps net-margin improvement; unified CEE technology stack in progress.

### Brazil and Other

**Brazil revenue +64% to $72m on Betnacional but organic fell 14%; Other regions fell 38% to $136m.**

- Brazil +47% CC; H1 $146m; organic revenue -14% and AMPs -6% as socio-economic measures including a self-exclusion register slowed the market.
- Flutter product integration in May caused a temporary engagement dip but a five-fold increase in BetBuilder penetration; iGaming generosity to improve in H2.
- Other regions -38% to $136m (-39% CC; H1 $298m, -30%), 25pp from PokerStars customer transfers, balance ongoing PokerStars decline.
- Management calls Brazil 'highly attractive' long term; for now an investment line with falling organic revenue.

### Costs and guidance

**FY26 guidance unchanged at $10.51bn revenue and $2.205bn adj. EBITDA, with Q2 beat absorbed by adverse FX.**

- S&M +19% to $446m, 16.9% of revenue (+120bps; Q1: 14.8%; H1 15.9% vs 15.6%); tech/R&D +20% to $128m; G&A +2% to $222m.
- $300m annualised run-rate savings on track for year-end on Italy, Brazil and PokerStars milestones.
- Guidance $10.51bn revenue (+12%), $2.205bn adj. EBITDA (flat); rates USD:GBP 0.742, USD:EUR 0.867, USD:AUD 1.422 at 31 July.
- Q3 ~24% of revenue (~$2.5bn) and ~23% of EBITDA (~$507m); implied Q4 ~$2.8bn revenue and ~$635m EBITDA [derived].
- ~23% Q4 margin requires UK mitigation, Snai synergies and a normal sports-results quarter to land.

## 5. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Revenue ($4,326m) and adj. EBITDA ($508m) beat consensus (c.$4.24-4.26bn / c.$485m) and the US came in $16m/$15m ahead of company guidance; early Q3 trading 'ahead of expectations' with June/July FanDuel's highest-ever active months and a record MLB week. _(mixed)_
- US iGaming revenue +14% to $577m with iGaming AMPs +14% and direct-casino AMPs +26%; 27% GGR share (28% incl. PokerStars US); high-teens FY growth reiterated with H2 > H1 and Alberta outpacing the Ontario launch. _(structural)_
- US structural sportsbook margin +40bps to 14.0% on World Cup SGP penetration (15% 2027 / 16% LT targets intact); sportsbook AMPs +8%, handle +2%; loyalty program at 70% of base with 82% positive survey response, universal by NFL kick-off. _(early)_
- Southern Europe & Africa +18% organic (iGaming +18%, sportsbook +17%); Sisal record Italian share in June, #1 online position extended; Snai migration completed April with Snai AMPs +30% in June — the integration thesis is working. _(structural)_
- Cost transformation phase 2: $500m gross opex + capex savings by 2029 on top of phase 1 (>$300m by 2027, running ahead) and ~$200m UK-tax mitigation; capex guide cut $40m to $815m; $45m of US opex savings already in H2 guidance; cost-to-achieve guided below 1:1. _(structural)_
- Market-making scaled from a standing start to $6m in Q2 and ~$50m guided for FY26 at high margin; Crypto.com venue for sports/novelties expands the Predicts catalogue ahead of NFL with 'modest' economic improvement over CME. _(early)_
- International promo intensity fell 20bps to 3.4% of handle and sports results were ~$37m/$23m favourable, reversing Q1's ~$100m/$77m headwind; CEE sportsbook net margin +310bps on multi-leg mix. _(one-off)_

**Lowlights**

- Fourth consecutive guidance cut: FY26 Group adj. EBITDA to $2.655bn (-7% YoY) from $2.97bn in February; US EBITDA midpoint $760m (-22% vs May, -28% vs February) with Q4 US EBITDA now ~$500m vs ~$700m. Revenue -$395m. _(structural)_
- US promotional intensity 5.4% of handle (+140bps YoY) and guided 'closer to 6%' in H2 (H2-25: 5.6%) versus a previously assumed decline — the net $270m 'investment' concedes the prior margin path was not compatible with customer growth in a ~5%-growth market. _(structural)_
- Group adj. EBITDA margin -1,020bps to 11.7%; International margin -670bps to 18.0% (organic EBITDA -22%) on the permanent UK duty step-up (21%→40%, $320m FY impact vs $85m first-order mitigation) plus World Cup marketing; Group cost of sales 60.4% of revenue (+720bps). _(mixed)_
- Leverage 4.3x (3.7x Dec-25) with LTM EBITDA ~$2.44bn; FCF incl. financing capex ex player funds -56% to $125m; OCF flattered by a $328m accruals inflow; interest paid +$102m to $228m; no new buyback. Target 2.0-2.5x is a multi-year proposition. _(watch)_
- $95m of historical tax provisions (India GST $62m, US sales-and-use tax $33m) and SDI guidance +$200m to ~$500m widen the GAAP/adjusted gap: Q2 GAAP net loss $(296)m, H1 net loss $(87)m, GAAP operating loss $(144)m against $508m adj. EBITDA. _(governance)_
- CEO exit announced alongside the cut (third leadership change in two quarters); Predicts investment no longer disclosed separately (was ~$(300)m); an immaterial constant-currency calculation error required regional restatements; ICFR material-weakness remediation still referenced. _(governance)_
- Brazil organic revenue -14% and AMPs -6% in a market slowed by government measures; Other regions -38% on PokerStars attrition; UKI sportsbook -2%; US management estimates the US OSB market grew only ~5% in H1 and assumes no H2 recovery. _(mixed)_

## 6. Forecast & Outlook

Guidance moved for the third time this year, and again the swing factor is the US. FY26 Group revenue midpoint falls $395m to $17.91bn (range $17.435-18.385bn; +9% YoY) and adj. EBITDA $210m to $2.655bn (range $2.395-2.915bn; -7% YoY), versus $18.305bn/$2.865bn in May and $18.4bn/$2.97bn in February. International is unchanged at $10.51bn/$2.205bn (Q2 beat netted against adverse FX at 31 July rates); unallocated corporate ($310m), interest ($640m) and the $250m completed buyback are unchanged; capex improves $40m to $815m and D&A ex acquired intangibles $20m to $730m; transaction/restructuring/integration costs rise $200m to ~$500m (incl. $95m tax provisions). The entire US EBITDA reduction ($970m → $760m) reconciles to five items: +$15m Q2 beat, +$50m market-making, +$45m operating cost savings, -$50m NFL one-week delay, -$270m net generosity/proposition investment — the 'measured view of market trends' sits inside the last item rather than as a separate buffer. Phasing: US ~20% of FY revenue in Q3 with EBITDA ~breakeven and Q4 ~$500m; International ~24%/~23% of FY revenue/EBITDA in Q3.

- **FY26 Group revenue (midpoint):** $17.91bn (-$395m vs May ($18.305bn); +9% YoY)
- **FY26 Group adj. EBITDA (midpoint):** $2.655bn (-$210m vs May ($2.865bn); -7% YoY)
- **FY26 US adj. EBITDA (midpoint):** $760m (-$210m vs May ($970m): +15 Q2 beat, +50 market-making, +45 opex savings, -50 NFL delay, -270 investment)
- **FY26 International adj. EBITDA (midpoint):** $2.205bn (Unchanged; Q2 beat offset by FX; revenue $10.51bn (+12%))

**Revenue is the easier half.** FY26 Group revenue of ~$17.9bn (+9%) looks achievable: International has beaten its own plan in each of the last two quarters, SEA is compounding at high-teens organic, and most of the US revenue cut is mechanical (generosity nets against revenue) rather than a demand call. We model Q3 Group revenue of ~$4.0bn [est.: US ~$1.48bn, International ~$2.52bn] and Q4 ~$5.3bn [est.]. **The risk is US EBITDA and it is asymmetric to NFL hold.** The $760m midpoint needs Q4 US EBITDA of ~$500m, a ~20% margin on ~$2.5bn of revenue in the quarter that last year delivered customer-friendly results and high churn. With H2 promo at ~6% of handle, each 100bps of Q4 hold is worth roughly $120-130m of quarterly revenue on ~$12-13bn of handle [est.], so the $0.6-0.92bn US range is appropriately wide; we would anchor slightly below midpoint (~$720-740m [est.]) until there is evidence that the generosity step-up is lifting ARPU, not just AMPs. **Group adj. EBITDA margin ~14.8% at midpoint vs ~17.4% in FY25 [derived]**, with Q3 Group adj. EBITDA ~$430m [est.: US ~breakeven, International ~$507m, corporate ~-$75m] and Q4 ~$1.08bn [est.] — Q4 carries ~41% of the year. **Leverage will not fall year-on-year.** On ~$300-400m of H2 free cash flow after ~$300m+ of restructuring cash [est.], YE26 net debt of ~$10.0-10.3bn over $2.655bn gives ~3.8-3.9x — below the 4.3x peak but above 3.7x at Dec-25 and far from the 2.0-2.5x policy range; buybacks should stay paused and the 2.0-2.5x target is realistically 2028+ absent portfolio action. **International is the valuation anchor:** a flat $2.2bn EBITDA year while absorbing a $320m UK duty hit is a creditable outcome, and the second-order UK share gains plus Snai/PokerStars synergies ($300m run-rate by YE) give visibility into 2027 margin recovery there. **Phase 2 savings are not a margin story:** management's framing ('offset inflation and known tax increases... capacity to invest') implies most of the $500m is reinvested; we would not model net margin accretion from it until the November detail.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- NFL hold and handle from week one (one-week-delayed start): Q3 US EBITDA ~breakeven and Q4 ~$500m are the explicit tests; H2 promo at ~6% of handle must show in AMP and ARPU trajectory into 2027, not just reactivations.
- Q3 results in November: phase 2 cost-program detail (what is gross vs net, cash cost to achieve in 2027-28), first update under CEO Dan Taylor, and whether the ~$(300)m Predicts investment is reinstated as a disclosure.
- FanDuel Predicts execution: One App nationwide launch and the CME→Crypto.com migration before NFL; market-making tracking to the $50m FY26 figure; Supreme Court/state actions (North Carolina 23% OSB tax, 6% on prediction markets) and any evidence of cannibalisation moving beyond 'low single-digit'.
- UK: evidence of competitor retrenchment and second-order share gains at Sky Bet/Paddy Power post-40% duty; delivery of the $85m first-order mitigation and the ~$200m 2027 savings; UKI organic growth needs to accelerate from +3%.
- Italy/SEA sustainability: high-teens organic growth post Snai migration, Sisal share trajectory, Türkiye product expansion; Brazil regulatory ecosystem and whether organic revenue stabilises after the May platform integration.
- Balance sheet: YE26 leverage versus the 3.7x Dec-25 starting point, interest cost trajectory ($640m guide), restructuring cash outflows against the $500m SDI guide, and any reinstatement of buybacks (we would view that as premature above 3.5x).
- US iGaming: high-teens FY growth with H2 > H1, Q4 cross-sell improvement vs last year, Alberta ramp, and legislative progress toward one new iGaming state by end-2027 (Virginia).
- Governance: ICFR material-weakness remediation status in the 10-Q/10-K, constant-currency restatement follow-through, and clarity on the Fox Option (18.6% of FanDuel) given the Boyd 5% buyout has already lifted interest costs.
