OCTOBER 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Mississippi

Released c. 18–19 Nov 2026 (Mississippi Gaming Commission October 2026 monthly reports; press coverage 19–24 Nov 2026) · Currency USD (market reports natively in US dollars; tables in $m unless stated) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Mississippi remains a $2.4bn-a-year, 100% land-based, low-tax (≈12% of GGR) casino market that is flat at the top line and structurally leaking sports-betting demand to mobile-enabled neighbours: October AGGR was $198.4m (-0.2% YoY; YTD -0.7%) while retail sports handle fell for a 15th straight month to $36.2m (-7.8%). The 2026 session once again saw the House pass statewide mobile wagering (100–11) and the Senate kill it in committee; with 2027 an election year and the Lt Governor publicly opposed, the operator read-through is a stable but ex-growth retail franchise with a binary, politically gated mobile option.

1Executive summary

  • Market size is flat at ~$2.43bn. Mississippi Gaming Commission (MGC) adjusted gross gaming revenue (AGGR, gross of tax) was $2,433.6m in CY2025, -0.01% on CY2024's $2,433.8m, after a -2.0% fall in 2024; Jan–Oct 2026 AGGR is $2,015.5m, -0.7% YoY, so 2026 is tracking to a third consecutive flat-to-down year.
  • October 2026 AGGR was $198.36m, -0.15% YoY ($198.66m in Oct-25), per MGC figures reported by rg.org/CDC Gaming; the trade press headline range (-0.2% to +0.2%) reflects rounding — the MGC dollar figures show a $0.3m decline. September had been +4.5% ($195.6m).
  • 100% land-based: online penetration is 0%. Mississippi has no iGaming, no statewide mobile sports betting and no online lottery; mobile sports apps work only while geofenced on casino property. This is the defining structural feature of the market.
  • Product mix is slot-dominated and tables are the only growth line. October slots (EGD) win was $162.0m (-2.8% YoY; ~82% of AGGR), table games $27.7m (+9.3%), retail sports $5.25m (+27.7%). CY2025: EGDs $2.02bn (+0.2%), tables $317.7m (down), sports $49.8m (AGA/MGC).
  • Geography is a three-speed market. Gulf Coast (Coastal region) $133.1m in Oct (+0.8%; 67% share; CY25 $1,590.5m, +0.5%); Northern/Tunica $40.7m (-0.3%; CY25 $522.1m, -1.9%); Central/Vicksburg–Natchez $24.6m (-4.6%; CY25 $321.0m, +0.7%). Growth capital and table-game demand sit on the Coast; the river markets are ex-growth.
  • Sports betting is in secular decline under the retail-only model. October handle $36.19m (-7.8% YoY; lowest October since 2018's launch season; -57% vs the Oct-21 record of $83.5m); YTD handle $233.9m (-18.9% vs $288.5m), half the $468.7m of Jan–Oct 2021. CY2025 handle was $379.1m (-16.2% vs $452.4m in 2024).
  • But sportsbook margin is rising on parlay mix. October taxable revenue $5.25m (+27.7%) on a 14.5% hold (≈10.5% a year ago); parlays were 23.3% of handle but 54.3% of revenue at a 33.8% hold (15.7% in Oct-25). Football handle fell 11.0% to $17.9m (49.4% of write) yet football revenue rose 30% to $1.36m.
  • Tax regime is low and GGR-based. State licence fee is graduated on monthly GGR — 4% on the first $50,000, 6% on $50,000–$134,000, 8% above $134,000 — plus local fees averaging 3–4% (statutory cap 4%), giving a combined ≈12%; sports wagering is taxed identically plus the 0.25% federal excise on handle. CY2025 direct gaming tax was $287.9m (-0.4%), an 11.8% effective rate.
  • Tax collections are tracking flat. Mississippi DOR FY2027 gaming-tax collections were $25.40m in July 2026 and $24.08m in August 2026 ($49.47m FYTD), split General Fund $26.6m / Special Bond Sinking Fund $6.0m ($3m per month to highway bonds) / local governments $16.9m. Implied October 2026 tax at 12% of AGGR is ~$23.8m (estimate).
  • Licence base: 25 commercial casinos (AGA State of the States 2026, CY2025), down from 26 in 2024, plus three Choctaw tribal casinos (Pearl River Resort: Silver Star, Golden Moon, Bok Homa) that fall outside MGC reporting. The MGC publishes revenue by region only, not by property, so operator shares are not disclosed.
  • Operator landscape is dominated by US multi-state incumbents — MGM (Beau Rivage), Seminole Hard Rock, Boyd (IP, Sam's Town), Caesars (Harrah's Gulf Coast, Horseshoe Tunica), Penn (Boomtown, Hollywood Gulf Coast, Hollywood/1st Jackpot Tunica, Ameristar Vicksburg), Golden Nugget, Churchill Downs, Bally's, Cherokee Nation Entertainment (Gold Strike) and regional independents (Scarlet Pearl, Island View, Palace, Treasure Bay, Silver Slipper/Full House). Ownership list from public records, not re-verified against the MGC property list this cycle.
  • Mobile sports betting failed for a fourth consecutive session. HB 4074 (Mississippi Mobile Sports Wagering Act) passed the House 100–11 on 25 Feb 2026 with a 22% tax on mobile GGR, a cut in the retail casino state rate from 8% to 6% (~$48m; Lt Gov calls it a '25% tax cut'), a $6m casino protection fund and $50m/yr to the state pension system (PERS) for ten years — then died in the Senate Gaming Committee (Chair Sen. David Blount) without a hearing; companion HB 1581 died at the 3 March committee deadline.
  • Political direction: Senate leadership is the binding constraint. Lt Gov Delbert Hosemann (7–9 Jul 2026) urged the Senate to 'continue to reject' mobile wagering, citing addiction risk, cannibalisation of 'billions' of bricks-and-mortar investment and unquantified revenue; Speaker Jason White and Ways & Means Chair Trey Lamar back it, and Gov. Reeves has said he would consider a bill. 2027 is a statewide election year and Hosemann is weighing a gubernatorial run — passage odds in the Jan–Apr 2027 session are low.
  • Grey-market substitution is unaddressed. A sweepstakes-casino ban (SB 2104, 2026) died at the same committee deadline, repeating 2025's House–Senate deadlock; neighbouring Louisiana (mobile since Jan 2022), Tennessee and Arkansas offer statewide mobile, and House proponents openly concede 'everybody who wants to place wagers on their mobile device does it already today'. The MGC publishes no enforcement statistics on offshore or sweepstakes activity.
  • Payments are conventional land-based rails — cash, TITO, markers and on-property geofenced apps; the extent of cashless/digital-wallet adoption at Mississippi properties is not disclosed in MGC monthly data [unverified]. Player winnings are subject to a 3% non-refundable state withholding (Miss. Code §27-7-901).
  • Responsible gambling is light-touch by benchmark standards: age 21, an MGC-administered voluntary self-exclusion list and the Mississippi Council on Problem & Compulsive Gambling helpline; no bespoke advertising code, deposit limits or affordability rules were identified. The University of Mississippi requested $2m (Jul 2026) for gambling-harm research — a sign the social-cost argument is being institutionalised in the mobile debate.
  • Competitive position vs neighbours is eroding at the margin. Tunica's -1.9% CY2025 and Central's -4.6% October reflect Arkansas (Southland) and Louisiana (land-based plus 21.5%-taxed mobile) competition; the Gulf Coast remains a resilient drive-in destination for Alabama/Florida panhandle demand because Alabama still has no commercial casinos.
  • Read-through for an operator: Mississippi is a mature, cash-generative retail market with one of the lowest tax rates in the US and no online channel. Entry is only via acquisition of (or partnership with) one of 25 licensees; the upside case is a casino-tethered mobile regime at 22% — on our estimates $250–350m of mobile GGR at maturity ($55–77m tax), well below the sponsor's ~$100m tax claim — but timing is politically gated to 2028+ in our base case.

3Deep-dive: Casino gaming (slots & tables) – regional dive

Flat $198.4m October: Coast +0.8% on tables, Tunica flat, Central -4.6% as slot win falls across all three regions.

Statewide AGGR trend

October 2026 statewide AGGR was $198,356,391 vs $198,663,938 in October 2025, down $0.31m (-0.15%); a mature market oscillating around $200m monthly.

  • September 2026 was $195.6m (+4.5%); ten-month AGGR $2,015.5m vs $2,030.0m (-0.7%).
  • On the CY2025 run-rate of $2,433.6m, 2026 outturn points to roughly $2.40–2.43bn (estimate).
  • 2026 monthly profile noisy: January +4.9% ($189.0m), February +5.8% ($200.6m), March c.-5% (~$214m), April +0.2% ($196.0m), May -2.6% ($217.9m), July -0.9% ($208.7m), September +4.5%, October -0.2%.
  • No evidence of either a consumer break or a growth inflection.

Slots versus tables

October EGD revenue fell 2.8% YoY to $162.0m with declines in every region, while table games rose 9.3% to $27.7m.

  • EGDs: Coastal -1.4% to $108.8m; Northern -4.7% to $32.1m; Central -6.4% to $21.1m.
  • Tables led by Coast (+9.5%) and Tunica (+12.0% to $5.9m); Central tables slipped 1.2% to $1.7m.
  • CY2025 split: EGDs $2.02bn (+0.2%), tables $317.7m (down), sports $49.8m; October table strength reverses the 2025 trend and warrants monitoring for Coast high-end play versus one-month hold effect.
  • Product lines do not sum to AGGR (~$3.4m residual in October), attributed to other games and rounding.

Gulf Coast

The Gulf Coast is the only region with a credible growth story: October AGGR $133.05m (+0.75%), 67.1% of the state.

  • CY2025 Coastal AGGR $1,590.5m (+0.5%), roughly 65% of total.
  • Drive-in destination market serving Alabama, the Florida panhandle and greater New Orleans.
  • Resort-scale properties (Beau Rivage, Hard Rock, IP, Golden Nugget, Harrah's, Scarlet Pearl, Island View) and the state's only meaningful non-gaming amenity base.

Northern and Central

Northern produced $40.67m (-0.3%) in October and Central $24.63m (-4.6%), both facing out-of-state competition.

  • Northern (Tunica, Greenville) CY2025 $522.1m (-1.9%), a decade-long erosion as Memphis-area demand migrates to Southland in West Memphis, Arkansas.
  • Central (Vicksburg, Natchez) posted +0.7% in CY2025 and is most exposed to Louisiana's land-based and 21.5%-taxed mobile offer.

Commercial implication

Organic growth is unavailable statewide; share gain comes from capex-led amenity competition on the Coast or consolidation of sub-scale river properties.

  • Casino count has fallen from 26 (2024) to 25 (2025).
  • With Tunica and Central revenue per property under pressure and the tax rate too low to be a lever, next moves are likely closures or ownership transfers rather than greenfield builds outside Harrison and Hancock counties.

4Deep-dive: Sports wagering (retail & on-premises mobile)

Handle down a 15th consecutive month to $36.2m (-7.8%), but a 14.5% parlay-driven hold lifted taxable revenue 27.7% to $5.25m.

Handle decline

October 2026 write was $36,191,686, down 7.8% from $39.3m, the weakest October since launch and the 15th consecutive YoY monthly decline.

  • Sports wagering legalised in 2018 (first bets 1 August 2018), confined to casino premises; mobile apps only while geolocated on property.
  • 2021's record October was $83.5m; handle has declined YoY every month since August 2025.
  • Ten-month handle $233.9m, -18.9% on 2025's $288.5m and -50% on 2021's $468.7m; through-October series: 2021 $468.7m, 2022 $383.6m, 2023 $363.0m, 2024 $338.1m, 2025 $288.5m, 2026 $233.9m.
  • Full-year 2025 handle $379.1m (-16.2% vs $452.4m in 2024; $474.3m in 2023).

Revenue and hold

October taxable revenue was $5,251,529, +27.7% YoY, at a 14.51% hold versus roughly 10.5% a year earlier, driven by parlays.

  • At the ≈12% combined rate, October yields ~$630k of tax.
  • Parlay handle fell 19.7% to $8.4m but parlay revenue rose 72.5% to $2.85m at a 33.8% hold (15.7% in Oct-25); parlays were 23.3% of write and 54.3% of revenue.
  • Football $17.9m (-11.0%; 49.4% of handle), revenue +30% to $1.36m; Central-region books lost $45,486 on football.
  • CY2025 revenue $49.8m (AGA, up from $41.6m in 2024, which had fallen 18.8%), implying ~13.1% annual hold; Sportsbook Review's $340.4m handle / $45.4m revenue treated as incomplete.

Regional split

The Coast took $25.18m of October write (69.6%) and $3.41m of revenue (13.5% hold); Central and Northern produced $1.84m on a 16.7% hold.

  • Central $7.06m (19.5%) and Northern $3.96m (10.9%) of write.
  • Coast football handle fell 18.7% to $12.7m while Central ($3.2m) and Northern ($2.0m) grew, consistent with Gulf Coast bettors migrating to Louisiana mobile apps since January 2022.
  • Hold volatility is real for a sub-$40m book: December 2024 produced negative statewide revenue (-$0.87m); ±5pp monthly hold swings are routine.

Mobile option value

Retail sports is 2.6% of October AGGR and perhaps $6m of annual tax; the vertical's value lies almost entirely in a casino-tethered statewide mobile regime.

  • Retail sports is an amenity, not a profit centre; operators carry fixed sportsbook cost against shrinking write.
  • Every House-passed draft is casino-tethered, so market access would flow through the 25 licensees.
  • HB 4074 (2026) proposed 22% of mobile GGR; on Louisiana/Tennessee per-capita benchmarks for 2.94m population we estimate $250–350m mature mobile GGR and $55–77m tax (estimate), versus the sponsor's ~$100m claim.
  • The $6m protection fund and 8%→6% retail rate cut show the political price of mobile will be paid by the state treasury, not operators.

5Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime

GGR-based 4/6/8% state tiers plus ≤4% local (≈12% all-in) — among the lowest in the US; licensing is property-tethered, county-opt-in and administered by the three-member MGC.

Statute and regulator

The Mississippi Gaming Control Act (1990), Miss. Code Ann. §75-76, is administered by the three-member MGC with the Department of Revenue collecting taxes.

  • MGC comprises three gubernatorially appointed commissioners and an Executive Director with investigations, enforcement, audit and laboratory divisions.
  • Casino gaming lawful only in counties opted in by referendum along the Mississippi River and Gulf Coast; post-Katrina (2005) legislation allowed Coast casinos onshore, now wholly land-based.
  • Licence types: operator, manufacturer and distributor, race book and/or sports pool, key-employee licences and work permits, plus a separate charitable-gaming regime.
  • Licence duration, application/investigation fees and annual device fee schedule not re-verified this cycle [unverified].

Tax rates

State licence fee is 4% on the first $50,000, 6% on the next $84,000 (to $134,000) and 8% above, plus local fees averaging 3–4% (cap 4%).

  • For any casino of scale the marginal and effective state rate is 8%; local share split between host city/county and county at large.
  • Sports wagering taxed at the same rates, ≈12% of taxable revenue, plus the 0.25% federal excise tax on handle.
  • On CY2025 AGGR of $2,433.6m, direct gaming taxes of $287.9m (DOR via AGA, -0.4%) equate to an 11.8% effective rate; 2024 $289.1m (-1.8%), 2023 $294.3m.
  • Players bear a 3% non-refundable state income-tax withholding on winnings (Miss. Code §27-7-901).

Distribution of receipts

The first $3m per month of the state's 8% goes to the Special Bond Sinking Fund for 1994 highway bonds, remainder to the General Fund.

  • FY2027 collections to date: July $25.40m, August $24.08m, $49.47m cumulative (cash basis, one-month lag): General Fund $26.6m, bond fund $6.0m, local transfers $16.9m.
  • Local transfers of roughly $100m a year to Harrison, Hancock, Tunica, Warren and other counties are the constituency making the Senate protective of bricks-and-mortar.

Benchmarks and entry

Mississippi's ≈12% is exceptionally operator-friendly versus Pennsylvania 54% on slots, New York 51% on mobile sports, Illinois graduated to 40%, Louisiana 21.5% mobile.

  • HB 4074 would have cut the retail state rate to 6% in exchange for a 22% mobile rate.
  • Fiscal risk is low and stable, but the market is closed: no new-licence pathway outside opt-in counties.
  • Practical entry means buying or partnering with an existing licensee, with full MGC suitability investigation of the acquirer and key persons.

6Deep-dive: Operator landscape & competitive position

25 commercial casinos owned largely by US multi-state groups; no property-level disclosure, Coast resorts hold ~65% of revenue, Tunica consolidating.

Property ownership

25 commercial casinos in 2025 (down from 26 in 2024), largely owned by US multi-state groups; MGC publishes no property-level win or shares.

  • Specific 2025 closure not identified in sources; ownership based on public records, not re-verified against the MGC property list.
  • Gulf Coast: MGM's Beau Rivage, Seminole Hard Rock Biloxi, Boyd's IP, Golden Nugget Biloxi, Caesars' Harrah's Gulf Coast, Penn's Boomtown Biloxi and Hollywood Gulf Coast, Full House's Silver Slipper, locally owned Scarlet Pearl, Island View, Palace, Treasure Bay.
  • Northern: Tunica (Caesars' Horseshoe, Cherokee Nation's Gold Strike acquired from MGM in 2023, Boyd's Sam's Town, Penn's Hollywood and 1st Jackpot, Fitz) and Greenville (Churchill Downs' Harlow's).
  • Central: Vicksburg (Penn's Ameristar, Churchill Downs' Riverwalk, Bally's Casino Vicksburg, WaterView) and Magnolia Bluffs in Natchez.

Tribal and lottery

The Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians' Pearl River Resort and Bok Homa under IGRA, and the Mississippi Lottery, are excluded from all figures.

  • Pearl River Resort (Silver Star, Golden Moon) revenue not reported by the MGC.
  • Mississippi Lottery Corporation (sales since November 2019) draw and scratch sales are not part of casino AGGR; FY2026 figures not retrieved [n/a].

Competitive position

The state is bifurcated: Coast demand intact at $1.59bn CY2025 (+0.5%) and October +0.8%, while river markets face Arkansas and Louisiana.

  • Coast benefits from Alabama's lack of commercial casinos and the Florida panhandle; table-game growth of 9.5% suggests the resort tier is winning higher-value play.
  • Northern faces Southland Casino Hotel in West Memphis (Arkansas statewide mobile since 2022); Central faces Louisiana land-based casinos, mobile since January 2022, tax raised to 21.5% in 2025.
  • Tunica revenue fell 1.9% in 2025; Central slots dropped 6.4% in October; Tunica property count roughly halved since the 2000s, further consolidation plausible.

Entry routes

Practical routes are acquisition of a Coast or river property, B2B supply, or positioning for a casino-tethered mobile licence.

  • Sub-scale river assets trade at low multiples but carry declining revenue.
  • On-premises sports apps mostly run by national brands (BetMGM at Beau Rivage, Hard Rock Bet, Caesars Sportsbook, FanDuel/DraftKings retail partnerships) [not re-verified], so platform supply to independents is a niche.
  • House drafts cap skins per casino and require revenue-sharing with the host; early partnerships with independents (Scarlet Pearl, Island View, Palace, Treasure Bay, Harlow's, WaterView, Magnolia Bluffs) are where optionality is cheapest.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling & enforcement

Conventional land-based rails, age 21, MGC self-exclusion and a helpline — no ad code, no affordability rules, and no enforcement data on offshore/sweepstakes leakage.

Payments

Every lawful wager is placed on a casino floor, so the payments stack is the standard US land-based one with no statewide remote channel.

  • Cash at cage and kiosks, ticket-in/ticket-out, credit markers for rated play, card/ATM access per operator policy; on-premises app accounts funded at cage or in-app while geolocated.
  • Adoption of cashless/digital-wallet systems under MGC technical standards not disclosed and not verified [unverified].
  • No mobile-money or e-wallet ecosystem; no remote channel for KYC/AML onboarding rules.
  • Winnings above federal thresholds trigger W-2G reporting; state takes 3% non-refundable withholding at source.

Responsible gambling

RG architecture is minimal: age 21, MGC voluntary self-exclusion and an industry-part-funded helpline, with no ad code, limits, affordability checks or levy.

  • Mississippi Council on Problem & Compulsive Gambling operates the helpline and treatment referrals.
  • University of Mississippi asked lawmakers in July 2026 for $2m for gambling research and public education, framed around mobile betting.
  • Lt Governor's July statement rested on addiction risk to young adults from '24/7 push notifications'.
  • If mobile is enacted, expect a materially heavier RG package than today's retail regime.

Enforcement

The Legislature has twice failed to ban online sweepstakes casinos, and the MGC publishes no enforcement statistics, so the grey market remains a competitor.

  • In 2025 the Senate ban died in conference entangled with the House mobile push; in 2026 SB 2104 died at the 3 March committee deadline.
  • Mississippi lacks explicit prohibitions adopted in Montana, Connecticut, New Jersey and New York.
  • House leaders concede mobile demand is already met illegally or across state lines.
  • A ban is more likely packaged with and traded against mobile legalisation than passed alone.

Charitable gaming

Charitable gaming (bingo) is separately regulated by the MGC; gross revenue data exist but were not retrieved for this period [n/a].

  • Regulated under the MGC's Charitable Gaming regulations.
  • Data available in the MGC's charitable-gaming series.

8Deep-dive: Legislative & political direction – statewide mobile wagering

Four House passages, zero Senate votes: HB 4074 (22% mobile tax, 8%→6% retail cut, $6m protection fund) died in Senate Gaming Committee; Lt Gov Hosemann restated opposition in July 2026 ahead of a 2027 election year.

2026 session

HB 4074 passed the House 100–11 on 25 February, the largest House majority yet, but died in the Senate without a hearing.

  • Rep. Casey Eure's HB 1581 attached mobile to tidelands and child-support provisions and died unreported at the 3 March deadline.
  • HB 4074 was double-referred to Senate Finance and Senate Gaming; Chair Sen. David Blount declined a hearing and it lapsed before early-April adjournment.
  • Terms: casino partnership required; 22% mobile GGR tax (up from 18.5%); retail state rate cut 8% to 6% (Hosemann: a '25% tax cut', ~$48m); $6m compensation fund; $50m a year for ten years to PERS.
  • Sponsor projected ~$100m annual revenue by reference to Tennessee ($125m) and Louisiana ($102m).

Political positions

Lt Gov Delbert Hosemann, weighing a 2027 run for governor, restated Senate opposition on 7–9 July 2026; House leaders called the stance 'tone deaf'.

  • Hosemann said mobile 'wouldn't create a single job', carries 'a social cost that is of increasing concern', threatens 'billions' invested in land-based casinos and raises athlete-integrity risks.
  • Speaker Jason White and Ways & Means Chair Trey Lamar said 'most every casino operator in Mississippi supports' mobile; Gov. Tate Reeves would consider a bill.
  • Prior history: 2023 task force (HB 606, House 109–5); HB 774 passed the House in 2024 and died in the Senate; 2025 House bill died without Senate hearing.
“everybody who wants to place wagers on their mobile device does it already today”Speaker Jason White and Ways & Means Chair Trey Lamar

Enactment probability

We assign roughly a 20–30% probability to enactment in 2027 and see 2028 as the first realistic session, implying go-live no earlier than 2029 (estimate).

  • Obstacle is institutional: Senate Gaming Committee has never been required to record a vote; local governments receiving ~$100m of annual fees are organised against change.
  • The retail-tax cut needed to buy casino support gives opponents the 'tax cut for casinos' line.
  • All 174 legislative seats and statewide offices are on the November 2027 ballot, making the Jan–Apr 2027 session the least favourable window.

Mobile economics

Enacted economics would be casino-tethered with 22%+ tax; we estimate $250–350m mature mobile GGR and $55–77m tax on 2.94m residents.

  • Skins per licensee, revenue share to host, likely protection mechanism for river properties.
  • Retail sports handle likely falls a further 30–50% on launch.
  • Sponsor's ~$100m tax figure implies ~$455m GGR (~$155 per capita), which looks aggressive.
  • Monitoring points: January 2027 bill filings, any Senate Gaming hearing, the Lt Governor race, and whether a sweepstakes ban is used as bargaining chip.

9Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Gulf Coast is a resilient destination franchise: $1,590.5m CY2025 AGGR (+0.5%; 65% of state), October $133.1m (+0.8%) with table games +9.5% — the only region where amenity-led capex is still earning growth. structural
  • One of the lowest tax regimes in US gaming: 8% state (4/6/8% tiers) plus ≤4% local, 11.8% effective in CY2025, applied identically to sports wagering; HB 4074 would have cut the retail state rate to 6%. structural
  • Casino-tethered mobile optionality: every House-passed draft routes market access through the 25 licensees with a 22% GGR tax; early partnerships with independents (Scarlet Pearl, Island View, Palace, Treasure Bay, Harlow's, WaterView) are the cheapest way to own that option. early
  • Sportsbook margin is improving even as volume shrinks: October hold 14.5% (vs ~10.5%), parlays 54.3% of revenue at a 33.8% hold, taxable revenue +27.7% to $5.25m and CY2025 revenue +19.7% to $49.8m. mixed
  • Stable fiscal and regulatory settings: no tax changes enacted since sports betting launched in 2018, a long-standing three-member MGC, and tax collections ($49.5m Jul–Aug FY27) tracking flat — low policy-shock risk for an incumbent. structural
  • Alabama's continued absence of commercial casinos protects the Coast's eastern feeder market, and any Alabama lottery/casino legislation remains stalled. watch

▼ Lowlights

  • No online channel and no near-term path to one: sports handle has fallen YoY for 15 straight months (-18.9% YTD to $233.9m; -50% vs 2021) as demand migrates to Louisiana/Tennessee/Arkansas mobile apps and offshore/sweepstakes sites. structural
  • Senate leadership veto: HB 4074 passed the House 100–11 but got no committee hearing; Lt Gov Hosemann is publicly opposed and may run for governor in 2027, making the Jan–Apr 2027 session an unfavourable window (est. 20–30% passage odds). governance
  • Ex-growth core: AGGR was -2.0% in 2024, flat in 2025 and -0.7% YTD 2026; October slots fell in all three regions (-2.8% statewide), and Northern/Central markets are structurally losing share to Arkansas and Louisiana. structural
  • Grey-market crowding with no enforcement visibility: sweepstakes bans failed in 2025 and 2026; the MGC publishes no illegal-market enforcement data, and House leaders acknowledge mobile demand is already served unlawfully. watch
  • Hold volatility in a small retail book: statewide sports revenue was negative in December 2024 (-$0.87m) and Central books lost on football in October 2026; a $36m monthly write cannot absorb sharp results. one-off
  • Shrinking licence base and property-level opacity: 26→25 casinos in 2025, with the MGC reporting by region only, so market-share and asset-level diligence depend on operator disclosures. mixed
  • Any mobile regime will be politically priced: a $6m casino protection fund, retail tax cut and PERS earmarks mean the state, not operators, captures most of the headline value, and launch is unlikely before 2029 (estimate). watch

Independent view. Our base case is a flat-to-slightly-down retail casino market through 2027 — Coast +0–2%, Tunica -2–4%, Central -2–5% — with aggregate AGGR holding the ~$2.4bn plateau and the effective tax rate unchanged at ≈12%. Retail sports betting continues to shrink 10–20% a year in handle while hold settles in the 12–15% range on parlay mix, keeping revenue roughly flat at $45–50m. We assign only 20–30% odds to mobile wagering passing in the 2027 session given the election calendar and the Lt Governor's stated position; a 2028 enactment with 2029 launch is our central scenario, at which point we estimate $250–350m of mobile GGR, a 22%+ tax, a 30–50% further contraction in retail sports handle, and a heavier RG/advertising package than today's regime. Leverage of the regulatory settings is toward the state treasury: any mobile bill will carry retail tax relief, a casino protection fund and earmarks, so the incremental value to operators is market access and data rather than tax arbitrage. Swing factors: a change in Senate leadership or committee chair; a bundled sweepstakes-ban/mobile compromise; Alabama legalising casinos or a lottery (negative for the Coast); Louisiana regulatory changes; and further Tunica closures that would reset the Northern base. For B2Tech, the actionable near-term play is B2B supply and partnership optionality with independent licensees rather than capital-intensive entry.
