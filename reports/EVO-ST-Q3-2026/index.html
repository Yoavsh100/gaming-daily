Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

Evolution AB

Nasdaq Stockholm: EVO.ST · Released 23 Oct 2026 · Currency EUR (tables in millions; ~USD at EUR/USD 1.12 as of 23 Oct 2026) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Shares opened ~1.6% lower at SEK 798.8 on the modest revenue/EPS miss, were +1.3% at SEK 822.20 by midday, and closed +3.6% at SEK 841.40 (prev. close SEK 812.00; ~623k shares traded) as the unchanged ~66% margin target and the cleared UKGC/Galaxy overhangs outweighed Brazil. Options had implied a ±5.6% move. (SEK/USD on the day not verified; USD equivalent n/a.)

The one-line verdict

Evolution's Q3-26 delivered its first year-on-year revenue growth in five quarters (+3.7% to EUR 526.1m, ~$589m; est. +4.0% at constant currency) and held the adjusted EBITDA margin at 65.8%, but the quality is mixed: the ~30% of revenue in the Americas and Africa grew ~18% while the ~70% in Europe (-4.7%, share loss conceded) and Asia (+1.6%, cyber-crime unresolved) shrank ~1.5%. EUR 12.1m (~$13.6m) of one-offs pushed profit for the period down 3.6%, EPS growth is entirely buyback-driven, and Brazil's 5 October ban removes a growth engine from Q4. Stabilisation, not re-acceleration — with the FY26 ~66% margin target unchanged.

Marginal miss vs consensus (~EUR 529m, ~$592m; Investing.com), the fifth straight quarter at or below the Street on revenue.
  • Margin held — on an adjusted basis: Adj. EBITDA EUR 345.9m (~$387m), +2.7% YoY, margin 65.8% (-60bps YoY, -10bps QoQ), ~1% above FactSet EBITDA consensus of ~EUR 343m.   • The one-offs clear two overhangs: EUR 5.6m (~$6.3m; GBP 4.75m) settlement with the UK Gambling Commission closing the licence review of Evolution Malta Holding opened in Dec 2024 (announced 15 Jul, fully paid), and EUR 6.5m (~$7.3m) of termination and legal costs after walking away from the $85m Galaxy Gaming acquisition in July (incl. a $5.2m break fee). 3Segment deep-dive: Live Casino

Back to +2.6% growth after four quarters of decline, but it is an Americas/Africa story — Europe is ceding share, Asia is flat, and Brazil goes dark in Q4.

Return to growth

Live returned to growth after four quarters of decline, net revenues up 2.6% to EUR 443.0m (~$496m), but the band has barely moved.

  • Up 1.3% sequentially, against a -3.6% print in Q2; nine-month run-rate still 1.4% lower at EUR 1,315.2m (~$1,473m).
  • Live is 84.2% of group revenue, down 90bps year on year as RNG outgrows it.
  • Five-quarter series EUR 431.7m, 438.6m, 434.9m, 437.3m, 443.0m (~$484m-$496m): oscillating in a EUR 430-445m band for over a year, only now edging above it.
  • No segment EBITDA disclosed; group adjusted margin of 65.8% (-60bps YoY) is the only read-across and effectively the Live margin.
“uneventful”Management

Americas and Africa

Live's recovery is an Americas-and-Africa story, with North America, Latin America and Africa all at records.

  • North America +13.6% to record EUR 84.3m (~$94m), +4.1% QoQ, helped by second Michigan studio and Monopoly Live roll-out; New Jersey studio next in pipeline.
  • Latin America +23.1% to record EUR 49.0m (~$55m), with Argentine studio acquired in Q1 for EUR 6.0m (~$6.7m) adding capacity.
  • Africa & other +23.7% to roughly EUR 27.0m (~$30m); CEO cautioned the region is small and volatile.
“hardcore focused business as usual”CEO

Europe

Europe fell 4.7% to EUR 173.7m (~$195m), with the CEO effectively acknowledging market-share loss in the most mature, regulated region.

  • +0.4% QoQ after +3.5% QoQ in Q2.
  • Management pointed to regulatory change and tax as external drags.
  • Second Lithuanian studio opened in the quarter; native-language tables remain the tactical response.
“it's not exciting and we should do better”CEO

Asia

Asia, largest region by IP at EUR 192.1m (~$215m), grew just 1.6% YoY and 0.8% QoQ as cyber-crime remains unresolved.

  • Management explicit that the cyber-crime problem affecting video distribution and unauthorised product use is 'not solved'.
“no quick solution”Management

Product and capacity

Headcount grew 8.1% YoY to 23,408, more than double revenue growth, so Live unit economics rely on restraint elsewhere, not operating leverage.

  • Launched Monopoly Roll'em and Disco Balls among three new branded titles; 2026 releases to exceed 110 games; Game Night due before year-end.
  • 2027 roadmap described as 'spectacular', containing some of the largest and most complex games ever built.
  • Studio count rose to 27 with one opening in the quarter (third this year); headcount +2.3% QoQ; other opex ex one-offs +0.7% YoY.
  • Game rounds index jumped to 435 (+22.5% YoY, +11.3% QoQ) while revenue grew 3.7%; management says correlation holds only 'over a very long time period'.
  • We read the gap as mix shift toward lower-value rounds, likely from weakly monetised markets or unauthorised use.

Brazil

Provisional Measure 1.394/2026 suspended licensed online betting; São Paulo studio closed 5 October, capping exposure below EUR 24.5m (~$27m) quarterly.

  • Brazil is 'well below half' of Latin American IP revenue, below 4.7% of group.
  • Market is not ring-fenced so some play may migrate to operators licensed elsewhere; CEO rejected Rothschild Redburn's suggestion closure could be net positive.
  • CEO called it a 'populist' overthrow of an 18-month-old framework; studio can reopen 'on short notice' if STF or Congress overturns the measure.
  • Staff supported rather than released, so Brazilian cost persists into Q4 without Brazilian revenue.
“total negative”CEO

Outlook

Structural growth rate has reset to low single digits; guidance to grow at least in line with the market in every region is not being met in Europe.

  • The ~30% of revenue in the Americas and Africa grew ~18%.
  • The ~70% in Europe and Asia shrank roughly 1.5%.

4Segment deep-dive: RNG

Second consecutive double-digit quarter (+10.1%) — RNG supplied 40% of group revenue growth from <16% of the base; North American localisation is the strategy.

Growth engine

RNG revenues rose 10.1% to EUR 83.1m (~$93m), the second consecutive double-digit quarter after years of flat-to-down.

  • Up 3.2% sequentially, following +14.0% in Q2, the first double-digit quarter in roughly three years.
  • Nine-month RNG revenue up 10.7% to EUR 241.8m (~$271m); series EUR 75.5m, 75.7m, 78.2m, 80.5m, 83.1m shows steady ~3% sequential compounding.
  • Share of group revenue 15.8%, up from 14.9% a year ago and 15.5% in Q2; 2026 YoY path roughly +8% (Q1), +14.0% (Q2), +10.1% (Q3).

Contribution and strategy

RNG added EUR 7.6m (~$8.5m) of the group's EUR 19.0m (~$21m) YoY increase, 40% of growth from under 16% of the base.

  • Without RNG the group would have grown ~2.3%.
  • Management credits North America's outperformance partly to 'particularly strong RNG performance'; localised North American RNG content planned, Ice Fishing slated for Q4 launch.
  • Monopoly-branded content under the Hasbro licence anchors the branded pipeline.
  • No RNG-specific KPIs (active titles, aggregation vs proprietary, game rounds, regional revenue) or segment margin disclosed.

AI positioning

Management frames AI dealers as a complement sitting alongside RNG rather than a substitute for Live studios.

  • CEO does not believe AI dealers 'will take over live as some people ask'; group is 'working hard' with AI across functions for efficiency.
  • Third-party AI-dealer products remove the studio capex barrier underpinning Evolution's Live moat and shorten rivals' time to market in regulated US states.
“is a possibility… it's like an RNG live game, it's a complement”CEO

Our judgment

RNG is a genuine if modest positive, accretive after three years of drag, but EUR 83m a quarter cannot carry a EUR 2.1bn group.

  • Base still small enough that ~10% growth is credible for several more quarters; North American localisation aligns with fastest-growing regulated region.
  • Comparison base stays soft through Q1-27 (Q4-25 RNG was EUR 75.7m, ~$85m), so double-digit rate is partly base effect.
  • Segment guidance is to grow at least in line with the market in each region.

5Segment deep-dive: Regional mix & regulatory exposure (group)

Asia is 37% of revenue by player IP but 4% by operator location — the grey-market gap is unchanged; Europe is losing share; Brazil removes a growth engine from Q4.

Two revenue views

Asia is 37% of revenue by player IP but 4% by operator location; ~EUR 173m (~$194m) is effectively grey-market exposure.

  • By operator location: Europe 64% (EUR 336.3m, -6.0%), North America 15% (EUR 81.0m, +11.0%), Latin America 15% (EUR 76.6m, +39.3%), Asia 4% (EUR 19.1m, +42.5%), Africa & other 2% (EUR 13.1m, +61.7%).
  • By player IP: Asia 37% (EUR 192.1m), Europe 33% (EUR 173.7m), North America 16% (EUR 84.3m), Latin America 9% (EUR 49.0m), Africa & other 5% (~EUR 27.0m).
  • Regulated markets were 49% of revenue (EUR 258.5m, ~$290m); mobile 77.0% of operator GGR, up from 74.0%.

Asia

Asia grew only 1.6% YoY and 0.8% QoQ to EUR 192.1m after -8.9% YoY / -3.7% QoQ in Q2; stabilisation is real but shallow.

  • Asked whether cyber-crime issues were solved, the CEO answered 'No'; company taking 'long-term steps' against video piracy and unauthorised redistribution.
  • Interim report no longer mentions the issue; it has become a running condition rather than a disclosed event.
  • Every 5% of Asian volatility is roughly 1.8 points of group growth, more than the entire Q3 YoY growth rate; game-rounds/revenue divergence (+22.5% vs +3.7%) most plausibly sits here.
“no quick solution”CEO

Europe

Europe is the structural worry: IP revenue -4.7% to EUR 173.7m, operator-location revenue -6.0% to EUR 336.3m, with share not being captured.

  • +0.4% QoQ after +3.5% QoQ in Q2; European IP revenue has declined from above EUR 200m (~$224m) per quarter in late 2024.
  • Management blamed regulatory change and tax but accepted it is not capturing its share of market growth; fix framed as 'a few stable quarters' of relationship work.

Americas and Africa

North America delivered another record at EUR 84.3m (+13.6% YoY, +4.1% QoQ) and remains the cleanest regulated growth story.

  • New Jersey studio is next.
  • Latin America (+23.1%) and Africa & other (+23.7%) both records; CEO stressed Latin America's Q3 growth 'didn't include any effects' from Brazil.

Brazil overhang

São Paulo studio closed 5 October under PM 1.394/2026; Q4 impact unquantified beyond Brazil being under EUR 24.5m (~$27m) quarterly, <4.7% of group.

  • Lula won the 25 October run-off with 51.76% having campaigned on the ban; resolution rests with the STF (constitutionality) and Congress (conversion).
  • Brazil is 'well below half' of LatAm IP revenue; market not ring-fenced; local staff retained for now.
  • Brazilian cost without revenue is a drag of perhaps 20-30bps on margin, which management says is absorbed within the unchanged ~66% FY target.

Our view

Regional picture supports stability rather than re-acceleration, and the grey-market risk premium is not going away with Asia at 37%.

  • Americas and Africa (30% of IP revenue) grew ~18%; Europe and Asia (70%) shrank ~1.5%.
  • Mix is drifting slowly toward regulated, lower-volatility revenue.
  • Brazil has just demonstrated that regulated revenue is not risk-free either.
