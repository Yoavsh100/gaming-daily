Singapore Pools

Released Annual Report 2025/26 published on singaporepools.com.sg on or before 13 Oct 2026 (exact release date unverified; FY ended 31 Mar 2026) · Currency SGD (S$) — tables in S$ millions unless marked bn; company is unlisted and reports annually only (no quarterly/H1 data exist) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: n/a — Singapore Pools (Private) Limited is unlisted: a wholly owned subsidiary of the Tote Board (a statutory board under Singapore's Ministry of Finance) with no traded equity or public debt. There is no market reaction, consensus or guidance to reference; the relevant 'stakeholder reaction' is the Tote Board's receipt of a flat S$583m contribution.

The one-line verdict

A third straight record year — turnover +6.2% to S$13.48bn with growth re-accelerating from +4% and no mega sporting event in the period — but the incremental dollar went almost entirely to prizes, opex and the Exchequer: betting duties & taxes rose 9% to S$2.49bn while the Tote Board contribution (the only "earnings" line the entity reports) was flat at S$583m (+1.4%). For sector investors the read-through is a still-growing Singapore gambling wallet and a monopoly lottery/tote model where the state captures ~88% of implied gross win before costs; this is a fiscal instrument first and an operating business second, and it discloses accordingly.

1Executive summary

  • Record FY26 turnover with growth re-accelerating. Stakes rose 6.2% YoY to S$13.48bn (Gaming Intelligence: "+6% to S$13.48bn"; company website "At a Glance" sourced to Annual Report 2025/26: S$13.5bn), following +4.0% in FY25 (S$12.69bn) and +7.0% in FY24 (S$12.2bn). Third consecutive record; 3-year CAGR from FY23's S$11.4bn is 5.7%.
  • There are no "expectations" to beat. Singapore Pools (Private) Limited is unlisted, wholly owned by the Tote Board (a statutory board under the Ministry of Finance), gives no guidance and has no consensus. The only benchmarks are the prior-year trend and the Budget's betting-duty line; against trend, +6% sits at the top of the post-reopening run-rate (ex the FY23 reopening/Qatar World Cup year).
  • The state was the main beneficiary of the growth. Betting duties & taxes rose 9.2% to S$2.49bn (FY25: flat at S$2.28bn), lifting the effective rate to 18.5% of turnover (+50bps). On the incremental S$790m of turnover, ~27c per S$1 went to duties versus an 18c blended average — consistent with mix reverting toward higher-duty lottery products after a sports/racing-led FY25, although the product split is not disclosed [inference, unverified].
  • The Tote Board contribution — the entity's de facto earnings line — stalled. S$583m, +1.4% YoY (FY25: +32% to S$575m; FY24: S$437m), or 4.3% of turnover (−20bps). Incremental flow-through to the surplus line collapsed to ~1c per incremental S$1 of turnover, versus ~28c in FY25.
  • FY26 prize payouts and operating expenses are not yet verified. The residual after duties and Tote Board contribution is S$10.41bn, exactly 77.2% of turnover — identical to FY25 (S$9.45bn prizes + S$353m opex = 77.2%). If opex held near the company's "~3%" framing (~S$0.37–0.40bn), prize payouts were ≈S$10.0bn (~74–74.5% payout ratio vs 74.5% in FY25) [derived estimate].
  • Implied gross win ≈S$3.45–3.47bn (+6–7% YoY) [derived: turnover less estimated prizes]. On that base, duties & taxes absorb ~72% and the Tote Board contribution ~17% — i.e. ~88% of gross win is captured by the state before operating costs. No commercial operator in our coverage runs anything close to this effective rate; it frames why opex discipline (~3% of stakes) is existential rather than optional.
  • The "97% returned to Singapore" narrative is intact. Prizes + duties + Tote Board contribution ≈S$13.1bn in FY26 [derived], after S$12.3bn (97.0%) in FY25 and S$11.87bn (97.3%) in FY24. Management has used this framing in every joint Chairman/CEO message since at least FY24.
  • Segment disclosure is nil — do not expect sportsbook KPIs. The company reports as a single entity across lottery (TOTO, 4D, Singapore Sweep), sports betting (football, motor racing) and horse racing (overseas simulcast). No handle, hold, net/structural margin, promo intensity, actives or ARPU are disclosed; our segment section is therefore built on the distribution waterfall plus qualitative product commentary from the FY24/FY25 reports.
  • No mega-event in the period — growth was base-driven, which is the better kind. FY26 (Apr 2025–Mar 2026) contained no World Cup or Euros; the FIFA World Cup 2026 (11 Jun–19 Jul 2026) falls entirely in FY27 Q1. A +6% year without an event tailwind implies underlying lottery/sports demand and online adoption are doing the work, and sets up an easier FY27 comparison.
  • Horse racing: first full year with zero local racing. Singapore Turf Club ceased local meetings in October 2024 (FY25 had ~6 months of Kranji racing); FY26 horse wagering was entirely overseas simulcast (Hong Kong, Australia, Japan, Korea, South Africa, UK via UK Tote commingling since Mar-24). Any product-level attrition was absorbed within the group total [product impact unverified].
  • Cost base: investment programme, not a transformation programme. No cost-reduction plan exists. FY25 opex rose 6.3% to S$353m (2.8% of turnover) on a transition to new-generation gaming systems, a new horse-racing broadcast studio and in-store AV upgrades. FY26 opex is unverified, but the flat Tote Board contribution against +6% turnover is consistent with opex growing faster than stakes [inference].
  • Capital position is not applicable in the conventional sense. The surplus is remitted to the Tote Board; there is no external equity, no public debt, no buyback and no dividend to third parties. The annual report publishes no balance sheet, cash flow or P&L — net debt, leverage, FCF and EPS are n/a by construction.
  • Parent read-through. The Tote Board Group (Tote Board + Singapore Pools + Singapore Turf Club) reported FY25 income of S$3.705bn (secondary source states +4% vs S$3.66bn, which does not reconcile arithmetically — flagged). Tote Board FY26 figures are not yet published; Singapore Pools' audited accounts sit inside the Tote Board report, not the Pools AR.
  • Regulatory backdrop stable but tightening at the margin. Regulated by the Gambling Regulatory Authority (since 2022); recertified at WLA Responsible Gaming Level 4. Management's stated priorities (FY24/25 ARs): customer retention and diversion from illegal operators, modernising the retail network, driving online adoption via the Singapore Pools Account. Watch safer-play measures on online account limits and advertising.
  • Management continuity. Chairman Kai S. Nargolwala and CEO Lam Chee Weng signed the FY25 joint message; the FY26 leadership statement could not be retrieved in this session [unverified]. No management change was reported by secondary sources.
  • Thesis read-through for listed peers. (a) Singapore's consumer gambling wallet is still compounding mid-single digits — supportive for Genting Singapore's mass segment and for lottery-monopoly comps (The Lottery Corp, Tabcorp, Allwyn) as evidence that monopoly lottery/tote turnover can grow 4–7% through the cycle at ~3% opex; (b) post-Kranji, Hong Kong and Australian racing bodies are the structural beneficiaries of Singapore simulcast demand; (c) duty-rate sensitivity: every 1pt of effective duty on S$13.5bn is ~S$135m, larger than any plausible opex saving.
  • What management is emphasising vs burying. Emphasised: record turnover, the 97% figure, community/CSR metrics. Not disclosed: product mix, online penetration, payout ratios by product, opex detail, and why the Tote Board contribution barely grew. A PM should treat the headline as fiscal data, not operating data.
  • Data confidence: Medium. Headline turnover, duties & taxes and Tote Board contribution are double-sourced (company website citing AR 2025/26 + Gaming Intelligence). FY26 prize payouts, opex, product/channel detail and management commentary are unverified — the AR 2025/26 PDF was not retrievable during research. Comparatives: AR 2023/24 (read in full) and four independent media reports of AR 2024/25.

3Segment deep-dive: Group value chain — turnover to Tote Board contribution (single reportable entity)

+S$790m of incremental stakes: ~27c per dollar to duties, ~1c to the Tote Board, ~72c to prizes and opex — the surplus line did not participate in the growth.

Turnover growth

Turnover rose 6.2% to S$13.48bn for the year to 31 March 2026, a third successive record with no obvious event or policy driver.

  • FY25 turnover was S$12.69bn; the company's own site rounds FY26 to S$13.5bn, citing the Annual Report 2025/26.
  • Growth re-accelerated from 4.0% in FY25, sits just below FY24's 7.0%; three-year compound rate 5.7% off FY23's S$11.4bn.
  • No World Cup or Euros, local horse racing already ceased, GST unchanged at 9% throughout.
  • Analyst reads it as underlying demand, most plausibly lottery jackpot cycles and migration to the Singapore Pools Account; no product or channel split to test this.

Distribution of stakes

Of S$790m incremental stakes, roughly S$210m went to duties, S$8m to the Tote Board and S$572m to prizes and operating costs.

  • Betting duties and taxes rose 9.2% to S$2.49bn; effective rate 18.5% of stakes versus 18.0%.
  • Tote Board contribution, the closest thing to a profit line, rose only 1.4% to S$583m, 4.3% of turnover against 4.5%.
  • In FY25 duties were flat (−0.4%) on 4% turnover growth and the Tote Board contribution jumped 32%.
  • Two years together look like a mix swing from sports/racing-weighted FY25 to lottery-weighted FY26; flagged as inference, the annual report does not say.

Prizes and opex residual

Turnover less duties less Tote Board contribution leaves S$10.41bn, 77.2% of stakes, exactly the FY25 ratio; prize payouts implied around S$10.0bn.

  • FY26 prize payouts and operating expenses not retrievable; FY25 was S$9.45bn prizes plus S$353m opex on S$12.69bn.
  • If opex grew in line with FY25 (S$332m to S$353m, +6.3%) to roughly S$370–400m, payout ratio was 74.2–74.5% versus 74.5% FY25 and 74.9% FY24.
  • Implied gross win around S$3.45–3.47bn, up 6–7%; duties absorbed ~72% and the Tote Board contribution ~17%.
  • State captures close to 88% of gross win before a single salary is paid; the 3% opex ratio is the only variable the company genuinely controls.

97% returned framing

The 97% returned to Singapore arithmetic holds at roughly S$13.1bn, but the surplus share fell to 4.3% of turnover while an investment programme runs.

  • Prizes, duties and Tote Board contribution combined: S$12.3bn (97.0%) in FY25 and S$11.87bn (97.3%) in FY24.
  • Stability is largely a function of a lottery-heavy payout ratio fixed by game design and a duty regime set by statute.
  • Tote Board contribution has gone from 3.6% of turnover in FY24 to 4.5% to 4.3%.
  • FY26 flatness coincides with a multi-year systems and retail investment programme whose cost is not separately disclosed.
“97% returned to Singapore and Singaporeans”Management

4Segment deep-dive: Products & channels — Lottery (TOTO / 4D / Singapore Sweep), Sports betting, Horse racing; retail vs Singapore Pools Account

No product-level numbers are disclosed; qualitatively, FY26 was the first full year without local horse racing and without a mega football event, which makes the +6% group print more creditable than it looks.

Disclosure and footprint

No product-level numbers are disclosed; FY26 was the first full year without local racing or a mega football event, making +6% more creditable.

  • Sole legal operator of lotteries, sports betting and horse-race wagering in Singapore across five products: TOTO, 4D, Singapore Sweep, sports betting, horse racing.
  • Retail estate at last published count (FY24): 84 branches, 3 Livewire venues, 9 off-course betting centres, 201 authorised retailers, plus the online Singapore Pools Account.
  • No actives, ARPU, handle, hold or promotional-intensity metrics exist in any filing.
  • Commentary draws on FY24 and FY25 reports plus inference; FY26-specific product commentary could not be retrieved.

Lottery

Duties grew 9% on 6% turnover growth, an incremental duty rate of ~27c per S$1 against an 18c blended average, pointing to lottery-led growth.

  • 4D and TOTO carry the bulk of stakes and duty burden; calendar anchored on TOTO Hongbao draw (17 Feb 2026, FY26 Q4), National Day draw, S$5m Singapore Sweep.
  • A large TOTO rollover cycle would explain the duty pattern and why the derived ~74% payout ratio did not drift up.
  • Mix cannot be verified from disclosures; the core lottery system refresh (completed May 2023) is an enabler, not the driver.

Sports betting

Sports is management's stated growth engine, but FY26 had no Euros or World Cup; the 48-team World Cup (11 Jun–19 Jul 2026) is an FY27 Q1 opportunity.

  • FY24 report added six football leagues including second-tier divisions in Germany, the Netherlands, Japan and Spain, new half-time/extra-time bet types, and cash-out piloted on the A-League in November 2023.
  • FIFA Club World Cup (June–July 2025, US venues, Asia-unfriendly kick-off times) was the only non-domestic tournament of note.
  • Cash-out and content expansion are framed as a counter to illegal operators, not margin tools; pricing and promotional intensity should not be modelled.
“strong growth momentum due to new leagues and more sporting events”Management

Horse racing

Racing is structurally impaired after the last Kranji meeting in October 2024; FY26 had no local racing and attrition is expected to continue.

  • FY25 contained roughly six months of local racing; all wagering now on overseas simulcasts, adding Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Germany alongside Hong Kong, Australia, Japan, Korea, South Africa.
  • Commingling arrangement with the UK Tote from March 2024 to stabilise dividends on UK races; new centralised betting control centre and broadcast studio built.
  • Group +6% absorbed any racing attrition, but the absorbing product appears to be lottery, not sports — a less durable substitute.
  • Racing turnover expected to keep shrinking as the local fan base ages out without a home track.

Channels and guidance

Online penetration is undisclosed; the flat FY26 Tote Board contribution is most simply explained by the systems and retail programme running ahead of turnover.

  • Priorities since FY24: customer retention, diversion from illegal online operators, retail modernisation, online adoption via the Singapore Pools Account with My Gaming Profile tools.
  • Safer Play Workgroup aligned to Gambling Regulatory Authority obligations and WLA Level 4 responsible-gaming recertification.
  • FY25 report flagged capital into a new-generation gaming system transition and in-store audio-visual upgrades; opex rose 6.3% to S$353m.
  • No segment guidance; the only forward statement is the mandate to counter illegal operators while minimising gambling harm, which caps marketing and pricing aggressiveness.

5Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Turnover +6.2% to a record S$13.48bn, re-accelerating from +4.0%, with no World Cup/Euros in the period and the first full year without local horse racing — the growth is base-driven, not event-driven. structural
  • Betting duties & taxes +9.2% to S$2.49bn (18.5% of turnover, +50bps): the monopoly's fiscal yield is rising faster than volume, which is what its owner cares about. structural
  • Tote Board contribution at a record S$583m — the surplus line has grown from S$437m (FY24) to S$575m to S$583m, +33% over two years. mixed
  • Cost ratio held at ~3% of turnover (2.7% FY24, 2.8% FY25, ~3% FY26 per company framing) through a multi-year systems/retail investment cycle — among the lowest of any gaming operator globally. structural
  • Prizes + opex residual exactly flat at 77.2% of turnover YoY implies the payout ratio (~74%) did not drift up on volume — no evidence of luck or jackpot-structure headwinds in the derived numbers. mixed
  • FY27 comparison is favourable: the expanded 48-team FIFA World Cup 2026 (11 Jun–19 Jul) sits entirely in FY27 Q1, and FY26 did not benefit from any comparable event. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Tote Board contribution grew only 1.4% on 6.2% turnover growth: flow-through of incremental stakes to the surplus line fell to ~1c per S$1 from ~28c in FY25. The owner got almost nothing from the record year. structural
  • Effective duty rate +50bps and incremental duty rate ~27c per S$1 point to a mix shift back toward higher-duty lottery products — a less durable growth source than sports, and one the company does not explain or quantify. watch
  • Disclosure is minimal: no product split, no payout ratio by game, no online penetration, no P&L, no balance sheet. FY26 prize payouts and opex could not be verified; the AR 2025/26 PDF was not retrievable during research. governance
  • Horse racing is in structural run-off after the October 2024 cessation of Kranji racing; overseas simulcast and UK Tote commingling slow but do not reverse attrition [product impact unverified]. structural
  • Opex appears to be growing faster than turnover (FY25 +6.3%; FY26 implied by the flat surplus) on a new-generation gaming system transition with no disclosed budget, timeline or completion date. watch
  • The counter-illegal-gambling mandate and GRA/safer-play obligations cap marketing, pricing and product innovation — the company cannot respond to illegal online competition with the promotional tools a commercial operator would use. structural
  • Tote Board Group FY24 comparator quoted by a secondary source (S$3.66bn, '+4%') does not reconcile with the S$3.705bn FY25 figure (+1.2%); parent-level growth is unverified. governance

Independent view. Turnover. We model FY27E stakes of S$14.0–14.4bn (+4–7%). The base case is trend growth of 4–5% (≈S$14.0–14.15bn) plus a World Cup uplift we size at only 1–2pts (S$150–250m): the 2026 tournament is the largest ever (104 matches) but North American kick-off times fall between ~01:00 and ~11:00 SGT, which historically dampens live-betting engagement relative to Qatar's evening slots, and sports is the smaller product. FY24 grew 7% while lapping the Qatar World Cup year, which tells us the event sensitivity of the group total is modest. Duties & taxes. At an 18.3–18.5% effective rate, S$2.56–2.66bn (+3–7%); the swing factor is mix — a sports-led World Cup year would pull the blended rate back toward FY25's 18.0%. Surplus/Tote Board contribution. S$560–650m. The range is wide because opex is undisclosed: if the systems transition keeps opex growing ~6–8% (≈S$400–430m) and the payout ratio holds ~74.5%, the contribution is roughly flat-to-up 5%; if the programme rolls off, there is S$50m+ of upside. Lottery jackpot luck (TOTO rollover sequences) is the other unmodellable variable and can move the line by tens of millions either way. Leverage. Not applicable — the entity remains debt-free and remits its surplus; no change expected. Key risks. (1) Budget 2027 changes to betting duty rates (each 1pt on S$13.5bn ≈ S$135m, larger than any opex lever); (2) GRA safer-play measures on online account limits or advertising; (3) continued racing attrition post-Kranji; (4) illegal online operator leakage, which the company cannot counter with promotions. Our stance on the print: a solid volume year whose economics accrued to the Exchequer rather than the owner; the FY27 question is whether the surplus line re-couples to turnover once the investment cycle peaks.
