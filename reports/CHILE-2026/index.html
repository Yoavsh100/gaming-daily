Chile

Released 24 Nov 2026 · Currency CLP (tables in CLP millions unless stated; ~US$ alongside at USD/CLP 1,017.66, BCCh dólar observado 24 Nov 2026) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Chile in late-2026 is two markets moving in opposite directions: a mature, SCJ-regulated land-based casino sector shrinking in real terms (9M-26 GGR CLP 401.9bn / ~$395m, -7.9% real for the 22 concession casinos; visits -11%) and a large, wholly unlicensed online market that the courts, Subtel and the tax authority are now squeezing (79 domains DNS-blocked since September; 19% VAT levied on offshore gaming margin with no licence attached). The Senate Economy Committee voted 3-1 on 9 Nov 2026 to replace the government's licensing bill with a ban or a Polla/Lotería monopoly, while the Kast government counters with a 22%-of-GGR licence regime and a total advertising and bonus ban; the choice between those two paths, due to start being resolved at the committee vote on 1 Dec 2026, is the single swing factor for any entrant.

1Executive summary

  • A two-speed market with no regulated online channel. Land-based casinos are the only licensed casino gaming in Chile: 25 venues (22 concessions under Ley 19.995 plus 3 municipal) produced GGR ("ingresos brutos del juego"/win, gross of tax) of CLP 549.3bn (~$540m) in FY-2025 and CLP 401.9bn (~$395m) in 9M-2026 (SCJ). Online betting and casino are entirely unlicensed; no regulator publishes an online GGR figure and press estimates of ">US$650m a year" are unverified and almost certainly a stakes/turnover-type number rather than GGR.
  • Land-based volumes are eroding in real terms. The 22 Ley 19.995 casinos booked CLP 372.8bn (~$366m) GGR in 9M-26, -7.9% real (-3.9% nominal vs CLP 388.1bn in 9M-25); the 3 municipal casinos CLP 29.2bn (~$29m), -5.9% real. Visits fell 11.25% to 4.07m while spend per visit rose 3.8% real to CLP 91,639 (~$90) — fewer, higher-value customers. Diario Financiero's August 2026 review put the seven-year decline at ~20% of revenue and ~30% of visits.
  • Casino tax take is falling faster than GGR. Taxes devengados by the 22 concession casinos were CLP 141.3bn (~$139m) in 9M-26 (-8.5% real): specific gaming tax CLP 61.7bn (~$61m, 20% of GGR net of VAT, split 50/50 regional governments/municipalities), VAT CLP 59.5bn (~$58m), entry tax CLP 20.1bn (~$20m, 0.07 UTM ≈ CLP 5,071/~$5 per visit). FY-25 taxes were CLP 194.2bn (~$191m), -4.7% real; effective burden ≈38% of GGR. On top, concession 'oferta económica' bid payments ran CLP 69.0bn (~$68m) in 2025 and CLP 54.4bn (~$53m) in 9M-26.
  • The online bill (Boletín 14.838-03) is in its fifth year. Introduced March 2022; approved by the Chamber 12 Dec 2023; Senate Economy Committee in general 2 Apr 2024 (unanimous); Senate floor in general 13 Aug 2025 (27-3-5); >570 amendments filed by mid-2026; a technical working group produced 'five axes' in Sept 2026 (platform control, user protection, ad limits, strict tax regime, blocking); article-by-article debate opened 27 Oct 2026; the Economy Committee has scheduled its vote for Tuesday 1 Dec 2026.
  • The Senate committee has backed prohibition, not licensing (9 Nov 2026, 3-1-1). Two amendments were approved: Ibáñez (FA)/Saavedra (PS) — ban on operating, offering or advertising online betting in Chile, with prison terms (up to 15 years per iGB) and carve-outs for state lotteries, authorised racetracks and land-based casinos; and Núñez (RN, Senate President)/Gatica (RN) — no private market, online reserved to Polla Chilena de Beneficencia and Lotería de Concepción, with court-ordered shutdowns. The texts are to be merged, then go to the Finance Committee, the Senate floor and back to the Chamber (third reading) — a cross-party ban coalition spanning RN to the Frente Amplio.
  • The Kast government still wants a licensed market — at 22% of GGR. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz backs a 22% tax on operators' gaming revenue, up from the Senate general text's 20% of gross gaming income + 19% VAT + a 1% responsible-gambling levy; Senator Walker wants 'a much higher rate'; Senator Sánchez's conditions are a cap on licences, access controls, high taxes and ad limits. Whether land-based casinos may go online splits the pro-regulation camp (Walker for, Ossandón against).
  • The government's own amendments (27 Oct 2026) are among the strictest in LatAm. Total ban on online-betting advertising — licensed or not — extending to agencies and media; ban on bonuses and promotions even outside advertising; mandatory biometric identity verification; default deposit and time limits set at account opening (tightening immediate, loosening only after 72h and re-confirmation); mandatory risk detection with forced pauses/suspensions; a single national self-exclusion register. Illegal advertising becomes a crime (presidio menor, mínimo a medio, plus 10–20 UTM ≈ CLP 0.72–1.45m / ~$712–1,424), aggravated if aimed at minors. The industry body aPAL objects that a total ad ban stops players distinguishing licensed from illegal sites.
  • Enforcement has moved from theory to DNS blocking. The Supreme Court (rol 18.080-2025, 30 Sep 2025, on protection actions by Lotería de Concepción and Polla) held online betting sites operate illegally and ordered ISPs to block them; in April 2026 it reversed an Appeals Court finding that blocking had been complied with; on 8 Aug 2026 the Santiago Court of Appeals set a permanent DNS-blocking procedure with lists validated by the SCJ. Subtel then ordered Entel, Movistar, Claro, GTD, WOM and VTR to block 42 domains (1 Sep 2026) and 37 more (21 Sep 2026) within 48 hours — covering Betano, Coolbet, JugaBet, 1xBet, Betsson, Rojabet, Betsala, MiCasino, Estelarbet, Betcris, KTO, Marathonbet, Betway, Betfair, Bet365 and Sporting Bet — with successive lists for mirrors promised.
  • Taxed but not licensed: the SII now collects 19% VAT on offshore gaming margin. SII Resolución Exenta N°69 (2 Jun 2026) opened the simplified non-resident digital-services VAT regime to online betting/casino operators; Ordinario N°1658 (8 Jul 2026) set the taxable base on the gaming margin (GGR); a list of 25 registered platforms was published 15 Jul 2026 and had grown to 39 by Sept 2026. The SII insists this 'does not validate the activity in any way' (8 Sep 2026) — operators therefore pay VAT on GGR, remain illegal, and are being blocked at the same time.
  • Land-based incumbents are fighting the SII regime in court. The Asociación Chilena de Casinos y Juegos (16 casinos, ~67% of venues) filed an amparo económico at the Santiago Court of Appeals (reported 21 Nov 2026) arguing the SII has 'organised the taxation of an activity prohibited by law', that the margin-based VAT base has no statutory footing, and that registered platforms use the nómina to resist blocking; it seeks annulment, withdrawal of the list and an interim order. Contraloría declined to rule (dictamen D348/2026, 10 Jul 2026).
  • Online operator landscape — concentrated visibility, weak apps. GR8 Tech's Operator Lens: Chile (Oct/Nov 2026) finds Betano, JugaBet and Coolbet hold 86% of online visibility among analysed brands; JugaBet leads organic search in both betting and casino terms; ~70% of traffic to key gambling sites is mobile yet few apps are well positioned; Instagram is the largest social channel; 1xBet leads live coverage at 846 events/hour (esports, virtuals, minor leagues); operators list 8,935 slots on average but only 11% of the 597 most-promoted titles appear at more than one operator.
  • Leaked Curaçao filings give the only hard online unit economics. Latamwin (W&C N.V.): 2024 gaming revenue before bonuses CLP 39.1bn (~$38m), +24.6% on CLP 31.4bn (~$31m); bonuses CLP 9.8bn (25% of gross); payment-processing costs CLP 9.2bn (~$9m, 24% of gross); marketing/affiliates CLP 2.6bn (~$2.6m); net profit CLP 11.2bn (~$11m). Estelarbet Chile 2024: stakes US$113.3m, deposits US$17.8m (6.4x recycling), GGR US$5.8m (5.1% hold; US$5.1m casino, US$0.7m sports), NGR US$4.1m, EBITDA US$1.1m, 6,437 daily actives. Rojabet (Media Entertainment N.V.) sales fell from US$46.2m (2023) to US$30.8m (2025) with losses each year; Betsala grew to €3.3m (2024), targeting €9.9m for 2026.
  • Payments are card/bank-transfer rails, not mobile money — and they are expensive. Chile has no M-Pesa-type ecosystem; grey operators rely on card acquiring, bank transfers and local PSP/wallet aggregators, which cost Latamwin ~24% of gross gaming revenue in 2024 — a drag that a licensed regime with mainstream acquiring would largely remove, but which the blocking campaign and any payment-blocking powers would worsen. The SCJ and Sernac warned on 24 Nov 2026 that prizes on blocked sites may go unpaid with no recourse.
  • State operators are the political centre of gravity. Polla Chilena de Beneficencia (state-owned; its Xperto product is the only legal online sports betting) and Lotería de Concepción (Universidad de Concepción) brought the blocking litigation, report mirror sites to the court, and would receive an online monopoly under the Núñez/Gatica amendment. Horse-racing (Teletrak) and the lotteries are exempted in the ban text. FY-2025 lottery sales were not retrievable in this research (n/a).
  • Political direction: regulate-with-restrictions versus prohibit. The Kast administration (in office since 11 Mar 2026) is pro-licensing but withdrew the bill's urgency on 22 Oct 2026 (oficio 192-374; 'sin urgencia' as of 14 Nov), while the Senate's cross-party ban bloc, the state lotteries and the land-based casino lobby (which is itself barred from going online) all push the other way. A separate Chamber initiative seeks to restrict the taxation of online betting (i.e., stop the SII de facto regularisation) [details unverified].
  • Macro/FX headwind. The peso is at its weakest since July 2022 — BCCh observado CLP 1,017.66 on 24 Nov 2026, spot above CLP 1,030 after a 3.9–5.9% copper slump — so USD-reported casino revenue is falling faster than CLP figures suggest (Yogonet's 9M-26 USD 444.7m used a ~905 rate; at today's rate the same CLP is ~$395m).
  • Read-through for B2Tech: do not launch into the grey market now; build a licence-ready position. Operating today means DNS blocking, 19% VAT with no rights, criminal exposure, and — critically — losing eligibility for the bill's transitional licences, reserved for applicants that have not operated illegally in Chile in the prior 12 months. The value play is a clean, Chile-domiciled licence applicant (or B2B platform partner to local casinos/lotteries if they are let online), modelled at 22–25% of GGR + 19% VAT + 1% RG levy with zero advertising and zero bonuses — a retention-, SEO- and app-led economics, where GR8 Tech's data says incumbents are weakest (apps, local SEO, differentiated content).
  • Base case (our estimate): no licensed private market before 2H-2027. We assign ~40% to a prolonged stalemate (bill stalls; de facto prohibition via blocking + VAT), ~35% to a licensed regime enacted by end-2027 with ≥22% GGR tax, a total ad ban and a capped number of licences, and ~25% to a statutory ban/state monopoly. Land-based GGR should finish 2026 at roughly CLP 530–540bn (~$520–530m, -2% to -4% nominal; estimate), with blocking offering only marginal relief.

3Deep-dive: Land-based casinos (SCJ-regulated)

Only licensed casino gaming in Chile; 9M-26 GGR CLP 401.9bn (~$395m), -7.9% real, visits -11%, tax take -8.5% real — a mature market in structural decline, barred from going online.

9M-26 GGR decline

Only licensed casino gaming in Chile; 9M-26 system GGR roughly CLP 401.9bn (~$395m), -7.9% real, a mature market in structural decline barred from going online.

  • SCJ (created by Ley 19.995) regulates 22 concession casinos plus three legacy municipal-concession venues; Q3-2026 release dated 3 Nov 2026.
  • 22 concession casinos: gross gaming income CLP 372,774m (~$366m) in 9M-26, -7.9% real (-3.9% nominal) vs CLP 388,096m (~$381m) in 9M-25.
  • Three municipal casinos added CLP 29,163m (~$29m), -5.9% real; Yogonet's read gives CLP 402,759m, immaterial difference, but it mis-signed the 22-casino change as +7.9%.
  • FY-25: CLP 509,826m (~$501m) concession plus CLP 39,483m (~$39m) municipal, CLP 549.3bn (~$540m) total, vs more than CLP 554bn (~$544m) in 2024 — flat nominal, roughly -4% real.

Visits and spend

Volume is weaker than revenue: 9M-26 visits 4,067,872, down 11.25%, while real spend per visit rose 3.8% to CLP 91,639 (~$90).

  • Spend per visit was CLP 86,019 (~$85) for FY-25 and CLP 87,649 (~$86) at H1-26; sector lives off a smaller, more intensive customer base.
  • Diario Financiero's August 2026 'radiografía' put the seven-year decline at about 20% of revenue and almost 30% of visits.
  • Monthly 2026 win: CLP 48,049m (~$47m) Jan, 44,257m (~$43m) Feb, 45,491m (~$45m) Apr, 47,553m (~$47m) May, 42,473m (~$42m) Jun — a CLP 42–48bn (~$42–47m) band, no acceleration.
  • Jan–Feb 2026: Dreams' Monticello (Santiago) CLP 20,110m (~$20m), 21.8% of system win; Enjoy Viña del Mar CLP 9,994m (~$10m). Dreams and Enjoy are the two dominant groups.

Taxation

Taxes are heavy and falling faster than revenue: 9M-26 concession-casino taxes CLP 141,329m (~$139m), -8.46% real; state take exceeds half of GGR including bid payments.

  • Specific gaming tax CLP 61,688m (~$61m; 20% of GGR net of VAT, 50% regional governments / 50% municipalities); VAT CLP 59,519m (~$58m; 19%).
  • Entry tax CLP 20,123m (~$20m; 0.07 UTM per entry — CLP 5,071 / ~$5 at November UTM of CLP 72,440).
  • FY-25 taxes CLP 194,214m (~$191m), -4.7% real, after more than CLP 196bn (~$193m) in 2024; effective burden ~38% of GGR before 'oferta económica'.
  • Concession bid payments added CLP 69,042m (~$68m) in 2025 and CLP 54,374m (~$53m) in 9M-26; industry's political priority is protection from online substitution, not expansion.

Partner or obstacle

Land-based sector is both potential B2B partner and obstacle: casinos cannot go online and the casino lobby is aligned with the Senate prohibition bloc.

  • Asociación Chilena de Casinos y Juegos (16 casinos, ~67% of venues) has gone to court against SII's VAT registration of offshore operators as de facto regularisation of illegal activity.
  • Lobby stays with prohibition bloc unless the bill grants casinos online rights; pro-regulation senators split (Walker for, Ossandón against, citing 'legal monopoly' risk).
  • Key question for a platform provider: online rights via existing concession holders (casinos as natural B2B customers) or a separate licence class open to new entrants.
  • Chamber-approved text envisaged a separate licence with SCJ renamed 'Superintendencia de Casinos, Apuestas y Juegos de Azar'.

4Deep-dive: Online betting & casino (unlicensed / offshore)

Large, growing and illegal: three brands own 86% of visibility, leaked filings show double-digit growth and 24%-of-GGR payment costs, and since September 2026 the state is DNS-blocking and VAT-taxing operators simultaneously.

Market size

No regulated online gambling exists; our estimate of 2025 online GGR is US$250–400m, half to three-quarters of land-based, and the press 'US$650m' figure is unverified.

  • Only legal online products: state lotteries (Polla's Xperto sports betting, Lotería online draws) and horse-racing tote.
  • Supreme Court ruling of September 2025 (rol 18.080-2025) makes private online sites — offshore-licensed, mostly Curaçao and Malta — illegal.
  • 'More than US$650 million a year' is unattributed to any regulator and looks like a stakes or deposits number rather than GGR.
  • Our US$250–400m working estimate is built from leaked filings and the three dominant brands' relative scale and should be labelled an estimate.

Competitive structure

Concentrated at the top: GR8 Tech finds Betano, JugaBet and Coolbet hold 86% of online visibility, with local JugaBet leading organic search.

  • GR8 Tech's Operator Lens: Chile published late Oct 2026, promoted 24 Nov 2026; these are visibility and product metrics, not revenue shares.
  • ~70% of traffic to key gambling sites is mobile yet few operators have strong app positions; Instagram is the largest social audience in most cases.
  • 1xBet leads live coverage with an average 846 events per hour, driven by esports, virtuals and minor competitions.
  • Average casino lobby carries 8,935 slots; only 11% of the 597 most-promoted titles appear at more than one operator — curation, not catalogue size, differentiates.

Curaçao Papers economics

Leaked filings show double-digit growth and ~38% net margins at Latamwin, with payment costs of 24% of gross GGR and bonuses of 25%.

  • W&C N.V. (Latamwin) 2024 NGR before bonuses CLP 39,083m (~$38m), +24.6% on CLP 31,357m (~$31m); bonuses CLP 9,796m (25%); operating revenue CLP 29,287m (~$29m).
  • Latamwin payment costs CLP 9,225m (~$9m, 24% of gross / 31% of net); marketing and affiliates CLP 2,625m (~$2.6m); draft net profit CLP 11,164m (~$11m).
  • Estelarbet 2024: stakes US$113.3m on deposits US$17.8m (6.4x recycling), GGR US$5.8m (5.1% hold; US$5.1m casino, US$0.7m sports), NGR US$4.1m, EBITDA US$1.1m, 6,437 daily actives; marketing to rise from US$0.41m to US$1.4m in 2025.
  • Rojabet (Media Entertainment N.V.) sales fell from US$46.2m (2023) to US$34.2m (2024) and US$30.8m (2025), sports halving to US$12.5m, net losses each year; Betsala grew from €0.54m (2022) to €3.3m (2024), targeting €5.6m (2025) and €9.9m (2026).
  • Pattern — casino-led hold, heavy bonusing, expensive payments, share consolidating to the big three — is typical of a grey market about to be regulated or closed.

Enforcement in 2026

Since September 2026 the state is DNS-blocking and VAT-taxing offshore operators simultaneously — the worst of both worlds for an operator.

  • Supreme Court in April reversed the Santiago Court of Appeals; on 8 Aug 2026 the Appeals Court fixed permanent DNS blocking of SCJ-validated domains with ISPs reporting execution.
  • Subtel ordered six ISPs (Entel, Movistar, Claro, GTD, WOM, VTR) to block 42 domains on 1 Sep 2026 and 37 mirrors on 21 Sep 2026, each within 48 hours; Polla committed to report mirrors.
  • Blocked brands include Betano, Coolbet, JugaBet, 1xBet, Betsson, Rojabet, Betsala, MiCasino, Estelarbet, Betcris, KTO, Marathonbet, Betway, Betfair, Bet365 and Sporting Bet.
  • SII Res. Ex. 69 (2 Jun 2026) and Ord. 1658 (8 Jul 2026) put offshore operators in the non-resident digital-services VAT regime on a gaming-margin base: 25 registered by 15 Jul 2026, 39 by September.
  • Result: 19% VAT on GGR with no licence, DNS blocking, criminal exposure for advertising under pending amendments, and SCJ/Sernac warning (24 Nov 2026) that winnings on blocked sites may go unpaid.

Implication for B2Tech

Entering the grey market now would cost 19% VAT plus ~24% payment friction, expose the brand to blocking, and disqualify B2Tech from transitional licences.

  • Finance Ministry summary reserves transitional licences for applicants not operating illegally in Chile in the preceding 12 months, meeting comparable-jurisdiction technical standards and solvency tests.
  • Government proposes criminalisation of advertising, adding brand exposure.
  • Opportunity under a licence regime lies where incumbents are weak per GR8 Tech — native apps, Chile-specific SEO, differentiated content.
  • Rules will likely prohibit advertising and bonuses, neutralising incumbents' main acquisition weapons.

5Deep-dive: Lottery, horse racing & state operators

Polla Chilena and Lotería de Concepción are the only legal online gambling and the plaintiffs behind the blocking regime; one Senate amendment would hand them the whole online market.

Statutory operators

Polla Chilena and Lotería de Concepción are the only legal online gambling; FY-2025 sales and GGR were not retrievable and are shown as n/a.

  • Polla Chilena de Beneficencia is state-owned under the Finance Ministry; its Xperto product is the only legal online sports-betting offer in the country.
  • Lotería de Concepción, owned by Universidad de Concepción, sells draw games including online.
  • Pari-mutuel horse-race betting through the hipódromos (Teletrak network) is authorised, including online.
  • Figures are shown as n/a rather than estimated.

Legal and political role

The lotteries are the plaintiffs behind the blocking regime and the de facto enforcement arm; one Senate amendment would hand them the whole online market.

  • Their protection actions produced the Supreme Court's 30 Sep 2025 ruling, the April 2026 reversal forcing ISP compliance, and the 8 Aug 2026 Appeals Court procedure.
  • Polla publicly committed (3 Sep 2026) to report mirror sites.
  • Senate President Paulina Núñez's amendment (approved in committee 9 Nov 2026) would reserve online gambling to Polla and Lotería outright.
  • The Ibáñez/Saavedra ban text exempts state lotteries, authorised racetracks and land-based casinos from the prohibition.

Scenarios for suppliers

In a monopoly outcome lotteries are the only addressable B2B customers; in a licensing outcome they are competitors with statutory brand advantage.

  • Monopoly route is tender-driven, low-margin but defensible.
  • As competitors they have a track record of litigating against private entrants.
  • Xperto's current scale is the best proxy for 'legal' online demand; the absence of published 2025 figures is a data gap worth closing before any investment decision.

6Deep-dive: Licensing & tax: current regime and the pending online bill

Casinos pay ~38% of GGR plus bid fees; online is taxed at 19% VAT with no licence; the bill offers 5-year licences at 20–22% of GGR with a clean-hands rule — but the Senate committee has voted for prohibition.

Existing casino regime

Under Ley 19.995 casinos pay an effective 37.9% of GGR plus bid fees, and no online licence of any kind exists.

  • SCJ grants concessions by competitive bid ('oferta económica'); statutory cap of 24 concessions outside legacy municipal casinos, 15-year terms [statutory details not re-verified].
  • Taxes: 20% specific gaming tax on GGR net of VAT (50% regional, 50% municipal), 19% VAT, entry tax of 0.07 UTM per visit (CLP 5,071 / ~$5 in Nov-26).
  • 9M-26 burden: CLP 141.3bn (~$139m) on CLP 372.8bn (~$366m) GGR, before bid payments of CLP 54.4bn (~$53m).
  • Concession casinos are not permitted to operate online.

VAT on offshore operators

SII now taxes online operators at 19% VAT on gaming margin with no licence; a casino-association court challenge could erase the registration list.

  • Res. Ex. N°69 (2 Jun 2026) extended the non-resident digital-services VAT regime to online betting, reversing the SII's 2023 exclusion; Ord. N°1658 (8 Jul 2026) fixed GGR as base.
  • 25 platforms registered by 15 Jul 2026, 39 by September.
  • Casino association's amparo económico (Santiago Court of Appeals, Nov 2026) attacks use of a digital-entertainment VAT event and a margin base set by administrative letter; Contraloría abstained (D348/2026).
  • If the court sides with casinos, the SII list disappears and operators' quasi-regularisation argument against blocking goes with it.
“does not validate the activity in any case”SII

The online bill

Boletín 14.838-03, approved in general by the Senate 27-3-5 on 13 Aug 2025, offers 5-year licences at 20% of GGR plus 19% VAT plus 1% RG levy with a clean-hands rule.

  • Converts SCJ into Superintendencia de Casinos, Apuestas y Juegos de Azar with licensing and supervisory powers; operators must be companies registered and domiciled in Chile.
  • A further earmark for sport was discussed in the Chamber text [unverified]; advertising permitted only by licensed platforms (Art. 51); illegal gambling criminalised.
  • Enforcement in force the first day of the month after publication; licensing regime operative once the reglamento is issued (six months).
  • Transitional licences only for applicants not operating illegally in Chile in the prior 12 months, meeting comparable-jurisdiction technical standards and solvency — favours new entrants and B2B-backed locals.

2026 amendment war

Text is being pushed in two incompatible directions: government retains licensing with ad and bonus bans and a 22% tax, while the Senate committee voted 3-1-1 for prohibition.

  • Kast government indications (27 Oct 2026; Undersecretary Vallebona): ban all online-betting advertising including licensees, agencies and intermediaries; ban bonuses; biometric KYC; default deposit/time limits with 72-hour loosening delay; single exclusion register.
  • Illegal advertising a crime: presidio menor mínimo a medio plus 10–20 UTM (CLP 0.72–1.45m / ~$712–1,424), aggravated if targeting minors; Finance Minister Quiroz added a 22% tax on gaming revenue on 24 Nov 2026.
  • Senate Economy Committee on 9 Nov 2026 approved Ibáñez/Saavedra (ban regardless of server location, prison up to 15 years, exemptions for lotteries, racetracks, casinos) and Núñez/Gatica (online reserved to Polla and Lotería, court-ordered shutdowns), to be merged.
  • Prohibition is partly a negotiating lever for a tougher licensing deal.
“did not want to take any”Senator Sánchez

Process and tax envelope

No statutory clock after urgency was withdrawn 22 Oct 2026; realistic licensed tax take is roughly 40–45% of GGR, the high end of LatAm.

  • Merged committee text goes to Senate Finance Committee, Senate floor, then back to the Chamber (which passed a licensing version in Dec 2023) for third reading and possibly a mixed commission.
  • Envelope if licensing survives: 22–25% of GGR plus 19% VAT plus 1% RG, with no advertising, no bonuses and a capped licence count.
  • Comparable to the land-based burden; LatAm peers are Brazil 12% GGR, Colombia 15% GGR + VAT, Peru 12% + 1% consumption levy.
  • Survivable for a low-CAC, product-led operator and hostile to bonus-driven acquisition models.

7Deep-dive: Payments, advertising, responsible gambling & enforcement

Card and bank rails rather than mobile money, costing grey operators ~24% of GGR; advertising faces a total ban even for licensees; DNS blocking is live and expanding; RG rules in the pipeline are among LatAm's tightest.

Payments

Chile is a card- and bank-rail economy; grey rails cost Latamwin 23.6% of gross GGR, making mainstream acquiring the single largest margin lever for a legal entrant.

  • Deposits to offshore sites run through card acquiring, bank transfers and local PSP/wallet aggregators, not mobile money.
  • W&C N.V. (Latamwin) paid CLP 9,225m (~$9m) to processors in 2024, 23.6% of gross and 31.5% of post-bonus revenue, over three times its CLP 2,625m (~$2.6m) marketing/affiliate spend; bonuses took a further 25%.
  • Estelarbet's plan implies 6.4x deposit recycling (US$113.3m staked on US$17.8m deposited) and a 5.1% blended hold, consistent with a casino-led market.
  • Senate working group's 'five axes' (Sept 2026) include blocking of illegal operators; payment-side blocking is a live option [specifics unverified].

Advertising

Government amendments of 27 Oct 2026 would ban all online-betting advertising, bonuses and promotions, even for licensees; B2Tech should plan for zero above-the-line marketing.

  • Betting brands have been prominent sponsors in Chilean football and digital media; GR8 Tech ranks Instagram as the largest social channel for most operators.
  • Penalties: licence revocation for licensees; criminal penalties for unlicensed advertising (presidio menor, 10–20 UTM ≈ CLP 0.72–1.45m / ~$712–1,424), aggravated when targeting minors.
  • Industry group aPAL argues a total ban prevents players identifying licensed sites and slows channelisation; senators' amendments add scheduling and black-and-white format rules [per Infogate].
  • Weight shifts to organic search (JugaBet already beats internationals), app-store presence (few strong positions per GR8 Tech) and CRM.

Responsible gambling

The government text's RG rules are among LatAm's tightest, implementable with a modern PAM but adding cost and friction.

  • Biometric identity verification at onboarding; default deposit and session-time limits, tightenable instantly but loosenable only after 72 hours and re-confirmation.
  • Mandatory detection of risk behaviour with forced pauses, deposit restrictions or temporary suspension; national register of excluded persons checked before account opening.
  • Ludopatía fund financed by the 1% levy; absolute bar on minors.
  • An SCJ with no online experience will lean on technical standards imported from comparable jurisdictions, as the transitional-licence test references.

Enforcement capacity

Enforcement has caught up with the law in 2026 and will not reverse whichever legislative path wins; never having operated in the grey market rises in value.

  • Judiciary established illegality (Supreme Court, Sept 2025), forced ISP compliance (April 2026) and institutionalised SCJ-validated DNS blocking (Aug 2026).
  • Subtel executed two waves (42 and 37 domains) in September with 48-hour deadlines and committed to rolling mirror updates; SII holds registered operators' identities and margin data.
  • SCJ and Sernac warn players winnings on blocked sites may be unrecoverable; VPNs, mirrors and crypto on-ramps remain but shrink the grey market and raise its cost.
  • A ban adds criminal liability for operators, advertisers and intermediaries; licensing adds the clean-hands filter.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Demonstrated, growing demand: leaked 2024 accounts show Latamwin's Chilean gaming revenue up 24.6% to CLP 39.1bn (~$38m) with a ~38% net margin, and Estelarbet turning US$113m of stakes on just US$17.8m of deposits — a casino-led market with high recycling that a licensed entrant could serve at far lower payment and legal cost. structural
  • Incumbents are strong on visibility but weak where it is cheapest to compete: Betano/JugaBet/Coolbet hold 86% of visibility, yet ~70% of traffic is mobile and few operators have strong app positions, and only 11% of top-promoted slots overlap across lobbies — app, local-SEO and content-curation whitespace (GR8 Tech). structural
  • The bill's clean-hands transitional licence (no illegal operation in Chile in the prior 12 months, proven technical standards and solvency) structurally favours a new, B2B-backed, Chile-domiciled applicant over today's grey operators — a rare case where not being in the market is an asset. structural
  • The Executive is still on the licensing side and has put a concrete number on the table — 22% of GGR (24 Nov 2026) — within a total envelope (≈22–25% + 19% VAT + 1% RG) that is heavy but survivable for a low-CAC, retention-led model; advertising and bonus bans would neutralise the incumbents' main acquisition advantage. mixed
  • Enforcement is now real and institutionalised (Supreme Court → permanent DNS procedure → 79 domains blocked in September; SII registration of 39 operators), so a future licensed market would start with meaningfully higher channelisation than Chile's grey past implies. structural
  • Land-based casinos (9M-26 GGR -7.9% real, visits -11%) are motivated partners for an online B2B platform if the final text lets concessionaires online — the Dreams/Enjoy duopoly and 22 concession venues are an addressable B2B base; the state lotteries are the fallback customer in a monopoly outcome. watch

▼ Lowlights

  • The Senate Economy Committee voted 3-1-1 on 9 Nov 2026 for prohibition (Ibáñez/Saavedra: full ban with prison terms up to 15 years; Núñez/Gatica: Polla/Lotería monopoly), with a cross-party bloc from RN to the Frente Amplio — a licensed private market is no longer the base case. structural
  • The government's own amendments ban all advertising (including by licensees) and all bonuses/promotions, criminalise illegal advertising (presidio menor + 10–20 UTM ≈ CLP 0.72–1.45m / ~$712–1,424) and impose biometric KYC and default limits — even the 'good' outcome is a no-marketing market. structural
  • Operating today means 19% VAT on gaming margin with no licence rights (SII Res. Ex. 69/2026), DNS blocking of 79 domains with rolling mirror updates, Sernac/SCJ warnings to players, and forfeiture of eligibility for transitional licences — the grey option is value-destructive. structural
  • No regulator data exists for online market size; the '>US$650m/yr' press figure is unverified and likely a stakes-type number — any business case rests on leaked operator filings and estimates (our estimate: US$250–400m GGR, 2025). watch
  • Timeline risk: the bill is in year five, carries no urgency (withdrawn 22 Oct 2026), faces a Finance Committee, a floor vote and a return to the Chamber; the casino association's amparo against the SII and Polla/Lotería litigation add further legal variables — 2027 enactment is far from assured. mixed
  • Payments friction: grey rails cost Latamwin ~24% of gross GGR in 2024; a ban would add payment-blocking and criminal liability for intermediaries, and even a licensed regime will inherit heavy RG-driven KYC costs. structural
  • FX and macro: the peso at CLP 1,017.66 observado (24 Nov 2026) — spot above 1,030, weakest since July 2022 after a copper slump — erodes USD-denominated revenue; land-based GGR is already falling 7.9% real. one-off

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The rule set in play has moved from the Senate's August-2025 general text (licensing by a renamed SCJ, 5-year licences, 20% GGR tax + 19% VAT + 1% RG levy, advertising permitted for licensees) to two mutually exclusive 2026 alternatives: the Kast government's October/November amendments (licensing at 22% of GGR with a total advertising and bonus ban, biometric KYC, default limits and a national exclusion register) and the Senate Economy Committee's 9 Nov 2026 prohibition amendments (no private market; Polla/Lotería monopoly; prison terms). The shift was driven by the Supreme Court's illegality finding, the state lotteries' litigation, the land-based casino lobby, and a cross-party political backlash over ludopatía and minors — not by any failure of demand. Meanwhile enforcement (DNS blocking, SII VAT registration) has already changed the operating reality irrespective of the bill.

Independent view. Our independent view (all probabilities and figures are estimates): ~40% prolonged stalemate — the merged ban text and the government's licensing text deadlock through the Finance Committee and floor, leaving de facto prohibition via blocking and VAT through 2027; ~35% a licensed private market enacted by end-2027, with a tax of 22–25% of GGR plus VAT and the 1% levy (≈40–45% of GGR all-in), a total advertising and bonus ban, biometric KYC and a capped licence count, with first licences no earlier than 2028 given the six-month reglamento; ~25% a statutory ban or Polla/Lotería monopoly. On market size, online GGR of roughly US$250–400m in 2025 (estimate) is our working figure; under licensing we would expect regulated GGR to reach only 50–60% of that in year one because of the no-ad/no-bonus regime and the clean-hands exclusion of incumbents, rising thereafter as blocking bites. Land-based casino GGR should finish 2026 at ~CLP 530–540bn (~$520–530m), -2% to -4% nominal (estimate), with 2027 flat-to-down; the tax take will fall faster than GGR as visits decline. Leverage direction for the state: tax burden on online is going up, not down — the only live debate is between 22% and 'much higher'. For B2Tech, the right posture is licence-ready and grey-free: a Chile-domiciled vehicle, a local-casino or lottery B2B track, and a product plan built for zero-marketing economics; commit capital only on the Senate floor vote, not the committee.
