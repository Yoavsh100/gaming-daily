SEPTEMBER 2026 · market & regulatory brief

New York

Released 7 Oct 2026 (NYSGC statewide mobile report, data through Sep-26); land-based and operator-level trade coverage 20–27 Oct 2026 · Currency USD (tables in millions unless stated; New York reports natively in USD so no FX conversion applies) · Data confidence: HIGH

The one-line verdict

September 2026 was a volume-strong, margin-weak month for New York mobile sports betting: a record-September $2.40bn handle (+5.0% YoY) produced only $159.4m GGR (-17.7%) on a 6.6% hold, the lowest September since launch, cutting the state's 51% tax take to $81.3m. The structural story is on the ground: Resorts World NYC's April conversion to a full commercial casino lifted statewide commercial-casino GGR 220% to $184.3m while draining VLT net win 37% to $124.8m. New York remains the largest, most transparent and most heavily taxed US betting market — a scale-only proposition for B2C, with the real white space in B2B supply to the three downstate casinos and in iGaming optionality that Albany has yet to unlock.

1Executive summary

  • Largest US betting market, and still growing on volume: NYSGC reports September 2026 mobile sports handle of $2,401.7m, +5.0% YoY and +38.7% MoM on the NFL/college restart — the busiest September on record and the sixth-highest month in state history; all-time mobile handle has passed $103.8bn since the 8 Jan 2022 launch.
  • But GGR fell hard on hold, not demand: GGR of $159.4m was -17.7% YoY as hold collapsed to 6.64% (Sep-25: 8.47%; Aug-26: 9.73%). Week ending 13 Sep produced only $17.0m GGR on $594.6m handle (2.9% hold) — a bettor-friendly NFL opening fortnight, not a structural shift. Six of eight active books made less money than a year ago.
  • Fiscal year-to-date tells the same story: FY2026/27 (Apr–Sep) handle $12,562.6m (+6.9%) but GGR $1,107.3m (-5.8%) and tax $564.8m (-5.8%); YTD hold 8.8% vs 10.0% a year earlier. A -$48.5m GGR week (w/e 14 Jun) tied to Knicks championship futures payouts (cash-basis accounting) is the single biggest drag.
  • FY2025/26 was a record base: handle $26,158.4m (+9.3%), GGR $2,598.4m (+21.2%, 9.93% hold), state tax $1,325.1m (+18.7%). The Office of the State Comptroller (OSC, Sep 2026) confirms mobile sports wagering is now the state's second-largest gaming revenue source after the Lottery; total state gaming receipts exceeded $5.1bn in SFY2026.
  • Tax regime — 51% of GGR, no promo deductions: the highest headline rate of any competitive US state. Net revenue to education in September was $81.3m (-17.7%); operators kept $78.1m. From FY2026/27 the earmark for problem-gambling education/treatment doubled from $6m to $12m, plus $5m for youth sports.
  • Duopoly intact, challenger emerging: FanDuel $904.7m handle (37.7% share, +14.5%) / $63.7m GGR (40.0%); DraftKings $840.1m (35.0%, -3.5%) / $56.0m (35.1%). Together 72.7% of handle and 75% of GGR. Fanatics is the only other book above 10% (handle $266.8m, +21.8%, 11.1% share) but held just 5.4%.
  • Long tail is sub-scale: Caesars $152.7m/5.2% hold; BetMGM $148.4m (-1%)/$10.0m; BetRivers $43.9m; theScore Bet (ex-ESPN Bet) $31.6m (-32.9%); Bally Bet $13.4m. Resorts World Bet, the ninth licensee, reports $0 handle. At a 51% tax, books below ~$150m monthly handle are unlikely to earn an economic return in-state.
  • Licensing is closed: nine mobile platform-provider licences were awarded via the 2021 RFA (10-year terms, $25m one-off fee each — statutory/RFA figure, not re-read this session). No mechanism exists for new entrants short of legislation or acquiring an existing licensee; the only recent 'entry' was Penn rebranding ESPN Bet to theScore Bet on its existing licence.
  • Land-based has been re-based by the downstate build-out: on 15 Dec 2025 NYSGC awarded all three downstate commercial licences — Resorts World NYC (Queens, 15-yr), Bally's Bronx (15-yr) and Metropolitan Park/Hard Rock (Queens, 20-yr) — at a $500m minimum licence fee each (RW paid an extra $100m) and ≥$500m capex. MGM's Empire City (Yonkers) missed out and remains a VLT racino.
  • RWNYC is already the state's dominant casino: it opened 240 live tables and ~2,500 Class III slots on 28 Apr 2026, added 1,400 slots in July (~3,900) and an 'International Room' high-limit area in October. September GGR $126.7m = 68.7% of the $184.3m statewide commercial total; the four upstate casinos were flat at $57.6m combined (Rivers $20.1m, +5.5%; del Lago $15.0m, +10.1%; RW Catskills $13.4m, -13.4%; Tioga $9.0m, -4.3%).
  • Casino tax is bid-your-own and RWNYC overbid: downstate rates were set by each winner's bid — RWNYC 56% slots / 30% tables; Bally's 30%/10%; Metropolitan Park 25%/10% (statutory floors 25%/10%). RWNYC is now disputing whether horseracing-support payments sit inside or on top of its 56% (critics put the all-in rate at ~72%); ten Queens legislators have asked the Commission to intervene.
  • VLT channel is shrinking by design: statewide video-gaming net win fell 37% to $124.8m in September as RWNYC's ~$70m+/month of VLT win moved into the commercial-casino line. Combined commercial + VLT win was ~$309m vs ~$256m a year earlier (+~21%, our estimate), i.e. table games and Class III slots are additive, not merely reclassified.
  • Lottery remains the fiscal anchor: traditional NY Lottery sales were ~$720m in September (sales, not GGR). Retail sports betting at the four upstate casinos was just $0.64m of GGR — mobile is 99.6% of the state's sports-betting GGR.
  • Responsible-gambling pressure is rising with the numbers: the NYSGC/OASAS 2025 report counted 7.2m unique mobile accounts, 791 under-age sign-up attempts and 2,545 HOPEline calls (+8.5% vs 2020), with mobile sports wagering the leading reason for calls since 2022. Comptroller DiNapoli (16 Sep 2026) called the $12m earmark small relative to $26bn of annual stakes and flagged prediction markets as a youth-access risk.
  • Rules of the road: 21+ for mobile sports and commercial casinos, 18+ for VLTs and lottery; wagers must be geolocated in-state and routed through servers at a licensed casino; bets on New York college teams and all high-school events are prohibited; the Commission vets prop-bet menus and leagues may petition to restrict markets (RPWB Law §1367(11), Rule 5329.13).
  • Prediction markets are the live threat to the tax base: CFTC-regulated sports event contracts (Kalshi, Polymarket, Crypto.com, plus DraftKings' and FanDuel's own exchange products) are taking volume outside the 51% regime; trade coverage credits them with NY's handle slowdown in H1-26. New York has so far relied on federal litigation rather than its own cease-and-desist route — direction of travel is the key 2027 swing factor.
  • iGaming remains unlegislated: no online casino or poker authorisation exists; successive Addabbo/Pretlow bills have stalled (2026 session outcome not re-verified this session). Legalisation would make NY the largest US iCasino market overnight, but the three new casino licensees and organised labour now have a strong interest in the sequencing.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: B2C mobile entry is effectively impossible (no licences) and uneconomic below top-three scale at 51%; the addressable opportunities are (i) platform, trading, risk and CRM supply to the eight active books fighting a hold problem, (ii) slot, ETG, systems and sports-kiosk supply to RWNYC's expansion (to 4,600+ slots/530 tables) and the Bally's/Met Park builds, and (iii) positioning for an iGaming RFA that would likely mirror the 2021 sports process.

3Deep-dive: Mobile sports betting

Record-September $2.40bn handle, but a 6.6% hold cut GGR 17.7% and the state's 51% take with it; FanDuel/DraftKings still 73% of stakes.

September handle and hold

Record-September $2,401.7m handle (+4.98% YoY, +38.7% on August) but a 6.64% hold cut GGR to $159.4m, -17.7%.

  • Weekly series ramped from $483m (w/e 6 Sep) to $611m (w/e 20 Sep) as NFL and college football returned.
  • Hold of 6.64% compares with 8.47% a year earlier and a 9.93% FY2025/26 average.
  • Week ending 13 September, the first full NFL week, returned just $17.0m on $594.6m (2.9%), a favourites-cover-and-parlays-cash fortnight.
  • No evidence of structural hold decline: October w/e 4–25 Oct tracking $2,471.8m handle, $244.0m GGR, 9.9% hold, potentially a record month.

Operator shares

FanDuel and DraftKings hold 73% of stakes; Fanatics buys share at a 5.4% hold with GGR -29.8%.

  • FanDuel $904.7m handle (+14.5% YoY, 37.7% share), $63.7m GGR (-18.0%, 40.0% share), 7.0% hold; roughly $32.5m September tax.
  • DraftKings $840.1m (-3.5%, 35.0%), $56.0m GGR (-16.0%, 35.1%), 6.7% hold; roughly $28.6m tax.
  • Fanatics $266.8m handle (+21.8%, 11.1%) but $14.4m GGR at 5.4% hold, weakest handle-to-revenue conversion among majors.
  • Caesars ($152.7m, 5.2% hold, $7.9m GGR) and BetMGM ($148.4m, -1%, 6.7%, $10.0m) are drifting.
  • BetRivers ($43.9m), theScore Bet ($31.6m, -32.9% vs ESPN Bet, GGR +1.8% at 8.9% hold), Bally Bet ($13.4m, 9.0% hold) sub-scale; Resorts World Bet dormant at $0.

Calendar-year trend

January–September 2026 handle $19.34bn (+3.7%) and GGR $1.75bn (-1.0%), decelerating from FY2025/26's +9.3%.

  • Deceleration partly comparables (2025 was first year with four full NFL quarters of Fanatics growth).
  • Partly leakage to CFTC-regulated sports event contracts, credited by trade press with absorbing a meaningful slice of New York volume since early 2026.
  • June 2026 Knicks championship produced a -$48.5m GGR week as futures were redeemed, illustrating the cash-basis convention.

Commercial implications

At 51% tax with no promo deduction, a book needs ~9–10% hold and top-three scale; a 200bp miss removes roughly a fifth of revenue.

  • Market closed to new B2C entrants: 2021 RFA fixed nine licences at $25m each for ten years.
  • Operator list unchanged since 2022 except brand swaps (PointsBet→Fanatics, ESPN Bet→theScore Bet).
  • Supplier angle: trading, pricing and parlay-risk technology for books below FanDuel/DraftKings, where hold deficits (Fanatics 5.4%, Caesars 5.2%) separate profit from loss.

4Deep-dive: Commercial casinos & the downstate build-out

RWNYC's conversion to a full casino tripled statewide commercial GGR to $184.3m; upstate is flat at $57.6m and the next two NYC casinos are years away.

Statewide commercial GGR

RWNYC's conversion tripled commercial casino GGR to $184.3m in September 2026, +219.8% on $57.6m.

  • Slots and electronic tables $129.3m (+217.8%), live tables $53.6m (+252.7%), poker $0.80m, retail sports betting $0.64m.
  • RWNYC became first downstate commercial casino on 28 April 2026 with 240 live tables and ~2,500 Class III slots; ~3,900 slots by July; 'International Room' opened October.
  • RWNYC $126.7m September GGR is 68.7% of state total, roughly $1.5bn annualised, larger than the four upstate casinos combined and among the highest-grossing US properties outside Nevada.

Upstate estate

Upstate is ex-growth at $57.6m, exactly the September 2025 statewide figure.

  • Rivers Schenectady $20.1m (+5.5%), del Lago $15.0m (+10.1%), Resorts World Catskills $13.4m (-13.4%), Tioga Downs $9.0m (-4.3%).
  • Tax of 10% on tables and 37–45% slots (Rivers 45%, RW Catskills 39%, del Lago and Tioga 37%), petitionable to 30% on hardship.
  • Retail sportsbooks irrelevant at $0.64m monthly GGR against $159.4m mobile.

Downstate licensing

Three downstate licences approved 15 December 2025 give RWNYC a NYC table and Class III monopoly for at least three to four years.

  • RWNYC 15-year licence; Bally's Bronx at Ferry Point 15-year with 3,500 slots/~250 tables/500 rooms planned; Metropolitan Park (Hard Rock/Steve Cohen) 20-year, opening targeted ~2030.
  • Each paid $500m minimum licence fee (RW volunteered an additional $100m) and committed ≥$500m capex, with monitored community-benefit agreements.
  • MGM's Empire City not selected and has no path to live tables; three Manhattan bids, Coney Island and the Avenir eliminated earlier.

Tax rates and dispute

Bid-your-own tax rates (RWNYC 56%/30%, Bally's 30%/10%, Met Park 25%/10%) leave RWNYC under acute margin pressure.

  • Statutory floors 25% slots and 10% tables; proceeds split between the MTA and education aid.
  • RWNYC argues its 56% was 'inclusive of racing support' payments to NYRA; Commission says they are additional, which critics say takes all-in slot rate to ~72%.
  • Ten Queens legislators asked chair Brian O'Dwyer to resolve in RWNYC's favour; RWNYC's application sought a most-favoured-rate clause.
  • Supplier read-through: large pipeline (RWNYC to 4,600 slots/530 tables, 6,000/800 longer-term; Bally's and Met Park from 2029–30) favouring participation and lease models over capex.

5Deep-dive: VLT racinos, lottery & retail

VLT net win -37% to $124.8m as RWNYC migrates to the casino line; the Lottery (~$720m September sales) remains the state's largest gaming receipt.

VLT net win

VLT net win fell 37% to $124.8m as RWNYC's Aqueduct floor migrated to the commercial casino line.

  • Implies roughly $198m a year earlier; Aqueduct was historically the largest VLT facility with ~$280m annual education contribution.
  • Combined commercial-casino GGR plus VLT win ~$309m for September 2026 versus ~$256m, an estimated +21%, our derivation not a regulator figure.
  • Suggests live tables and Class III product generate incremental NYC spend rather than relabelling it.

Remaining VLT estate

Empire City's loss in the downstate race leaves MGM with the clearest cannibalisation risk in the state.

  • Estate: Empire City (~4,500–5,200 terminals), Jake's 58 (authorised 1,000 to 2,000 terminals), RW Hudson Valley, Saratoga, Finger Lakes, Batavia Downs, Hamburg, Vernon Downs, Nassau OTB allocation at Aqueduct.
  • VLTs are part of the Lottery: central system in Schenectady determines outcomes, net proceeds to Aid to Education, operators paid tiered agent commission, marketing allowance and capital award.
  • Minimum age 18 against 21 for commercial casinos and mobile sports.
  • Empire City is 15 miles from RWNYC's live tables; a 2027 push for legislative relief (lower education retention, ETG expansion or fourth downstate licence) is plausible.

Lottery and legacy verticals

Lottery sales of ~$720m in September remain the state's largest gaming receipt, with mobile sports second.

  • SFY2016–SFY2026 total state gaming revenue grew 33% to more than $5.1bn, but excluding mobile sports the legacy take fell 0.8%.
  • Decade of stagnation explains Albany's dependence on the 51% mobile tax and wariness of prediction markets and iGaming cannibalisation.
  • Upstate retail sportsbooks immaterial ($0.64m GGR, 0.4% of sports GGR); tribal casinos (Seneca, Oneida, Akwesasne) do not publish win.
  • Niagara County's campaign to protect its share of Seneca compact payments shows tribal revenue-sharing is a live political file.

6Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime

Closed, expensive and transparent: nine mobile licences at $25m and 51% of GGR; three downstate casino licences at $500m+ with bid-your-own tax rates from 25% to 56%.

Regulator and mobile framework

NYSGC publishes the most granular disclosure of any US state; nine ten-year mobile licences at $25m were scored heavily on tax rate.

  • Mobile operates under Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law §1367 and Tax Law §1367 as amended in the FY2022 budget; geofenced, servers at licensed commercial casinos.
  • Commission controls the wagering menu, bars New York college teams and high-school events, vets props and lets leagues petition under Rule 5329.13.
  • The $25m fee is per the RFA/statute, not re-read this session; bidders collectively offered 51%.

Mobile tax economics

At 51% of cash-basis GGR with no promo deduction, a point of hold is worth roughly $24m a month, $12m to the state.

  • Operators retain 49%, plus integrity/league data costs and the federal 0.25% handle excise.
  • Proceeds to education, with $5m a year for youth sports and from FY2026/27 $12m (doubled from $6m) for problem gambling via OASAS.
  • September tax $81.3m versus $98.8m a year earlier; FY2025/26 delivered $1,325.1m, the state's second-largest gaming revenue source per the Comptroller.

Land-based licensing

RWNYC's 56%/30% bid is the highest casino tax rate in the country and the bid-your-own-rate model shifts regulatory risk onto operators.

  • Upstate under the 2013 Act: 10% tables; 37–45% slots, petitionable to 30%.
  • Downstate terms: $500m minimum fee (RWNYC paid $600m), ≥$500m capex, 15-year (RWNYC, Bally's) or 20-year (Metropolitan Park) terms, floors of 25% slots/10% tables, annual licence fees, community-benefit agreements.
  • Live dispute over whether NYRA racing-support payments sit inside RWNYC's rate is the clearest warning.
  • Video lottery agents hold Lottery agent licences, so Empire City and Jake's 58 cannot offer live tables.

iGaming and fantasy

Online casino and poker are not authorised; any authorisation would be tethered to existing licence holders.

  • Addabbo and Pretlow bills reintroduced each session since 2023 (terms have included 30.5% GGR tax and $2m licences); none reached a floor vote; 2026 outcome not verified.
  • Fantasy sports licensed separately under Article 14 at 15% GGR tax.
  • New B2C brands must enter via partnership or acquisition; for B2B, supplier registration with the Commission is the gating item.

7Deep-dive: Responsible gambling, advertising, payments & enforcement direction

7.2m accounts, HOPEline calls +8.5% since 2020 and a Comptroller spotlight on prediction markets: the political direction is tighter, not looser.

Problem gambling

7.2 million mobile accounts in 2025 and HOPEline calls of 2,545 (+8.5% versus 2020) set a tighter political tone.

  • 7.2m accounts is roughly 36% of the 21+ population, our estimate; 791 attempted under-age openings.
  • Mobile sports wagering the leading reason for HOPEline calls since 2022; Comptroller welcomed doubling of earmark to $12m.

Payments and enforcement

Payments run on conventional US rails and enforcement is modest and administrative, with $19,655 of September fines typical.

  • ACH/online banking, debit, PayPal, prepaid Play+ and cash-at-retail; credit-card funding permitted by statute but increasingly restricted.
  • Proposed credit-card deposit ban and bonus advertising curbs not enacted; status not verified this session, treat as [unverified].
  • Mandatory geolocation and KYC at registration caught the 791 under-age attempts; Commission publishes notices of violation by operator.

Prediction markets

Whether the Commission or Attorney General moves against prediction markets in 2027 is the single most important regulatory swing factor.

  • Kalshi, Polymarket and Crypto.com list sports event contracts accessible from New York without state licence, 51% tax, 21 age floor or college-team prohibition.
  • DraftKings and FanDuel have launched their own exchange products to defend share.
  • Trade coverage attributes H1-2026 handle slowdown and September's 'record despite prediction markets' framing to this leakage; Comptroller flagged youth access.
  • Unlike New Jersey, Maryland, Nevada and Ohio, New York has issued no cease-and-desist orders, relying on federal litigation.

Operator direction

More disclosure and higher compliance cost argue for long-tail consolidation and suppliers leading with KYC, affordability and limit-setting tooling.

  • Inspector general notes mobile wagering now generates the majority of gaming complaints.
  • Widening gap between licensed-channel obligations and the untaxed prediction-market channel; scale players absorb it, sub-scale books cannot.
  • Consolidation candidates: Bally Bet, theScore Bet, BetRivers, dormant Resorts World Bet.
  • Tooling needed irrespective of how the iGaming and prediction-market debates resolve.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Scale: New York is the largest US sports-betting market by handle ($26.2bn FY2025/26, $2.40bn in September alone) with volume still growing (+6.9% FY YTD) and October tracking a record on a normalised ~9.9% hold — the demand base is intact. structural
  • Downstate casino build-out is real and front-loaded: RWNYC is already printing $126.7m/month of GGR on a partial floor (to 4,600+ slots/530 tables), with Bally's Bronx and Metropolitan Park to follow — a multi-year hardware, systems, ETG and sports-kiosk procurement pipeline for suppliers. structural
  • Hold is the lever, and it is fixable: the market's September 6.6% hold versus a 9.9% full-year average, and sub-5.5% holds at Fanatics and Caesars, create demand for pricing, parlay-risk and trading technology among the books that cannot out-promote FanDuel/DraftKings. mixed
  • Share can still move: Fanatics grew handle 21.8% to an 11.1% share and theScore Bet/Bally Bet grew GGR — the duopoly's 73% is not a ceiling, and consolidation of the sub-scale tail (Resorts World Bet dormant; Bally Bet at $13m handle) is a licence-acquisition route into an otherwise closed market. structural
  • iGaming optionality: no state would add more iCasino GGR on authorisation than New York; any bill will tether licences to existing casino/mobile holders, so partnerships struck now with RWNYC, Bally's, Hard Rock, MGM Empire City or the upstate four are the call option. early
  • Best-in-class data transparency — weekly handle and GGR by operator and facility — lets a supplier benchmark client performance and price risk with a precision unavailable in most jurisdictions. structural
  • The Knicks-driven June GGR hole (-$48.5m week) and the September hold miss are timing effects under cash-basis accounting, not evidence of structural demand loss; FY2025/26 GGR grew 21% on a 9.9% hold. one-off

▼ Lowlights

  • 51% of GGR with no promotional deduction is the most punitive competitive-state tax in the US; September shows a 200bp hold miss turns into a 17.7% revenue decline with the state's share fixed. Only top-three scale is economic — FanDuel and DraftKings alone paid ~$61m of September's $81m tax. structural
  • The mobile market is closed: nine licences from the 2021 RFA, no new-entrant mechanism, and a $25m fee that has already been sunk by incumbents. Entry is only via acquisition of a dormant or sub-scale licensee. structural
  • Prediction markets are eroding the licensed channel untaxed and unsupervised by Albany; New York has not followed other states with cease-and-desist orders, leaving handle and the state's $1.3bn tax base exposed until federal courts or the Commission act. watch
  • Bid-your-own tax rates have produced an untenable 56%/30% at RWNYC and a dispute over whether racing-support payments push the effective rate to ~72% — a template for how aggressively New York extracts rent and a warning for anyone pricing a future iGaming or fourth-casino bid. governance
  • Political and responsible-gambling pressure is intensifying: 7.2m accounts, HOPEline calls +8.5%, 791 under-age attempts, the Comptroller's public warning and a doubled problem-gambling earmark point to tighter advertising, deposit (credit-card) and affordability rules in the 2027 session. watch
  • Legacy verticals are ex-growth: the four upstate casinos are flat at $57.6m, VLT net win is down 37% on reclassification and the OSC shows non-mobile gaming revenue fell 0.8% over a decade — Empire City (MGM) now faces direct cannibalisation from RWNYC's tables with no licence remedy. structural
  • iGaming has stalled for four consecutive sessions and the newly licensed casinos, their lenders and labour now have a $1.5bn+ reason to sequence online casino behind bricks-and-mortar openings in 2029–30; the 2026 session outcome could not be verified here. mixed

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The guidance bridge here is fiscal rather than corporate. The state's FY2025/26 base was $26.2bn mobile handle, $2.60bn GGR (9.9% hold) and $1.33bn tax. Six months into FY2026/27 handle is +6.9% but GGR is -5.8% on an 8.8% hold, so the Division of the Budget's mobile-tax line is tracking below plan despite record volume; the swing is entirely hold. Land-based moves the other way: RWNYC's licence has added roughly $125m a month of commercial-casino GGR (taxed at 56%/30%) that did not exist in the FY2025/26 base, more than offsetting the ~$70m/month of VLT win it removed. Net, we expect total state gaming receipts to rise in FY2026/27 even if mobile tax is flat — which is exactly the mix shift Albany designed.

Independent view. Our independent view is that New York stays a volume-growth, margin-volatile market for the next 12–24 months: mobile handle compounds mid-single digits as Fanatics and the exchanges contest the duopoly, while GGR and the 51% tax line oscillate with hold. The structural growth engine shifts to land-based — RWNYC should exit 2027 at or above a $1.8bn GGR run-rate as its floor expands, making it the single most important procurement account in the US northeast, while Bally's and Metropolitan Park move from fees to construction. On rules, direction of travel is tighter: we expect the 2027 session (Jan–Jun) to bring an advertising/bonus-messaging tightening and a renewed credit-card-deposit ban attempt, and a 50/50 chance that the Commission or Attorney General moves formally against sports event contracts if federal litigation does not resolve first. We assign less than 30% probability to iGaming passing in 2027; the three downstate licensees now have the economic incentive and the lobbying budget to delay it until their properties open, and the Comptroller's framing of problem gambling makes a new online vertical politically expensive. Leverage, in a market sense, is rising: the state's reliance on mobile tax (second-largest gaming receipt, $1.3bn) and on RWNYC's 56% slot rate means Albany will defend the tax base — against prediction markets and against RWNYC's rate-relief request — rather than cut rates. For B2Tech the actionable position is B2B: trading/risk and CRM supply to the hold-challenged books, floor and systems supply to the three downstate casinos, and a standing partnership option on an eventual iGaming RFA.

What to watch next quarter

  • RWNYC racing-support/tax dispute — Commission ruling or legislative fix; any concession would reset the perceived cost of the downstate licences and the template for future bids.
  • Prediction markets — federal appellate outcomes on CFTC pre-emption and whether NYSGC/the Attorney General issue cease-and-desist orders; monthly handle growth below ~5% with hold normal would signal leakage is structural.
  • Hold normalisation — October 2026 weekly data (9.9% on $2.47bn for w/e 4–25 Oct) versus the 8.8% FY YTD; a full-year hold below 9.3% leaves mobile tax short of the FY2025/26 base.
  • 2027 legislative session (Jan–Jun) and the Governor's FY2028 Executive Budget — iGaming (Addabbo/Pretlow), credit-card deposit ban, advertising restrictions, VLT age to 21, Empire City relief.
  • Downstate construction milestones — Bally's Bronx and Metropolitan Park financing, fee payments and ground-breaking; RWNYC phase-two (hotel/expanded floor) and slot count toward 4,600+.
  • Consolidation of the mobile tail — dormant Resorts World Bet licence, Bally Bet and theScore Bet sub-scale; a licence transfer would be the first real new-entrant path since 2022.
  • Tribal compacts — Seneca compact renewal terms and the Niagara County revenue-share dispute; any state concession would reopen exclusivity-zone questions for western New York.
  • NYSGC/OASAS 2026 problem-gambling report (due early 2027) — account counts, under-age attempts and HOPEline volumes will frame the advertising and affordability debate.
