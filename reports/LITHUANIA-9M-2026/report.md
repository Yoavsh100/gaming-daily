# Lithuania () — 9M 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: EUR (tables in € millions; ~USD at an assumed EUR/USD 1.16 — see confidence note) · Results released 23 Nov 2026**

> Lithuania's regulated market grew 14% to €229m (~$265.6m) GGR in 9M 2026, but it is now a remote-slots market: online is 78% of GGR and remote Category A slots alone are 57%, while retail fell 6.5% and online betting slipped 2%. The policy direction is unambiguously restrictive — a progressive 22/29/36% GGR tax from 1 July 2027 (Government-approved 27 Nov 2026), a full advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028 and a mandatory cross-operator player card plus cashless venues from 2029 are all in the pipeline. For an entrant the arithmetic is a fast-growing, 12-licensee, slots-led market whose marginal tax on scale is about to rise from 22% to 36% just as acquisition channels close.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market size: €229m (~$265.6m) GGR in 9M 2026, +14% YoY.** The Gambling Supervisory Authority (Lošimų priežiūros tarnyba, LPT, under the Ministry of Finance) published Jan–Sep 2026 data on 23 Nov 2026. GGR is defined as stakes minus winnings paid, excludes lotteries, and compares with ~€200.5m (~$232.6m) in 9M 2025. H1 2026 was €153.6m (~$178.2m, +16.8%), so Q3 2026 was ~€75.4m (~$87.5m), only ~+9% YoY (derived) — growth is decelerating.
- **Online is 78% of the market and rising.** Remote GGR was €179.0m (~$207.6m), +21.8% from €146.97m (~$170.5m); its share rose from 73% (9M 2025) and 74% (FY2025). Remote overtook land-based in 2020 per LPT and has widened the gap every period since. Q3 2026 remote GGR was ~€59.1m (~$68.6m), +16% YoY (derived).
- **Slots are the market: remote Category A machines = 57% of all GGR.** Remote Cat A (unlimited-stake) slots generated €131.6m (~$152.7m), +~30% YoY; remote table games €17.7m (~$20.5m), +24%. These two products delivered essentially all market growth; remote Cat B machines collapsed −67% to €0.61m (~$0.7m) and online bingo was €284.
- **Betting is stalling, even in a World Cup year.** Remote betting GGR fell 2% to €29.1m (~$33.8m) (H1 was +0.9% at €21.5m), implying Q3 2026 of ~€7.6m (~$8.8m), roughly −10% YoY (derived); land-based betting was €4.9m (~$5.7m), down (H1 −20.6%). Sportsbook is only ~15% of gambling GGR — Lithuania is a casino-led market.
- **Retail is in structural decline: €50.0m (~$58.0m), −6.5%.** Land-based Cat B halls €22.2m (~$25.8m), casino Cat A machines €12.1m (~$14.0m, slightly up), table games €10.8m (~$12.5m, down), betting shops €4.9m. Installed machines fell to 3,736 at 30 Sep 2026 from 3,914 (−4.5%); eight land-based licensees, unchanged. H1 land-based GGR was €33.7m (~$39.1m, −5%).
- **Lottery growing in line with gambling.** Ticket sales (turnover base) €131.5m (~$152.5m), +9%; prizes €74.2m (~$86.1m), +9.3% (56% payout); lottery operators' gross revenue (sales − prizes) €57.3m (~$66.5m), ~+9% (derived). FY2025 lottery sales were ~€163m (~$189.1m) implied by the €29.3m (~$34.0m) lottery tax paid at 18%.
- **Tax take €74.19m (~$86.1m), +15%.** Gambling operators paid ~€50m (~$58.0m) (22% of GGR) and lottery operators €24.2m (~$28.1m) (18% of ticket sales) in Jan–Sep 2026; FY2025 take was €86.9m (~$100.8m) (gambling €57.6m / lottery €29.3m). Quarterly tax periods, payable by the 15th of the following month.
- **Current tax regime (Law on Lottery and Gambling Tax).** All gambling — slots, table games, bingo, totalizator, betting and remote — is taxed at 22% of GGR since 1 Jan 2025 (20% from Jul 2022; before that 13% remote, 18% betting/bingo and fixed per-device fees of €130–€2,300/month for slots and tables). Lotteries pay 18% of the nominal value of distributed tickets (turnover base) since 1 Jan 2023. Gambling is VAT-exempt.
- **Progressive GGR tax approved by Government on 27 Nov 2026, effective 1 Jul 2027 if passed.** MoF draft: 22% on annual GGR up to €3m (~$3.5m), 29% on €3–5m, 36% above €5m (~$5.8m); lottery 18%→20%. MoF (Finance Minister Taurimas Valys) expects +€9.9m (~$11.5m) in 2027 and +€33.2m (~$38.5m) in 2028; the coalition Democrats who initiated it cite ~€36m (~$41.8m). Our arithmetic: a €50m-GGR operator moves from 22% to ~35% effective; a €20m operator to ~33%.
- **Licensing: six licence classes, LPT-issued, plus venue/platform permits.** Gaming Law No. IX-325 provides licences for (i) table games & Cat A machines (casinos), (ii) Cat B machines (max stake/prize limited), (iii) bingo, (iv) totalizator, (v) betting, (vi) remote gambling. Under the 2016 remote framework a remote licence is reserved for Lithuanian-registered companies that also hold a land-based licence (land-based anchoring) — a material entry barrier [not re-verified here]. Licence fees and minimum capital thresholds: n/a [unverified].
- **Operator landscape: 12 licensed gambling companies (LPT, 30 Jun 2026), 8 of them land-based.** LPT does not publish operator shares. Brands generally recognised as leaders are Olympic Casino Group Baltija (OlyBet), TopSport, Betsafe (Betsson), Optibet (Entain/Enlabs), 7bet, Uniclub Casino, Tonybet and Casino Tornado; lotteries are led by Olifėja (Teleloto/Eurojackpot) with Žalgirio loto and Perlas — names from analyst knowledge, not verified in this research.
- **Responsible-gambling architecture already in force.** From 1 Nov 2025: a mandatory responsible-gambling control system (player-set limits), fines re-based as a percentage of operator revenue and differentiated by breach, and a unified minimum-age threshold for land-based venues (LPT: 'suvienodinta lošėjų amžiaus riba'; exact threshold for remote play [unverified]). From 1 May 2026 every remote platform must be connected to LPT's LAKIS control system and upload play data at least every 30 days.
- **Self-exclusion is mass-market: 99,601 cumulative requests at 30 Sep 2026** (98,049 at 31 Aug — c.1,550 new per month) in a country of ~2.9m people. The register is cross-operator and covers remote play; it is the template for the proposed player card.
- **Player card, cashless venues and affordability checks are before the Seimas (since 10 Nov 2026).** The Government bill mandates a physical/digital card for every player online and retail from 1 Jan 2029, tracking stakes, winnings and limits across all operators (today limits only bind at single-operator level); removes cash from gambling venues; adds income/affordability checks for high spenders; and expands LPT powers while dropping some 'excessive' operator requirements from May 2027. Industry (ALVA) is lobbying against; the committee stage is under way.
- **Advertising: tightened from summer 2025, total ban from 1 Jan 2028.** The Nov 2024 Gaming Law amendments (signed 22 Nov 2024) restricted gambling advertising from mid-2025 and legislate a complete ban from 2028; bonuses/incentives are already heavily restricted [exact scope not re-verified]. Brand-building windows close in 13 months — a structural first-mover advantage for incumbents.
- **Enforcement is active and data-driven.** LPT blocks unlicensed domains and in Sept/Oct 2026 added Polymarket — the first prediction market listed as illegal betting; ALVA estimated (2025) offshore operators take ≥€50m (~$58.0m) a year from Lithuanian players, and local press (15min, Nov 2026) puts lost tax in the millions. LPT forced one remote operator to restate stakes/winnings reporting from session-based to per-bet (GGR unchanged), and a multi-million-euro LPT fine on Olympic Casino, reduced by a court, is under further appeal (LRT, 27 Nov 2026).
- **Payments: euro bank/card rails with mandatory ID; cash on its way out.** Remote accounts require identity verification and euro deposits via bank transfer/cards (crypto not permitted; method shares n/a); retail still takes cash today but the pending bill removes it. There is no mobile-money layer — the payments risk here is regulatory (card/affordability) rather than infrastructural.
- **Political direction: fiscal and social tightening from a left-leaning coalition.** In July 2025 the MoF told the Seimas a 22–37% progressive tax was premature; sixteen months later a near-identical structure is Government policy, bundled into the 2027 budget. Expect the Seimas to pass both the tax and the player-card package in some form by H1 2027.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** The top line is attractive (+14% market, +22% online, slots-led, 22% GGR tax, no VAT, euro, EU), but (a) the marginal tax on scale goes to 36% from Jul-27, (b) acquisition channels shut in Jan-28, (c) cross-operator limits and affordability checks from 2029 target exactly the high-value slots play that is 57% of GGR, and (d) land-based anchoring plus a 12-licence structure make greenfield entry slow. The realistic route is acquiring an existing licensee; value creation will come from product, CRM and compliance efficiency rather than marketing-led share capture.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online gambling (remote casino & betting)

*€179.0m (~$207.6m) GGR, +21.8%: a remote-slots market where betting is flat-to-down and Cat A slots now carry 57% of the whole industry.*

### Remote GGR growth

**Remote GGR reached €178.99m (~$207.6m) for Jan–Sep 2026, +21.8%, 78% of all gambling GGR, but Q3 growth slowed to +16%.**

- Prior-year Jan–Sep remote GGR was €146.97m (~$170.5m).
- H1 print was €119.91m (~$139.1m, +24.8%), so Q3 contributed ~€59.1m (~$68.6m), +16% YoY.
- Q3 is a visible step down from the +25% H1 pace.
- First evidence that the 1 Nov 2025 limit regime and mid-2025 advertising restrictions are biting on new-player flow.

### Casino product mix

**Remote Category A slots produced €131.6m (~$152.7m), roughly +30% YoY, 74% of remote GGR and 57% of total market GGR.**

- Cat A slots are the unlimited-stake class; Cat B has capped stakes and prizes.
- H1 slots were €85.7m (~$99.4m, +~33%), so Q3 slots were ~€45.9m (~$53.2m), +~25%.
- Remote table games (live casino) added €17.7m (~$20.5m), +24%, after €12.3m (~$14.3m, +33.9%) in H1.
- Growth engine is casino content, RTP management and VIP intensity — precisely the behaviours the pending player card and affordability checks are designed to constrain.
- Product mix is extreme by European standards.

### Remote betting

**Remote betting is the weak link: GGR €29.1m (~$33.8m), −2% YoY, with derived Q3 ~€7.6m (~$8.8m) about 10% below Q3 2025.**

- H1 betting was €21.50m (~$24.9m, +0.9%); Q3 2025 was ~€8.4m (~$9.7m).
- Decline came despite the FIFA World Cup falling in June–July 2026.
- LPT does not publish stakes by product, so margin cannot be separated from volume.
- Sportsbook is now ~16% of remote GGR; a betting-led entrant would be fishing in the shrinking part of the pond.
- Remote Cat B machines (−67% to €0.61m, ~$0.7m) and online bingo (€284) are immaterial.

### Market structure

**Market is tight with 12 licensed gambling companies in total (LPT, 30 Jun 2026), eight also running land-based venues.**

- LPT does not disclose operator-level GGR or shares.
- One remote operator was required to restate stakes and winnings from a session-based to a per-bet basis; GGR was unaffected.
- The episode signals LPT now audits statistical returns line by line ahead of LAKIS-fed supervision.

### Commercial implication

**Online Lithuania is a high-growth but concentrated casino market where growth is slowing, marketing is being switched off and tax on scale is stepping up.**

- Total advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028.
- 36% marginal tax above €5m GGR from 1 Jul 2027.
- Returns will accrue to operators with existing databases and efficient CRM rather than to new brands.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Land-based (casinos, Cat B halls, betting shops)

*€50.0m (~$58.0m) GGR, −6.5%: machines, venues and betting shops are all shrinking, and the cashless proposal would accelerate the decline.*

### Retail GGR decline

**Land-based GGR fell to €50.0m (~$58.0m) in Jan–Sep 2026, −6.5%, from €53.5m (~$62.1m), with the decline accelerating in Q3.**

- LPT notes lower stakes, lower prizes and lower revenue across the segment.
- After a −5% H1 (€33.7m, ~$39.1m), derived Q3 of ~€16.3m (~$18.9m) is ~10% below Q3 2025.
- Land-based is now 22% of gambling GGR, from 27% a year ago and roughly 50% before 2020.

### Product and estate

**Category B machines remain the largest retail line at €22.2m (~$25.8m) but eased; only casino Cat A machines grew, to €12.1m (~$14.0m).**

- Table games fell to €10.8m (~$12.5m); H1 was flat at €7.67m, so Q3 tables were weak.
- Betting shops fell to €4.9m (~$5.7m) after a −20.6% H1 (€3.85m, ~$4.5m).
- Installed base fell to 3,736 machines at 30 Sep 2026 from 3,914 (−4.5%), having been 3,819 at mid-year.
- Eight companies hold land-based licences, unchanged.

### Regulatory drivers

**Regulation is the main driver of decline, with the pending cashless and player card measures set to accelerate it.**

- Since 1 Nov 2025 slot halls, bingo halls and betting/totalizator shops apply the unified minimum entry age previously reserved for casinos.
- The same amendment package introduced revenue-based fines.
- Pending Government bill would remove cash from all gambling venues and require a player card at the door from 2029.
- Retail lobby ALVA argues these measures will push play to the unlicensed market rather than reduce it.

### Licensing gateway

**Land-based matters mainly as a licensing function: the eight retail licensees are the gateway to the online market.**

- Remote licences have historically been tied to holding a land-based licence.
- Consolidation of a shrinking retail footprint creates acquirable licensed platforms.
- Any buyer should underwrite retail GGR at a −5% to −10% annual run-rate and assume the cash ban lands in 2027–28.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Lottery

*Ticket sales €131.5m (~$152.5m), +9%; a turnover-taxed (18%, proposed 20%) segment run by a handful of incumbents and closed to gambling licensees.*

### Sales and regime

**Lottery ticket sales were €131.5m (~$152.5m) in Jan–Sep 2026, +9% YoY, under a separate turnover-taxed regime of 18%, rising to 20%.**

- Regulated under the Law on Lotteries; taxed at 18% of nominal ticket value, rising to 20% from 1 July 2027 under the Government draft approved 27 Nov 2026.
- Prizes were €74.2m (~$86.1m), +9.3%; operators' gross lottery revenue €57.3m (~$66.5m).
- H1 sales were €87.7m (~$101.7m, +8.6%), so Q3 sales of ~€43.8m (~$50.8m) grew ~10%.

### Payout and tax

**Payout ratio is stable at ~56% of sales; operators paid €24.2m (~$28.1m) tax in the period, consistent with the 18% rate on a quarterly lag.**

- H1 tax was €15.76m.
- FY2025 sales were ~€163m (~$189.1m), implied by €29.3m of tax.
- Lottery is roughly 37% of the combined gambling-plus-lottery gross revenue pool of ~€330m.

### Effective burden

**The effective burden is heavy: 18% of sales on a 56% payout equals ~41% of gross lottery revenue, going to ~45% at 20%.**

- This is why the Democrats' €36m (~$41.8m) headline is weighted toward gambling.
- Lottery increment is only ~€3–3.5m a year on current volumes.

### Incumbents and entry

**The segment is dominated by Olifėja with Žalgirio loto and Perlas, and offers no realistic entry route for a gambling operator.**

- Olifėja runs Teleloto, Eurojackpot and Vikinglotto; names from analyst knowledge, LPT publishes no shares.
- Lottery licensing is a distinct track; instant online lotteries ('didžioji momentinė loterija') are registered product-by-product with LPT.
- Relevance to B2Tech is limited to competition for the same wallet and the political cover a lottery tax rise gives for the gambling tax rise.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*Flat 22% GGR tax, no VAT and quarterly settlement today; a 22/29/36% progressive scale from 1 Jul 2027 would lift the effective rate on any scaled operator to ~33–35%.*

### Legal framework

**Six licensable activities under Gaming Law No. IX-325, supervised by LPT under the Ministry of Finance, with remote licences historically reserved for land-based licensees.**

- Framework comprises the Gaming Law, the Law on Lotteries and the Law on Lottery and Gambling Tax.
- Activities: table games and Category A machines (casinos), Category B machines, bingo, totalizator, betting and remote gambling; each needs a licence plus operating permit per venue or platform.
- Remote gambling licensable since 2016; reserved for Lithuanian-registered companies already holding a land-based licence, hence only a dozen participants [2016-era rule; not re-verified].
- Licence fees, minimum share-capital thresholds and licence duration: n/a [unverified].

### Current tax

**Since 1 Jan 2025 all gambling pays a flat 22% of GGR, VAT-exempt, settled quarterly; 9M 2026 state receipts were €74.19m (~$86.1m), +15%.**

- Rate was 20% from 1 Jul 2022–2024; pre-2022 structure was 13% remote, 18% betting/bingo/totalizator plus fixed per-device fees of €130–€2,300 per month.
- Lotteries pay 18% of ticket sales.
- Tax period is the calendar quarter, payable by the 15th of the following month.
- ~€50m (~$58.0m) came from gambling operators; the arithmetic (22% × €229m = €50.4m) ties out.

### Progressive scale

**The draft 22/29/36% progressive scale from 1 July 2027 would lift the effective rate on any scaled operator to ~33–35%.**

- MoF draft registered ~11 Nov 2026, approved by Government 27 Nov 2026: 22% on first €3m (~$3.5m) GGR, 29% on €3–5m, 36% above €5m (~$5.8m); lottery to 20%.
- MoF guides to +€9.9m (~$11.5m) in 2027 (half-year) and +€33.2m (~$38.5m) in 2028; the Democrats (Tomilinas) cite ~€36m (~$41.8m).
- On FY2025 gambling GGR of €274.1m (~$318.0m) the 2028 figure implies blended rate rising from 22% to roughly 32–33%.
- Worked examples: €5m GGR → 24.8% effective; €20m → 33.2%; €50m → 34.9%; €100m → 35.4% (our calculations).

### Draft scrutiny

**Thresholds appear per legal entity rather than per group, and the reform coincides with the 2028 ad ban and 2029 player card.**

- Per-entity thresholds invite structuring and are likely to be closed by anti-fragmentation language.
- 2027–29 modelled GGR should carry both a higher tax rate and a lower top line.
- ALVA and others (per 15min) warn the reform could reduce receipts via leakage to unlicensed sites.
- MoF rejected an almost identical 22–37% proposal in July 2025 as premature before adopting it in November 2026 — the political line has moved, not the economics.

### Fines and compliance

**Fines were re-based in November 2025 to percentages of operator revenue, and compliance cost is now closer to Nordic than Baltic norms.**

- Fines differentiated by breach type (LPT director Virginijus Daukšys).

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Responsible gambling, advertising, payments & enforcement

*Limits, LAKIS and revenue-based fines are live; the ad ban (2028), player card and cashless venues (2029) are legislated or in the Seimas — Lithuania is converging on the Nordic control model.*

### Control trajectory

**The November 2024 amendment package set a path to a complete gambling advertising ban from 1 January 2028 and LAKIS-based central supervision.**

- Signed by the President on 22 Nov 2024; tightened advertising rules from summer 2025; unified minimum age for all land-based venues from 1 Nov 2025; protections 'against ruinous play'.
- From 1 Nov 2025 operators must run a responsible-gambling control system in which players set their own limits.
- From 1 May 2026 every remote platform must connect to LAKIS and transmit play data at least every 30 days.
- Bonuses and other inducements are already treated as restricted promotion [exact scope not re-verified].

### Self-exclusion

**Self-exclusion is a mass phenomenon: 99,601 cumulative requests at 30 Sep 2026, implying c.1,550 new registrations per month against ~2.9m population.**

- Up from 98,049 a month earlier.
- The register binds all licensees, online and retail, and is proof of concept for the Government's next step.

### Player card bill

**The Government bill tabled 10 Nov 2026 would mandate a player card for every player from 1 January 2029; our base case is passage in 2027.**

- First proposed by MoF in April 2026; card records stakes, winnings and self-set limits across all operators, closing the cross-operator limit gap.
- Adds affordability/income checks for high spenders, removes cash from venues and expands LPT powers; supervisory and 'excessive-requirement' changes effective from May 2027.
- LPT supports the card, citing a customer who attempted to set a €100m monthly loss limit; ALVA and the land-based lobby oppose it.
- Budget and Finance Committee is weighing submissions; base case is 2029 start date intact.

### Payments

**Payments are euro-denominated and bank-centric; the real change is card-based identification and loss of cash in retail.**

- Remote accounts require verified identity and funding via bank transfer or cards; crypto not accepted; method shares not disclosed.
- LAKIS now ties account activity to the regulator.
- Cash removal will push remaining slot-hall and betting-shop customers either online or offshore.

### Enforcement

**Enforcement has broadened to novel products, with Polymarket blocked in September/October 2026 as the first prediction market treated as unlicensed betting.**

- Unlicensed market size is contested: ALVA estimated in 2025 offshore sites take at least €50m (~$58.0m) a year; 15min (Nov 2026) puts foregone tax in the millions.
- LPT forced one remote operator to move from session-based to per-bet reporting and is litigating a multi-million-euro fine against Olympic Casino.
- Compliance bar is high but predictable; commercial risk is that the 2028 ad ban and 2029 card lock in incumbents' customer bases as channelisation pressure from the tax rise builds.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Online GGR +21.8% to €179.0m (~$207.6m) with remote Cat A slots +~30% to €131.6m (~$152.7m): a regulated, euro-denominated EU casino market still compounding at double digits, with online penetration (78%) already above every Western European benchmark. _(structural)_
- Simple fiscal mechanics today — 22% of GGR, no VAT, quarterly settlement, no per-device fees since 2022 — and a tax take that reconciles cleanly to reported GGR (22% × €229m ≈ €50m paid), i.e. no hidden levies or turnover taxes on gambling. _(structural)_
- Retail consolidation (machines −4.5% to 3,736; land-based GGR −6.5%; eight licensees) is creating acquirable licensed platforms — and because remote licences have been tied to land-based licences, buying a shrinking retail estate is the practical key to the online market. _(structural)_
- LAKIS data feeds (from May 2026) and the 2029 player card will reward operators with strong compliance tech, CRM and limit-management products; a B2B/tech-led operator can monetise this as a differentiator against legacy incumbents rather than treat it purely as cost. _(early)_
- Betting is only ~16% of remote GGR and shrinking (−2% 9M; ~−10% Q3E) — low-hanging product share for a superior sportsbook, provided acquisition can be done through owned channels before the 2028 ad ban. _(mixed)_
- Active channelisation enforcement (domain blocking, Polymarket listed as illegal betting, revenue-based fines, line-item audit of operator returns) protects the licensed pool from offshore leakage — valuable if the tax rise does not overshoot. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Progressive GGR tax from 1 Jul 2027 — 22% ≤€3m, 29% €3–5m, 36% >€5m — lifts the effective rate on a €20–50m operator to ~33–35% from 22%; Government-approved 27 Nov 2026 and bundled into the 2027 budget, so passage probability is high. MoF targets +€33.2m (~$38.5m) in 2028. _(structural)_
- Total advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028 (restrictions already in force since summer 2025) freezes market shares around incumbents' databases; a new brand has ~13 months of constrained marketing before the window shuts. _(structural)_
- Mandatory cross-operator player card, affordability checks and cashless venues from 2029 (bill in Seimas) target exactly the high-intensity slots play that is 57% of market GGR; expect VIP-driven GGR compression of the kind seen in Nordic card regimes. _(structural)_
- Growth is decelerating — Q3 2026 total GGR ~+9% vs +16.8% in H1 and remote ~+16% vs +25% — while retail is shrinking faster (Q3 ~−10%); the FY2026 exit rate is weaker than the 9M headline suggests. _(watch)_
- Entry barriers and opacity: only 12 licensees, land-based anchoring for remote licences, no published operator shares, and licence fees/capital thresholds not disclosed in LPT's public statistics — greenfield entry is slow and due diligence on any target will rely on private data. _(structural)_
- Enforcement risk is real money: fines are now a percentage of revenue, LPT is litigating a multi-million-euro penalty against the largest casino group, and it has publicly corrected an operator's statistical reporting — compliance failures will be priced in euros and reputation. _(governance)_
- Unlicensed-market leakage (industry estimate ≥€50m/yr offshore) is likely to rise as the tax, ad ban and card land together in 2027–29; the regulator's own data-management project and blocklist are the only counterweight. _(mixed)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The regime bridge runs from today's flat 22% GGR tax, restricted-but-permitted advertising and single-operator limits to a 2027–29 end-state of a 22/29/36% progressive tax (Government-approved 27 Nov 2026, effective 1 Jul 2027), a total advertising ban (1 Jan 2028) and a cross-operator player card with cashless venues and affordability checks (bill in Seimas, card from 1 Jan 2029). The remote channel drives the market numbers — Cat A slots +~30% and live tables +24% delivered all of 9M 2026's +14% — while betting (−2%) and retail (−6.5%) subtract. On the fiscal side the moving parts are MoF's +€9.9m (~$11.5m) 2027 / +€33.2m (~$38.5m) 2028 yield estimates versus the Democrats' ~€36m (~$41.8m), against a 9M 2026 take of €74.19m (~$86.1m) that is running +15%.

- **FY-25 gambling GGR (actual, LPT):** €274.1m (~$318.0m) (+13% YoY)
- **FY-26E gambling GGR (our estimate):** €305–312m (~$354–362m) (+11–14%; online ~€243m (+20%), retail ~€67m (−7%))
- **FY-26E lottery & gambling tax take (estimate):** ~€98m (~$113.7m) (+13% vs €86.9m)
- **Marginal GGR tax rate on >€5m from 1 Jul 2027:** 36% (+14pp vs 22% flat today)

**Market:** we expect FY2026 gambling GGR of ~€305–312m (~$354–362m) (+11–14%; estimate, built from 9M actuals plus Q4 2025's €73.6m base growing 9–12%), with remote ~€243m (+20%) and land-based ~€67m (−7%). 2027 should still grow high-single to low-double digits in H1 on slots momentum, but we would model **FY2027 growth of only ~5–8%** as the tax rise (Jul-27) and ad-ban anticipation take effect, and **flat-to-low-single-digit growth in 2028–29** once advertising stops and the card launches; retail continues at −5% to −10% a year and the cash ban could take that to −15%. **Operator margins:** the blended gambling tax rises from 22% to an estimated ~32–33% of market GGR in 2028, i.e. roughly 10–13 points of EBITDA margin for a scaled operator, partly offset by the forced marketing saving from the ad ban; small operators under €3m GGR are unaffected, which will encourage entity-level structuring that the Seimas is likely to pre-empt. **Fiscal:** tax take rises mechanically to ~€98m in 2026 and, if the draft passes unchanged, to ~€115–130m in 2028 (estimate) — unless channelisation slips, which is the swing factor the industry is arguing and the MoF is discounting. **Rules direction:** tighter on every axis — tax, advertising, identification, payments — with LPT gaining data (LAKIS), powers (bill) and appetite (revenue-based fines, Olympic litigation). **Leverage/entry:** the rational entry is acquisition of a sub-scale licensee with a retail licence and an existing database, priced on post-2027 tax and post-2028 zero-marketing economics, not on 2026 growth.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Seimas votes on the Law on Lottery and Gambling Tax (progressive 22/29/36%, lottery 20%) within the 2027 budget cycle (Dec 2026) — check whether thresholds become group-level and whether the 1 Jul 2027 date holds.
- Committee-stage amendments to the Gaming Law bill: player-card start date (1 Jan 2029), scope of affordability checks, the cash ban timetable and the May 2027 'excessive requirement' relief for operators.
- LPT FY2026 statistics (expected ~Mar 2027): Q4 remote growth versus the ~+16% Q3 run-rate, remote betting trend, and whether land-based decline accelerates past −10%.
- Implementation guidance for the 1 Jan 2028 advertising ban (treatment of owned channels, sponsorship, affiliates) and any transitional carve-outs.
- Enforcement metrics: blocklist additions after Polymarket, outcome of the Olympic Casino fine appeal, first LAKIS-driven limit-compliance sanctions, and any official (rather than ALVA) estimate of offshore leakage.
- Self-exclusion register growth (c.1,550/month) as a leading indicator of the addressable-base erosion the player card will formalise.
