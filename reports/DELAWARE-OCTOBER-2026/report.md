# Delaware () — OCTOBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (market reports in USD; tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released Nov 2026 (Delaware Lottery unaudited monthly tables for the Oct-26 accounting period, posted ~10–19 Nov 2026; trade coverage 27 Nov 2026)**

> October 2026 confirms Delaware as a small, state-run, single-vendor market whose growth now comes almost entirely from online casino: combined sports + iGaming wagers hit a record $396.3m (+16.6% YoY) and iGaming NGR rose 18.8% to $13.05m, while sports handle fell 8.4% and retail casino win slipped 1.4% to $38.4m. The state's take is rising faster than the market (sports state share +23.6%) because the Lottery keeps 50–90% of net proceeds under a revenue-share, not a tax. For an operator the market remains closed — entry is only via the Lottery's vendor contract (RSI/BetRivers, base term to ~2028) or B2B content supply — and the 2024 multi-operator bill is dead.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Record combined wagering, driven by online casino.** Delaware Lottery tables show sports handle of $26.01m plus iGaming bets of $370.24m = $396.25m in October 2026, +16.6% YoY (Oct-25: $339.8m) and +6.0% on September; iGaming bets grew 18.9% while sports sales fell 8.4%.
- **iGaming is the growth engine.** NGR (bets less promo deductions less player winnings) was $13.05m, +18.8% YoY, +1.6% MoM: online slots $9.97m (+28.0%), online tables $3.03m (-2.5%), poker $38.6k (-52.3%). FYTD (Jul–Oct FY27) NGR is $52.29m (+31.4%); FY26 full year was $135.93m (+59% per secondary arithmetic on Lottery tables, implying FY25 ≈ $85.5m).
- **Sports: fewer bets, fatter hold, more for the state.** Tickets fell 17.5% to 1.22m and handle 8.4% to $26.01m, but hold rose to 17.6% (13.9% a year ago), lifting gross win 16.0% to $4.57m, net proceeds (after $1.58m vendor fees) 18.9% to $2.82m and the state share 23.6% to $1.80m. Secondary coverage ranks it the sixth-best handle month since full-scale launch on 5 Jun 2018.
- **Retail casinos are flat-to-down.** CDC Gaming, citing the Lottery, puts October casino win at $38.4m (-1.4%): VLTs $35.1m (-1.0%), table games $3.0m (-6.8%). Delaware Park +1.3% to $16.8m was the only grower; Bally's Dover -3.7% to $12.4m; Harrington -3.3% to $9.2m. Prior months: Jul -3.7%, Aug -2.6%, Sep -5.4%.
- **Total market ≈ $54.3m in October; online casino ≈ 24%.** Summing retail casino win ($38.4m), iGaming NGR ($13.05m) and sports net proceeds ($2.82m) gives ~$54.3m: retail ~71%, online casino ~24%, sports ~5%. FYTD ≈ $210.5m. The Lottery does not split sports between BetRivers mobile and retail books, so true online penetration is understated.
- **Structure: a state-conducted lottery monopoly.** All casino gaming, iGaming and sports wagering are Lottery games under 29 Del. C. ch. 48; the State Lottery Office (Dept. of Finance) is operator-regulator and the Division of Gaming Enforcement handles licensing investigations/enforcement (the tables are explicitly 'before operating expenses for Lottery and Division of Gaming Enforcement'). Three racinos are 'video lottery agents'; one contracted vendor (Rush Street Interactive) runs all online product.
- **No open licensing route.** RSI won the Lottery's 2023 procurement (two bidders) for a five-year contract with annual renewal options and launched BetRivers-branded casino sites for all three racinos plus the single BetRivers sportsbook app on 27 Dec 2023 (replacing the prior 888 platform). There is no application window; suppliers reach players via the RSI platform (Hacksaw Gaming launched slots through BetRivers in Nov 2026).
- **The 'tax' is a revenue share — and it is steep.** Sports: net proceeds (sales − prizes − vendor fees) are split 50% State / ~10% purses / ~40% commission at casino sportsbooks and ~90% State at Lottery retailers (verified from the October table arithmetic); blended state share was 64.0% of net proceeds, 39.5% of gross win, 6.9% of handle. Video lottery: ~37.5% General Fund / ~44% agent commission / ~10.7% purses / ~7.8% vendor (Lottery website). iGaming and table-game splits are not disclosed monthly [unverified this cycle].
- **Vendor economics are large.** Sports vendor fees were $1.58m in October (34.6% of gross win; 6.1% of handle; +15.3% YoY) and $4.89m FYTD (36.2% of gross win). In FY26 vendors took $13.37m of $37.11m gross win — about the same as the state's $13.11m.
- **Operator concentration.** Delaware Park holds 54.0% of sports handle (incl. allocated mobile) and 46.1% of iGaming NGR; Harrington 19.9% / 30.2%; Bally's Dover 10.8% / 23.7% but is the fastest grower (iGaming NGR +30.3% YoY, FYTD +60.8%; sports net proceeds +35.4%).
- **Parlay-card retailers punch above their weight.** Lottery retailers took 15.2% of handle ($3.96m, +11.8%) but produced 34.8% of net proceeds ($0.98m, +44.3%) and 49.0% of the state share ($0.88m) because the state keeps ~90% of retailer proceeds.
- **Promo intensity is minimal.** iGaming promo/adjustment deductions were $2.45m, 0.66% of bets (0.62% a year ago; 0.71% FY26) — an order of magnitude below multi-operator states, a direct consequence of single-vendor exclusivity.
- **The acquisition funnel is slowing.** New iGaming registrations fell to 3,963 (-36.2% YoY; -25.1% MoM) and 18,474 FYTD (-21.9%) versus a 5,245 monthly average in FY26; growth is now spend-per-player and retention, consistent with year three of a monopoly platform in a ~1m-population state.
- **Hold rates are stable in iGaming.** Slots NGR/bets 4.42% (4.53% Oct-25; 4.51% FY26); tables 2.10% (2.23%; 2.02%). Slots are 76.4% of NGR, up from 70.9% a year ago — the mix is drifting toward higher-margin slots.
- **Legislation to open the market is stalled.** HB 365 (152nd General Assembly, Reps Cooke/Bush, Apr 2024) would have allowed each racino two mobile sportsbook skins (six total) at a $500k five-year licence, 18% of adjusted gross sports receipts to the General Fund and 1.5% to purses, codifying the Internet Sports Lottery Legislative Working Group's recommendation. The Lottery and Finance Secretary opposed it (revenue loss, contract risk, federal excise exposure) and it died without a Senate vote at the Jun 2024 adjournment; no successor had been enacted as of Nov 2026 [2025–26 session activity not verified].
- **Federal excise is a hidden structural feature.** Because wagers are state-conducted lottery products, the 0.25% federal excise on sports handle does not apply; a private-operator model would trigger it — one of the state's stated objections to opening the market.
- **Payments, RG and advertising are low-friction and lightly specified.** Standard US regulated rails (ACH/online banking, debit, e-wallets, cage) apply; no Delaware-specific advertising code; Lottery/DGE self-exclusion and a 21+ age floor are the core safeguards [method mix, limits and RG statistics not published; unverified this cycle]. College wagering on Delaware schools is restricted per secondary sources.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Delaware is not enterable as a B2C operator before the RSI base term ends (~late 2028), and even then only as the single Lottery vendor. The TAM is small (~$37m sports gross win and ~$136m iGaming NGR in FY26; ~$160–170m iGaming NGR FY27 est.). Realistic plays: (i) B2B content/platform supply to RSI, (ii) position for the 2028 re-procurement, (iii) support a racino-backed multi-skin bill. Model any entry at a 50%+ state take on sports unless the statute changes.
- **Data caveats.** Monthly tables are unaudited; retail casino figures are secondary and the prior-year October base ($38.9m implied vs $35.1m reported last year) suggests an accounting-period mismatch; Gaming Intelligence's combined-revenue figure ($15.7m) is slightly below our arithmetic from the Lottery tables ($15.86m = $2.82m sports net proceeds + $13.05m iGaming NGR).

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Sports betting (racino sportsbooks, Lottery parlay-card retailers, BetRivers mobile)

*Handle -8.4% to $26.0m but a 17.6% hold lifted net proceeds 18.9% and the state's cut 23.6%; retailers' parlay cards now deliver half the state share.*

### Handle and tickets

**Handle fell 8.4% to $26.01m in the 28 Sep–25 Oct 2026 period, up 3.7% on September; tickets dropped 17.5%.**

- Prior-year handle was $28.40m; September 2026 was $25.09m.
- Ticket count fell to 1.22m, lifting average ticket to $21.25 from $19.14.
- Lottery pools BetRivers mobile with each racino's retail book and shows parlay-card retailers separately.
- Mobile share is not disclosed — the single biggest gap in the dataset for modelling online penetration.

### Hold and gross win

**A 17.6% hold drove gross win to $4.57m (+16.0% YoY) against 13.9% a year earlier; two months above 17% should not be annualised.**

- Prizes paid $21.45m; September hold was 17.3%.
- FYTD hold 15.5% versus 12.5% prior-year period; FY26 full-year 14.7% on $252.46m handle, gross win $37.11m.
- Parlay-heavy mix is structurally high-hold, but 17%+ is unusual even for Delaware.
- Reversion to 13–15% would cut gross win by roughly a fifth at constant handle (estimate).

### Vendor fees and net proceeds

**Vendor fees of $1.58m (34.6% of gross win, +15.3% YoY) left net proceeds of $2.82m, up 18.9%.**

- Vendor fees were 6.1% of handle; FYTD they run 36.2% of gross win versus 34.6% a year earlier.
- Vendors (RSI/Kambi for mobile, Scientific Games for retail, per secondary sources) capture over a third of gross win before public distribution.
- FY26 vendor fees were $13.37m, roughly equal to the state's own $13.11m.

### Distribution mechanics

**The state took $1.80m, 64.0% of net proceeds (61.5% a year ago), 39.5% of gross win and 6.9% of handle.**

- Racino sportsbooks split net proceeds 50% State / ~10% purses / ~40% commission; Delaware Park $1.18m → $590k State, $113k purses, $477k commission.
- Lottery retailers hand ~90% to the State ($980k net → $883k), purses ~10%, retailer commission apparently funded outside net proceeds.
- FY26 state share was $13.11m, 5.2% of handle.

### Channel performance

**Delaware Park's handle fell 15.4% to $14.05m yet net proceeds grew 11.9%; retailers are 15.2% of handle but 49.0% of the state share.**

- Delaware Park 54.0% of handle, net proceeds $1.18m; it carries most retail and, by Lottery allocation, most mobile volume.
- Harrington $5.19m (-9.5%; net -10.5% to $399k); Bally's Dover $2.82m (+12.3%; net +35.4% to $259k).
- Lottery retailers $3.96m (+11.8%; net +44.3% to $980k), 34.8% of net proceeds.
- Per dollar wagered the parlay-card network is the most fiscally productive channel, explaining the Lottery's attachment to it.

### FYTD trend

**FYTD handle rose 3.0% to $87.38m while gross win jumped 27.5% to $13.52m and state share 27.0% to $4.96m.**

- Tickets 3.80m (-10.6%); net proceeds $8.20m (+26.8%).
- Flat handle with rising hold is the signature of a maturing single-app market with parlay migration and thinner pricing than NJ, PA, MD.
- Secondary sources cite calendar-2025 handle of $253.3m and revenue of $20.7m (8.2% hold on their definition).

### Commercial implication

**The ~$37m annual gross win pool, split ~$13m General Fund and ~$13m vendors, makes the vendor position the real prize, not a licence.**

- No single entrant could materially change the TAM.
- The state objects to opening the market on revenue and federal excise grounds.

> “shift State revenues to sports wagering companies and also trigger federal excise taxes” — Finance Secretary

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Online casino (iGaming: internet video lottery, table games, poker)

*NGR +18.8% to $13.05m on record bets of $370m; slots carry the growth (+28%) while registrations fall 36% — the market is deepening, not widening.*

### Bets and NGR

**Record bets of $370.24m (+18.9% YoY) produced NGR of $13.05m (+18.8% YoY; +1.6% MoM), with slots at 76.4% of NGR.**

- Online slots $225.49m (+31.0%); online table games $144.75m (+3.9%); bets +6.2% MoM.
- NGR by product: slots $9.97m (+28.0%), tables $3.03m (23.3%; -2.5%), poker $38.6k (-52.3%).
- Slots were 74.0% of FY26 NGR, so mix keeps drifting toward the higher-margin product.

### Hold and promo intensity

**Hold rates are stable and promo deductions of $2.45m were just 0.66% of bets, the fingerprint of a single-vendor market.**

- Slots hold 4.42% (4.53% a year ago; 4.51% FY26); tables 2.10% (2.23%; 2.02% FY26).
- Promo ratio was 0.62% a year ago and 0.71% in FY26, a fraction of bonusing in multi-operator states.
- No competitor bids up acquisition cost, so economics accrue to the vendor, racinos and state rather than players.

### FYTD and forecast

**FYTD NGR is $52.29m (+31.4%) on bets of $1,425m (+25.3%); we estimate FY27 NGR of $160–170m.**

- FY26 (Jul-25–Jun-26) NGR $135.93m on bets of $3,947m, +59% YoY by secondary arithmetic, implying FY25 ≈ $85.5m.
- October annualises to ~$157m.
- Growth is decelerating from the step-change after RSI replaced the 888 platform on 27 Dec 2023 but remains double-digit.
- Forecast assumes Nov–Jun at +15–20% on FY26's ~$96m for those months.

### Player funnel

**New registrations fell 36.2% YoY to 3,963; growth now comes from spend-per-player and retention, not acquisition.**

- Registrations -25.1% MoM; FYTD 18,474 (-21.9%) versus a 5,245 monthly average in FY26.
- Expected profile in year three of a monopoly platform serving roughly one million residents.
- Early warning that online growth will converge toward retail trends within two to three years absent new product or a competitive catalyst.

### Performance by skin

**Delaware Park led with $6.02m (46.1% share; +17.8%) while Bally's Dover grew fastest at +30.3% to $3.09m.**

- Harrington $3.94m (30.2%; +12.4%); Bally's Dover 23.7% share, FYTD +60.8%.
- All skins share the same BetRivers platform, game library and sportsbook; differentiation is brand and loyalty cross-sell.
- Hacksaw Gaming's November 2026 slot launch via BetRivers shows RSI's aggregation is the only realistic B2B route for suppliers.

### Disclosure and economics

**Delaware iGaming is a ~$150–170m NGR pool with 0.7% promo intensity and no second platform — inaccessible until re-procurement.**

- Monthly NGR is reported before vendor fees and before Lottery/DGE operating expenses.
- Statutory split between State, purses, agents and RSI's vendor fee are not disclosed monthly [unverified this cycle]; only in annual financial statements.
- Excellent unit economics for the incumbent vendor.

**Three racinos generated $38.4m of casino win (-1.4% YoY); only Delaware Park grew, +1.3% to $16.8m.**

- VLTs $35.1m (-1.0%); table games $3.0m (-6.8%), per CDC Gaming citing the Lottery.
- Delaware Park VLT +4.8% to $15.3m, tables -24.9% to $1.5m.

### Racino economics

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, tax & revenue distribution

*No licences to apply for: a state-conducted lottery with three racino agents and one online vendor; the state keeps 50–90% of sports net proceeds and ~37.5% of VLT win.*

### Regulatory structure

**All gaming is conducted as lottery games by the Delaware State Lottery Office; there are no commercial casino or sportsbook licences.**

- Authority is Title 29, Chapter 48 of the Delaware Code; the Division of Gaming Enforcement handles licensing investigations and enforcement.
- Lottery tables are reported 'before operating expenses for Lottery and Division of Gaming Enforcement'.
- The three racetracks are 'video lottery agents'; the online operator is a contracted Lottery vendor.

### Online vendor contract

**Rush Street Interactive won the 2023 single-vendor procurement with a five-year contract running to late 2028, plus annual renewal options.**

- Two companies applied (Delaware Business Times).
- BetRivers casino sites for all three racinos and the single sportsbook app launched 27 Dec 2023.
- No application window exists for additional operators; suppliers reach the market via RSI's platform.

### Sports distribution

**State took 64.0% of sports net proceeds in October; the '50% tax' is a revenue share that avoids the 0.25% federal excise.**

- Net proceeds = sales − prizes − vendor fees.
- Racino sportsbooks: 50% State / ~10% purses / ~40% commission; Lottery retailers: ~90% State / ~10% purses.
- Federal excise exemption was a point the Finance Secretary used against HB 365.

### Casino and iGaming splits

**Video lottery distributes ~37.5% General Fund, ~44% agent commission, ~10.7% purses, ~7.8% vendor; table and iGaming splits are unverified.**

- Table games state share not verified this cycle [unverified].
- iGaming statutory split and RSI vendor fee not disclosed in monthly tables [unverified].
- Secondary sources cite an annual fee of roughly $4.5m shared by the three casinos pro rata to video-lottery share [secondary, unverified].

### HB 365 reform

**HB 365 (April 2024) proposed six racino-tethered skins at 18% of adjusted gross receipts but died at the 30 Jun 2024 adjournment.**

- Sponsored by Reps Franklin Cooke and William Bush, 152nd General Assembly; up to two mobile sportsbooks per racino.
- $500,000 five-year licence fee, 18% to General Fund, 1.5% to purses, codifying the Working Group's multi-operator recommendation.
- Lottery and Finance Secretary opposed on revenue, contract-risk and federal-excise grounds; no Senate vote.
- No successor measure enacted as of November 2026 (2025–26 session activity not verified).

### Entry options

**Delaware's sports take is the highest in the US; entry is via the ~2028 procurement, B2B supply to RSI, or a multi-skin bill.**

- State take: 50% of net proceeds at casinos, ~90% at retailers, versus 18% proposed under HB 365.
- iGaming economics are set by contract rather than statute.
- Lottery's revenue-maximising mandate and absence of federal excise weigh against change.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & political direction

*Low-friction US payment rails and minimal bonusing (0.66% of bets); no Delaware-specific ad code; the political centre of gravity is the Lottery's revenue mandate, with the 2028 vendor renewal the only hard catalyst.*

### Payments

**Standard US regulated rails apply and nothing suggests payment friction constrains growth; iGaming bets of $370m are 14x sports handle.**

- Methods include ACH/online banking, debit cards, e-wallets and cash at the racino cage; no mobile-money analogue.
- Lottery publishes no deposit-method mix; BetRivers' Delaware-specific methods and limits not verified [n/a].
- Slots turnover grew 31% YoY.

### Responsible gambling

**Safeguards are self-exclusion, a 21+ age floor and the state helpline; promo intensity of 0.66% of bets shows minimal bonus stimulation.**

- Administered by the Lottery with the DGE; programme details and statistics not published monthly [unverified].
- iGaming promo/adjustment deductions were $2.45m versus 0.71% of bets in FY26.
- Minimal bonusing is by design of the single-vendor model.

**No Delaware-specific advertising code exists; marketing is a Lottery/RSI decision rather than a competitive arms race.**

- No code comparable to Ontario or the UK; state exposure to multi-operator ad saturation is nil.
- College wagering on Delaware-based schools and college player props restricted per secondary sources [unverified].

**Status quo is favoured: the Lottery keeps 64% of sports net proceeds and avoids federal excise; the 2028 vendor renewal is the only hard catalyst.**

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Online casino is in structural growth: NGR +18.8% YoY in October, +31.4% FYTD, with slots +28% and hold stable at ~4.4% — a ~$160–170m FY27 NGR pool (est.) with only one platform serving it. _(structural)_
- The B2B door is open: Hacksaw Gaming's November 2026 launch via BetRivers shows content and platform suppliers can reach Delaware players through RSI's aggregation without any state licence. _(structural)_
- Monopoly economics: promo deductions are just 0.66% of iGaming bets and sports hold is running 15–18%, so revenue per dollar wagered is far higher than in open multi-operator states — attractive for whoever holds the vendor contract. _(structural)_
- A defined re-procurement window: RSI's five-year base term from Aug 2023 ends in late 2028; the Lottery's 2023 tender drew only two bidders, so a credible challenger faces thin competition for the only B2C route into the state. _(early)_
- The state's own Working Group has already recommended up to six mobile skins tethered to the racinos; a re-filed HB 365-style bill (18% of adjusted gross receipts, $500k five-year licence) would be a step-change in operator economics versus today's 50% revenue share. _(watch)_
- Retail parlay cards are a durable niche: Lottery retailers took 15% of handle but delivered 49% of the state's sports share, a product format that keeps the retail network politically protected and commercially relevant. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- The market is closed: no licence application exists, all online product runs through one Lottery vendor to ~2028, and HB 365 died without a Senate vote in June 2024 with the Lottery and Finance Secretary opposed. _(structural)_
- Tiny addressable pool: FY26 sports gross win was $37.1m (state share $13.1m, vendor fees $13.4m) and October handle fell 8.4% with tickets -17.5%; even a 100% share would be immaterial to a multi-state operator. _(structural)_
- Punitive state take: 50% of sports net proceeds at casino books and ~90% at retailers (64% blended in October), the steepest effective rate in the US; iGaming and table-game splits are undisclosed monthly, adding modelling risk. _(structural)_
- iGaming acquisition is decelerating: new registrations fell 36% YoY to 3,963 (18,474 FYTD, -22%), so growth now depends on spend-per-player — the online growth rate will converge toward retail trends within two to three years absent a catalyst. _(watch)_
- Retail is eroding: casino win -1.4% in October after -5.4% in September and -3.7% in July; table games -6.8% to -19.7% across the quarter; two of three racinos declined — cannibalisation by the skins is visible. _(mixed)_
- Sports hold of 17.6% (vs 13.9% a year ago) flatters the state's +23.6% take; normalisation to 13–15% would cut gross win by roughly a fifth at constant handle (estimate), exposing the underlying handle decline. _(one-off)_
- Governance and data opacity: monthly tables are unaudited, mobile vs retail sports volume is not disclosed, retail casino figures could only be sourced second-hand this cycle with an unexplained prior-year base mismatch ($35.1m vs ~$38.9m), and legislative activity in the 2025–26 session could not be verified. _(governance)_
- Substitution risk from outside the state framework: multi-operator apps in NJ/PA/MD and federally regulated prediction markets are marketed to Delaware users, eroding the case for — and the value of — single-vendor exclusivity. _(early)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

A market has no guidance, so the bridge compares the FY2026 base (Jul-25–Jun-26) with the FY2027 run-rate implied by Jul–Oct 2026. iGaming is the driver: FYTD NGR +31.4% against FY26's +59%, so growth is halving but still double-digit; sports gross win is +27.5% FYTD on flat handle (+3.0%) purely through hold; retail casino win is tracking roughly -3% (secondary). Regulatory moving parts are few: RSI's exclusive contract (base term to ~late 2028), no multi-operator bill since HB 365 died in June 2024, no tax or distribution change proposed, and the FY2028 budget cycle (Jan–Jun 2027) as the next formal point at which the Lottery's revenue forecast and any expansion bill would surface.

- **FY26 iGaming NGR (base):** $135.9m (+59% YoY (secondary arithmetic on Lottery tables))
- **FY27 iGaming NGR (estimate):** $160–170m (FYTD +31.4%; Oct annualised $157m; Nov–Jun assumed +15–20%)
- **FY26 sports state share (base):** $13.1m (handle $252.5m; hold 14.7%)
- **FY27 sports state share (estimate):** $15–17m (FYTD +27.0%; assumes hold normalises to ~15% and handle flat)

**Market:** we expect FY27 iGaming NGR of $160–170m (+18–25%) with growth decelerating through 2027 as registrations fall and the player base saturates; sports handle flat to -5% with hold reverting toward 14–15%, leaving net proceeds up low-double-digits for the fiscal year but flat-to-down by mid-2027 on tougher comparatives; retail casino win -2% to -4% as the skins cannibalise VLT play. **Total gaming revenue** (retail win + iGaming NGR + sports net proceeds) should therefore grow low-to-mid single digits to roughly $650–670m in FY27 (estimate), with online casino's share rising to ~26–27%. **State take** rises faster than the market because the online and parlay channels carry the highest public shares. **Rules:** no change in 2027; the realistic catalyst is the 2028 vendor renewal, and we put the probability of a multi-operator mobile sports bill passing before that renewal at well under one in three — the Lottery's revenue mandate, the federal-excise argument and the racinos' comfortable skin economics all favour continuity. **Swing factors:** a re-filed racino-backed skins bill with purse protections; a Lottery decision to re-tender rather than extend RSI; evidence of leakage to prediction markets or neighbouring states large enough to change the Finance Department's arithmetic; and any national federal action on prediction markets. **For B2Tech:** treat Delaware as a B2B content/platform opportunity now and a 2028 vendor-tender option later; a B2C entry case does not exist under current law, and any model should carry a 50%+ state share on sports and undisclosed iGaming economics until the statute changes.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Monthly iGaming registrations (3,963 in Oct, -36% YoY) — the saturation gauge; sustained sub-4,000 months would confirm convergence toward retail growth rates.
- Sports hold reverting from 17–18% toward 13–15% — the main risk to the state's +24% take and to the Lottery's defence of the single-app model.
- FY2028 Governor's budget (Jan 2027) and the 2027 legislative session for any re-filed multi-operator mobile bill (HB 365 template: six skins, $500k/5-yr, 18% of AGR, 1.5% purses).
- Lottery signalling on the RSI contract (base term to ~late 2028): annual extension versus re-tender, and whether the next RFP contemplates more than one vendor.
- Retail casino win trend (Jul–Oct -1% to -5%) and table-game weakness; a second year of decline strengthens the racinos' case for additional online skins.
- Reconciliation of the retail casino accounting periods (Oct-25 base $35.1m vs ~$38.9m implied) once the Lottery's video-lottery table is accessible.
- Prediction-market and cross-border leakage: any Delaware DGE/Lottery statement on Kalshi-type event contracts, and NJ/PA/MD geolocation-blocking data if published.
