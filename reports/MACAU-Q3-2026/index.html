Q3 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Macau

Released 16 Oct 2026 (DICJ Q3-26 game-type statistics; monthly Sept GGR released 1 Oct 2026) · Currency MOP (Macau pataca; tables in MOP billions unless stated) with ~USD alongside at USD/MOP 8.07 · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Macau's casino GGR fell 3.8% YoY to MOP 60.22bn (~$7.46bn) in Q3-26 — the first quarterly decline since the post-COVID reopening — as mass/premium-mass baccarat dropped 7.9% and the market absorbed the World Cup, softer Chinese high-end demand and tougher hold comps; VIP stabilised (+6.3% QoQ, flat YoY) and slots grew 18.8%. For an operator, Macau remains a closed, six-concession, land-based-only market taxed at an effective 40% of GGR until 31 Dec 2032: the only realistic entry is B2B (EGMs, systems, content), where the slot segment is the one structurally growing line.

1Executive summary

  • First quarterly YoY decline of the recovery cycle. DICJ data for Q3-26 (Jul–Sep) put GGR from games of fortune at MOP 60.22bn (~$7.46bn), down 3.8% YoY and 1.3% QoQ, against an implied Q3-25 base of MOP 62.6bn (~$7.76bn). IAG rounds the same release to MOP 60.4bn/−3.7%; the AGB/Focus GN/Yogonet figure of MOP 60.22bn is used here.
  • Mass/premium-mass baccarat is the problem line. Mass baccarat GGR fell 7.9% YoY and 4.4% QoQ to MOP 33.61bn (~$4.17bn); its share of GGR slipped to 55.8% from 58.3% a year earlier. The MOP 2.88bn (~$357m) absolute drop exceeded the total market decline, i.e. everything else net-grew.
  • Total baccarat (the Gaming Intelligence hook) was MOP 50.52bn (~$6.26bn), −5.4% YoY, the lowest in six quarters; baccarat remains ~84% of casino GGR, underlining Macau's product concentration.
  • VIP has stabilised rather than recovered. VIP baccarat GGR was MOP 16.91bn (~$2.10bn), +0.1% YoY and +6.3% QoQ after an 18.8% sequential fall in Q2-26; share rose to 28.1%. 9M-26 VIP GGR is MOP 52.38bn (~$6.49bn), +9.9% YoY. Caveat: DICJ's VIP definition is table-designation based and runs far above the concessionaires' own VIP disclosures.
  • Slots are the only structurally growing product. Slot GGR rose 18.8% YoY to MOP 4.09bn (~$0.51bn), 6.8% of GGR (5.5% a year ago); 9M-26 slot GGR is MOP 12.18bn (~$1.51bn), ~+19% (derived). Live multi-game (LMG) electronic tables fell 8.0% to MOP 1.14bn (~$0.14bn).
  • Monthly cadence shows four consecutive YoY declines. July MOP 20.26bn (~$2.51bn, −8.4%, World Cup final 19 Jul), August MOP 21.89bn (~$2.71bn, −1.2%), September MOP 18.06bn (~$2.24bn, −1.2%, lowest month of 2026, −17.5% MoM). 9M-26 GGR is MOP 187.1bn (~$23.2bn), +3.2% YoY but ~15% below 9M-19's MOP 220.3bn (~$27.3bn).
  • Channel: 100% land-based. Macau licenses no online casino or online betting; online penetration of the regulated market is 0%. Non-casino regulated wagering is limited to the Macau Slot sports lottery (football GGR MOP 152m/~$18.8m, +49% on the World Cup; basketball MOP 44m/~$5.5m, +4.8%) and the SLOT Chinese lottery; horse racing ceased in 2024.
  • Licensing is closed until 2032. Law 16/2001 as amended by Law 7/2022 caps the market at six concessions (SJM Resorts, Galaxy Casino, Venetian Macau/Sands China, Wynn Resorts (Macau), Melco Resorts (Macau), MGM Grand Paradise), each a 10-year contract from 1 Jan 2023 to 31 Dec 2032, extendable by up to three years only exceptionally. Market-wide caps: 6,000 tables and 12,000 machines.
  • Tax base is GGR, effective rate 40%. Special gaming tax 35% of GGR (art. 27) plus fixed contributions of 2% (public foundation) and 3% (urban development, tourism, social security) — Law 7/2022 removed the former 'up to' wording, lifting the levies from an actual 4% to a fixed 5%. The 5% can be waived on GGR from foreign (non-Greater-China) players under AR 54/2022. Gaming profits are exempt from complementary (profits) tax through 31 Dec 2027.
  • Premiums and minimum-yield rules add a fixed-cost floor. Each concessionaire pays a fixed premium of MOP 30m (~$3.7m) p.a. plus MOP 300,000 (~$37k) per VIP table, MOP 150,000 (~$18.6k) per mass table and MOP 1,000 (~$124) per machine (minimum charge equivalent to 500 tables/1,000 machines). A 'special premium' of 35% of any shortfall applies if annual GGR per table is below MOP 7m (~$0.87m) or per machine below MOP 300,000 (~$37k).
  • Fiscal take is running ahead of GGR. The Financial Services Bureau collected MOP 74.56bn (~$9.24bn) of gaming taxes in 9M-26, +5.9% YoY (tax lags GGR by one month), equal to 84.1% of the government's MOP 88.71bn (~$10.99bn) current revenue and 80.6% of the MOP 92.53bn (~$11.47bn) full-year budget (of which MOP 82.60bn/~$10.24bn is the 35% special tax; junket-commission tax budgeted at just MOP 150m/~$18.6m).
  • Government forecast is a floor, not a target. The 2026 budget assumes GGR of MOP 236bn (~$29.2bn); Chief Executive Sam Hou Fai said on 10 Oct that no downward revision is being considered. With 9M-26 at MOP 187.1bn, Q4 needs only MOP 48.9bn (~$6.06bn), or MOP 16.3bn (~$2.02bn)/month — a bar the market will clear even in a severe Q4.
  • Sell-side is converging on ~+1% for FY26. J.P. Morgan cut FY26 to MOP 249bn (~$30.9bn, +1% vs FY25's MOP 247.4bn/~$30.7bn, ~85% of 2019); Seaport models Q4 −5%; Bloomberg consensus growth has fallen to ~3% from 7% at the start of the year; one bank's October call is −6% to MOP 22.6bn (~$2.80bn).
  • Demand mix is deteriorating even as volume hits records. August visitor arrivals were a record 4.5m (+6.1%) but same-day visitors rose 11.7% while overnight visitors fell 2.4%; GGR per visitor fell 7% to MOP 4,888 (~$606) in August and is MOP 5,386 (~$667) for 8M-26, below both 2025 and pre-COVID. Hotel occupancy was 93.9% in August. The Macau Economic Association expects Q3-26 GDP −2% on the gaming slowdown.
  • China policy is the overhang. Seaport links premium softness to weaker mainland macro and tax enforcement on offshore trusts/wealth; CreditSights sees capital controls as a sentiment drag on high rollers but not a direct GGR hit because funding channels are already tightly controlled. Four of the six concessionaires flagged premium volume diversion during the World Cup.
  • Junkets are structurally marginal. Law 16/2022 confines gaming promoters to one concessionaire each, bans revenue-sharing and caps commissions at 1.25% of rolling; the gaming-credit law (effective Aug 2024) reserves credit extension to concessionaires. The budgeted MOP 150m (~$18.6m) junket-commission tax implies commissionable rolling of ~MOP 12bn (~$1.5bn) a year at the cap — a rounding error against VIP GGR of MOP 52bn+. Licensed junket count for 2026 is [unverified in this session].
  • Enforcement is tightening, not loosening. Gaming-related crime rose 12.3% in 9M-26; Law 7/2024 (effective Oct 2024) criminalised illegal currency exchange for gambling and strengthened sanctions on illegal/online gambling; casino entry is 21+, smoking is confined to non-gaming lounges, and gaming advertising is tightly restricted in Macau and criminalised for mainland audiences under PRC law.
  • Non-gaming obligations are large and binding. The six concessions carry MOP 118.8bn (~$14.7bn) of pledged investment (Galaxy alone MOP 28.35bn/~$3.5bn, of which only MOP 0.9bn/~$0.11bn gaming), with a +20% uplift once annual GGR exceeds MOP 180bn (~$22.3bn) — a trigger already met in 2024 (FY24 GGR MOP 226.8bn/~$28.1bn); the incremental amount is [unverified in this session].
  • Read-through for B2Tech: there is no B2C route into Macau before 2033 and no online route at all. The addressable opportunity is B2B — EGM/ETG cabinets, slot content, systems and compliance tech to six well-capitalised concessionaires chasing a slot segment growing ~19% a year in a market where everything else is flat-to-down; the 12,000-machine cap and MOP 300,000 (~$37k) minimum yield per machine shape the floor-space economics. Regional spill-over (Philippines, Vietnam, UAE 2027, Thailand if legalised) is the better B2C lens for the same customer base.

3Deep-dive: Mass market (mass & premium-mass baccarat, other tables, electronic tables)

Mass baccarat −7.9% YoY to MOP 33.61bn (~$4.17bn) is the whole story of the quarter: record footfall, falling spend per head.

Mass baccarat GGR

Mass baccarat GGR fell 7.9% YoY and 4.4% QoQ to MOP 33.61bn (~$4.17bn), a third straight sequential decline.

  • Prior quarters: MOP 36.56bn (~$4.53bn) in Q1-26 and MOP 35.2bn (~$4.36bn) in Q2-26.
  • DICJ's 'mass baccarat' bundles grind mass and premium mass; operators and sell-side locate the weakness in premium mass.
  • World Cup (11 Jun–19 Jul) diverted play; Chinese high-end demand softened under weaker macro and tighter tax enforcement on offshore wealth.
  • The MOP 2.88bn (~$357m) YoY fall in this line exceeded the MOP 2.38bn (~$295m) fall in total GGR.

Broader mass segment

Mass including slots fell 5.2% YoY to MOP 43.31bn (~$5.37bn), 71.9% of GGR.

  • Compares with MOP 45.13bn (~$5.59bn) in Q2-26 (+0.8% YoY then) and MOP 46.48bn (~$5.76bn) in Q1-26.
  • Residual other mass tables (sic bo, roulette, fan tan, blackjack, poker variants) ~MOP 5.61bn (~$0.70bn), down ~2.6% YoY on our derivation.
  • Live multi-game electronic terminals fell 8.0% to MOP 1.14bn (~$0.14bn) from MOP 1.30bn (~$0.16bn) in Q2.
  • Notable because LMG had been a growth product and is where ETG suppliers compete.

Volume versus yield

Volume is not the problem; yield is: record 4.5m August visitors (+6.1% YoY) but GGR per visitor fell 7% to MOP 4,888 (~$606).

  • Hotel occupancy was 93.9% in August.
  • 8M-26 GGR per visitor MOP 5,386 (~$667), below both 2025 and pre-COVID.
  • Macau Economic Association attributes the gap to mix shift: same-day visitors +11.7%, overnight visitors −2.4% in August.
  • Concerts at Galaxy Arena and Venetian Arena lifted premium visitation in early August; non-gaming calendar moves premium mass at the margin but does not offset a cyclical downturn in Chinese wealth sentiment.

Hold and comps

Part of the −7.9% is a base effect from high-hold Q3-25, but the two-quarter sequential decline is a demand signal, not variance.

  • Seaport flags the tough hold comparison persisting into October.
  • Sequential declines of −3.8% then −4.4%.
  • Macau Economic Association estimates Q3-26 GDP −2%, confirming gaming is now a drag on the territory's economy.

Commercial implication

A 5–8% mass decline compresses EBITDA faster than the headline; CLSA and Goldman expect Q3 property EBITDA to fall YoY.

  • Mass is where the six concessionaires earn their margin (no junket commissions, lower reinvestment).
  • Operators will protect premium-mass floors with product refreshes and ETG/slot capacity (yield per square metre rising) rather than add tables.
  • Constraints: 6,000-table cap and MOP 7m (~$0.87m) per-table minimum-yield rule.
  • Any mass-table capacity reallocation to electronic formats benefits machine and systems vendors.

4Deep-dive: VIP baccarat & gaming promoters (junkets)

VIP GGR MOP 16.91bn (~$2.10bn) stabilised (+6.3% QoQ, flat YoY) under an in-house premium-direct model; junkets are now a residual channel.

VIP baccarat GGR

VIP baccarat GGR of MOP 16.91bn (~$2.10bn) stabilised, +0.1% YoY and +6.3% QoQ, under an in-house premium-direct model.

  • Recovers part of the 18.8% sequential collapse in Q2-26 (MOP 15.90bn/~$1.97bn), attributed to low hold and the World Cup.
  • Still 13.6% below the Q1-26 peak of MOP 19.56bn (~$2.42bn, +35.4% YoY).
  • Share of GGR rose to 28.1% (26.1% in Q2; 27.0% in Q3-25).
  • 9M-26 VIP GGR MOP 52.38bn (~$6.49bn), +9.9% YoY, decelerating from +15.2% at the half-year.

Definitional caveat

DICJ 'VIP' captures a large slice of premium-direct play that operators book as premium mass; comparisons with junket-era VIP are not like-for-like.

  • DICJ classifies VIP by table designation inside the integrated resorts.
  • Concessionaires' own disclosures report VIP/rolling-chip as low-to-mid teens share of revenue in most cases.
  • Pre-2022 junket-era VIP was ~50%+ of GGR.

Junket channel

The junket channel has been legislated into irrelevance; 2026 budget projects junket-commission tax of just MOP 150m (~$18.6m).

  • Law 16/2022 restricts each promoter to a single concessionaire, bans revenue-share, caps commissions at 1.25% of rolling-chip turnover, requires annual licence renewal.
  • Gaming-credit law (effective Aug 2024) permits only concessionaires to extend gaming credit.
  • Tax at the 1.25% cap implies commissionable rolling of only ~MOP 12bn (~$1.5bn) a year [derived; exact tax base not confirmed].
  • Number of licensed promoters for 2026 could not be verified in this session.

Macro and policy overhang

Analyst checks point to VIP being range-bound rather than re-accelerating.

  • CreditSights (29 Sep): China capital-control tightening unlikely to hit GGR directly as funding channels already tightly policed, but could dampen high-roller sentiment.
  • Seaport (early Oct) cites tax enforcement on offshore trusts and weaker mainland macro as drags on wealthy customers.
  • Citi's July weekly checks showed VIP rolling volume up only 0–2% WoW even as the World Cup schedule thinned.

Commercial implication

VIP is now a concessionaire-owned, credit-backed premium-direct business with no third-party operator or agent model for a new entrant.

  • Supplier value is indirect: premium rooms drive demand for high-limit ETG and table-management/AML systems.
  • Regulatory direction (credit law, illegal-exchange crackdown, PRC cross-border gambling criminalisation) is unambiguously toward fewer intermediaries and more surveillance.

5Deep-dive: Slots/EGMs, sports lottery and the (absent) online channel

Slots +18.8% YoY to MOP 4.09bn (~$0.51bn) are the only structural growth line; Macau Slot football GGR +49% on the World Cup; no online channel exists or is planned.

Slot GGR growth

Slot GGR grew 18.8% YoY to MOP 4.09bn (~$0.51bn), the only structural growth line, lifting share to 6.8% of GGR.

  • −0.5% QoQ from MOP 4.11bn (~$0.51bn); share was 5.5% a year earlier.
  • Quarterly growth run: +21.6% (Q1), +17.0% (Q2), +18.8% (Q3); 9M-26 slot GGR MOP 12.18bn (~$1.51bn), ~+19% on our derivation.
  • Growth from premium-mass players migrating to high-denomination cabinets and smoke-free electronic floors, and operators' active floor re-mixing.
  • Not from volume tourism: same-day visitors rising but their spend is low.

Capacity economics

The 12,000-machine cap, not the yield test, is the binding constraint, supporting replacement-cycle and premium-cabinet demand rather than unit expansion.

  • Allocation examples: Venetian 3,700 and Galaxy 1,700 machines per 2022 contract summaries.
  • Variable premium MOP 1,000 (~$124) per machine; minimum-yield test MOP 300,000 (~$37k) GGR per machine per year.
  • Tables: MOP 150,000–300,000 (~$18.6k–37k) premium and MOP 7m (~$0.87m) minimum yield.
  • Installed base yields well above the minimum on average.
  • Live multi-game ETGs fell 8.0% to MOP 1.14bn (~$0.14bn), suggesting operators prioritise true slots over stadium-style ETG.

Sports lottery

Sports lottery is tiny and monopoly-run: football GGR +49.0% to MOP 152m (~$18.8m), basketball +4.8% to MOP 44m (~$5.5m).

  • Macau Slot (SLOT) holds the concession for football and basketball; World Cup final on 19 July fell inside the quarter.
  • Together ~0.3% of casino GGR.
  • Macau Jockey Club ended horse racing in 2024, so pari-mutuel wagering is effectively nil.
  • World Cup moved ~MOP 1–2bn (~$0.12–0.25bn) of casino GGR per month but only ~MOP 50m (~$6m) of lottery GGR.

Online channel

Online penetration of the regulated market is 0% and there is no legislative initiative to change that.

  • Macau licenses no online casino, poker or sports betting.
  • Law 7/2024 (effective Oct 2024) sharpened criminal sanctions on illegal gambling including online promotion and illegal currency exchange.
  • PRC criminalised organising cross-border gambling for mainland residents in 2021; DICJ maintains a public list of illegal gambling websites.
  • Political priority is economic diversification ('1+4'), not channel expansion.

Commercial implication

For B2Tech, the slot line is the only part of the Macau P&L growing double digits; sportsbook or iGaming product has no legal landing.

  • Slots served through six buyers with regulator-approved EGM technical standards (DICJ EGM certification).
  • Sports-lottery concession is a monopoly and online is closed.
  • Relevant Asian online opportunities sit outside the SAR (Philippines PIGO, etc.).

6Deep-dive: Licensing & tax: the concession regime (Law 16/2001 as amended by Law 7/2022)

Six 10-year concessions to 31 Dec 2032, 35% special tax + 5% fixed levies = 40% of GGR, premiums and minimum-yield rules — stable, closed and expensive.

Concession structure

Six 10-year concessions run 1 Jan 2023 to 31 Dec 2032 under Law 16/2001 as amended by Law 7/2022; stable, closed and expensive.

  • Regulator is DICJ under the Secretary for Economy and Finance; Chief Executive (Sam Hou Fai since Dec 2024) holds key discretionary powers.
  • Concessionaires: SJM Resorts, Galaxy Casino, Venetian Macau (Sands China), Wynn Resorts (Macau), Melco Resorts (Macau), MGM Grand Paradise.
  • Sub-concessions abolished; maximum term 10 years with exceptional extensions of up to three years.
  • Minimum share capital MOP 5bn (~$0.62bn); Macau-permanent-resident managing director holding at least 15% of capital.
  • Gaming only in concessionaire-owned premises, ending satellite casinos after transition to 31 Dec 2025; exact closure count [unverified in this session].

Tax base and rate

35% special gaming tax plus fixed 2% and 3% levies equals 40% of GGR from 2023, up from an effective 39%.

  • Special tax (art. 27) payable monthly by the 10th of the following month to the Financial Services Bureau.
  • Law 7/2022 deleted 'up to' language under which levies had been charged at 1.6% and 2.4%.
  • Under AR 54/2022 the 5% may be reduced or waived on GGR from foreign (non-mainland/HK/Taiwan) players, effectively 35%.
  • Gaming profits exempt from the 12% complementary tax for 1 Jan 2023–31 Dec 2027; renewal is the next fiscal decision point.
  • Junket commissions attract a separate withholding, budgeted at MOP 150m (~$18.6m) for 2026.

Premiums and minimum yield

Fixed premium MOP 30m (~$3.7m) plus per-unit premiums, with a 35% special premium on shortfalls below minimum yields of MOP 7m per table and MOP 300,000 per machine.

  • Variable premium: MOP 300,000 (~$37k) per VIP table, MOP 150,000 (~$18.6k) per mass table, MOP 1,000 (~$124) per machine; floor of 500 tables and 1,000 machines.
  • Minimum yield MOP 7m (~$0.87m) per table and MOP 300,000 (~$37k) per machine, reviewable by the Chief Executive.
  • Market-wide cap 6,000 tables and 12,000 machines, e.g. Venetian 1,680 tables/3,700 machines; Galaxy 1,000/1,700.
  • Separate annual 'utilisation fee' for casino space that reverted to the government on 31 Dec 2022.

Fiscal dependence

Gaming taxes of MOP 74.56bn (~$9.24bn) in 9M-26, +5.9% YoY, were 84.1% of government current revenue, so rates will not rise.

  • Tax lags GGR by a month, reflecting Dec-25–Aug-26 GGR; current revenue MOP 88.71bn (~$10.99bn).
  • 2026 budget expects MOP 92.53bn (~$11.47bn) gaming taxes (MOP 82.60bn/~$10.24bn from the 35% special tax) on assumed GGR of MOP 236bn (~$29.2bn).
  • 40% on Q3-26 GGR implies ~MOP 24.1bn (~$2.99bn) quarterly levies before premiums [derived].
  • 40% is already the highest headline casino tax among major jurisdictions; government leans on concessionaires for non-gaming investment instead.
  • MOP 118.8bn (~$14.7bn) pledged across six contracts, with +20% uplift once annual GGR exceeds MOP 180bn (~$22.3bn), triggered in 2024.

Direction of travel

No change to rates, caps or concession count before 2032; regime offers contractual certainty but no entry route.

  • 2027 Policy Address and budget (Nov 2026) will set a new GGR assumption and may revisit the foreign-player levy waiver.
  • Renewal of the profits-tax exemption beyond 2027.
  • DICJ scrutiny of non-gaming investment delivery and the post-satellite footprint.
  • Possible tightening of minimum-yield thresholds if GGR per table stays below 2019 levels.
  • For a supplier: six solvent, tax-paying buyers contractually obliged to keep investing.

7Deep-dive: Payments, AML, responsible gambling, advertising & enforcement

A cash-and-chip market inside China's capital-control perimeter, with enforcement intensity rising (gaming-related crime +12.3% in 9M-26) and no advertising runway.

Payments reality

Macau is a HKD-chip, cash-settled market inside China's capital-control perimeter with no licensed online wallet layer.

  • Mainland visitors face the US$50,000 annual individual quota, UnionPay card controls and Macau's cross-border cash declaration regime.
  • Law 7/2024 (effective Oct 2024) criminalised unlicensed currency exchange for gambling, targeting 'money-changer' gangs that funded premium play.
  • CreditSights' September note: funding channels already so tightly controlled that incremental Chinese capital-control tightening is unlikely to show up in GGR directly.
  • Casino credit only by concessionaires under the gaming-credit law (effective Aug 2024).
  • Digital-yuan or card-at-cage innovations not a feature of the regulated floor [status unverified in this session].

AML/KYC

Gaming-related crime rose 12.3% in 9M-26, arguing for continued aggressive enforcement rather than relaxation.

  • Concessionaires file large-transaction and suspicious-transaction reports to the Financial Intelligence Office (GIF) under DICJ AML instructions.
  • The 2022–2024 legislative package (gaming, junket, credit, illegal-gambling laws) shifted liability onto concessionaires and removed intermediaries.
  • Crime data from Macau police reported by IAG, combined with four months of GGR declines.

Responsible gambling

Entry and employment restricted to persons aged 21+ (Law 10/2012); DICJ runs self-exclusion and third-party exclusion schemes.

  • Smoking permitted only in enclosed smoking lounges with no gaming; full-floor ban since 2019.
  • Responsible gambling programme runs through DICJ and the Social Welfare Bureau.
  • Published 2026 self-exclusion or problem-gambling prevalence figures could not be verified in this session.

Advertising

Gambling advertising is heavily restricted and PRC Criminal Law Art. 303 means no concessionaire markets gaming to the mainland.

  • Art. 303 (amended 2021) criminalises organising or soliciting mainland residents for cross-border gambling.
  • Promotion is confined to non-gaming (concerts, MICE, F&B, hotels), where the 2H26 event calendar is used to pull premium visitation.

Commercial implication

The compliance perimeter is tighter than any Western market a B2B supplier would serve, but compliance technology is a growing procurement line.

  • Product must pass DICJ technical certification; payments innovation on the floor is effectively off-limits.
  • Any marketing or affiliate activity aimed at Chinese players is a criminal exposure.
  • Surveillance, KYC/STR automation, table-management and chip tracking are growing procurement lines for six well-funded buyers.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Slots are a structural growth line: GGR +18.8% YoY to MOP 4.09bn (~$0.51bn) in Q3-26 and ~+19% for 9M-26 (MOP 12.18bn/~$1.51bn), with share up to 6.8% — the one product the six concessionaires are actively expanding under a 12,000-machine cap, which sustains premium-cabinet replacement demand for EGM suppliers. structural
  • Demand volume is at record levels — 4.5m visitors in August (+6.1%), 93.9% hotel occupancy — so the market's problem is yield mix, not footfall; a stabilisation in Chinese high-end sentiment would flow straight to premium-mass GGR. mixed
  • Regulatory certainty is unusually high: the 35% + 5% = 40% GGR levy, premiums, inventory caps and six concessions are contractually fixed to 31 Dec 2032, and the profits-tax exemption runs to end-2027; there is no live proposal to raise rates. structural
  • The government's FY26 GGR assumption of MOP 236bn (~$29.2bn) needs only MOP 48.9bn (~$6.06bn) in Q4 — a very low bar — and gaming taxes are already +5.9% YoY at MOP 74.56bn (~$9.24bn), removing any fiscal pressure for a tax or premium increase. one-off
  • VIP has found a floor: +6.3% QoQ to MOP 16.91bn (~$2.10bn) and +9.9% for 9M-26, now run premium-direct by concessionaires with credit under their own control — a cleaner, higher-margin structure than the junket era. structural
  • The non-gaming calendar works: concerts at Galaxy and Venetian arenas visibly lifted premium visitation in early August, and MOP 118.8bn (~$14.7bn) of contractually pledged investment keeps the integrated-resort product improving through 2032. structural
  • Six solvent, tax-paying buyers with mandatory DICJ product certification create a concentrated but durable B2B channel for EGMs, ETGs, systems and compliance technology — the only realistic Macau entry for a non-concessionaire. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • Closed market: no new concession before 2033, sub-concessions abolished, satellites wound down, and no online licensing of any kind — there is no B2C route for an outside operator, and the sports-lottery is a single-concession monopoly. structural
  • Mass/premium-mass baccarat fell 7.9% YoY and 4.4% QoQ to MOP 33.61bn (~$4.17bn) with GGR per visitor down 7% to MOP 4,888 (~$606); the two-quarter sequential decline signals demand, not variance, and compresses operator EBITDA faster than headline GGR. mixed
  • Chinese policy overhang: tax enforcement on offshore trusts/wealth and capital-control tightening are cited by Seaport and CreditSights as drags on high-end demand; four consecutive monthly YoY declines (Jun–Sep) and FY26 growth consensus cut to ~1–3% from 7%. watch
  • The world's highest headline casino levy — 40% of GGR plus fixed/variable premiums, a 35%-of-shortfall minimum-yield premium (MOP 7m/~$0.87m per table) and MOP 118.8bn (~$14.7bn) of mandated non-gaming capex — leaves thin margin for error if GGR stays ~15% below 2019. structural
  • Enforcement intensity is rising, not falling: gaming-related crime +12.3% in 9M-26, criminalised currency exchange (Law 7/2024), concessionaire-only credit, and PRC criminal exposure for any marketing to mainland players — compliance cost and legal risk are structurally high for any vendor or partner. governance
  • Data definitions are unreliable for modelling: DICJ's 'VIP' (28.1% of GGR) is far above the concessionaires' own VIP disclosures, and trade-press 9M totals diverge (MOP 187.1bn vs a mislabelled 181.3bn); operator-level market shares are not published by the regulator. watch
  • Regional competition for the same Chinese premium customer is building — UAE (Wynn Al Marjan, 2027), Philippines and Singapore today, Thailand and Japan (Osaka, 2030) later — while Macau's product is 84% baccarat and cannot add online. structural

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The official FY26 anchor is the budget's MOP 236bn (~$29.2bn) GGR assumption, reaffirmed by Chief Executive Sam Hou Fai on 10 Oct pending October data; it is a fiscal floor rather than a forecast, since 9M-26 GGR of MOP 187.1bn (~$23.2bn) leaves only MOP 48.9bn (~$6.06bn) to find in Q4 against a Q4-25 base of ~MOP 66.1bn (~$8.19bn). The sell-side has moved the other way: J.P. Morgan now has FY26 at MOP 249bn (~$30.9bn, +1%), Seaport models Q4 −5%, and one bank has October −6% at MOP 22.6bn (~$2.80bn). The swing is entirely in mass/premium-mass baccarat — VIP is flat and slots are adding ~MOP 0.6bn (~$75m) a quarter YoY — and is driven by Chinese high-end demand (macro, offshore-wealth tax enforcement, capital controls) and tough hold comps rather than anything regulatory.

Independent view. Market: our base case is FY26 GGR of ~MOP 250bn (~$31.0bn), +1% (estimate), with Q4 down ~5% on tough hold comps and soft premium mass; a flat Q4 would give ~MOP 253bn (~$31.4bn, +2.3%) and −8% would give ~MOP 248bn (~$30.7bn, +0.2%). Comps ease materially from Q2-27 (World Cup base) and the 2027 calendar lacks a comparable sporting drag, so we expect a return to low-single-digit growth in 2027, still ~15% below 2019 — with slots compounding mid-teens and mass baccarat flat-to-down until Chinese wealth sentiment turns. Margins: operator EBITDA falls faster than GGR in 2H26 (CLSA/Goldman), and the 40% levy plus minimum-yield premiums make the government's take almost fully variable-proof; expect gaming tax of ~MOP 99–100bn (~$12.3–12.4bn) for FY26 (estimate, 40% × GGR), well above the MOP 92.53bn (~$11.47bn) budget. Rules: no change to the 35%/40% rate, the six concessions or the inventory caps before 2032; the 2027 Policy Address (Nov 2026) will reset the GGR assumption (likely ~MOP 240–250bn/~$29.7–31.0bn, estimate) and may expand the foreign-player levy waiver as a diversification tool; the profits-tax exemption is likely renewed beyond 2027. Enforcement direction is one-way tighter — expect further action on illegal exchange, cross-border online gambling and junket residue, and closer DICJ auditing of non-gaming investment delivery. For B2Tech: treat Macau as a B2B-only, slot-and-systems opportunity with six buyers, and as the demand bellwether for Chinese premium play across Asia — not as a licensing target.
