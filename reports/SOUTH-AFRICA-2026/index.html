2026 · market & regulatory brief

South Africa

Released Oct 2026 (NGB Annual Report 2025/26 tabled; MTBPS 21 Oct 2026; audited FY2024/25 statistics published 2 Oct 2025 / Apr 2026) · Currency ZAR (South African rand), tables in billions unless stated; ~USD alongside at USD/ZAR 16.6 · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

South Africa is now a R84.5bn (~$5.1bn) GGR market whose growth is entirely online bookmaking: betting is 70% of GGR, ~86% of it online, and the land-based estate is shrinking. The rules are moving against the online channel — a proposed 20% national tax on online GGR (decision Feb 2027), new advertising norms, and an NGB site-blocking programme — while interactive casino remains illegal and licensing stays provincial. For B2Tech the prize is real but the licence-to-tax arbitrage that built the market is closing.

1Executive summary

  • Market size: R84.46bn (~$5.09bn) GGR in FY2025/26 (1 Apr 2025–31 Mar 2026), +13.4% on the audited FY2024/25 figure of R74.50bn (~$4.49bn). Turnover (stakes, including recycled winnings) reached ~R1.76tn (~$106bn) vs R1.50tn (~$90bn). FY2025/26 figures come from the NGB Annual Report tabled in October 2026 as relayed by The Citizen/Focus Gaming News; Gauteng's contribution is flagged as provisional and unaudited.
  • Growth is decelerating but still double-digit. Audited GGR grew 36.9% (FY21/22), 25.7% (FY23/24) and 25.6% (FY24/25); FY25/26 slowed to ~13%. Post-COVID four-year average growth was 34.1% vs 5.9% pre-pandemic (NGB). Turnover growth also slowed from +30.7% to ~+17%.
  • Betting is the market: R51.97bn (~$3.13bn) GGR in FY24/25, 69.8% of the total and +44.7% YoY. Bookmaker sports/'other contingencies' alone was R47.89bn (~$2.89bn, +49.3%); bookmaker horse racing R3.52bn (~$212m, +12.3%); totalisators (4Racing, Gold Circle) R0.57bn and shrinking ~10% a year.
  • Online penetration: 86% of betting GGR and ~60% of all gambling GGR is online (NGB estimate; R44.46bn/~$2.68bn disclosed by six provinces). SARB's March 2026 Quarterly Bulletin independently reports online betting GGR of R44bn vs R29bn a year earlier (85.5% of betting revenue), corroborating the NGB figure. Because Gauteng, KZN and Free State do not disaggregate, the true online share is higher than 60%.
  • Land-based is in structural decline. Casino GGR fell 4.1% to R16.65bn (~$1.00bn, 22.3% share; 36 of a maximum 41 casinos operating after Mayfair closed in Jan 2025); bingo −8.6% to R1.73bn (~$104m); LPMs flat at R4.15bn (~$250m). Casino share of GGR has collapsed from 81.9% in FY10/11 to 22.2%.
  • Geography follows licences, not population: Western Cape (R23.1bn/~$1.39bn, 31%) and Mpumalanga (R22.2bn/~$1.34bn, 30%) now out-earn Gauteng (R13.5bn/~$0.81bn, 18%) because national-reach online bookmakers are licensed there. Mpumalanga books 54% of all betting turnover (R609bn/~$36.7bn) at a 3.5% hold; Gauteng's betting hold is 15% — a retail/horse-racing profile.
  • Licensing is provincial and concurrent (Constitution Sched. 4A; National Gambling Act 7 of 2004). Nine Provincial Licensing Authorities issue casino, bookmaker, totalisator, route/LPM and bingo licences; the NGB oversees norms, the national register of excluded persons and statistics. 315 bookmakers, 553 bookmaker outlets, 325 tote outlets, 2,546 LPM site operators (15,743 machines) and 73 bingo halls (13,452 EBTs) were operational at 31 Mar 2025. Bookmaker licence caps: Gauteng 131, KZN 48, Northern Cape 19; elsewhere unlimited 'subject to market demand'.
  • Online casino ('interactive gambling') remains illegal under s11 NGA; only online betting via a provincially licensed bookmaker is lawful. The 2008 Amendment Act that would have created national interactive licences was never proclaimed; the National Gambling Amendment Bill (B27-2018) is still in the NCOP process and the private-member Remote Gambling Bill (B11-2024) is stalled. In practice, bookmakers in WC/MP/LP offer casino-style 'fixed-odds' content (numbers, virtuals, crash games), which is the legal grey zone driving the numbers.
  • Tax regime today: provincial GGR taxes plus 15% VAT on GGR plus 27% CIT. Gauteng (GN 570/1997): casino 9%, bingo 12%, route/LPM 15%, totalisator 9% + 8.5% SDF levy, bookmakers 6.5% + 1% SDF levy. Western Cape: casinos 8–19% progressive, LPM 10–20%, bingo 10–15%, tote 6%, bookmakers 3% tax + 3% levy on winning bets. Industry (SABA) says online bookmakers 'typically' pay 6.5% of GGR; with VAT the effective burden is ~18–19% of GGR.
  • Tax take: R5.81bn (~$350m) in FY24/25 (+20.1%), i.e. 7.8% of GGR; R6.44bn (~$388m) in FY25/26 (+10.8%, 7.6%). Betting delivered 58.9% (R3.42bn/~$206m, effective 6.6%), casinos 29.5% (R1.72bn/~$103m, 10.3%), LPMs 9.0% (12.7%), bingo 2.5% (8.4%). Provinces depend on this: own-revenue is only ~3% of provincial budgets (Treasury).
  • The big swing factor: National Treasury's proposed 20% national tax on online betting and interactive GGR (discussion paper, Nov 2025). It would sit on top of provincial taxes, be administered by SARS, apply even to unlawful interactive revenue, and raise 'over R10bn' (~$0.6bn). Comments closed Feb 2026; the Minister told Parliament in the 21 Oct 2026 MTBPS that the final design comes in the Feb 2027 Budget, then a Money Bill. Combined burden would be 26–29% before VAT, ~38–40% with VAT (SABA, Whitesmans, Lurie Inc).
  • Operator landscape: a dozen scaled bookmakers, three casino groups. Hollywoodbets is widely regarded as the #1 online bookmaker [share unverified — NGB publishes no operator shares]; Betway (Super Group: Africa segment revenue $262m in Q3-26, +17%, 34% EBITDA margin — not SA-only), Supabets, Lottostar, World Sports Betting, Gbets, Playabets, Sportingbet, Easybet, Goldrush and SunBet (Sun International) are the other scaled brands. Casinos: Sun International, Tsogo Sun, Peermont. The National Lottery (Lotteries Act; NLC) sits outside NGB statistics.
  • Payments are bank-rail, not mobile-money. Instant EFT (Ozow, Capitec Pay, Stitch/SiD), debit cards and cash vouchers (1Voucher, OTT) dominate deposits; credit-card gambling is widely blocked by banks and exchange control forbids cards/forex for offshore gambling (Treasury). Gambling licensees are FICA 'accountable institutions' (KYC/AML). Minimum deposits of R10–R50 (secondary sources) underpin mass-market reach.
  • Participation is the highest in the world: 32.3% of South African internet users aged 16+ visited a gambling site/app in the past month (GWI Digital 2026, #1 of 48 markets; UK 19.6%, US 10.2%). Betting is 1.6% of the CPI basket, the 12th-largest weight (Treasury/StatsSA).
  • Responsible gambling and advertising are tightening. Industry contributed R57.4m (~$3.5m) to the National Responsible Gambling Foundation in FY25/26; the NRGF counselling line took 83,541 calls (5,443 treatment-related; 3,541 people treated). The dtic promised advertising 'norms and standards' and regulations before July 2026 [publication not verified]; the ARB consulted on a tougher Gambling Code (comments 28 Feb 2026) and signed an enforcement MoU with the NGB in Sept 2026.
  • Enforcement is escalating against offshore/illegal online casino. Government 'declared war' on illegal online gambling (Jul 2026); the NGB issued an EOI (30 Jun 2026, extended to 4 Sep 2026) for a provider to monitor and IP-block illegal sites; s16 NGA lets banks confiscate illegal winnings to the NGB. Illegal online gambling is said to drain >R50bn (~$3bn) a year (ITWeb/CNBC Africa) — basis unverified.
  • Employment and transformation: 33,169 direct jobs (−3% YoY) in FY24/25; FY25/26 casinos 13,328, bookmakers 12,230. B-BBEE is measured on the generic scorecard (casino operators average Level 1.7, LPM 2.4, bingo 3.1); provincial boards weigh empowerment in licence awards.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: enter via a provincial bookmaker licence (WC or MP are the proven national-reach hubs), build on 'fixed-odds' content and instant-EFT rails, and underwrite the business at a 38–40% effective GGR tax. A 20% national tax plus advertising limits would compress promo-led growth and favour scale incumbents with retail estates and low CAC; it also raises the odds that the eventual national interactive regime arrives with a high tax attached rather than as a liberalisation.

3Deep-dive: Sports betting & online bookmaking

R52bn (~$3.1bn) and 70% of GGR; 86% online, concentrated in Western Cape and Mpumalanga licences; the whole market's growth and the whole of Treasury's target.

Betting GGR growth

Betting GGR was R51.97bn (~$3.13bn) in the year to 31 March 2025, up 44.7% on R35.91bn, the only mode to grow.

  • Bookmaker sports betting R47.89bn (~$2.89bn, +49.3%), continuing 57%, 61%, 55% and 49% annual growth since FY2021/22.
  • Bookmaker horse racing R3.52bn (~$212m, +12.3%); totalisators (4Racing in eight provinces, Gold Circle in KZN) shrank again to R0.57bn (~$34m).
  • Betting turnover R1,125.6bn (~$67.8bn), 75% of all stakes, implying blended hold of 4.6% and 4.3% on bookmaker sports.
  • Low hold signals volume dominated by high-frequency products (numbers/lottery betting, virtuals, crash-style 'fixed-odds' games) rather than match betting.
  • Quarterly GGR R11.6bn, R12.1bn, R14.6bn, R13.7bn; Q4 decelerated to +41% YoY from +50% in Q3; FY25/26 betting growth likely mid-to-high teens [estimate] as total market slowed to 13.4%.

Online channel split

Online betting GGR in six disaggregating provinces was R44.46bn (~$2.68bn), 86% of betting GGR and 59.7% of total gambling GGR.

  • Reserve Bank March 2026 Quarterly Bulletin independently puts online betting at R44bn vs R29bn a year earlier (85.5% of betting revenue).
  • Gauteng, KZN and Free State do not split data and are booked as retail, so the NGB's '60% of all gambling is online' is a floor.
  • Mpumalanga booked R21.33bn (41%) on R609bn turnover (3.5% hold); Western Cape R19.52bn (37%) on R343bn (5.7%).
  • Gauteng, with 140 bookmakers, took only R4.66bn (9%) at a 15% hold, a retail and horse-racing profile.
  • Betting taxes/levies R3.42bn (~$206m), 59% of gambling tax, effective 6.6% of GGR; Mpumalanga (41%) and Western Cape (36%) collected the bulk.

Supply and operators

Supply is consolidating as revenue grows: operational bookmakers fell from 349 to 315 (−7.1%) and employment slipped from 12,899 to 12,230 in FY25/26.

  • Outlets held at roughly 550–600 and totalisator outlets at 325.
  • NGB publishes no operator shares; Hollywoodbets generally regarded as largest, with Betway (Super Group), Supabets, Lottostar, World Sports Betting, Gbets, Playabets, Sportingbet, Easybet, Goldrush and SunBet [shares unverified].
  • Super Group's Africa segment grew revenue 17% to $262m in Q3-26 at a 34% adjusted EBITDA margin, and $839m (+29%) over nine months.
  • Market behaviour is promo-heavy (sign-up bonuses, free bets, wagering requirements), where a 20% national tax would bite first.

Entrant logic

Entry case is a bookmaker licence in Western Cape or Mpumalanga, underwritten at a 38–40% effective GGR tax with WC/MP holds as margin ceiling.

  • Content stack weighted to numbers, virtuals and live 'bet-games' plus a conventional sportsbook, with instant-EFT/voucher cashiering for small frequent stakes.
  • Counter-case: product mix sits in a legal grey zone that dtic and Treasury describe as 'interactive gambling remains illegal'.
  • Treasury explicitly wants to end provinces competing on headline tax; advertising restrictions are coming.
  • Assume promotional intensity falls.

4Deep-dive: Land-based: casinos, LPMs & bingo

R22.5bn (~$1.36bn) of GGR shrinking 3–4% a year; a capped, high-tax, high-RTP estate that still supplies 41% of provincial gambling tax.

Casinos

Casinos generated R16.65bn (~$1.00bn) GGR in FY2024/25, down 4.1% after a flat FY2023/24, on turnover of R292.8bn (~$17.6bn, −1.5%).

  • Every quarter was below prior year (Q4 R4.01bn vs R4.31bn); 36 of a maximum 41 casinos operating after Mayfair (Eastern Cape) closed January 2025.
  • 21,370 slots and 890 tables; Gauteng hosts seven casinos, 8,226 slots and 45% of casino GGR (R7.49bn/~$451m), then KZN (R3.55bn, 21%) and Western Cape (R2.70bn, 16%).
  • Average return-to-player 94% (North West 87–88%); casino taxes R1.72bn (~$103m), effective 10.3%, reflecting Gauteng's 9% flat rate and Western Cape's 8–19% scale.
  • NGB FY25/26 report calls casino, bingo and LPM revenue 'relatively flat'; casino headcount fell from 13,759 to 13,328.
  • Sun International, Tsogo Sun and Peermont own the bulk; NGB does not publish operator shares.

Limited payout machines

LPMs are the most resilient land-based product: R4.15bn (~$250m) GGR (−0.04%) on R54.7bn turnover across 2,546 site operators and 15,743 machines.

  • Route-operated slots in bars, taverns and bingo halls, capped at five per site with low stakes/prizes.
  • Taxed at effective 12.7% (R525m/~$32m); Gauteng's route-operator rate is 15%. Western Cape (22%) and KZN (21%) lead.
  • Route-operator licences capped at two in Eastern Cape, five in Gauteng and two each in Northern and Western Cape, entrenching incumbents (e.g., Vukani/Galaxy, Goldrush).

Bingo and totalisators

Bingo produced R1.73bn (~$104m) GGR, down 8.6%, from 73 outlets and 13,452 EBTs across six provinces.

  • Taxes R146m (~$8.8m, 8.4% effective; 12% headline in Gauteng).
  • Western Cape, Free State and Northern Cape have 'no roll-out currently'; Gauteng caps bingo at 11 licences.
  • Totalisator horse racing has declined every year since FY2018/19 and is now R337m (~$20m).

Relevance to operators

Land-based matters as acquisition channel, political benchmark and partner pool rather than as a growth vehicle.

  • Retail outlets and casino floors remain the cheapest customer-acquisition channel for an online brand, more so if advertising is curbed.
  • Casino licences carry socio-economic obligations and B-BBEE scrutiny (operators average Level 1.7); Treasury explicitly contrasts this with 'few local benefits' online operators.
  • Casino groups are natural partners or acquirers for entrants needing a provincial licence and local balance sheet.

5Deep-dive: Licensing & legal framework

A 2004 statute, nine provincial regulators, an unproclaimed 2008 online law and two stalled bills: lawful online = betting via a provincial bookmaker licence; interactive casino = illegal.

Regulatory structure

Gambling is a concurrent national–provincial competence: the NGA 2004 sets norms, the NGB oversees, and nine provincial authorities license exclusively.

  • Provincial Licensing Authorities license casinos, bookmakers, totalisators, route/LPM operators and bingo under their own provincial Acts.
  • NGB (under dtic) keeps the national register of excluded persons, monitors conduct and statistics, and leads illegal-gambling enforcement.
  • National Lottery regulated separately by the National Lotteries Commission under the Lotteries Act 1997; operator licence moved from Ithuba to Sizekhaya Holdings in 2025/26 [secondary].

Online legality

Online betting via a provincial bookmaker licence is lawful; interactive casino is illegal under s11 NGA, with the 2008 online law never proclaimed.

  • National Gambling Amendment Act 10 of 2008 would have created national interactive licences but has no legal effect.
  • Amendment Bill B27-2018 passed the National Assembly in 2018, still in NCOP mandate process late 2025, public participation mooted February 2026 [status as of Nov 2026 unverified].
  • Remote Gambling Bill B11-2024 lapsed with the sixth Parliament and has not progressed.
  • Western Cape, Mpumalanga and Limpopo license bookmakers to accept online bets nationally and offer fixed-odds on numbers, virtuals and live games: legally 'betting', economically 'gaming'.

Licence caps

Licence supply is capped by product and province: 41 casinos nationally, bookmakers capped at 131 in Gauteng, 48 in KZN, 19 in Northern Cape.

  • Casino caps: EC 5, FS 4, GP 7, KZN 5, LP 3, MP 4, NW 4+1 pending, NC 3, WC 5.
  • Route-operator licences 2 (EC), 5 (GP), 2 (NC), 2 (WC), no cap elsewhere; totalisators one per province except KZN (two); bingo 15 (EC), 11 (GP), no roll-out in WC, FS, NC.
  • Bookmaker licences unlimited in other provinces subject to demand and viability; granted after probity, financial, B-BBEE and socio-economic review with public objection periods.
  • Fees not verified [n/a]; no server-location rule, but SARB exchange control and s16 NGA ring-fence payments domestically.

Strategic implications

The framework rewards early licences in 'open' provinces; interactive-casino upside should not be in a base case.

  • Expect 6–12 months [estimate] for a bookmaker licence, a local company with meaningful black ownership, and products vetted by the licensing province.
  • Eventual national online regime (2018 Bill, revived remote bill, or Treasury tax instrument) is being designed for harm reduction, not liberalisation.

6Deep-dive: Tax regime & the proposed 20% national online gambling tax

Today ~18–19% of online GGR all-in (6.5% provincial + VAT); Treasury wants +20% on top, worth >R10bn (~$0.6bn), with the design due in the Feb 2027 Budget.

Current tax regime

Provincial gambling tax was R5.81bn (~$350m) in FY24/25 (7.8% of GGR) and R6.44bn (~$388m) in FY25/26 (7.6%).

  • Provinces levy and keep GGR taxes; they raise only ~3% of budgets from own revenue.
  • Gauteng (GN 570 of 1997): casinos 9%, bingo 12%, route/LPM 15%, totalisators 9% plus 8.5% levy, bookmakers 6.5% plus 1% levy, horse-racing bets 6% plus 6%.
  • Western Cape: casinos 8% (below R14.2m) to 19% (above R71.0m), LPMs 10–20%, bingo 10–15%, totalisator 6%, bookmakers 3% tax plus 3% levy on winning bets.
  • Treasury characterises bookmakers at 6–7% (elsewhere 6–9%) and casinos 10–15%; KZN, Mpumalanga, Limpopo rates not verified [n/a].
  • Plus 15% VAT on GGR (effective 12–13% per SABA), 27% corporate tax and skills levy; recreational winnings untaxed. Effective rates: betting 6.6%, casinos 10.3%, LPMs 12.7%, bingo 8.4%.

Treasury proposal

Treasury proposes a 20% national GGR tax on online betting including interactive gambling, on top of provincial tax, worth 'over R10 billion' (~$0.6bn) a year.

  • Discussion paper 'The case for a national online gambling tax' published Nov 2025; comments closed Feb 2026; collected by SARS using existing provincial returns.
  • Applies to locally based operators' interactive revenue 'to the extent' earned even though interactive gambling is illegal.
  • Aims: internalise social cost of problem gambling, stop provinces competing on rates, and raise roughly 1.7x the current provincial take.
  • Benchmarks 11 jurisdictions at 20% and 16 above; cites UK (RGD 21%, GBD 15%), Australian POCTs (15–25%), and Kenya's repealed 2019–20 20% stake excise.

Industry response and timing

Industry puts the combined rate at 26.5% pre-VAT and 38–40% all-in; final design due in the February 2027 Budget, earliest effect FY2027/28 [estimate].

  • SABA, Whitesmans Attorneys, Lurie Inc/SAROGA warn of offshore migration, provincial revenue erosion and job losses; Treasury's own figure is 26–29% pre-VAT.
  • Constitutional challenge flagged: a national tax on a provincially licensed activity must proceed as a s77 Money Bill.
  • Godongwana's 21 October 2026 MTBPS confirmed consultations ongoing, with legislation to follow the Budget announcement.
“the tax design, its linkages to the existing regulatory framework, and the implications for provinces and the currently regulated market”Finance Minister Godongwana

Commercial implications

A pure-online model priced for 18–19% effective tax would lose roughly a fifth of gross margin; stress-test entry at 38–40% all-in.

  • At Betway-type Africa EBITDA margins of ~34% the hit is survivable for scale players but passed through as lower bonuses, tighter odds and higher wagering requirements.
  • ~4–6% hold products trimmed hardest; smaller licensees already exiting (315 bookmakers from 349) would consolidate.
  • Upside: Treasury lands at 10–15% or offsets VAT; downside: 20% plus a VAT-inclusive base.
  • Assume provincial headline competition, the arbitrage that made WC and MP hubs, disappears.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & enforcement

Bank-rail payments and world-leading participation meet a 2026 tightening cycle: advertising norms, an NGB–ARB enforcement pact and an IP-blocking programme against offshore casino.

Payments

The cashier stack is domestic bank rails and retail vouchers, with exchange control and s16 NGA deterring offshore play.

  • Instant EFT via Ozow, Capitec Pay and Stitch/SiD, Visa/Mastercard debit, 1Voucher and OTT vouchers, ATM and retail-counter deposits; minimum deposits R10–R50 per secondary guides.
  • Credit-card gambling widely declined; Authorised Dealers must refuse foreign-currency and card payments for offshore gambling.
  • s16 NGA obliges banks to withhold unlawful winnings and remit to the NGB in trust, a real if patchily enforced deterrent.
  • Licensees are FICA accountable institutions: KYC, source-of-funds checks, FIC reporting, payouts only to verified accounts in the player's name.

Participation and harm

South Africa ranks first of 48 markets with 32.3% of internet users aged 16+ visiting a gambling site monthly (GWI Digital 2026).

  • Comparators: Ghana 21.5%, UK 19.6%, Nigeria 18%, Kenya 16%, US 10.2%; a measure of visits, not spend. Betting is 1.6% of the CPI basket, 12th-largest weight.
  • Protections: minimum age 18, credit ban, national excluded-persons register, mandatory problem-gambling messaging.
  • NRGF received R57.4m (~$3.5m) in FY25/26: 1.26m helpline contacts, 83,541 counselling calls, 5,443 treatment-related, 3,541 treated.
  • Survey reporting (Yazi, stokvel studies) and parliamentary questions on gambling-linked suicide risk have made harm the dominant political frame.

Advertising

Advertising rules are moving fastest; expect restrictions on timing, media, bonuses and sponsorship within 12 months rather than a whistle-to-whistle ban [view].

  • Section 15 NGA and 2004 regulations already prohibit targeting minors, false claims and ads without responsible-gambling warnings.
  • dtic told Parliament in February 2026 it would publish 'norms and standards' and regulations before July 2026 [publication not verified as of 11 Nov 2026].
  • ARB consulted on Gambling Code amendments (comments due 28 Feb 2026); NGB–ARB memorandum of understanding formalised September 2026.
  • A betting operator publicly apologised in October 2026 after a promotion reached a preschool.

Enforcement

Enforcement is a stated government priority, including an NGB programme to identify, monitor and block illegal gambling websites.

  • NGB chairs an Illegal Gambling Task Team with SAPS and provincial boards and has run joint raids on illegal machines.
  • Expression of Interest issued 30 June 2026 (deadline extended to 4 September 2026) for a specialist website-blocking provider, with evidence for prosecution under the NGA.
  • Ministers 'declared war' on illegal online gambling in July 2026, citing unlicensed sites draining over R50bn (~$3bn) a year; basis unverified, read as advocacy.
  • Offshore casino brands face rising payment and DNS friction; licensed operators' compliance will be audited more actively by ARB and PLAs as the political reward for being 'licensed and responsible' rises alongside tax cost.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Scale and momentum: R84.5bn (~$5.1bn) GGR growing 13% even after a 26% year, with ~60%+ online — the largest regulated online betting pool in Africa and one of the deepest per-capita anywhere (32.3% monthly participation). structural
  • Lawful national-reach online betting under a single provincial bookmaker licence, with unlimited licence supply in WC/MP/LP/EC/FS and a permissive 'fixed-odds' content envelope (numbers, virtuals, live games) that monetises like casino at 6.5% provincial tax. structural
  • Mature payments and identity rails: instant EFT, Capitec Pay, vouchers and FICA-grade KYC let an operator reach banked and unbanked customers at R10–R50 tickets without mobile-money dependency or operator-borne wallet float. structural
  • Land-based decline (casino −4.1%, bingo −8.6%) is pushing incumbent casino groups toward online partnerships and selling retail-acquisition capacity cheaply — a route to licences, B-BBEE-compliant local structures and low-CAC distribution. mixed
  • Consolidation among bookmakers (349 → 315 operational) and a coming tax/advertising squeeze favour scaled, well-capitalised entrants able to acquire sub-scale licensees rather than build greenfield. early
  • Enforcement against offshore casino (IP blocking, bank withholding of winnings, ARB–NGB pact) raises the value of a licensed position: the >R50bn (~$3bn) 'illegal' pool, whatever its true size, is addressable only by licensees. watch

▼ Lowlights

  • Treasury's proposed 20% national tax on online GGR would lift the all-in burden from ~18–19% to 38–40%; final design in the Feb 2027 Budget, likely effective FY2027/28 [estimate]. Promo-led growth and 4–6% hold products are most exposed. structural
  • Interactive casino remains illegal (NGA s11; 2008 Act unproclaimed; B27-2018 and B11-2024 stalled): the casino-style 'fixed-odds' content underpinning WC/MP revenue sits in a grey zone that a national regulator or SARS could re-characterise. governance
  • Advertising restrictions (dtic norms and standards, ARB Gambling Code, NGB–ARB MoU) are arriving in a harm-dominated political climate; expect limits on bonus advertising, timing and sports visibility that raise CAC for new brands. structural
  • Growth is decelerating (GGR +25.6% → +13.4%; turnover +30.7% → ~+17%) and the NRGF/survey evidence of harm (5,443 treatment-related calls; stokvel and grocery-money studies) gives regulators cover for stake, deposit or product limits. watch
  • Provincial fragmentation: nine regulators, differing tax bases (GP 6.5%+1%; WC 3%+3% on winning bets), caps on bookmaker licences in GP/KZN/NC and unverified licence fees; Treasury explicitly intends to end inter-provincial rate competition. governance
  • Data quality: Gauteng's FY25/26 numbers are provisional; three provinces do not split online/retail; the NGB's own text contradicts its tables on outlet counts and casino shares — model on audited FY24/25 figures and treat FY25/26 as indicative. one-off
  • Macro and FX: a rand at ~16.6/USD, 15% VAT and weak consumer incomes mean USD-reported returns are volatile and the political optics of R1.76tn 'wagered' (turnover, not spend — as SARB itself cautioned) keep the sector a fiscal target. mixed

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The regulatory 'bridge' from the audited FY2024/25 base to today runs through four moving parts. The NGB's 2025/26 Annual Report (tabled Oct 2026) lifts the market from R74.5bn (~$4.49bn) to R84.46bn (~$5.09bn) GGR and provincial taxes from R5.81bn to R6.44bn (~$388m), with growth slowing from 26% to 13% and still entirely online-betting-led. Against that base, National Treasury's 20% national tax on online and interactive GGR (worth >R10bn/~$0.6bn) moved from discussion paper (Nov 2025) through consultation (closed Feb 2026) to a confirmed design announcement in the February 2027 Budget (MTBPS, 21 Oct 2026). In parallel the dtic promised advertising norms and standards (by Jul 2026; publication unverified), the NGB tendered for site-blocking (EOI Jun–Sep 2026) and signed an enforcement MoU with the ARB (Sep 2026), while the structural fix — a national interactive-gambling licence via the 2018 Amendment Bill or a remote-gambling bill — has not advanced.

Independent view. Our base case is that the market reaches ~R92–95bn (~$5.5–5.7bn) GGR in FY2026/27 (+9–12%) [estimate], with betting passing 72% of GGR and online passing 65%, as sports-betting growth normalises toward the mid-teens and land-based drifts 2–4% lower; provincial tax take rises to ~R7.0bn (~$420m) [estimate]. On tax, we expect Treasury to confirm a national online GGR tax in February 2027 but at a rate and base softened from the paper — most likely 10–15% with transitional relief or VAT interaction addressed — effective no earlier than FY2027/28 [view]; a straight 20% on top of VAT would, on Treasury's own Kenya analysis, push volume offshore and cut the provincial base it needs. The legislative response (B27-2018 or a successor) is more likely to be reshaped around the tax than to legalise interactive casino within two years. Advertising rules will tighten first (bonus/inducement advertising, time-of-day, sports kit), raising CAC and favouring brands with retail estates. Operator margins in the online segment, currently supported by ~34% regional EBITDA margins at the leader, should compress by roughly 10–15 points under a 20% scenario and 5–8 points under a 10–15% scenario [estimate], driving further consolidation among the 315 licensed bookmakers. Leverage in the sector is low and self-funded; the risk is regulatory, not balance-sheet. Swing factors: the February 2027 rate; whether SARS or the provinces administer it; the published scope of 'interactive' revenue; the dtic advertising regulations; and the NGB blocking programme's effectiveness against offshore casino.
