# Michigan () — SEPTEMBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released 22 Oct 2026**

> Michigan's regulated online market booked $355.7m of combined iGaming + online sports betting gross receipts in September 2026 (+17.5% YoY, +5.4% MoM) with iGaming ($300.2m GR / $288.2m AGR, +18.4% YoY) doing almost all of the work and OSB AGR up 88% on an easy, promo-heavy comp. The rules are stable today but every swing factor sits in 2027: the iGaming free-play deduction falls to zero on 1 Jan 2027, a new governor inherits a structural budget gap that the outgoing administration tried to plug with a 36% top iGaming tier and a per-bet levy, and the market remains closed to new entrants absent a Detroit-casino or tribal skin.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market size (online): $355.7m of combined internet gaming and online sports betting gross receipts in September 2026**, per the Michigan Gaming Control Board (MGCB) release of 22 Oct 2026 — +5.4% on August ($337.6m) and +17.5% on September 2025 ($302.7m). On the taxable (adjusted gross receipts, AGR) basis the market was $313.0m, +22.0% YoY. Jan–Sep 2026 online GR is $3,175.7m (+18.9% YoY).
- **iGaming is the engine: $300.2m GR and $288.2m AGR in September (+15.9% / +18.4% YoY).** The −0.9% MoM print is purely calendar (30 vs 31 days); AGR per day rose 2.4% to $9.6m. YTD iGaming AGR is $2,600.9m (+23.3%), and the trailing-12-month (Oct-25 to Sep-26) AGR is $3,392.6m — Michigan is tracking to the first $3.5bn+ iGaming AGR year in any US state.
- **OSB is mature and promo-driven: handle $544.8m (+3.9% YoY), GR $55.5m (+27.3% YoY) on a 10.2% hold, AGR $24.9m (+88.2% YoY).** The AGR jump reflects promo deductions falling to 55% of GR from 70% a year ago; YTD OSB AGR is actually down 6.9% to $242.8m because promo deductions ran at 48% of GR YTD vs 39% in 2025. Handle growth of 5.8% YTD says the sportsbook customer base is largely built out.
- **Online dominates the MGCB-reported market: 77% of September's $459.3m of reported gross gaming revenue** (iGaming $300.2m + OSB $55.5m + Detroit slots/tables $102.8m + Detroit retail sportsbook $0.8m). Caveat: Michigan's 23 tribal land-based casinos do not publish revenue and the Michigan Lottery (incl. iLottery) sits outside the MGCB, so true online penetration of all gambling is lower — n/a.
- **Retail is flat-to-low-growth: Detroit's three casinos reported $102.8m of slots/table AGR (+4.7% YoY, +2.3% YTD)**, split MGM Grand 47% / MotorCity 32% / Hollywood Casino at Greektown 21%; retail sportsbook QAGR was just $0.77m on $12.4m handle — the on-property book is a rounding error at 1.4% of OSB AGR.
- **Tax regime (iGaming): graduated 20–28% of AGR** (28% marginal above $12m annual AGR — every one of the 15 operators sits in the top tier), plus a 1.25% City of Detroit wagering tax for the three commercial licensees (29.25% all-in). The tax base is GR less a capped free-play deduction: 4% of GR in 2026 (year 5), and **zero from 1 January 2027** under MCL 432.303(a) — a structural ~1.1pt-of-GR increase in effective tax (~$40m/yr at a $3.6bn GR run-rate; our estimate).
- **Tax regime (OSB): 8.4% of AGR** (+1.25% city for Detroit casinos), with promotional/free-play deductions **uncapped** — the reason state OSB tax was only $1.8m in September on $55.5m of GR (3.3% of GR; 7.4% of AGR). Fantasy contests are taxed at 8.4% of adjusted revenue; Detroit casinos pay 8.1% state + 10.9% city on slots/tables.
- **Tax take: operators paid $62.4m to the State in September (iGaming $60.6m, OSB $1.8m; +20.9% YoY)**, $14.0m to the City of Detroit (iGaming $13.5m, OSB $0.55m) and $8.3m to tribal local governing bodies. YTD state online receipts are $557.5m (+24.7%); fiscal-year 2026 (Oct-25 to Sep-26) iGaming state tax/payments reached $703.9m and OSB $31.0m (our aggregation of MGCB monthly files). Remaining Internet Gaming Fund balances flow to the School Aid Fund.
- **Licensing is a closed, skin-limited system: 15 operator licences (3 Detroit casinos + 12 federally recognised tribes), one platform each**; all 15 offer iGaming and 13 offer OSB. Fees under the Lawful Internet Gaming Act / Lawful Sports Betting Act are modest — $50,000 application, $100,000 initial licence, $50,000 annual (5-year term); suppliers $5,000/$5,000/$2,500 — but there is no path to market without displacing an incumbent platform partner.
- **Operator landscape (iGaming AGR share, Sep-26): FanDuel 24.7%, BetMGM 21.1%, DraftKings 14.9%, BetRivers 7.5%, Fanatics 6.7%, Hard Rock Bet 6.4%, Caesars/WSOP 5.2%**, with Golden Nugget, theScore Bet, bet365, BetPARX, FireKeepers (NeoGames), Soaring Eagle (GAN), Caesars Horseshoe and Four Winds (Strive) making up the remaining 13.6%. Top-3 concentration is 60.7%, down from 67.9% a year ago as Hard Rock Bet (relaunched on the Hannahville licence in Dec-25), Fanatics and bet365 took share from FanDuel (−2.2pt) and BetMGM (−4.3pt).
- **OSB is a duopoly-plus: FanDuel 34.2% and DraftKings 28.2% of September handle (62.4% combined), BetMGM 10.4%, Fanatics 9.0%, bet365 5.5%.** Promo intensity is extreme at the challengers — bet365 deducted 129% of its GR (negative AGR of −$1.3m) and Hard Rock Bet 93%, versus 51–53% at FanDuel/DraftKings and 36% at BetMGM.
- **Tribal vs commercial: tribal-licensed skins generated 51.6% of September iGaming AGR ($148.6m vs $139.6m).** Tribal payments are routed 70% Internet Gaming Fund / 10% Michigan Strategic Fund / 20% local governing body; commercial tax goes 65% IGF / 5% Agriculture Equine Industry Development Fund (capped $3m) / 30% City of Detroit, and the IGF carries a first-call guarantee to top up Detroit if the city's gaming tax receipts fall below $183m.
- **Fiscal politics are the key regulatory risk and they just reset.** Gov. Whitmer's FY2027 executive budget (11 Feb 2026) proposed a 36% top iGaming tier above $185m AGR, a $0.25/$0.50 per-wager OSB levy (~$38.8m) and elimination of the OSB promo deduction (~$21.1m) — ~$195–200m in total. The Senate stripped all three in May 2026 (bipartisan) and the FY2027 budget was signed on 21 Jul 2026 with no gaming-tax change. Whitmer is term-limited; preliminary results show Jocelyn Benson (D) winning the 3 Nov 2026 governor's race, and the FY2028 executive budget (Feb 2027) is the next test.
- **Enforcement is the most aggressive of any US state on prediction markets.** The Attorney General sued Kalshi under the Lawful Sports Betting Act in March 2026, won a TRO (29 Jun, $120k/day) and a preliminary injunction (2 Sep); Robinhood (Sep), Coinbase (7 Oct) and Polymarket/QCX LLC (28 Oct, positions closed by 25 Nov 2026) have all agreed to halt sports event contracts in Michigan. MGCB also issued 32 cease-and-desist letters to offshore sites on 8 Oct 2026 and treats sweepstakes casinos as illegal gaming under MCL 432.
- **Product scope is widening at the margin: MGCB added fixed-odds horse racing to the OSB catalogue on 30 Oct 2026**, allowing licensed online books to offer fixed-price race bets alongside pari-mutuel — modest handle but a signal that the regulator is willing to expand the catalogue administratively rather than legislatively.
- **Responsible gambling framework is statutory and funded:** 21+ age, geolocation, self-exclusion via the Internet Gaming and Sports Betting Responsible Gaming Database (plus the Disassociated Persons List for casinos), free Gamban licences for residents, 1-800-GAMBLER on all materials, and $500,000 a year each from the Internet Gaming and Internet Sports Betting Funds to the Compulsive Gaming Prevention Fund. Payment-method statistics are not published by MGCB (n/a).
- **Read-through for an operator weighing Michigan:** this is the deepest, most profitable iGaming pool in the US (c.$3.4bn TTM AGR, 28% headline tax with no city levy for tribal skins), but entry is only via acquiring or replacing an incumbent platform on one of 15 licences; the 2027 free-play sunset and a probable renewed tax push argue for underwriting at ≥30% effective iGaming tax and for OSB as a customer-acquisition channel rather than a profit centre (8.4% nominal, but 48% of GR is being handed back in promos YTD).
- **Benchmark check:** the lead trade-press article (Gaming Intelligence, 27 Oct 2026) headlines the same $355.7m / +18% figure; our YoY of +17.5% uses the MGCB's September 2025 release ($302.7m) rather than rounded figures. All headline numbers reconcile to the MGCB operator-level workbooks to within $0.1m.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — iGaming (online casino & poker)

*$288.2m AGR, +18.4% YoY: the largest and fastest-compounding regulated iGaming pool in the US, with share drifting from the FanDuel/BetMGM duopoly to Hard Rock, Fanatics and bet365.*

### September revenue

**September AGR was $288.2m (+18.4% YoY) on $300.2m GR, the first time Michigan iGaming cleared $300m GR in a 30-day month.**

- The −0.9% MoM is a calendar artefact: August had 31 days; per-day AGR rose 2.4% to $9.6m.
- YoY +18.4% is above August's +17.6% and the Detroit land-based market (+4.7%); cannibalisation arguments remain unsupported, retail is growing slowly.
- YTD AGR is $2,600.9m (+23.3%) versus $2,109.6m in Jan–Sep 2025; calendar 2025 closed at $2,901.3m AGR and $3,087.0m GR.
- The Oct-25 to Sep-26 fiscal year reached $3,392.6m AGR and $3,552.7m GR.
- On the current run-rate calendar 2026 AGR lands around $3.5–3.6bn (our estimate, not an MGCB figure).

### Free-play sunset

**The free-play deduction falls to nil from 1 January 2027, lifting effective tax ~1.1 points of GR, roughly $40m a year, without any vote.**

- AGR equals GR less free play; the Lawful Internet Gaming Act caps the deduction at 10% of GR years 1–3, 6% year 4, 4% year 5, 0% year 6 (MCL 432.303(a)).
- Year 1 is the January following launch; for the January-2021 cohort that means 6% in 2025 (observed 6.1% Sep-25), 4% in 2026 (observed 4.0% Sep-26).
- At 28% marginal tax the sunset adds ~1.1 points of GR, roughly $40m a year at a $3.6bn GR run-rate (our estimate).
- Operators relying on free play will absorb the cost or cut bonusing; expect the GR-to-AGR gap to collapse to zero in the January 2027 release.

### Tax paid

**Tax paid to the State was $60.6m in September (+19.3% YoY) and $538.7m YTD (+24.6%); total burden including the city levy was $82.4m.**

- Commercial AGR $139.6m carried a 28% liability of $39.1m: 70% ($27.4m) to the State (65% Internet Gaming Fund + 5% Agriculture Equine Industry Development Fund).
- Detroit received 30% ($11.7m) plus the 1.25% city wagering tax ($1.7m), matching the reported $13.5m.
- Tribal AGR $148.6m carried $41.6m: 80% to the State ($33.3m: 70% IGF + 10% Michigan Strategic Fund), 20% ($8.3m) to tribes' local governing bodies.
- Total $82.4m equals 28.6% of AGR and 27.5% of GR, the second-highest effective iGaming rate among US states after Pennsylvania.
- There is no slot/table differential and no separate poker rate.

### Market share

**Share is fragmenting at the top: FanDuel and BetMGM lost share YoY to Hard Rock Bet, Fanatics and bet365.**

- FanDuel (MotorCity) led with $71.3m AGR / 24.7% (vs 26.9% Sep-25); BetMGM (MGM Grand Detroit) $60.8m / 21.1% (vs 25.4%); DraftKings (Bay Mills) $43.0m / 14.9% (vs 15.6%).
- Hard Rock Bet ceased on the Hannahville licence July 2025, resumed December 2025 booking $33.6m AGR in its first month and $18.6m / 6.4% in September.
- Fanatics (Lac Vieux Desert, ex-PointsBet) $19.2m / 6.7% (vs 5.4%); bet365 (Little Traverse Bay Bands) $6.3m / 2.2% (vs 0.7% under the prior platform).
- BetRivers (Little River) steady at $21.7m / 7.5%; Caesars/WSOP (Grand Traverse) slipped to $14.9m / 5.2%, down 10.7% MoM.
- Tail: Golden Nugget $9.2m, theScore Bet (Greektown, replacing ESPN BET/Barstool) $7.5m, BetPARX $4.2m, FireKeepers/NeoGames $4.2m, Soaring Eagle/GAN $3.4m, Caesars Horseshoe (Sault Ste. Marie, replacing Wynn) $2.3m, Four Winds/Strive $1.6m; all clear the $12m threshold and pay 28%.

### Commercial implication

**Michigan shows a 28% GGR tax, one skin per licence and a 15-licence cap can coexist with 18–23% growth five years post-launch.**

- For an entrant the constraint is access, not economics: no unallocated licences, so entry means buying or displacing a platform partner.
- The Hard Rock/Hannahville, bet365/LTBB and Caesars Horseshoe/Sault Ste. Marie transitions show this is possible, typically with a tribal licensee.
- Underwrite at ≥30% all-in tax from 2027 and assume no free-play shelter.
- Tribal skins avoid the 1.25% Detroit levy but route 20% of their payment to a local governing body, a relationship cost that is part of the deal, not a tax.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Online sports betting (OSB)

*$544.8m handle, 10.2% hold, $24.9m AGR: NFL kick-off lifted GR 27% YoY but 55% of it was handed back as promos — Michigan OSB is a low-tax, low-margin acquisition channel, not a profit pool.*

### Handle and hold

**Handle of $544.8m rose 64.9% MoM on the NFL restart but only 3.9% YoY; volume has matured and growth comes from hold at 10.2%.**

- Sep-25 handle was $524.4m; YTD handle of $3,876.1m is +5.8%.
- Hold was 10.2% in September (8.3% a year earlier) and 12.0% YTD (11.6% in 2025) as parlay and same-game-parlay mix deepens.
- Gross receipts were $55.5m (+27.3% YoY) and $465.8m YTD (+9.3%).
- Hold volatility persists (14.9% January 2025, 6.9% March 2025); September 2026 hold ranged from 5.8% at Caesars to 15.8% at Hard Rock Bet.

### Promotional deductions

**Promotions are hollowing out the taxable base: operators deducted $30.6m (55.2% of GR) in September to reach $24.9m AGR, with no statutory cap.**

- Under the Lawful Sports Betting Act AGR is GR less free play and promotional credits with no cap, unlike iGaming.
- A year ago the deduction was 69.7%, so AGR is +88.2% YoY on a GR gain of only 27%.
- YTD deductions ran at 47.9% of GR versus 38.8% in 2025, so YTD AGR fell 6.9% to $242.8m even as GR rose 9.3%.
- bet365 deducted $6.0m against $4.7m GR (AGR −$1.3m); Hard Rock Bet 92.7% of GR; Caesars 56.0%; FanDuel 50.6% and DraftKings 52.7%.
- Negative-AGR operators pay no tax and get no credit, so the State's take ($1.8m, 7.4% of aggregate AGR) sits below the 8.4% headline.

### Tax and reform proposals

**OSB tax is 8.4% of AGR plus 1.25% to Detroit for commercial licensees; September State take was $1.83m and a rejected reform package remains live.**

- All-in 9.65% commercial, 8.4% tribal; distribution 65% Internet Sports Betting Fund / 5% AEIDF (≤$3m) / 30% Detroit commercial, 90% ISBF / 10% Michigan Strategic Fund tribal.
- September State take $1.83m (+138% YoY, +13.7% MoM), Detroit $0.55m; YTD State receipts $18.8m (+26.1%); calendar 2025 was $27.1m on $435.9m AGR.
- Governor Whitmer's FY2027 budget called 8.4% the 28th of 30 legal states and lowest among neighbours.
- It proposed $0.25 per wager on an operator's first 20m bets and $0.50 thereafter (~$38.8m/yr) and eliminating the promo deduction (~$21.1m/yr).
- Both were stripped by the Senate in May 2026 and absent from the budget signed 21 Jul 2026, but the revenue case is written, scored and published; a per-wager levy would hit the FanDuel/DraftKings micro-bet model hardest.

### Concentration

**Concentration is the highest of any Michigan vertical: FanDuel and DraftKings hold 62% of handle but only 60% of AGR.**

- FanDuel $186.3m handle (34.2%); DraftKings $153.7m (28.2%); BetMGM $56.6m (10.4%, but 20.6% of AGR on 14.0% hold and lighter promos).
- Fanatics $48.9m (9.0%, up from 8.7% under PointsBet); bet365 $30.1m (5.5%); Caesars $21.3m (3.9%); Hard Rock Bet $20.0m (3.7%).
- theScore Bet $15.2m (2.8%); BetRivers $9.1m (1.7%); GAN, BetPARX, FireKeepers and Four Winds share the final 0.7%.
- Thirteen of 15 licensees run a sportsbook; Golden Nugget and Caesars Horseshoe do not. BetMGM converts 10% of handle into 21% of AGR.

### Catalogue and competition

**Fixed-odds horse racing was added administratively on 30 Oct 2026, and unlicensed prediction-market substitutes have all withdrawn from Michigan.**

- Fixed-odds race bets sit alongside pari-mutuel; small handle but the first material product expansion since launch.
- Kalshi (court-ordered), Robinhood, Coinbase and Polymarket withdrew sports event contracts between June and October 2026; Polymarket open positions close by 25 Nov 2026.
- If those venues held even low-single-digit share, Q4 2026 licensed handle should see a modest tailwind.
- We would read YoY handle acceleration above the ~4–6% trend in Oct–Dec as evidence.

### Commercial implication

**Michigan OSB is a low-tax, low-margin acquisition channel: 3.3% of GR effective tax in September after promos, with incumbents' promo cadence setting the price of share.**

- Lowest effective tax of any large US online state and 10%+ structural hold.
- A new skin without a national brand (bet365, Hard Rock at negative-to-nil AGR) is buying customers for iGaming cross-sell and should be modelled that way.
- Michigan OSB AGR of $242.8m YTD is 9% of iGaming AGR.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*Fifteen single-skin licences, cheap fees, a 28% graduated iGaming tax (29.25% in Detroit) and 8.4% on OSB — stable since 2021, with the free-play sunset on 1 Jan 2027 the only scheduled change and a rejected $200m tax package the live political one.*

### Legal framework

**Online gaming runs under two 2019 Acts with 15 single-skin licences per vertical, all allocated, overseen by the most transparent US regulator.**

- Lawful Internet Gaming Act (PA 152 of 2019, MCL 432.301–432.322) and Lawful Sports Betting Act (PA 149 of 2019, MCL 432.401–432.419), signed 20 Dec 2019, first launches 22 Jan 2021.
- Detroit casinos fall under the Michigan Gaming Control and Revenue Act (1996), tribal casinos under federal IGRA compacts, fantasy under the Fantasy Contests Consumer Protection Act.
- The MGCB (Treasury-housed; Executive Director Henry Williams) licenses, audits and publishes monthly operator-level results.
- Eligibility is limited to the three Detroit casino licensees and tribes with a Class III compact; each contracts a single platform provider, capping the market at 15 skins.

### Licence fees

**Fees are immaterial: $50,000 application, $100,000 on initial five-year licence and $50,000 annually (MCL 432.306(9) / 432.406(9)).**

- Platform and supplier licences cost $5,000 application, $5,000 initial and $2,500 annual; fantasy operators $10,000 initial / $5,000 annual.
- Investigative costs can be assessed on top.
- A single month of a top-three operator's iGaming tax ($10–14m) exceeds the lifetime licence fees of the entire market.

### iGaming tax

**iGaming tax is graduated to 28% above $12m calendar-year AGR, effectively flat 28%, plus 1.25% Detroit city tax, on a base that equals GR from 1 January 2027.**

- Tiers: 20% below $4m, 22% $4m–$8m, 24% $8m–$10m, 26% $10m–$12m, 28% above $12m; even Four Winds ($16.5m AGR YTD) is above the top threshold.
- Free play is capped at 4% of GR in 2026 and eliminated from 1 January 2027 for the 2021 cohort; MGCB tax-form instructions carry the same schedule.
- Commercial distribution: 65% Internet Gaming Fund / 5% AEIDF (capped $3m) / 30% City of Detroit; tribal: 70% IGF / 10% Michigan Strategic Fund / 20% local governing body.
- IGF first tops up Detroit if city gaming taxes fall below $183m (capped at 55% of deposits less the city's 30%), then MGCB costs, $500,000 Compulsive Gaming Prevention Fund, $2m First Responder Presumed Coverage Fund, balance to School Aid Fund.

### OSB and other taxes

**OSB pays 8.4% of AGR with an uncapped promo deduction; combined September 2026 public take across all verticals was $105.9m.**

- OSB adds 1.25% city for Detroit licensees; distribution 65/5/30 commercial and 90% ISBF / 10% MSF tribal. Fantasy pays 8.4%, 100% to the Fantasy Contest Fund.
- Detroit casinos pay 8.1% State plus 10.9% city on slots/tables and 8.4% + 1.25% on retail sports betting.
- September: ~$70.8m to the State (online $62.4m + casino $8.3m + retail SB $0.03m), ~$26.8m to Detroit, $8.3m to tribal governing bodies.

### 2026 tax proposals

**A ~$195–200m a year gaming tax package was proposed in the FY2027 executive budget and rejected; the FY2028 budget in February 2027 is the next decision point.**

- The 11 Feb 2026 budget sought a 36% iGaming tier above $185m annual AGR (capturing FanDuel, BetMGM, DraftKings; Caesars just below at $182.1m in 2025).
- It also sought a $0.25/$0.50 per-wager OSB charge and ending the OSB promo deduction, earmarked for the Medicaid Benefits Trust Fund.
- Both chambers passed budgets without the measures (May 2026); the enacted FY2027 budget (signed 21 Jul 2026; $75.19bn gross per NASBO) has no gaming-tax change.
- The Citizens Research Council flags a $600–900m structural gap; Whitmer is term-limited; preliminary results show Jocelyn Benson (D) elected governor 3 Nov 2026.
- The Legislature has until 1 July 2027 to act for an October 2027 start.

### Commercial implication

**Model iGaming at ~29–30% of GR all-in from 2027 and treat a 30–36% top tier within two budget cycles as a material probability, not a tail.**

- iGaming: 28% plus 1.25% if commercial, on a base equal to GR from 2027.
- The OSB 8.4%/uncapped-promo regime is the most generous in the US for a scaled book and the most exposed to reform.
- A per-wager levy at proposed rates would have cost roughly 5–7% of 2025 OSB GR at FanDuel/DraftKings bet frequencies (our estimate), more than doubling their effective sportsbook tax.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Enforcement, prediction markets, responsible gambling & payments

*Michigan has become the template for state action against sports event contracts — Kalshi enjoined, Robinhood, Coinbase and Polymarket withdrawn by agreement — while running a continuous offshore cease-and-desist programme.*

### Prediction markets

**Michigan is the first state with all four major prediction-market venues out by agreement or order, leaving licensed books the sports-outcome market.**

- The Attorney General sued KalshiEx LLC in March 2026; Ingham County Circuit Court issued a TRO on 29 Jun 2026 ($120,000-per-day penalty, licensed geolocation provider required).
- A preliminary injunction on 2 Sep 2026 requires geofencing of Michigan.
- Polymarket filed a pre-emptive federal suit in March 2026; Robinhood withdrew in September pending Sixth Circuit appeals; Coinbase agreed to halt on 7 Oct 2026.
- On 28 Oct 2026 the MGCB announced QCX LLC (Polymarket US) will stop new sports contracts and close open positions by end of day 25 Nov 2026, enforcement stayed while it complies.
- Federal pre-emption questions remain live in the Sixth Circuit and could reopen the channel.

### Offshore and sweepstakes

**The MGCB issued 32 cease-and-desist letters to offshore operators on 8 Oct 2026, continuing a programme running since 2024 that keeps the competitive set clean.**

- Messaging centres on the absence of self-exclusion and dispute protections at unlicensed sites.
- Sweepstakes-casino models are treated as unlicensed gaming under MCL Chapter 432 and pursued via cease-and-desist rather than new legislation; none operates openly in Michigan.
- This cleaner competitive set partly explains why iGaming growth has not decelerated five years post-launch.

### Responsible gambling

**Minimum age is 21 across all MGCB verticals, with statutory funding of $500,000 a year from each online fund to the Compulsive Gaming Prevention Fund.**

- Geolocation and KYC are mandated at account level; self-exclusion runs through the online Responsible Gaming Database and the Disassociated Persons List for Detroit casinos.
- Residents can claim a free Gamban licence via the MGCB; 1-800-GAMBLER and 'Don't Regret the Bet' appear on every release and licensee communication.
- Operators must offer deposit, wager and session limits under MGCB internet gaming rules [rule text not re-verified in this pass].
- Advertising must carry responsible-gaming messaging and may not target under-21s [per MGCB administrative rules; wording not re-verified].

### Payments

**The MGCB publishes no deposit-method mix, limits or processing statistics (n/a), and there is no state-level deposit-limit mandate.**

- Funding is card/ACH/wallet-based in the US norm.
- Whether credit-card funding is permitted is not addressed in the sources read and is marked [unverified].
- Deposit limits exist only as operator-offered tools.

### Commercial implication

**Michigan's enforcement posture is an asset for licensees, removing substitutes rather than adding compliance cost, but signals a regulator and AG willing to litigate quickly.**

- The next four dated events: 10 Nov 2026 Board meeting, 25 Nov Polymarket close-out, January 2027 free-play sunset, February 2027 executive budget.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Deepest regulated iGaming pool in the US and still compounding: $288.2m AGR in September (+18.4% YoY), $3.39bn trailing-12-month AGR, growth accelerating rather than fading five years after launch. _(structural)_
- Transparent, predictable regime: monthly operator-level publication, graduated 20–28% tax unchanged since 2019, licence fees of $100k/$50k p.a. that are immaterial, and no slot/table/poker rate differential. _(structural)_
- Lowest effective sportsbook tax among large US online states — 8.4% of AGR with uncapped promo deductions produced a State take of just 3.3% of GR in September — plus 10%+ structural hold on parlay mix. _(structural)_
- Competitive set is being cleaned up for licensees: Kalshi enjoined, Robinhood/Coinbase/Polymarket withdrawn by agreement by 25 Nov 2026, 32 offshore C&D letters in October, sweepstakes treated as illegal — a Q4 handle tailwind and a durable moat. _(mixed)_
- Share is contestable despite the cap: Hard Rock Bet went from zero to 6.4% of iGaming AGR within nine months of relaunching on a tribal licence; FanDuel and BetMGM have ceded 6.5 points combined in a year — a platform-swap entry is proven. _(structural)_
- Tribal skins (12 of 15 licences, 51.6% of iGaming AGR) avoid the 1.25% Detroit city levy and offer the realistic access route for a new platform partner. _(structural)_
- Catalogue is expanding administratively — fixed-odds horse racing added to OSB on 30 Oct 2026 — signalling a regulator willing to widen product without legislation. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- No open licences: all 15 operator licences (3 Detroit casinos + 12 tribes) are allocated with one platform each; entry requires buying or displacing an incumbent partner, with the tribe or casino as gatekeeper. _(structural)_
- Free-play deduction sunsets to 0% on 1 Jan 2027 under MCL 432.303(a), lifting effective iGaming tax by ~1.1pt of GR (~$40m/yr market-wide, our estimate) with no legislative action required. _(structural)_
- A scored $195–200m/yr tax package (36% iGaming tier >$185m AGR, $0.25–0.50 per-wager OSB levy, end of OSB promo deduction) was rejected in 2026 but sits on the shelf as a new governor inherits a budget the Citizens Research Council says is $600–900m short. _(watch)_
- OSB economics are being competed away: promo deductions ran at 48% of GR YTD (39% in 2025), YTD OSB AGR fell 6.9% despite +9.3% GR, and challengers (bet365 −$1.3m AGR, Hard Rock 93% promo/GR) are buying share at a loss. _(mixed)_
- Concentration: FanDuel + DraftKings hold 62% of OSB handle and FanDuel + BetMGM + DraftKings 61% of iGaming AGR; a sub-scale skin is a cross-sell vehicle, not a standalone business. _(structural)_
- OSB volume has plateaued (+3.9% YoY handle in NFL kick-off month, +5.8% YTD); growth depends on hold and promo discipline, both of which are volatile month to month. _(structural)_
- Litigation-first enforcement culture cuts both ways: the AG and MGCB move quickly against perceived unlicensed activity (TRO within three months of filing), and federal pre-emption rulings in the Sixth Circuit could reopen prediction-market competition at short notice. _(governance)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

Nothing in Michigan's tax or licensing code changed in the September 2026 reporting period, but the trajectory did: the FY2027 executive budget's ~$200m gaming-tax package (36% iGaming top tier, per-wager OSB levy, OSB promo-deduction repeal) was stripped in May and the budget was signed on 21 Jul 2026 without it, while enforcement removed Kalshi, Robinhood, Coinbase and Polymarket from the sports-outcome market between June and October. The scheduled change is statutory — the iGaming free-play deduction drops from 4% of GR to zero on 1 January 2027 — and the unscheduled one is political: a term-limited governor hands a structurally short budget to a successor whose FY2028 proposal lands in February 2027.

- **iGaming free-play sunset (1 Jan 2027):** ~+1.1pt of GR (Effective tax rises from ~27.5% to ~28.6% of GR; ~$40m/yr market-wide (estimate))
- **Rejected FY2027 tax package:** ~$195–200m/yr (36% tier >$185m AGR + $0.25–0.50/wager + OSB promo repeal; shelved, not withdrawn)
- **Prediction-market exits:** 4 venues out by 25 Nov 2026 (Licensed OSB handle tailwind in Q4-26; Sixth Circuit pre-emption risk remains)
- **Fixed-odds horse racing:** Added 30 Oct 2026 (Incremental OSB catalogue; low single-digit $m handle (estimate))

**Market:** we expect calendar-2026 iGaming AGR of roughly $3.5–3.6bn (+20–23%) and GR of ~$3.65–3.75bn (estimates), decelerating to low-to-mid-teens growth in 2027 as the free-play sunset removes a promotional lever and the base compounds; OSB handle should stay in a +3–8% band with hold around 11–12%, so OSB AGR direction will be set by promo intensity rather than volume — we assume promos ease back toward 40% of GR once the bet365/Hard Rock share grabs normalise. **Tax:** the probability that Michigan raises iGaming and/or OSB taxes within two budget cycles is high; the specific 2026 proposal already carries a fiscal score and Michigan's 8.4% OSB rate is below every neighbour (Illinois, Ohio, Indiana). Our base case is a top-tier iGaming increase to 30–36% on the largest operators and either a per-wager charge or a promo-deduction cap on OSB effective October 2027 at the earliest, with the Detroit 1.25% levy untouched. **Licensing:** no new licences; expect one or two further platform swaps on tribal paper (Caesars Horseshoe and Four Winds are the sub-$3m/month skins most exposed). **Enforcement:** the prediction-market agreements hold unless the Sixth Circuit finds CEA pre-emption, in which case Michigan would litigate rather than accommodate. **Leverage for an entrant:** none — Michigan rewards scale and brand; a B2B/platform play supplying a tribal licensee is the lower-risk exposure.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- FY2028 executive budget (Feb 2027): whether Gov.-elect Benson re-tables the 36% iGaming tier, per-wager OSB levy or promo-deduction repeal, and the Senate/House response before the 1 Jul 2027 deadline.
- January 2027 MGCB release (published ~Feb 2027): confirmation that iGaming GR = AGR as the free-play cap hits 0%, and any operator pullback in bonusing.
- Sixth Circuit rulings on Kalshi/Coinbase/Robinhood pre-emption and Polymarket's close-out of positions by 25 Nov 2026 — any reversal reopens sports event contracts.
- Q4-2026 OSB handle YoY vs the +4–6% trend as a read on how much volume the prediction-market exits return to licensed books.
- Promo-deduction ratio in OSB (48% of GR YTD) — persistence above 45% into 2027 would strengthen the fiscal case for a cap.
- Platform changes on the five smallest tribal skins (Caesars Horseshoe, Four Winds/Strive, Soaring Eagle/GAN, FireKeepers/NeoGames, BetPARX) — the only entry route into the market.
- MGCB Board meeting 10 Nov 2026 and the Executive Director's monthly reports for rule-making on advertising, fixed-odds racing and sweepstakes enforcement.
