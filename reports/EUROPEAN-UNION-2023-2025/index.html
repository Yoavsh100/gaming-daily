2023-2025 · market & regulatory brief

European Union

Released 24 Nov 2026 (ECA/GCI "Online Gaming 2023–2025: Illegal Gambling in EU27 Europe"; wire release 25 Nov 2026) · Currency EUR (tables in € billions unless stated; ~USD alongside at EUR/USD 1.095) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

The ECA-commissioned GCI study sizes the EU27 online betting-and-gaming marketplace at €128.0bn (~$140.2bn) GGR in 2025, of which only €36.5bn (~$40.0bn, 28%) sits with locally licensed operators and €91.6bn (~$100.3bn, 72%) with operators lacking the national licence required — a cumulative €224.8bn (~$246.2bn) "illegal" take and €56.3bn (~$61.6bn) of forgone tax over 2023–25. The regulated base is corroborated by EGBA/H2 (EU27+UK online €47.9bn in 2024, implying ~€36–37bn ex-UK) and is growing high-single digits, but the black-market figure is 5–8x the competing Euromat/Regulus (€12bn net, ~25%) and H2 (~€18bn, ~27%) estimates and should be treated as an upper bound. For an operator the EU remains 27 separate, expensive (avg €1.1m initial fee, 24% avg GGR tax) national regimes with no passporting; the actionable opportunities are the markets re-regulating in 2026–28 (Finland, Austria, Ireland) and the better-channelised Nordic and Southern European markets, not an "EU-wide" play.

1Executive summary

  • Market structure — one online marketplace, two industries. GCI's monitoring for the European Casino Association (ECA) puts EU27 online GGR (sports betting, casino and poker incl. crypto; lotteries, DFS, prediction markets and land-based excluded) at €78.4bn (~$85.8bn) in 2023, €114.3bn (~$125.2bn) in 2024 and €128.0bn (~$140.2bn) in 2025; over three years the marketplace totalled €320.8bn (~$351.3bn), split €95.9bn (~$105.0bn, 30%) locally licensed vs €224.8bn (~$246.2bn, 70%) unlicensed.
  • Regulated base is growing, but losing share. Licensed online GGR rose from €25.8bn (~$28.3bn) in 2023 to €33.6bn (~$36.8bn) in 2024 (+30%) and €36.5bn (~$40.0bn) in 2025 (+8.6%); its share of the online marketplace fell from 33% to 29% to 28%. Of the €49.7bn (~$54.4bn) increase in annual GGR over 2023–25, unlicensed operators captured €39.0bn (~$42.7bn), or 78%.
  • Illegal growth is decelerating, which the headline obscures. Unlicensed GGR grew +53% in 2024 (€52.6bn→€80.6bn) but only +13.6% in 2025 (→€91.6bn, ~$100.3bn); the 2026–28 trajectory depends on whether that deceleration is enforcement-driven or a calendar effect (2025 had no major international football tournament).
  • The headline black-market number is contested by 5–8x. Euromat's Regulus/Helios study (Sept 2026; 28 markets = EU27 ex-Malta/Luxembourg + UK, Serbia, Montenegro) puts illegal online NET revenue at ~€12bn (~$13.1bn), ~25% of the sector and tripled since 2019 (~18% CAGR); H2 Gambling Capital is reported at ~€18bn (~$19.7bn), ~27%. The regulated base is consistent across all three; the disagreement is entirely about the unlicensed denominator and GCI's audience-based 'value-per-visit' method, which counts every transacting domain (mirrors included).
  • Regulated base corroborated by EGBA/H2. EGBA's Key Figures 2025 (H2 data, EU27+UK, includes grey-market online) show total European GGR of €123.4bn (~$135.1bn) in 2024 (+5%), online €47.9bn (~$52.5bn, 39% share, +12% from €42.9bn in 2023) and land-based €75.5bn (~$82.7bn). Stripping the UK's ~€11bn online implies an EU27 online figure of ~€36–37bn — in line with GCI's regulated €33.6–36.5bn and IMGL's €35–40bn 2025 estimate. EGBA/H2 project online at €51.1bn (~$56.0bn, 40%) in 2025 and €66.8bn (~$73.1bn, 45%) by 2029 (6.9% CAGR).
  • Casino dominates online demand; sports is better channelised. GCI's 2025 product split is 70% casino / 30% sports for the total online marketplace; regulated GGR is 41% sports / 59% casino while illegal GGR is 25% / 75%. Derived values: regulated sports ~€15.0bn (~$16.4bn) vs illegal ~€22.9bn (~$25.1bn) (legal channelisation ~40%); regulated casino ~€21.5bn (~$23.5bn) vs illegal ~€68.7bn (~$75.2bn) (~24%). H2 puts EU27+UK licensed/grey online casino at €21.5bn and online sports at €13.7bn in 2024.
  • Regional leak is worst in Eastern Europe, best in the Nordics. 2025 unlicensed share: Eastern 81% (€30.7bn of €37.7bn), Western 74% (€36.9bn of €50.1bn — the largest region), Southern 60% (€17.1bn of €28.4bn), Northern 58% (€6.9bn of €11.9bn). Regulated GGR grew fastest in Southern Europe (+12%) and slowest in Western Europe (+6.5%) in 2025.
  • Licensing regime — 27 national licences, no passporting. There is no EU gambling directive; each member state licenses and taxes separately under CJEU-sanctioned national competence, and the ECA itself asks for 'national licences, European cooperation' rather than an EU regulator. GCI's 2025 benchmark: average initial multi-product licence fee €1.1m (~$1.2m) in the EU27 vs €8,183 (~$9.0k) at transnational licensors (Anjouan, Curaçao, Isle of Man, Malta); recurring €355k (~$389k) vs €33k (~$36k); EU regulators also typically demand bank guarantees/share capital.
  • Tax regime — GGR-based in most states, averaging 24%. GCI's average gaming tax across GGR-regime member states is 24.0% (vs 2.3% transnational), with corporate tax averaging 18.9% vs 8.8%. Turnover-based betting taxes persist in a minority (e.g. Germany, Poland, Ireland [not re-verified this session]); recent increases include the Netherlands (to 34.2% in 2025, 37.8% from 2026) and Sweden (22% from July 2024) [background, unverified in-session]. A BGC-commissioned H2GC study forecasts an 11ppt channelisation decline in the Netherlands after the 2025 rise and the KSA itself forecast a 9% fall in 2025 tax receipts.
  • Tax 'loss' figures are internally inconsistent. ECA quotes €22.9bn (~$25.1bn) forgone in 2025 and €56.3bn (~$61.6bn) over three years (≈25% of illegal GGR), while GCI's September 2026 Campaign for Fairer Gambling edition quotes €22.0bn (~$24.1bn) at a 24% average rate on the same €91.6bn base. Both are gross hypotheticals that assume full substitution into licensed play at headline rates.
  • Operator landscape. EGBA's nine members (bet365, Entain, Betsson, Evoke, FDJ United, Flutter, LeoVegas, Super Technologies/Superbet, Tipico) generated €18.0bn (~$19.7bn) online GGR in 2025 (+34%, from €13.5bn), hold 401 online licences across 22 jurisdictions and claim ~30% of Europe's online GGR — equivalent to ~49% of GCI's EU27 regulated figure. On the illegal side GCI counted 6,238 transacting destinations in 2025 (4,921 in 2023; 6,220 in 2024) and 17,501 affiliates (21,106 in 2023); Euromat finds 25 brands drive ~64% of black-market traffic. A consolidated EU-wide licensed-operator count does not exist (n/a).
  • Payments are the chosen enforcement lever. The ECA argues that if banks, card schemes and PSPs stopped settling with unlicensed operators 'their business model would break'; GCI describes a compartmentalised 'triangle trade' (licensing, sites/apps and content in different jurisdictions) that gives unlicensed operators banking continuity and payment resilience, with crypto play included in its estimates. Payment-method mix is not disclosed (n/a).
  • Advertising and platform exposure is overwhelmingly illegal. GCI's audience-exposure metric attributes 91% of gambling content actively engaged with by EU27 consumers in 2025 to unlicensed operators (85% in 2023); 121m Europeans saw online gambling content in 2025, 88m from unlicensed sources. Cloaked-ad brokers (over half EU-based) sell into all 27 states with 85% approval rates and promise 1,100+ monthly first-time depositors; slots content on Kick is 'blocked' in six states but effective only in Greece; 19% of Kick's outbound traffic went to Stake during 2026 World Cup peaks.
  • Responsible-gambling rules are being marketed against. Unlicensed sites advertise 'casinos not on CRUKS' (NL) and 'not on ROFUS' (DK), targeting self-excluded players; Euromat counts 'significant advertising restrictions' in 46% of the 28 markets studied (incl. BE, BG, HR, CY, DE, IT, LV, LT, NL, PL, RO, ES), product bans in 14% (CY, FR, DE, PL) and consumer-price taxation in 29% (AT, HR, DE, IE, PL, PT) as the structural drivers of leakage.
  • Political direction — enforcement, not harmonisation. The ECA's July 2026 European Parliament roundtable (hosted by MEP Lukas Mandl; Commission, AMLA, Eurojust and the Europol JPSG attending) coincided with the Commission's proposal to reform Europol's mandate; the ECA wants regulator–police–FIU information sharing and platform/app-store/search take-downs; Euromat calls for a European taskforce. Expect EU action through AML, Europol and platform rules rather than a gambling directive.
  • Market-opening pipeline is real. Finland moves from the Veikkaus monopoly to multi-operator licensing (go-live 2027), Austria's government approved a reform to open the online market on 25 Nov 2026 ahead of its 2027 licence expiry, and Ireland's new regulator published draft eligible-game lists for its licensing regime this week — three new licensed casino/betting markets inside 24 months.
  • Event cadence matters. GCI flags 2025 as a gap year; the 2026 World Cup generated 17.8bn qualifying (90s+) illegal stream views in Europe, 95% carrying unlicensed gambling ads, and the 2026 Champions League final 51.3m (93%). Over 80% of illegal sports streams in the EU27 carried unlicensed gambling ads in 2024–25.
  • Read-through for B2Tech. The licensed addressable pool is ~€36.5bn and compounding ~8–11% a year; entry costs ~€1m+ per market plus 20–40% GGR tax and rising compliance; the illegal competitor set is real in every state but its scale is contested. Prioritise (i) the 2026–28 openings (FI, AT, IE), (ii) Northern/Southern markets with 40%+ regulated share and GGR-based tax, and (iii) products where regulation does not structurally handicap the legal offer (avoid building a casino-led case in FR, PL, DE under current rules).

3Deep-dive: Market size & channelisation (regulated vs illegal)

A €128bn online marketplace in which licensed operators hold 28% by GCI's count — but 73–75% by the Euromat and H2 methods; the regulated base (~€36.5bn) is the only number all sources agree on.

Total marketplace size

EU27 online GGR reached €128.0bn (~$140.2bn) in 2025, up 12.0% from €114.3bn in 2024 and 63% from €78.4bn in 2023.

  • Scope: sports betting, casino and poker including crypto play; lotteries, daily fantasy, prediction markets, sweepstakes, social casino and land-based excluded.
  • Licensed operators earned €36.5bn (~$40.0bn) in 2025, up 8.6% after +30% in 2024; unlicensed earned €91.6bn (~$100.3bn), up 13.6% after +53%.
  • Three-year split is €95.9bn regulated against €224.8bn unlicensed, i.e. 30/70 of a €320.8bn (~$351.3bn) marketplace.
  • Illegal operators absorbed €39.0bn of the €49.7bn increase in annual GGR (78% of growth); GCI equates the 2025 illegal take to over €250m a day.

Regional pattern

Eastern Europe is 81% unlicensed, Western Europe is the largest region at €50.1bn and 74% unlicensed, Northern Europe is best channelised at 42% regulated.

  • Eastern Europe: €30.7bn of €37.7bn unlicensed, reflecting monopolies (Poland's online casino, Hungary, Slovenia) and turnover taxes.
  • Western Europe illegal GGR more than doubled in 2024 (+104.5%) before slowing to +15.7% in 2025; France's casino ban, Germany's GlüStV and Dutch tax rise cited.
  • Southern Europe is 60% unlicensed on €28.4bn but delivered the fastest regulated growth (+12% to €11.3bn, ~$12.4bn).
  • Illegal growth in 2025 was strongest in Southern (+22.1%) and Northern (+21.1%) Europe and weakest in Eastern Europe (+5.9%), possibly nearing saturation.

Corroboration of estimates

The regulated base (~€36.5bn) is robust across sources; the unlicensed figure is not, with GCI's estimate 5–8x Euromat's and H2's.

  • EGBA/H2 put EU27 plus UK online GGR at €47.9bn in 2024 (39% of €123.4bn); ex-UK roughly €36–37bn, consistent with GCI's €33.6bn regulated and IMGL's €35–40bn.
  • H2 data cited by the Dutch regulator shows the licensed EU online market growing 11% from 2024 to 2025.
  • Euromat's Regulus/Helios study (Sept 2026) finds ~€12bn illegal net revenue across 28 markets, ~25% of the sector, up ~3x since 2019 (≈18% CAGR); H2 reported at ~€18bn or ~27%.
  • GCI's higher count is driven by its audience-based 'value-per-visit' model, inclusion of crypto casinos and counting every active transacting domain separately.
“assessment of large complex online marketplaces is limited by the availability and completeness of data”GCI

Audience indicators

Population impact was 23.03% in 2025 with 16.76% reached by unlicensed operators; audience exposure was 91% unlicensed, up from 85% in 2023.

  • 121m Europeans saw online gambling content and 88m of them saw unlicensed content; population impact was 29.26% in Eastern Europe.
  • Audience exposure was 96% unlicensed in Eastern Europe and 81% in Northern Europe.
  • GCI presents exposure as an early-warning indicator of where future GGR will flow rather than a share measure.

Commercial implications

The licensed pool of ~€36.5bn compounds at 8–11% a year, but licensed economics must assume an offshore crypto-friendly competitor with no tax or KYC friction.

  • Casino is the larger and faster product within the licensed pool.
  • EGBA's nine members grew 34% to €18.0bn (~$19.7bn) in 2025, suggesting scale incumbents are taking share inside the licensed pool.
  • Whether the unlicensed competitor set is €12bn or €92bn changes the pricing and bonus intensity to plan for.
  • In Eastern Europe and product-restricted Western markets the practical competitor has no tax, no KYC friction and 3x advertising ROAS.

4Deep-dive: Online sports betting

Sports is ~30% of EU27 online GGR and the better-channelised product (~40% licensed), but event-driven peaks — and the illegal streams that monetise them — are where unlicensed recruitment concentrates.

Sports share and channelisation

Sports is 30% of 2025 EU27 online GGR (~€37.9bn derived) with roughly 40% legal channelisation, far better than casino's ~24%.

  • Share was 32% in 2024; sports is 41% of regulated GGR (~€15.0bn) but only 25% of illegal GGR (~€22.9bn).
  • Regulated sports share is highest in Western Europe (47%, reflecting France) and lowest in Northern Europe (37%).
  • Illegal sports share is highest in Southern (31%) and Eastern (30%) Europe and lowest in Western Europe (20%), where the unlicensed offer is overwhelmingly casino.

H2/EGBA series

H2/EGBA put EU27 plus UK online sports betting at €13.7bn in 2024, up 11%, versus GCI's ~€36bn EU27-only total.

  • €13.7bn was 29% of online GGR, up from €12.3bn in 2023; €14.5bn forecast for 2025 and 6.9% CAGR to €19.2bn by 2029.
  • Online horse racing adds €2.5bn (~$2.7bn).
  • The gap is the clearest illustration of how much larger GCI's unlicensed count is than the industry-standard series.

National product mixes

Sports leads online in Cyprus (74%) and Slovakia (64%); turnover taxes on betting structurally compress the legal price.

  • France's €3.8bn online market is betting- and poker-led because online casino is prohibited; Germany is lottery- and betting-heavy (lottery 37% of online).
  • Betting is taxed on turnover in Germany (5.3% of stakes), Poland (12%) and Ireland (2%) [background, not re-verified].
  • Euromat cites turnover taxation as a leakage driver in AT, HR, DE, IE, PL and PT.

Event cadence

2025 was a gap year with no major tournament, a plausible contributor to illegal growth slowing to +13.6%; 2026 World Cup streams carried 17.8bn illegal views.

  • 95% of 17.8bn qualifying World Cup illegal stream views carried ads for operators not licensed in the viewer's jurisdiction; 51.3m for the Champions League final (93%).
  • More than 80% of illegal sports streams viewed in the EU27 carried unlicensed gambling ads in both 2024 and 2025.
  • During World Cup peaks 19% of outbound traffic from the Kick streaming platform went to Stake.
“one click away from a bet”ECA

B2Tech read

Sports betting is the lower-leakage, lower-friction entry product, but the 2026 World Cup / 2028 Euros cycle will set the acquisition cost curve.

  • Turnover-tax markets require a pricing model built around margin per stake rather than GGR.
  • Illegal streaming and cloaked advertising compete for the same audience at the same moments.

5Deep-dive: Online casino, poker & crypto

Casino is 70% of the EU27 online marketplace and the product where licensed operators are weakest (~24% channelisation by GCI's count); where it is banned or handicapped, the leak is structural.

Casino share and channelisation

Casino is 70% of EU27 online GGR in 2025 (~€90.2bn derived) and only about one euro in four is earned under a local licence.

  • Share was 68% in 2024; casino is 59% of regulated GGR (~€21.5bn) but 75% of illegal GGR (~€68.7bn).
  • Unlicensed GGR is most casino-heavy in Western Europe (80%) and Northern Europe (78%), least in Southern Europe (69%).

H2/EGBA series

H2/EGBA show licensed-plus-grey online casino at €21.5bn for EU27+UK in 2024, up 14%, compounding 7.5% to €30.8bn by 2029.

  • Casino was 45% of online GGR in 2024, up from €18.9bn in 2023; €23.2bn forecast for 2025; fastest-growing product after online lottery.
  • Online poker is flat at €1.5bn (2.3% CAGR) and bingo/other €1.6bn.
  • H2's EU27+UK casino figure coincides with GCI's EU27-only regulated casino, so the product cut should be treated as indicative.

Regulatory drivers

Country mixes reflect regulation as much as taste; product bans in CY, FR, DE, PL and monopolies in AT, FI, HU, PL, SI are primary leakage drivers.

  • Casino exceeds 60% of online GGR in Malta (73%), Estonia (72%) and Latvia (63%); France and Cyprus prohibit online casino for licensed operators.
  • Poland reserves online casino to the state; Germany permits slots and poker under a €1 stake limit and 5.3% turnover tax [background, not re-verified].
  • GCI lists KYC, self-exclusion, age limits, RG tooling, cross-sell limits and taxation as frictions exploited, plus 'product bleed' from prediction markets.
“reflect fully offshore casino activity”EGBA

Unlicensed ecosystem

The 25 largest illegal brands account for ~64% of black-market traffic and Kick's slots category is accessible in 21 of 27 member states.

  • Stake migrated from Twitch (banned Oct 2022) to Kick (launched Dec 2022 under common ownership).
  • Six 'blocks' (BE, FR, GR, IT, MT, SK) are effective only in Greece; 26 of 27 states had affiliate advertising for unlicensed casinos on Kick.
  • Unlicensed casinos explicitly market self-exclusion avoidance ('casinos not on CRUKS', 'not on ROFUS').

Commercial implications

Greenfield casino opportunities are Finland from 2027, Austria post-reform and Ireland; France, Poland and Germany should not anchor a casino-led business case.

  • Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands and Southern Europe are mature GGR-tax casino markets where share is won on product and CRM.
  • Pricing and bonus intensity must assume an unlicensed competitor paying ~2% gaming tax and no KYC friction.

6Deep-dive: Land-based, lottery & retail (EU27 + UK)

Retail is still 61% of European GGR (€75.5bn) and growing ~1.8% a year; lottery is the single largest product (€38bn) and the fastest-growing online vertical (7.7% CAGR) — but it sits behind state monopolies.

Total European market

Total EU27 plus UK GGR was €123.4bn in 2024, up 5%, with land-based €75.5bn (61%) and online €47.9bn (39%).

  • H2 forecasts €127.7bn for 2025 with online crossing 40% at €51.1bn, and €149.2bn by 2029 with online at 45%.
  • Land-based is forecast to compound 1.8% a year to €82.4bn by 2029 against 6.9% for online.
  • GCI excludes land-based entirely; EGBA/H2 is the only consolidated view.

Land-based product mix

Lottery is the largest product overall at €38.0bn and online lottery has the strongest growth outlook of any online vertical (7.7% CAGR).

  • Retail: lottery €30.9bn (41%, +1.7% CAGR), machines €24.9bn (33%, +1.6%), casino €8.5bn (11%, +2.5%), sports €6.4bn (9%, +2.5%), horse racing €3.2bn, bingo/other €1.6bn.
  • Online lottery of €7.1bn is forecast to reach €10.4bn by 2029; strongest in Germany (37% of online GGR) and Denmark (26%).

Country structure

Markets are retail-heavy outside the Nordics and the UK; Italy was Europe's largest at €21.0bn in 2023 with only 21.7% online.

  • UK €19.8bn with €11.1bn (56%) online; Germany €14.4bn (22.6% online); France €14.0bn (27% online, €3.8bn); Spain lowest online share at 14.2%.
  • Sweden (68.3%), Finland and Denmark (68.1%) are most digital; 13 countries have online shares above 50%.

Land-based lobby

The ECA and Euromat are land-based constituencies arguing for enforcement against unlicensed online, not liberalisation, and oppose higher taxes on licensees.

  • ECA represents licensed casinos and online arms, chaired by the CEO of Casinos Austria and Austrian Lotteries; Euromat represents gaming-machine and amusement sector.
  • Lottery and most casino concessions remain state-owned or exclusive in every member state.
  • Private online markets opened progressively: Malta 2004, Italy 2006, France 2010, Spain 2011, Denmark 2012, Portugal 2015, Sweden 2019, Germany/Netherlands 2021, Finland 2026+, Austria 2027.

Operator implications

Retail matters as an omnichannel share lever in Italy and Spain, as lottery monopolies become online competitors, and because the land-based lobby shapes EU enforcement.

  • Licences in Italy and Spain are often tied to concessions.
  • Lottery monopolies (FDJ, Veikkaus, Lotto, SISAL-type operators) are increasingly licensed online competitors.
  • The land-based lobby is the loudest voice on EU-level enforcement, more than the online associations.

7Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime (EU framework and national)

No EU gambling law and none coming: 27 national licences averaging €1.1m to enter and 24% of GGR to operate, versus €8k and 2.3% offshore — the cost asymmetry that the ECA says 'must not become an advantage for those who ignore the law'.

EU framework

There is no EU gambling directive and none coming; regulation is national, with EU leverage running through AML, Europol, Eurojust and platform rules.

  • National restrictions are permitted under Treaty free-movement rules where justified by consumer-protection or crime-prevention objectives and applied proportionately.
  • The new Anti-Money Laundering Authority attended the ECA's July 2026 roundtable; the Commission has proposed reforming Europol's mandate.
  • Malta's 2023 'Bill 55' shielding MGA licensees from foreign judgments is the live test, with the Commission having opened legal action.
“national licences, European cooperation against criminals”ECA

Cost asymmetry

Average EU27 initial licence fee is €1.1m and gaming tax 24.0% of GGR, against €8,183 and 2.3% at transnational licensors.

  • Average recurring fees €355k against €33k; average corporate tax 18.9% against 8.8%.
  • EU regulators additionally require bank guarantees and share capital that transnational licensors (Malta excepted) do not.
  • GCI stresses Malta is both an EU member and a transnational licensor; the ECA has adopted the 'there is no grey market' doctrine.
“does not provide authorization to operate in every jurisdiction”GCI

Tax bases rising

Most states tax online GGR but rates are moving up, with the Netherlands at 37.8% from 2026 and Sweden at 22% from July 2024.

  • Germany, Poland and Ireland tax betting stakes and Portugal taxes sports-betting turnover [background, not re-verified].
  • Netherlands rose from 30.5% to 34.2% in 2025; a BGC-commissioned H2GC study forecasts an 11ppt fall in Dutch channelisation.
  • The Dutch regulator forecast tax receipts down 9% in 2025 versus 2024 and described licensed gambling as stagnating while the black market grows.
  • Euromat identifies consumer-price taxation in AT, HR, DE, IE, PL and PT as a leakage driver.
“every additional burden placed on legal operators strengthens the position of illegal operators”ECA

Licensing timelines

A two-decade market opening is not yet complete; Finland goes live 2027, Austria approved reform on 25 Nov 2026, Ireland is drafting licensable games.

  • Malta 2004, Italy 2006, France 2010 (betting, horse racing, poker only), Spain 2011, Denmark 2012, Portugal 2015, Sweden 2019, Germany and Netherlands 2021.
  • Hungary, Poland (casino) and Slovenia retain monopoly or near-monopoly structures.
  • No consolidated EU licence register exists; EGBA's nine members hold 401 online licences across 22 jurisdictions; per-state counts n/a.

Strategic conclusion

Multi-market EU entry costs roughly €1m-plus per licence, 20–40% of GGR in tax, 12–24 months lead time and a separate compliance stack per state.

  • Country-level rates are background reference points flagged '†', not re-verified because the research tool budget was exhausted.
  • Confirm with ADM, DGOJ, ANJ, GGL, KSA, Spelinspektionen, Spillemyndigheden, SRIJ and HGC before use.
  • There is no credible route to serve EU consumers lawfully from a single transnational licence.

8Deep-dive: Payments, advertising, responsible gambling & enforcement ecosystem

Licensed operators carry the AML, RG and advertising burden while the unlicensed sector buys 85%-approved cloaked ads, streams to 17.8bn World Cup views and markets 'not on CRUKS' — the policy answer now forming is payments and platform enforcement, not more rules for licensees.

Payments

Both trade bodies now prioritise payment disruption, but GCI's 6,238 transacting destinations imply existing blocking powers are not biting.

  • GCI describes a 'triangle trade' separating licensing, sites/apps and content across jurisdictions so there is no single point of failure.
  • Crypto-funded play is included in GCI's unlicensed estimate; Stake is its principal case study.
  • No payment-method mix for the EU27 is disclosed by any source read (n/a).
“all payment services stopped transferring money to illegal operators, their business model would break”ECA chairman

Advertising asymmetry

Licensed advertising is heavily restricted in roughly 46% of 28 markets while cloaked-ad brokers sell into every EU27 state with 85% platform approval.

  • Restricted markets include BE, BG, HR, CY, DE, IT, LV, LT, NL, PL, RO, ES; brokers promise 3x ROAS and 1,100-plus first-time depositors a month via Google and Meta.
  • More than half of the brokers are EU-based.
  • Affiliates promoting unlicensed operators fell from 21,106 (2023) to 17,501 (2025), which GCI reads as consolidation.
  • AI search assistants recommending operators without licence checks are a new channel; the ECA wants search engines and AI providers to perform due diligence.

Responsible gambling

Self-exclusion registers like CRUKS and ROFUS are used as search hooks by unlicensed sites; population impact reached 23.03% with exposure 91% unlicensed.

  • 16.76% of EU27 residents were reached by unlicensed content; population impact was 29.26% in Eastern Europe.
  • Kick's slots category is nominally blocked in six member states but functionally in one; 26 of 27 states carried unlicensed affiliate advertising on Kick.
“the only accessible gambling options”GCI

Enforcement

Enforcement remains national while activity is cross-border; the ECA, Euromat and GCI all call for coordinated disruption of payments, advertising and platforms.

  • ECA's July 2026 European Parliament roundtable (MEP Lukas Mandl; Commission, AMLA, Eurojust, Europol JPSG, regulators) was timed to the Europol mandate reform.
  • ECA's November asks: regulator–police–FIU information sharing, payment and advertising disruption, platform/app-store/search take-downs.
  • Euromat calls for a European taskforce; GCI proposes a 'Monitor, Police, Enforce, Optimize' cycle across the whole marketplace.
  • The ECA explicitly warns governments not to respond by raising taxes on licensees.

B2Tech implications

Expect tighter AML, restrictive advertising in large Western and Southern markets, and RG tooling as table stakes; channelisation gains are years away.

  • AMLA operational from 2027 [background].
  • Self-exclusion, deposit-limit and affordability tooling will be required in every licence application.
  • The enforcement consensus is finally pointing at payments, platforms and affiliates, the channels that most directly determine acquisition costs.

9Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Licensed online demand is compounding: EU27 regulated online GGR €36.5bn (~$40.0bn) in 2025, +8.6% after +30% in 2024 (GCI), with H2 showing the licensed EU online market +11% in 2025 and EGBA's nine members +34% to €18.0bn (~$19.7bn) — scale incumbents are gaining share inside the licensed pool. structural
  • Three greenfield licensed markets open inside 24 months: Finland (monopoly to licensing, go-live 2027), Austria (government approved reform on 25 Nov 2026; licences expire 2027) and Ireland (new GRAI regime, draft eligible-game list published this week) — all are casino-inclusive, GGR-tax regimes where there is no entrenched licensed competitor base. structural
  • Casino is 70% of the EU27 online marketplace and the fastest-growing licensed product (H2: online casino 7.5% CAGR to €30.8bn (~$33.7bn) by 2029 for EU27+UK); where regulation allows a full casino product, the addressable pool is largest. structural
  • Sports betting is the better-channelised product (~40% licensed by GCI's count vs ~24% for casino) and the 2026 World Cup / 2028 Euros cycle provides acquisition peaks; the illegal-growth deceleration to +13.6% in 2025 may partly reverse in 2026 but licensed operators also benefit from tournament demand. mixed
  • Northern (42% regulated) and Southern Europe (40%; regulated GGR +12% in 2025 to €11.3bn, ~$12.4bn) offer the best channelisation and GGR-based tax regimes — the lowest-risk entry points for a multi-market build. structural
  • The enforcement consensus (ECA, Euromat, GCI, Commission/Europol/AMLA engagement) has shifted to payments, platforms, app stores, search/AI and affiliates — the channels that set licensed operators' acquisition costs; any effective action lifts licensed channelisation without new costs to licensees. early
  • Transnational-licence arbitrage is being closed politically ('there is no grey market'), with Malta's Bill 55 under EU legal action — a durable advantage for operators that invest in local licences over those running 'dot-com' models into the EU. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Tax creep on licensees: average EU27 gaming tax 24% of GGR (GCI) with recent rises in the Netherlands (37.8% from 2026) and Sweden (22%) [background]; H2GC forecasts an 11ppt channelisation decline in the Netherlands after the 2025 rise and the KSA itself forecast tax receipts −9% — governments facing €22.9bn (~$25.1bn) of claimed 'lost' tax may be tempted to raise rates further on the operators they can reach. structural
  • Entry and compliance costs are high and non-portable: average initial multi-product licence fee €1.1m (~$1.2m), recurring €355k (~$389k), plus bank guarantees/share capital, 27 separate regimes, no passporting and separate AML/RG/advertising stacks per state; transnational licences cost €8,183 (~$9.0k) but confer no EU authorisation. structural
  • The size of the illegal competitor set is contested by 5–8x (GCI €91.6bn/72% vs H2 ~€18bn/27% vs Euromat ~€12bn net/25%); GCI's methodology (audience-based value-per-visit, every transacting domain incl. mirrors, crypto included) is proprietary and industry-commissioned, so policy — and any business case — rests on numbers that cannot be independently reconciled. governance
  • Advertising restrictions are 'significant' in ~46% of markets (Euromat: BE, BG, HR, CY, DE, IT, LV, LT, NL, PL, RO, ES) while unlicensed operators buy cloaked ads with 85% approval and 3x ROAS and reach 91% of engaged audiences — licensed CAC is structurally higher in exactly the largest markets. structural
  • Product bans and monopolies cap the legal offer in major markets: online casino prohibited for licensees in France and Cyprus, state monopoly in Poland, restricted slots/poker-only with €1 stake caps and 5.3% turnover tax in Germany [background], monopolies in Hungary and Slovenia — Eastern Europe is 81% unlicensed and Western Europe 74%. structural
  • Illegal-market growth remains double-digit (+13.6% to €91.6bn, ~$100.3bn in 2025) with the operator count flat at ~6,200 — i.e. the surviving unlicensed brands are getting bigger and more professional (25 brands ≈64% of traffic per Euromat), and illegal streaming of the 2026 World Cup (17.8bn views, 95% with unlicensed ads) shows the recruitment engine is intact. watch
  • Headline figures carry internal inconsistencies — 2025 tax loss €22.9bn (ECA) vs €22.0bn (GCI/CFG), total €128.0bn vs €128.1bn — and the land-based constituency behind the report has an interest in portraying online leakage as maximal; treat the numbers as advocacy-grade, not regulator-grade. governance

10Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The bridge from the 2023 baseline to the 2025 release is a marketplace that grew €49.7bn (~$54.4bn) in annual GGR, of which licensed operators kept €10.7bn (~$11.7bn) and the unlicensed sector €39.0bn (~$42.7bn) by GCI's count; the regulated base moved from €25.8bn to €36.5bn (~$40.0bn) and its share from 33% to 28%. The moving parts for 2026–28 are (i) three market openings (Finland 2027, Austria post-2027 licence expiry, Ireland's GRAI regime), (ii) continued tax escalation in mature markets (Netherlands 37.8% from 2026, Sweden 22%) that H2GC expects to cost channelisation, (iii) an EU-level enforcement pivot toward payments, platforms, affiliates and search/AI via Europol, AMLA and platform rules rather than a gambling directive, and (iv) the contested black-market denominator, where the policy narrative (72% illegal) is far more aggressive than the industry-standard series (25–27%).

Independent view. Our independent view: the licensed EU27 online pool should reach roughly €39–41bn (~$43–45bn) in 2026 and €42–45bn (~$46–49bn) in 2027 (estimate; +8–10% a year, in line with H2's 6.9% EU27+UK CAGR plus the Finnish, Austrian and Irish openings), with online casino compounding faster than sports and online lottery the quiet outperformer. We do not expect licensed share to recover on GCI's measure within two years — enforcement against payments and platforms will take time to bite and the 2026 World Cup will have re-accelerated illegal acquisition — but we also think the 72% figure overstates the economic black market; for planning we would assume an unlicensed competitor set of 25–35% of online GGR in well-regulated Northern/Southern markets and 50%+ in Eastern Europe and product-restricted Western markets. Tax direction is up, not down: governments will read €22.9bn of 'lost' tax as a reason to extract more from licensees rather than to lower rates, so model 2–4pp of GGR-tax creep in mature markets by 2028 and margin compression where turnover taxes persist. Regulatory direction is national licensing plus EU-level enforcement: expect AMLA-driven AML tightening from 2027, DSA/platform pressure on Kick, Meta and Google, payment-blocking expansion, and continued legal attrition of the Malta 'dot-com' model — all of which raise the relative value of holding local licences. Leverage for an entrant is therefore in timing (apply early for FI/AT/IE), product (full casino under GGR tax), and operating cost (shared compliance stack across 5–8 GGR-tax markets rather than a 27-state footprint).

What to watch next quarter

  • Finland: licence application window, go-live on 1 Jan 2027 and the final tax rate and RG rules for private operators
  • Austria: passage of the November 2026 reform through parliament, licence tender timetable ahead of the 2027 expiry, and whether a GGR tax replaces the 40% monopoly levy
  • Ireland: GRAI licence categories, fees and timing of the first licensing windows after the draft eligible-games list
  • Netherlands: 2026 channelisation and tax-take data under the 37.8% rate as the live test of the tax-versus-leakage trade-off
  • EU enforcement: Europol mandate reform, AMLA's first supervisory actions against gambling PSPs/operators, and any Commission or CJEU outcome on Malta's Bill 55
  • Platform action: whether Kick/Stake, cloaked-ad brokers and AI search assistants are addressed under platform rules, and any EU-level payment-blocking coordination
  • Next GCI/ECA data (2026 edition, mid-2027) and the Euromat/H2 updates — specifically whether the illegal-growth deceleration (+13.6% in 2025) held through the 2026 World Cup
  • Germany's GlüStV evaluation and France's periodic online-casino debate — the two largest product-restricted Western markets where any opening would move the regulated pool by billions
