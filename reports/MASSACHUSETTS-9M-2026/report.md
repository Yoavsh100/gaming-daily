# Massachusetts () — 9M 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (market reports in USD; tables in $m unless stated) · Results released 20 Oct 2026 (MGC September 2026 Revenue Report; 9M = Jan–Sep 2026)**

> Massachusetts is a ~$2.0bn (LTM taxable gaming revenue) mature, closed, high-margin market whose growth engine has downshifted: 9M 2026 sports handle rose only 2.0% to $5.99bn (online $5.93bn), with taxable sports revenue +6.0% to $611m carried entirely by hold expansion (10.2% vs 9.8%), while land-based casino GGR fell 2.4% to $880m on Encore weakness. The commercial picture is a DraftKings/FanDuel fortress (74.5% of handle, 78.5% of revenue) under a regulator that is now the most interventionist in the US on bettor limits and enforcement, with a live 51%-tax / prop-ban bill (S.302) and no iGaming path before 2027 — attractive for incumbents' margins, unattractive for a new entrant.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market size and shape — ~$2.05bn LTM taxable gaming revenue, 9M 2026 $1,491m (+0.8% YoY).** Composed of $611.4m taxable sports wagering revenue (GGR-basis, +6.0%) and $879.8m land-based casino GGR (-2.4%). Sports is now 41.0% of the regulated base (38.7% a year ago); there is no legal online casino. Source: MGC September 2026 and September 2025 revenue reports, summed Jan–Sep.
- **Sports handle growth has effectively stalled: 9M 2026 handle $5,994m, +2.0% YoY; online $5,927m, +2.3%.** Quarterly: Q1 $2,132m (-1.4%), Q2 $1,926m (+2.5%), Q3 $1,937m (+5.5%). September 2026 handle was $772.9m, -3.4% YoY — the first September decline since the March 2023 launch. FY2025 handle was $8,529m, so 2026 is tracking roughly flat-to-low-single-digit growth.
- **Revenue is outgrowing handle purely on hold: 9M hold 10.20% vs 9.82% (+38bp); September hold 9.21% vs 6.54%.** Taxable sports revenue rose 6.0% to $611.4m and September revenue jumped 36.0% to $71.2m on a smaller handle — classic parlay/SGP mix and favourable results, not volume. Treat the 9M growth rate as fragile: Q2 revenue was -1.0% YoY even with a 10.7% hold.
- **Online is the whole sports market: 98.9% of handle and 99.1% of taxable revenue.** Retail (three casino sportsbooks) took $67.3m handle in 9M 2026 (-19.4%) for $5.2m revenue; September retail handle fell 37.7%. Retail sports is commercially irrelevant except as the tether for casino-linked online skins.
- **A two-operator fortress: DraftKings 49.5% of handle / 50.8% of revenue; FanDuel 24.9% / 27.6% (combined 74.5% / 78.5%).** Fanatics is the only material share-taker (#3, 9.9% of handle, handle +34.5% YoY); BetMGM 7.7% (-3.2%), Caesars 3.4%, theScore Bet (ex-ESPN Bet) 2.8% (-11.9%), Bally Bet 0.6%. DraftKings' home-state advantage and 10.5% hold give it $312m of 9M revenue in one state.
- **Land-based casinos are flat-to-down: 9M GGR $879.8m (-2.4%).** Encore Boston Harbor $529.2m (-4.7%; September -22.1% to $46.1m, both tables and slots down), MGM Springfield $214.6m (+1.1%), Plainridge Park slots parlor $136.0m (+1.1%). September casino GGR of $82.7m (-13.6%) was the weakest September since 2020. MGC publishes no commentary; the Encore drop is unexplained and a watch item.
- **Tax regime — GGR-based, no promotional deductions, with negative-month carry-forward.** Online sports (Category 3) 20% of adjusted gross sports wagering receipts; retail (Cat 1/2) 15%; Category 1 resort casinos 25% of GGR; Category 2 slots parlor 49%; DFS 15% of net adjusted receipts. Effective 9M rates: sports 19.96%, casino 28.7% (blend). Federal 0.25% excise is deductible; promo credits are not (per 23N §3 definition quoted in the MGC glossary).
- **Tax take 9M 2026 $374.6m (+0.5%): sports $122.0m (+6.0%), casino $252.6m (-2.0%).** Plainridge pays $66.7m on $136m GGR (49% rate) versus MGM Springfield's $53.6m on $215m. September alone: $38.4m total (+1.6%). FY2025 sports tax was ~$168m; the 51% proposal would, on 2025 revenue, lift it to ~$430m (estimate, static).
- **Licensing is closed and capped.** Expanded Gaming Act 2011 (G.L. c.23K): max three Category 1 resort casinos (only two awarded — Region C never licensed) and one Category 2 slots parlor. Sports Wagering Act 2022 (G.L. c.23N): up to 15 online licences — 8 tethered to land-based partners (2 per casino, 1 per racetrack) and 7 untethered — but only 7 online books are live, so roughly 8 slots sit unused [slot arithmetic unverified this session]. Licence fee is $5m per 5-year term [unverified this session]; first licences expire 2028, putting renewals on a 2027–28 timetable.
- **The tax-hike / product-ban bill is live but not enacted.** Sen. John Keenan's S.302 'Bettor Health Act' cleared its Senate committee 5-0 in March 2026: online tax 20%→51%, ban on player props and in-game betting, ban on sports betting ads during televised games, affordability checks tied to account balances. No enactment was found before the 31 Jul 2026 end of formal session [status unverified]; expect a refile in January 2027 with the same sponsor and a more receptive AG.
- **iGaming is off the table until at least the 2027–28 session.** H.4431 (Rep. Muradian), which would have created Chapter 23O and let the three casino licensees offer online slots/tables via the MGC, was sent to study by the Joint Committee on Economic Development and Emerging Technologies on 16 Mar 2026 — procedurally dead. Sweepstakes casinos remain unregulated and untouched; the MGC says it does not regulate them.
- **Prediction markets are being pushed out — a defensive positive for licensees.** AG Campbell sued Kalshi (Sept 2025); Suffolk Superior Court granted a preliminary injunction (20 Jan 2026) barring sports event contracts to Massachusetts customers and denied a stay (Feb 2026); Kalshi's appeal was argued at the Supreme Judicial Court on 4 May 2026 with 38 state AGs filing in support. The SJC outcome could not be verified as of 6 Nov 2026 — it is the single biggest near-term swing factor for licensed handle.
- **Enforcement is frequent, granular and public.** 13 event-catalog penalties in 2026 to date ($5,000–$25,000; MGM Springfield $25,000 in June, Caesars $10,000 in March) after a $60,000 BetMGM penalty in Dec 2025 (incl. 2,564 prohibited soccer-card wagers). The 5 Nov 2026 agenda adds briefings on Fanatics (refer-a-friend bonuses; failure to send limit notices), BetMGM (out-of-state wagers), FanDuel (proxy wagering) and penalty votes on Caesars (unauthorised events, one involving a Massachusetts college team) and MGM Springfield.
- **First-in-US bettor-limitation transparency rule.** Adopted 5-0 on 26 Feb 2026 and effective 1 Jun 2026, operators that limit an account must tell the patron why and which markets are affected (205 CMR 238.30(11)); 205 CMR 255 play-management (bettor-set limits, monthly notifications) goes to final adoption on 5 Nov 2026. This directly constrains the risk-management playbook that underpins 10%+ holds.
- **Responsible-gambling and advertising rules are strict and statutory.** Minimum age 21; no wagering on in-state college teams outside tournaments of four-plus teams; no high-school events; credit-card funding of sports accounts prohibited [per 23N, unverified this session]; MGC advertising code (205 CMR 256) bars targeting under-21s and college campuses; unified Voluntary Self-Exclusion across casinos and sports; GameSense and PlayMyWay at casinos. A 'Youth Sports Betting Safety Coalition' run by the AG adds political pressure.
- **Lottery has gone digital, adding a state-owned online competitor.** The Massachusetts Lottery launched iLottery (online tickets and e-instants, 21+, Aristocrat Interactive platform) on 27 Jul 2026, two years after FY25 budget authorisation. The Lottery is already one of the largest per-capita in the US (FY sales and profit figures not verified in this session) and will now compete for the same mobile wallet as sports and any future iGaming.
- **DFS is shrinking as pick'em migrates to sportsbooks.** September 2026 gross fantasy receipts $11.5m (-35% YoY), net adjusted receipts $1.66m (-42%), tax $0.25m at 15%. In Sept 2025 DraftKings was $9.3m of $17.8m gross, PrizePicks $3.8m, Underdog $1.7m; the 2026 report no longer itemises operators in the summary.
- **Read-through for B2Tech: a licence is theoretically obtainable, a business is not.** Entry would require either an untethered Category 3 award through a reopened MGC process or a tethered skin from Encore/MGM/the racetracks, a $5m fee, 20% GGR tax with no promo deduction, a limit-notification regime, and a market where the top two hold 78.5% of revenue and the #3–#7 share $127m of 9M revenue between them. Massachusetts is a market to serve via B2B/platform supply to an incumbent or to monitor for iGaming (2027+), not to enter as a B2C sportsbook.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Sports wagering — online (Category 3) and retail

*Handle +2.0% to $5.99bn, revenue +6.0% to $611m on a 10.2% hold; DraftKings/FanDuel hold 78.5% of revenue and the only share mover is Fanatics.*

### Handle

**9M 2026 wagers settled were $5,994.3m, up 2.0% on $5,877.1m, with online +2.3% and retail -19.4%.**

- Online handle $5,927.0m (+2.3%); retail $67.3m (-19.4%); Sept 2025 handle later revised from $800.26m to $800.46m.
- Monthly handle ran $813m in January, 2026 low of $571m in August, rebounded to $773m in September with the NFL.
- September was still 3.4% below the record $800m of September 2025.
- Quarterly pattern Q1 -1.4%, Q2 +2.5%, Q3 +5.5% shows modest re-acceleration, helped by comparison base rather than new demand driver.

### Revenue and hold

**Taxable revenue was $611.4m, +6.0%, on a 10.20% hold versus 9.82% in 9M 2025; the growth spread is entirely hold.**

- Taxable revenue is gross receipts less winnings paid and federal excise; promotional credits are not deductible.
- Q1 hold 10.36%; Q2 10.73% yet revenue -1.0% YoY on a tough comp; Q3 9.49% (+17.7%) against September 2025 hold of 6.5%.
- September 2026 revenue $71.2m (+36.0%) and tax $14.2m (+36.1%) say more about results and parlay mix than market expansion.
- FY2025 revenue $841.7m on $8,529m handle (9.87% hold); LTM to September 2026 $876.2m on $8,646m.

### Operator structure

**DraftKings and FanDuel hold 74.5% of handle and 78.5% of revenue; Fanatics is the only operator gaining meaningfully.**

- DraftKings: $2,966.8m handle (49.5%), $312.3m revenue (50.8%), 10.5% hold, $62.5m tax; FanDuel: $1,494.7m (24.9%), $169.8m (27.6%), 11.4% hold.
- Fanatics (tethered to Plainridge Park) handle +34.5% to $592.7m (9.9%), revenue +43% to $49.7m, at an 8.4% hold suggesting it is still buying share.
- BetMGM slipped 3.2% to $460.2m (7.7%); Caesars grew 10.1% to $204.4m but at 6.8% hold earns only 2.3% of revenue.
- theScore Bet (Penn's replacement for ESPN Bet from late 2025) fell 11.9% to $167.0m; Bally Bet is sub-scale at $37.2m.
- Five books outside the duopoly shared $127.1m revenue over nine months, roughly $25m each before 20% tax and marketing.

### Retail

**Retail is a rounding error and shrinking: $67.3m handle for $5.2m revenue across three casino sportsbooks.**

- Encore $33.1m handle, Plainridge $24.8m, MGM Springfield $9.4m; MGM Springfield booked zero taxable revenue in several months as negative results carried forward under 23N.
- No Category 2 (racetrack/simulcast) retail sportsbook is reporting revenue.
- Retail matters only because each casino licence carries up to two online skins and racetracks one each — the tether route for Caesars, BetMGM, Fanatics and theScore Bet.

### Demand-side rules

**Massachusetts' 10%+ holds are earned under a regulator actively policing the tools used to protect that hold.**

- Bettors must be 21+; in-state college teams off-limits outside tournaments of four or more teams; high-school events banned.
- MGC event catalog enforced wager-by-wager; officiating markets such as soccer cards prohibited since December 2023.
- From 1 Jun 2026 any limited account must receive a notice stating the reason and markets affected; Fanatics already faces a non-compliance briefing.

### Commercial opportunity

**Massachusetts is high-ARPU, low-growth; absent a differentiated product or casino-tether partnership, the economics for a new entrant do not clear.**

- ~$845 of handle per resident over nine months (estimate, ~7.1m population), with handle growth below US averages.
- Incremental opportunity for a new Category 3 entrant is the ~21% of revenue not held by DraftKings/FanDuel, against four well-funded national brands.
- 20% GGR tax and a $5m five-year licence fee [fee unverified this session].

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Land-based casinos — Encore Boston Harbor, MGM Springfield, Plainridge Park

*9M GGR $879.8m (-2.4%) and tax $252.6m (-2.0%); Encore's September slump (-22%) drags an otherwise flat market.*

### 9M GGR

**9M 2026 GGR was $879.8m (-2.4% from $901.8m) with taxes of $252.6m (-2.0%); the base has been flat around $1.2bn for four years.**

- Encore Boston Harbor (Wynn; Region A Category 1) is 60.2% of the market at $529.2m but fell 4.7%.
- MGM Springfield (Region B Category 1) grew 1.1% to $214.6m; Plainridge Park Casino (Penn; Category 2 slots-only) grew 1.1% to $136.0m.
- FY2025 casino GGR $1,195m; LTM to September 2026 $1,173m.
- September GGR: 2022 $95.8m, 2023 $93.8m, 2024 $92.1m, 2025 $95.7m, 2026 $82.7m.

### September weakness

**September 2026 GGR of $82.7m (-13.6%) was the lowest September since 2020, driven by Encore at $46.1m (-22.1%).**

- Encore table GGR $19.1m versus $23.9m and slots $26.9m versus $35.3m a year earlier.
- MGM Springfield $22.0m (-1.2%); Plainridge $14.6m (+2.4%).
- MGC report gives no explanation; a one-month table-hold swing cannot explain a 24% slot decline, so check for property-specific disruption before extrapolating.
- Monthly 2026 GGR otherwise ran $92m–$108m, peaking in March ($107.8m) and May ($105.9m).

### Tax structure

**Tax is punitive and asymmetric: Category 1 resorts pay 25% of GGR, the Category 2 slots parlor 49%; blended effective rate is 28.7%.**

- Plainridge remitted $66.7m on $136.0m of GGR in 9M 2026, more than MGM Springfield's $53.6m on $214.6m; Encore paid $132.3m.
- Casino tax allocated by statute across local aid, transportation, education, debt reduction and the Public Health Trust Fund (percentages not reproduced here).

### Licensing closed

**No realistic greenfield land-based entry exists; the only route for a new operator is M&A of an existing licensee.**

- Expanded Gaming Act (2011) capped the Commonwealth at three resort-casino licences (one per region) and one slots parlor; Region C was never awarded.
- The Mashpee Wampanoag tribal project that complicated Region C has not produced a casino.
- Category 1 licences were $85m for 15 years and Category 2 $25m for five years [fee/term figures unverified this session].
- First resort-casino renewals fall in 2033–34 (MGM Springfield opened Aug 2018, Encore Jun 2019); Plainridge is on a rolling five-year cycle.

### Relevance to online

**For an online-first operator, a casino partnership is the tether route for sports skins and the likely gateway to any future iGaming market.**

- Each casino licence carries up to two online sports skins.
- If Chapter 23O is ever enacted, the shelved H.4431 text would have restricted iGaming licences to existing casino licensees.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Lottery, DFS and the unregulated fringe (iLottery, sweepstakes, prediction markets)

*iLottery live since 27 Jul 2026, DFS shrinking (-42% net receipts in Sep), sweepstakes untouched, Kalshi enjoined pending the SJC.*

### iLottery

**The State Lottery launched online sales and e-instant games on 27 Jul 2026, creating a state-owned digital competitor in a state with no legal online casino.**

- Platform is Aristocrat Interactive; launch came two years after the FY2025 budget authorised iLottery and after slipping from a late-2025 target.
- Play restricted to 21+, aligning with casino and sports rules.
- Historically one of the highest per-capita lotteries in the US and the state's largest gambling revenue source (FY figures not verified, omitted).
- Lottery revenue is not reported through the MGC and does not appear in the figures above.

### Daily fantasy sports

**DFS is shrinking — September 2026 net adjusted receipts $1.66m (-42.5%) — and is a sub-$5m-a-year tax line, no longer a strategic channel.**

- Regulated by registration with the MGC and taxed at 15% of net adjusted gross fantasy receipts under the 2022 sports wagering law.
- September 2026 gross receipts $11.5m (-35.4% YoY); tax $0.25m.
- In September 2025 DraftKings accounted for $9.3m of $17.8m gross receipts, PrizePicks $3.8m and Underdog $1.7m.
- Decline consistent with pick'em-style products migrating into licensed sportsbooks' parlay menus.

### Sweepstakes casinos

**Sweepstakes-model casinos are the de facto online casino in Massachusetts today, untaxed, unmeasured and unregulated by the MGC.**

- Not addressed when H.4431 was sent to study in March 2026; trade sources count 25+ accessible sites.
- Attorney General issued cease-and-desist letters to two offshore casino/sports operators in June 2025, signalling appetite to act under existing law.
- No sweepstakes-specific statute or enforcement wave emerged in 2026.

### Prediction markets

**Kalshi is enjoined from accepting Massachusetts sports contracts pending the SJC; the outcome decides whether a zero-tax competitor re-enters.**

- AG Campbell sued KalshiEX in September 2025, alleging unlicensed sports wagering dressed as event contracts (moneyline, spread and totals contracts).
- Suffolk Superior Court granted a preliminary injunction on 20 Jan 2026 until licensed by the MGC; emergency stay denied in February.
- Appeal argued before the Supreme Judicial Court on 4 May 2026; 38 attorneys general filed in support of the Commonwealth; decision not verified as of 6 Nov 2026.
- If upheld, Massachusetts becomes a template for excluding CFTC-regulated sports contracts; if reversed, a no-licence competitor re-enters against a 20%-taxed market.

### Operator implication

**The AG's posture protects licensed handle from prediction markets, but the same office frames all sports betting as a public-health harm.**

- The AG backs the Keenan bill's restrictions.
- The enforcement energy that removes Kalshi is the same energy that proposes a 51% tax.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing and tax regime

*Capped, closed licensing; GGR-based taxes of 20%/15% (sports), 25%/49% (casino), 15% (DFS); S.302 would take online sports to 51%.*

### Statutory framework

**Two statutes define a capped, closed market: c.23K (2011) for casinos and c.23N (2022) for sports wagering with 15 online slots, seven live.**

- c.23K created the five-member MGC (chair Jordan Maynard, appointed by Governor Healey October 2024); authorised three Category 1 resorts and one Category 2 slots parlor; only Regions A and B licensed.
- H.5164 (signed 10 Aug 2022) created Category 1 (casinos; retail plus up to two skins), Category 2 (racetracks; retail plus one skin), Category 3 (untethered online, capped at seven).
- Retail launched 31 Jan 2023, online 10 Mar 2023; 15 online operators permitted (8 tethered, 7 untethered), seven live, implying material unused capacity [slot arithmetic unverified].
- Sports licences run five years at a $5m fee [unverified this session], so first renewals fall in 2028.

### Tax bases and rates

**All taxes are gross-revenue measures with no promotional deduction: 20%/15% sports, 25%/49% casino, 15% DFS.**

- Adjusted gross sports wagering receipts are total gross receipts less winnings paid and federal excise (0.25% of handle), per c.23N §3.
- Negative months are carried forward against later months, visible in MGM Springfield's zero-tax months.
- 9M 2026 sports collections were $121.2m online (20%) and $0.8m retail (15%).
- Sports tax distributed by statute to the General Fund, Gaming Local Aid, Workforce Investment Trust, Public Health Trust and Youth Development funds (percentages not verified).

### S.302 tax threat

**Senator Keenan's 'Bettor Health Act' would raise the online rate from 20% to 51%, ban props and in-game wagers, and restrict advertising.**

- Reported out of committee 5-0 in March 2026; 51% matches New York, Rhode Island and New Hampshire.
- Would bar sports betting advertising during televised games and require affordability assessments tied to account balances; trade reports cite a $1,000 daily wager cap (lower reliability).
- No enactment found before the formal session ended 31 Jul 2026 [status unverified].
- Static calculation on FY2025 revenue of $842m implies state sports tax rising from ~$168m to ~$430m (estimate), but product bans would shrink the base since parlays and props drive the 10%+ hold.

### iGaming legislation

**H.4431 was sent to study unanimously on 16 Mar 2026, effectively ending iGaming for the 2025–26 session; earliest realistic enactment is 2027–28.**

- H.4431 (Rep. David Muradian) would have added Chapter 23O and limited online casino licences to existing casino operators via the MGC.
- Headwinds: the Lottery's new iLottery and problem-gambling concerns; tailwind: the state's budget appetite.

- A casino tether is the only route to a future iGaming licence as currently drafted.
- The regulator's 2026 enforcement cadence — penalties of $5,000–$60,000 are small but public and frequent — adds compliance cost disproportionate to revenue available outside the duopoly.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising and enforcement

*Credit cards barred, 21+, first-in-US limit-notice rule from 1 Jun 2026, and an enforcement docket that touches every major operator.*

### Payments and geolocation

**Credit-card funding is barred and geolocation must place the bettor in-state; BetMGM and FanDuel face 5 Nov 2026 briefings.**

- Credit cards barred [per c.23N; not re-verified]; deposits via debit, ACH/online banking, PayPal-type wallets, prepaid cards and cash at casino cages; no mobile-money dimension.
- BetMGM allegedly accepted out-of-state wagers under c.23N §13(b) and 205 CMR 243/247.
- Proxy wagering prohibited (c.23N §11(a); 205 CMR 248.17); FanDuel briefing follows a podcaster's on-air admission that a relative bet through his account, self-reported and voided.

### Responsible gambling

**The MGC adopted a first-in-US rule (205 CMR 238.30(11)) requiring operators to notify limited patrons of the reason and markets, effective 1 Jun 2026.**

- One Voluntary Self-Exclusion list covers casinos, sports or both; GameSense advisors and PlayMyWay at casinos; mandatory play-management tools online.
- Rule adopted 5-0 on 26 Feb 2026; Fanatics already cited for alleged non-compliance.
- On 5 Nov 2026 the Commission votes final adoption of amended 205 CMR 255, moving limit-reminder cadence to monthly/30 days.
- The Keenan bill would add affordability checks against account balances and, per trade reports, a daily wager cap.

### Advertising

**Advertising under 205 CMR 256 is already restrictive and the direction of travel is tighter.**

- No content appealing to or depicting under-21s, no college campus advertising or partnerships, mandatory problem-gambling messaging, restrictions on inducement language.
- AG's Youth Sports Betting Safety Coalition (formed March 2024) and S.302's proposed ban on ads during televised games signal tightening.
- Fanatics' refer-a-friend bonuses are alleged to breach 205 CMR 247.09(4).

### Enforcement

**Enforcement is routine: 13 sports wagering penalties in 2026, all event-catalog breaches, from $5,000 to $25,000, following a $60,000 BetMGM penalty.**

- MGC 2026 table (updated 24 Aug 2026): MGM Springfield $25,000 on 22 Jun 2026; Caesars $10,000 on 12 Mar 2026.
- December 2025 BetMGM $60,000 penalty bundled six matters including 2,564 prohibited soccer-card wagers.
- 5 Nov 2026 meeting (No. 601) adds Caesars (unauthorised events, one involving a Massachusetts collegiate team) and MGM Springfield (unapproved offering) penalty votes.
- Fines are immaterial financially but each generates a public decision and press cycle, and repeat findings feed into the 2028 licence-renewal record.

### Operator implication

**Compliance is a standing cost centre that scales with headcount, not revenue — a meaningful drag for a sub-5%-share operator.**

- Covers event-catalog controls, geofencing, promotional-term review and limit-notice workflows.
- A sub-5%-share operator would yield perhaps $15–25m of annual GGR in the state.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Premium monetisation: 9M 2026 hold of 10.2% (FanDuel 11.4%, DraftKings 10.5%) on a GGR tax of 20% leaves one of the better net margins among large US states — provided the product mix (parlays/props) survives S.302. _(structural)_
- Regulatory moat against prediction markets: the AG's preliminary injunction against Kalshi (Jan 2026), backed by 38 state AGs at the SJC, protects licensed handle from a zero-tax competitor if upheld. _(watch)_
- Unused licence capacity: the statute allows 15 online operators but only 7 are live; tethered skins at Encore (1), MGM Springfield (1) and the two racetracks appear available, giving a B2B/skin route without a competitive RFA [slot count unverified]. _(structural)_
- Share is still moving below the duopoly: Fanatics grew handle 34.5% to 9.9% share in 9M 2026 and Caesars +10.1%, showing a tethered challenger can take share from BetMGM/theScore Bet with product and loyalty rather than promo spend. _(early)_
- Future iGaming optionality via casino partnership: the shelved H.4431 would have confined online casino licences to the three casino licensees; a tether with Encore or MGM Springfield positions an operator for a 2027–28 reopening. _(early)_
- Stable, transparent regulator: monthly operator-level handle/revenue/tax disclosure, a fixed five-member commission, and predictable rulemaking cycles reduce execution risk relative to emerging markets. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Tax-rate cliff: S.302 (51% online tax, prop/in-play ban, TV ad ban, affordability checks) cleared committee 5-0 in March 2026 and will almost certainly be refiled in January 2027; at 51% the #3–#7 operators' combined 9M revenue of $127m would not cover marketing and compliance. _(structural)_
- Growth has stalled: handle +2.0% in 9M 2026 and -3.4% in September; Q1 was negative. The 6% revenue growth is hold-driven and reverses with results — Q2 revenue was -1.0% YoY. _(structural)_
- Duopoly lock-in: DraftKings (home state) and FanDuel hold 74.5% of handle and 78.5% of revenue; the remaining five brands average ~$25m of 9M revenue each before 20% tax. _(structural)_
- The most interventionist US regulator on risk management: mandatory limit-notification (from 1 Jun 2026), play-management rules, event-catalog penalties on every operator in 2026, and an AG who frames sports betting as a public-health harm. _(governance)_
- No iGaming before 2027–28 at the earliest, while the Lottery's iLottery (live 27 Jul 2026) and unregulated sweepstakes sites absorb the casual digital casino wallet in the meantime. _(structural)_
- Land-based weakness: casino GGR -2.4% in 9M 2026 and Encore -22% in September with no regulator commentary — relevant to any tether partner's health and to the 49%/25% tax base the state relies on. _(watch)_
- Kalshi reversal risk: if the SJC sides with Kalshi, a federally-regulated, untaxed sports-contract venue returns to Massachusetts against a 20%-taxed licensed market — the outcome was unverifiable as of 6 Nov 2026. _(one-off)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The 9M 2026 print moves the Massachusetts base case from 'growth market' to 'mature, hold-driven market': sports handle of $5.99bn (+2.0%) and revenue of $611m (+6.0%) versus a prior-year pace of double-digit growth, with the land-based segment flat-to-down ($880m, -2.4%). The division driving the change is online sports — handle growth of 2.3% is the slowest since launch — while the swing in revenue is almost entirely a 38bp hold uplift. The moving parts for the next 12–24 months are regulatory rather than commercial: the fate of S.302 (51% tax, prop/in-play ban), the SJC's Kalshi ruling, the 2028 licence-renewal cycle, and whether iGaming is refiled in the 2027–28 session.

- **FY2025 sports revenue (actual):** $841.7m (handle $8.53bn; hold 9.87%)
- **FY2026E sports revenue (estimate):** ~$880–900m (+5–7%: handle ~flat to +2%, hold ~10%)
- **FY2026E casino GGR (estimate):** ~$1,170–1,185m (-1% to -2% vs $1,195m)
- **S.302 scenario — state sports tax:** ~$430m vs ~$175m (static 51% on FY2026E base; before product-ban base erosion)

Our independent view: **revenue grows low-to-mid single digits, not volume.** We expect FY2026 sports handle of roughly $8.5–8.7bn (estimate, ~flat to +2%) and taxable revenue of $880–900m (estimate), with Q4 dependent on NFL/NBA results; casino GGR finishes around $1.17–1.19bn (estimate). **Margins for incumbents stay attractive** at a 20% GGR tax and 10% hold, but we assign a meaningful probability (we would say roughly one-in-three over two sessions) that Massachusetts legislates some combination of a higher rate and product restrictions by 2028, given a Senate committee that voted 5-0, an AG aligned with the sponsor, and a 2028 licence-renewal window that gives the legislature leverage. **Leverage/structure direction:** expect further consolidation to five or six online brands — theScore Bet and Bally Bet are sub-scale — and expect any new entrant to arrive via a casino tether rather than a fresh Category 3 award. **iGaming**: not before 2027–28; the Lottery's iLottery and the AG's public-health framing make even that uncertain, and any bill will likely restrict licences to casino licensees. **Kalshi**: an SJC affirmance would be the most valuable regulatory development of 2026 for licensees; a reversal would be a 2027 handle headwind. For B2Tech, the actionable positions are B2B platform/content supply to an incumbent or tethered partner, and a watching brief on Chapter 23O — not a B2C sportsbook launch.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Supreme Judicial Court decision in Commonwealth v. KalshiEX (argued 4 May 2026): affirmance locks prediction markets out; reversal reopens a zero-tax competitor.
- Refiling of S.302 (Bettor Health Act) in January 2027 and whether the House and Governor Healey engage — the 51% rate and prop/in-play ban would reset operator economics and the hold structure.
- Q4 2026 / FY2026 MGC revenue reports (December report due ~mid-Jan 2027): whether handle re-accelerates with the NFL/NBA season or Q1-2026-style declines return; whether Encore Boston Harbor's September slump persists.

- MGC enforcement outcomes on 5 Nov 2026 (Fanatics, BetMGM, FanDuel, Caesars, MGM Springfield) and any escalation from five-figure penalties to licence conditions.
