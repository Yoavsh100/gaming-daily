# Great Britain () — 2025/26 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: GBP (tables in £ millions unless stated; ~USD alongside at GBP/USD 1.34 — see confidence note) · Results released 17 Sep 2026 (Industry Statistics FY2025/26); 19 Nov 2026 (Gambling Commission speech on LA inspections / enforcement)**

> Great Britain remains the largest fully-regulated online gambling market in Europe and is still growing — FY2025/26 GGY £17.5bn (~$23.4bn), +4.4%, with online casino (+£736m, ~$986m) delivering effectively all of the growth while online betting and betting shops went backwards. But the state is now taking a far larger share: Remote Gaming Duty doubled to 40% of GGY from 1 April 2026, remote betting duty rises from 15% to 25% in April 2027, a statutory levy (~1.1% of online GGY) started in April 2025, and enforcement — local inspections +29%, £26m of new anti-illegal-gambling funding, FATF-driven AML focus — is visibly tightening. For B2Tech the market offers scale, legal certainty and mature payments, but post-tax online casino economics now favour only the largest, most efficient operators, and the regulatory direction of travel is unambiguously restrictive.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Market size and growth: GB is a £17.5bn (~$23.4bn) GGY market, +4.4% YoY in FY2025/26 (April 2025–March 2026).** Excluding the National Lottery and society lotteries, licensed GGY was £13.2bn (~$17.7bn), +4.7%. Source: Gambling Commission Industry Statistics (official statistics, published 17 Sep 2026). GGY = stakes less prizes/winnings, before duty, levy and bonus costs — i.e. a gross, not net, measure.
- **Growth is almost entirely online casino.** Online casino GGY rose £736m (~$986m) to £5.7bn (~$7.6bn) — ≈+15% implied — against a £728m (~$976m) rise for the whole licensed market. Slots alone were £4.8bn (~$6.4bn), i.e. 84% of online casino and 27% of all licensed GGY. The regulator itself (Director of Policy Ian Angus, 19 Nov 2026) says online casino is “basically driving the entire headline figure for growth.”
- **Online penetration: the remote casino, betting and bingo (RCBB) sector made £8.3bn (~$11.1bn), +6.9%, equal to 47% of total GGY and 63% of GGY excluding lotteries.** Land-based sectors (arcades, betting, bingo, casino) made £4.9bn (~$6.6bn), +1.1%; lotteries account for the ~£4.3bn (~$5.8bn) balance (implied).
- **Online betting is shrinking in GGY terms.** Remote betting GGY was £2.4bn (~$3.2bn) — football £1.2bn (~$1.6bn), horse racing £769.3m (~$1.0bn). With RCBB up only ~£536m while casino rose £736m, remote betting plus bingo fell by roughly £0.2bn (c.−7%, implied, rounding-sensitive). Remote bingo was £147.8m (~$198m).
- **Retail is a two-speed estate.** Betting-shop GGY fell 3.3% to £2.4bn (~$3.2bn) (machines £1.2bn, −0.9%, = 49.4% of shop GGY) and the shop count fell for a 12th consecutive period to 5,617 (−208, −3.6%). Adult gaming centres grew 11.3% to £761.4m (~$1.0bn) and their premises rose from 1,417 to 1,449; retail bingo +8.2% to £703.8m (~$943m); casinos flat (+0.4%) at £933.9m (~$1.3bn). Total premises 8,081 (−2.0%).
- **Gaming machines are a £2.7bn (~$3.6bn) product (+4.3%) across all venues, but still ~£100m (~$134m) below the pre-2019 B2 stake-cut level** per the Commission — a reminder that stake caps have permanent, not transitory, revenue effects.
- **Regulator and legal framework: the Gambling Act 2005 (as amended), with the Gambling Commission licensing operators, personal licence-holders and the National Lottery, and 300+ local licensing authorities licensing premises** under a statutory co-regulatory model. The Commission licensed 2,154 operators (−1.1%) holding 3,097 licensed activities (+0.4%) at 31 March 2026.
- **Tax regime — the big change.** Autumn Budget 2025 (26 Nov 2025, enacted via Finance Act 2026 ss.86–88) raised Remote Gaming Duty on online casino/bingo/slots GGY from 21% to 40% from 1 April 2026; created a 25% remote-betting rate within General Betting Duty (vs 15%) from 1 April 2027, with remote bets on UK horseracing excluded; and abolished the 10% Bingo Duty on retail bingo from 1 April 2026. Tax base throughout is GGY/profit, not turnover (Lottery Duty is the exception at 12% of ticket sales).
- **Tax take: HMRC collected a record £3.83bn (~$5.1bn) of betting and gaming duties in FY2025/26, +5.9% (+£215m)** — 22% of total GGY on a blended basis. The OBR projects ~£5bn (~$6.7bn) in 2026/27 and ~£6bn (~$8.0bn) by 2030/31 (secondary citation of OBR Nov-2025 forecast). Early FY26/27 data: April–July 2026 receipts £1.933bn (~$2.6bn), +19%, with RGD alone now 50% of the total (July RGD ≈£590m vs £283m a year earlier).
- **No early sign of volume leakage after the RGD hike.** July’s ~£590m (~$791m) RGD receipt (the first quarter paid at 40%) implies quarterly remote-gaming GGY of ~£1.5bn — an annualised ~£5.9bn, in line with FY25/26’s £5.85bn of online casino + bingo (our estimate). The industry’s black-market argument is, so far, not visible in the Exchequer data.
- **Statutory levy replaced voluntary RET contributions from 6 April 2025** — 1.1% of GGY for remote operators, lower bands (0.5%/0.2%/0.1%) for land-based and lotteries (rates from the Gambling Levy Regulations 2025; not re-verified in this pass). On FY25/26 GGY that is ~£110m (~$147m) p.a. (our estimate), on top of duty. Effective fiscal take on online casino GGY from April 2026 is therefore ~41% before licence fees.
- **Operator landscape: concentrated at the top, long tail below.** Leaders are Flutter (Sky Bet, Paddy Power, Betfair, tombola), Entain (Ladbrokes, Coral), bet365, Evoke (William Hill, 888), Betfred, Rank (Grosvenor, Mecca), FDJ United (Unibet/32Red) and Allwyn (National Lottery). The Commission does not publish market shares — n/a. Smaller books are already exiting: BresBet and Bet St George surrendered licences on 4 Sep 2026 (secondary).
- **Customer base: 25.7m active online accounts at end-March 2026, 32.4m new registrations in the year (−3.0%), customer funds held £886.6m (~$1.2bn), −13.9%** — the first signs of a plateau in acquisition even as GGY grows, consistent with tighter affordability checks and the £150/month financial-vulnerability trigger.
- **Participation is stable: 47% of adults gambled in the past four weeks in 2025 (GSGB), but only 27% once lottery-only players are excluded; in-person participation 28% (17% ex-lottery).**
- **Payments: card-based, bank-transfer and e-wallet market; credit cards banned for gambling since April 2020; no mobile-money layer.** Operators must prompt deposit-limit setting at registration (from Oct 2025) and run light-touch financial vulnerability checks at £150 (~$201) net deposits/month (standing rules, not re-verified this pass).
- **Responsible-gambling rules tightened materially in FY25/26:** online slot stake caps of £5 (~$7) per spin for 25+ and £2 (~$3) for 18–24s (from April/May 2025); bonus wagering requirements capped at 10x and mixed-product promotions banned (Dec 2025); product/channel-level marketing opt-in (Jan 2026); new category-B machine rules (sessional data, design restrictions) with the bulk effective June 2027.
- **Advertising: CAP/BCAP ‘strong appeal’ rules and the industry whistle-to-whistle code apply; Premier League clubs’ voluntary front-of-shirt ban starts in 2026/27.** The Commission’s new remit also targets advertising and payments that support unlicensed sites (Sue Young, Nov 2026).
- **Enforcement is rising, not falling.** Local authority inspections of premises +29% in FY25/26 (“unprecedented”); the Commission ran 160+ investigations, processed 2,000 intelligence reports and imposed £19.6m (~$26m) of penalty packages over the year (Commission enforcement report via CMS); headline cases include Platinum Gaming £10m, Evolution Malta £4.75m (supplying unlicensed sites), Spreadex £2.0m. A £26m (~$35m) three-year Budget allocation funds illegal-gambling work, £7m drawn in year one; joint police raids in Manchester, Doncaster, Birmingham, Bristol and Sheffield.
- **Political direction: further tightening at local level.** Gambling Impact Assessments came into force in November 2026 (councils may weigh crime, deprivation, public-health data and premises clustering); MHCLG’s 2 Nov 2026 High Street paper flags a review of the Act’s ‘aim to permit’ principle and a DCMS consultation is expected; the Commission’s next five-year Corporate Strategy (April 2027) prioritises crime prevention and AML ahead of the UK’s FATF evaluation.
- **Read-through for B2Tech: GB is a scale, not a margin, market.** Legal certainty, deep payments rails and a £5.7bn online casino pool are real, but a 40% RGD + 1.1% levy + tiered licence fees + affordability friction means a new entrant needs either a differentiated product/brand or a B2B (platform/content) angle rather than a me-too B2C casino. The sports-betting pool is contracting in GGY and faces a 10-point duty rise in April 2027; retail betting is in structural decline; AGC/bingo are the only growing land-based niches and are now in the political cross-hairs.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online — remote casino, betting & bingo (RCBB)

*£8.3bn (+6.9%) sector where slots carry everything: online casino +£736m while betting went backwards; 40% RGD now applies.*

### Sector size and mix

**RCBB GGY was £8.3bn (~$11.1bn) in FY2025/26, up 6.9%, 63% of licensed GGY ex-lotteries (47% of headline £17.5bn).**

- Data is operator-reported GGY before RGD, GBD, levy and bonus/free-bet costs; operators' net revenue is materially lower.
- Online casino £5.7bn (~$7.6bn): slots £4.8bn (~$6.4bn), other casino ~£0.9bn (~$1.2bn, implied).
- Remote betting £2.4bn (~$3.2bn): football £1.2bn (~$1.6bn), horse racing £769.3m (~$1.0bn), other ~£0.43bn (~$576m, implied).
- Remote bingo £147.8m (~$198m).

### Growth arithmetic

**Online casino GGY rose £736m (~$986m), ≈15%, while remote betting plus bingo fell roughly £0.2bn, around 7%; the most important fact.**

- Whole licensed market rose £728m (~$976m); RCBB rose only ~£536m.
- Betting decline came in a year with no major summer football tournament and customer-friendly results; split is our rounding-sensitive derivation.
- GB online betting pool is not growing in GGY terms; casino compounds at double digits despite £5/£2 slot caps and affordability checks.

### Customer metrics

**Acquisition is plateauing as yield grows: registrations fell 3.0% to 32.4m and customer funds fell 13.9% to £886.6m (~$1.2bn).**

- Active accounts at end-March 2026 were 25.7m; an account is not a person, multi-accounting across ~20 brands is the norm.
- Lower balances consistent with £150/month light-touch checks, mandatory deposit-limit prompts from October 2025 and shrinking bonus liabilities.
- December 2025 brought a 10x cap on wagering requirements and a ban on mixed-product promotions.

### Duty changes

**Remote Gaming Duty rose from 21% to 40% on 1 April 2026, roughly £1.1bn (~$1.5bn) extra annual duty at unchanged volumes (our estimate).**

- Estimate based on FY25/26 online casino plus bingo GGY of ~£5.85bn, before the 1.1% statutory levy.
- From 1 April 2027 a 25% remote-betting rate applies except UK horseracing (15%); adds ~£160m (~$218m) p.a. on ~£1.6bn (estimate).
- April–July 2026 receipts £1.933bn (~$2.6bn), +19%; RGD 50% of total; July RGD ~£590m (~$791m) vs £283m.
- Implies quarterly remote-gaming GGY of ~£1.5bn, flat on FY25/26 run-rate (our estimate); no volume shock.

> “not an adviser on taxation” — The Commission

### Consolidation and suppliers

**The online sector is consolidating around scale; top-tier operators can absorb a 19-point duty rise, sub-scale white-label books cannot.**

- Top tier: Flutter, bet365, Entain, Evoke, Betfred, FDJ United, Rank and Bally's; Commission publishes no market shares (n/a).
- BresBet and Bet St George surrendered licences in September 2026; the latter in administration by October (secondary).
- Evolution Malta's £4.75m (~$6m) settlement in July 2026 covered games on unlicensed GB-facing sites; Stakelogic fined £122,835 for game-cycle speed.
- B2B content providers are now squarely inside the Commission's perimeter.

### B2Tech implication

**The online opportunity is real but narrow: a £5.7bn casino pool growing ~15% against 40% RGD and heavy compliance friction.**

- Mature card/open-banking payments and a single national licence covering England, Scotland and Wales.
- Costs include 40% RGD, 1.1% levy, GGY-tiered fees, affordability/marketing-consent friction and 2026 advertising constraints.
- Greenfield B2C casino needs a differentiated brand or a B2B platform/content entry (gambling software licence) for acceptable return.
- A pure sportsbook faces a shrinking GGY pool and a 10-point duty rise in 16 months.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Land-based — betting shops, casinos, bingo, arcades & machines

*£4.9bn (+1.1%) with AGCs (+11%) and bingo (+8%) growing while betting shops (−3.3%) and the shop estate (−208) keep shrinking; local-authority scrutiny intensifying.*

### Sector overview

**Land-based GGY was £4.9bn (~$6.6bn), up 1.1%, a real-terms decline the Commission conceded on 19 November 2026.**

- 8,081 licensed premises at 31 March 2026 (−2.0%): 5,617 betting shops (−208, −3.6%, 12th consecutive decline), 1,449 AGCs (from 1,417).
- ~1,015 other premises (casinos, bingo halls, licensed FECs; implied).
- Gaming machines yielded £2.7bn (~$3.6bn), +4.3%, still ~£100m (~$134m) below pre-April 2019 B2 stake cut from £100 to £2.

### Betting shops

**Betting shops remain the largest land-based vertical at £2.4bn (~$3.2bn) but fell 3.3%; closures will not slow.**

- Over-the-counter betting losing share to online; shop machines £1.2bn (−0.9%) now 49.4% of shop GGY.
- Estate dominated by Entain, Betfred, William Hill (Evoke) and Paddy Power (Flutter) plus independents.
- Commission's reference to 'shop closures this year' against the November 2025 Budget suggests continued closures.
- Betfred's Done Brothers fined £825,000 in December 2025 for AML and social-responsibility failings around B3 machines.

### AGCs, bingo, casinos

**AGCs are the growth story at £761.4m (~$1.0bn), +11.3%, with retail bingo +8.2% to £703.8m (~$943m) and casinos flat.**

- Arcades overall £800.1m (~$1.1bn), +10.7%; FECs flat at £38.6m; AGC premises +32 to 1,449.
- Growth supported by 2025 relaxation of 80/20 machine ratio to 2:1 (not re-verified) and cheap high-street sites.
- Bingo machines £461.7m almost twice bingo games £242.1m; Bingo Duty abolition from 1 April 2026 worth ~£24m (~$32m) a year (estimate).
- Casinos £933.9m (~$1.3bn): games £695.0m, machines £238.9m; Grosvenor settlement autumn 2026 (secondary); 1968-Act machine reforms a modest tailwind.

### Local enforcement

**Local authority inspections rose 29% in FY25/26, which the Commission called 'unprecedented' after a third of councils inspected nothing a year earlier.**

- Commission handled over 150 council compliance queries and ran joint operations in Manchester, Doncaster, Birmingham, Bristol and Sheffield.
- An August South Yorkshire operation found 16 illegal betting terminals, cash and gold, leading to three arrests.
- Reports of illegal pub card games ('Chase the Joker', 'Play Your Cards Right') increased.
- First £7m of a £26m (~$35m) three-year Budget allocation funds illegal-gambling work including physical venues.

### Policy on clustering

**Policy is moving against clustering: Gambling Impact Assessments in force November 2026 and the 'aim to permit' principle under review.**

- Licensing authorities can weigh crime, deprivation, antisocial-behaviour, public-health data and premises concentration for new applications.
- MHCLG's 2 November 2026 High Street paper flags 'aim to permit'; DCMS consultation expected, a potentially fundamental shift.
- New category-B machines must offer gambling-management tools and sessional win/loss/time data; sub-stake win celebrations banned; most rules from June 2027.

### Operator read-through

**Betting shops suit only cash-generative run-off; AGC and bingo grow but face tougher 2027 licensing; casinos stable but capital-intensive.**

- Trade press flags Machine Games Duty as a target in the next Budget (unconfirmed).
- B2Tech, with no GB retail footprint, has little reason to enter except via machine content/technology under a gaming-machine technical licence.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Lotteries — National Lottery & society lotteries

*~£4.3bn of implied GGY; National Lottery sales £7.9bn (+0.9%) under Allwyn, society lotteries £1.2bn (+5.7%); not open to commercial operators.*

### Lottery GGY

**Lotteries account for roughly £4.3bn (~$5.8bn) of implied GGY, the gap between £17.5bn headline and £13.2bn ex-lottery.**

- National Lottery ticket sales £7.9bn (~$10.6bn), +0.9%; prizes £4.5bn (~$6.0bn), −0.6%; good causes £1.7bn (~$2.3bn), +2.8%.
- Commission's key facts box rounds good causes to £1.6bn while detailed text says £1.7bn; inconsistency flagged.
- Lottery Duty is 12% of ticket sales, the only GB gambling tax on turnover; Q2 FY26/27 receipts fell 15% to £225m.

### Allwyn licence

**Allwyn operates the National Lottery under the fourth licence, a 10-year licence from February 2024, outside the competitive market.**

- Commission 2024/25 operating costs up 49% to £60.3m (~$81m), partly from The New Lottery Company litigation; trial began October 2025 (secondary).
- Draft 2027–32 strategy lists 'a successful National Lottery' among five priorities; licence transition remains a live supervisory issue.

### Society lotteries

**Large society lotteries sold £1.2bn (~$1.6bn), +5.7%, the fastest-growing lottery segment and the only one open to commercial B2B operators.**

- Prizes £335.6m (~$450m), +6.1%; good causes £498.5m (~$668m), +2.8%.
- Commercial participation only as an external lottery manager; exempt from Lottery Duty but pays statutory levy at lowest band.
- Sales caps per society constrain scale; irrelevant to a casino/sportsbook strategy except as a CRM-adjacent product.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing regime & operator landscape

*Single national operating licence + local premises licences; 2,154 operators (−1.1%) and 3,097 activities; GGY-tiered fees; the long tail is thinning.*

### Regulatory framework

**Great Britain is regulated under the Gambling Act 2005, amended in 2014 to a point-of-consumption basis; Northern Ireland has separate law.**

- Commission issues operating licences across casino, betting, intermediary, bingo, machine, gambling software and lottery/ELM activities, plus personal licences.
- Councils issue premises licences and permits and inspect premises; co-regulation of 'equal status' strengthened by 29% inspection rise and Gambling Impact Assessments.

### Licensee base

**2,154 operators (−1.1%) held 3,097 activities (+0.4%) at 31 March 2026, consistent with consolidation.**

- No local-ownership, local-server or data-localisation requirements; offshore hosting allowed subject to technical standards.
- Fees comprise application and annual fees tiered by GGY band and activity; current schedule not retrieved, marked n/a.
- Quarterly regulatory returns harmonised since 1 July 2024; Commission warned of escalating enforcement for non-submission (trade press, May 2026).
- Two operators had licences suspended for not participating in GAMSTOP.

### Operator landscape

**The market is concentrated at the top and fragmented below; market shares are n/a as the Commission publishes none.**

- Online leaders: Flutter, bet365, Entain, Evoke, Betfred, FDJ United, Bally's and Rank.
- Retail led by Entain, Betfred, Evoke, Flutter (shops), Rank and Genting (casinos), Buzz Bingo and Mecca, Merkur, Admiral and Luxury Leisure (AGCs).
- Evolution's £4.75m settlement (July 2026) and Stakelogic's fine establish suppliers are directly accountable for their products.

### Policy direction

**Policy is heading toward more conditions and local discretion; the compliance burden is Europe's highest and still rising.**

- In train: Gambling Impact Assessments (Nov 2026), 'aim to permit' review, category-B machine standards (June 2027), updated LCE policy (March 2026).
- New five-year Corporate Strategy from April 2027 with five priorities including preventing crime ahead of the UK's FATF mutual evaluation.
- Licensing process is transparent and non-discretionary, but ongoing AML, safer-gambling, technical and data obligations are heavy.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Tax regime — seven duties, one Budget shock

*Record £3.83bn receipts (+5.9%) in FY25/26 before the 1 April 2026 RGD doubling to 40%; OBR sees ~£5bn in FY26/27; remote betting to 25% in April 2027.*

### Seven duties

**HMRC administers seven gambling duties, all on gross profit except Lottery Duty, now paired with Europe's highest online casino rate.**

- RGD (online casino, slots, bingo), GBD (3%/10% spread betting rates), Pool Betting Duty 15%, MGD 5%/20%.
- Gaming Duty banded 15%–50%, Bingo Duty 10% (abolished 1 April 2026), Lottery Duty 12% of ticket sales.
- Standing rates not changed in Budget 2025 were not re-verified in this pass.

### FY25/26 receipts

**Receipts hit a record £3.83bn (~$5.1bn), +5.9% or +£215m (~$288m), roughly 22% of GGY and 0.4% of HMRC receipts.**

- RGD was the largest duty at £1.16bn (~$1.6bn) in FY2024/25 (+13%); FY25/26 by-duty split n/a.
- Commission and HMRC figures are not reconcilable: GB-facing GGY by financial year versus UK cash receipts with one-month lag.

### Budget 2025 changes

**Autumn Budget 2025 raised RGD to 40% from 1 April 2026, added 25% remote-betting duty from 2027, and abolished Bingo Duty.**

- Legislated in Finance Act 2026 ss.86–88 and Schedule 13; UK horseracing stays at 15% to protect the racing levy base.
- Static estimates: +£1.1bn (~$1.5bn) p.a. RGD, +£160m (~$218m) p.a. remote betting, −£24m (~$32m) Bingo Duty.
- Consistent with OBR path of ~£4bn (FY25/26), ~£5bn (FY26/27), ~£6bn (FY30/31), cited from a secondary summary.

> “in-person good, remote bad” — CMS legal update

### Early evidence

**April–July 2026 receipts of £1.933bn (~$2.6bn), +19%, contradict industry warnings that 40% RGD would push play offshore.**

- RGD 50.0% of total, GBD 16.6%; April–June flat (+0.3%) as first 40% quarter paid in July: RGD ~£590m vs £283m (+108%).
- Back-solve £590m / 40% ≈ £1.5bn quarterly GGY, ~£5.9bn annualised: no measurable volume decline.
- GBD £161m (−14%), Lottery Duty £225m (−15%), MGD £162m (+5%), Gaming Duty £58m (+2%).
- SBC (October 2026) reports MGD may be the next Budget target; treated as unconfirmed.

### Statutory levy

**The statutory levy, in force 6 April 2025, is worth ~£110m (~$147m) a year, taking all-in online casino take to ~41%.**

- Rates: 1.1% remote operators and software licensees, 0.5% land-based casinos and betting, 0.2% bingo/AGC/FEC, 0.1% lotteries and others (not re-verified).
- Hypothecated to NHS-led treatment, prevention and research.
- At ~41% before fees and VAT-irrecoverable costs, marketing intensity, bonus generosity and affiliate economics must compress.

## 8. Segment deep-dive — Compliance, enforcement & the illegal market

*Enforcement intensity rising on every axis — LA inspections +29%, £19.6m penalties, supplier liability, £26m anti-illegal funding, FATF-driven AML focus.*

### Local inspections

**Local authority inspections rose 29% in FY2025/26, called 'unprecedented' by the Commission, with density per premises up over a third.**

- A year earlier roughly a third of councils had inspected no premises in 12 months.
- Premises fell 2% and betting shops 3.6%, amplifying inspection density.
- 150+ council compliance queries fielded; joint police operations in Manchester, Doncaster, Birmingham, Bristol and Sheffield covering machines, casinos, laundering and immigration.

### National enforcement

**FY25/26 penalty packages of £19.6m (~$26m) mark a re-acceleration from £4.2m in FY2024/25.**

- 160+ investigations, ~2,000 intelligence reports, hundreds of compliance assessments, plus personal management licence surrenders and warnings (CMS, July 2026).
- Prior years: £13.4m across 19 operators in FY2023/24, £60.1m in FY2022/23.
- Named outcomes: Platinum Gaming £10m, Spreadex £2.022m, ProgressPlay £1m, Videoslots £650k, Betfred retail £825k, Lottomart £360k, Petfre £240k.
- After year-end: Petfre £900k, Evolution Malta £4.75m, QuinnBet £609k, Grosvenor settlement; over 9,700 compliance activities in FY2024/25, more than double.

### Illegal market

**The illegal-market strategy now targets payments, advertising and supplier channels, funded by £26m (~$35m) over three years.**

- Sue Young (November 2026) set out targeting services sustaining unlicensed operators rather than only website takedowns.
- Evolution settlement shows the Commission reaches upstream to suppliers; £7m drawn in year one.
- No published FY25/26 illegal-market size estimate (n/a); HMRC post-April receipts show no leakage so far.

### AML focus

**Money laundering is rising up the agenda, with 'preventing crime' second of five priorities ahead of the UK's FATF mutual evaluation.**

- Expect more AML-led licence reviews (Platinum, Spreadex, ProgressPlay, QuinnBet pattern), source-of-funds scrutiny and personal-licence actions.
- Commission operating costs £60.3m in FY24/15, +49%, headcount +11.5%, which historically precedes licensee fee increases.

> “a priority for Government” — The Commission

### B2Tech implication

**The regime is predictable but unforgiving; penalties scale to eight figures and the compliant stack is a fixed cost favouring incumbents.**

- LCCP is prescriptive, settlements are published with named failings, and PML holders carry personal exposure.
- Compliance costs raise barriers against low-quality competitors, partly why incumbents' margins have held despite rising tax.

## 9. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

*Mature card/open-banking rails with a credit-card ban; the strictest player-protection ruleset in Europe tightened again in FY25/26 (stake caps, £150 checks, bonus cap, opt-in marketing).*

### Payments

**Payments are bank- and card-based with credit cards banned since April 2020; customer funds fell 13.9% to £886.6m (~$1.2bn).**

- Debit cards, open-banking, PayPal and Apple/Google Pay dominate; crypto not accepted in practice; no mobile-money layer.
- Withdrawals return to originating method; customer funds held under one of three disclosed protection ratings.
- Fund decline reflects lower balances and tighter deposit management, not fewer accounts; payment rules not re-verified.

### Online player protection

**Player protection tightened decisively in FY2025/26: slot caps, £150 (~$201) checks, 10x bonus cap and opt-in marketing consent.**

- Slot stakes capped at £5 (~$7) for 25+ from 9 April 2025 and £2 (~$3) for 18–24s from 21 May 2025.
- £150 net monthly deposit check threshold lowered from £500 in February 2025; October 2026 implementation reports requested; enhanced assessments piloted.
- Deposit-limit prompts from 31 October 2025; 10x wagering cap and mixed-product ban from 19 December 2025; separate consent from 1 January 2026.
- Registrations −3.0%, balances −13.9%, casino GGY +15%: spend concentrating among engaged, verified customers.

### Land-based protection

**New category-B machines must carry gambling-management tools and sessional displays, with most rules applying from June 2027.**

- Sessional data available to staff to trigger interactions; sub-stake wins not celebrated; time-to-result shortening features prohibited.
- Existing machines exempt; staged implementation shaped by industry evidence on cost and 'alert fatigue'.
- Age verification and cashless-payment rules for machines sit alongside.

### Advertising and participation

**Advertising is governed by CAP/BCAP, ASA, industry code and LCCP; participation stable at 47% of adults in 2025 (27% excluding lottery-only).**

- Whistle-to-whistle TV ban, 20% safer-gambling messaging, 2022 'strong appeal to under-18s' rule.
- Premier League front-of-shirt gambling sponsorship ban from the 2026/27 season (not re-verified).
- Illegal-market work targets advertising and affiliate channels; affiliates remain the licensee's responsibility.
- Commission uses GSGB rather than operator data to judge harm; problem-gambling prevalence n/a.

### Entrant implications

**The responsible-gambling stack is both the largest compliance cost and the strongest moat, only paying back at scale.**

- Affordability logic, interaction triggers, consent management, bonus engines and GAMSTOP integration must be built before go-live.
- B2Tech's platform needs GB-specific configuration: age-band stake caps, 10x bonus cap, consent granularity, levy and RGD accounting.

## 10. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale and legal certainty: a £17.5bn (~$23.4bn) GGY market with a single point-of-consumption licence covering England, Scotland and Wales, GGY-based (not turnover) taxation, transparent LCCP rules and no local-ownership or server-localisation requirements — the deepest regulated online pool in Europe. _(structural)_
- Online casino is compounding: +£736m (~$986m, ≈+15%) to £5.7bn (~$7.6bn), slots £4.8bn, despite stake caps and affordability checks — demand has proven highly resilient to regulation, and early HMRC data show no volume loss after the RGD hike. _(structural)_
- Supplier/B2B route: gaming-machine technical and gambling-software licences let a platform/content provider monetise the market without carrying 40% RGD on its own P&L; incumbents under margin pressure will seek cheaper, compliant tech and content. _(structural)_
- Consolidation tailwind: 2,154 operators (−1.1%) and visible exits (BresBet, Bet St George) mean distressed brands, databases and licences may be available cheaply; a 41% statutory take favours acquirers with low-cost platforms. _(mixed)_
- Retail niches still growing: AGCs +11.3% and premises +32, retail bingo +8.2%, with Bingo Duty abolished from April 2026 (~£24m p.a. relief, est.) and 2:1 machine ratios — for machine-content suppliers a growing addressable estate. _(mixed)_
- Mature payments and identity infrastructure: debit-card, open-banking and e-wallet rails plus public-record affordability data make KYC/affordability compliance automatable at scale; GAMSTOP and industry data-sharing are already built. _(structural)_
- Enforcement raises the quality bar: record inspections, supplier liability (Evolution) and illegal-market disruption funded by £26m protect licensed operators' share from unlicensed competition — a benefit to compliant entrants. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Fiscal squeeze: Remote Gaming Duty 21%→40% from 1 April 2026 (est. +£1.1bn p.a. sector-wide) plus a 1.1% levy puts the statutory take on online casino GGY at ~41% before fees; remote betting duty rises 15%→25% from 1 April 2027 (est. +£160m). Headline rates are now among the highest in regulated Europe on a GGY base. _(structural)_
- The sportsbook pool is shrinking: remote betting GGY £2.4bn with an implied c.−7% decline (with bingo), retail betting −3.3%, betting shops −208 to 5,617 — and the horseracing carve-out skews the 2027 duty rise onto football and other sports. _(structural)_
- Acquisition is plateauing: new registrations −3.0% (32.4m), customer balances −13.9% (£886.6m); affordability checks at £150/month, deposit-limit prompts, 10x bonus caps and product-level marketing opt-in all raise CAC and lower LTV for a new brand without an existing database. _(structural)_
- Enforcement and personal exposure: £19.6m of penalties in the year (vs £4.2m prior), AML-led licence reviews, FATF-driven supervision from 2027, and PML-holder sanctions — compliance failures are public, GGY-scaled and career-ending. _(structural)_
- Political direction is restrictive at every level: Gambling Impact Assessments (Nov 2026), a government review of the 'aim to permit' presumption, new machine standards (Jun 2027), a Commission strategy led by crime prevention, and trade-press speculation of a Machine Games Duty rise at the next Budget — further tightening is more likely than relaxation. _(watch)_
- Data gaps limit precision: the Commission publishes no operator market shares; the FY25/26 by-duty HMRC split and the current licence-fee schedule were not retrievable in this pass; the National Lottery good-causes figure is internally inconsistent (£1.6bn vs £1.7bn) in the source. _(one-off)_
- Rising regulator cost base (operating costs +49% to £60.3m in FY24/25, National Lottery litigation ongoing) historically precedes licence-fee increases; the fee burden on top of 40% RGD is an unquantified upside risk to cost of compliance. _(watch)_

## 11. Forecast & Outlook

The 'bridge' here is fiscal, not corporate: HMRC collected £3.83bn (~$5.1bn) in FY2025/26 at a 21% RGD rate, and the OBR projects ~£5bn (~$6.7bn) in FY2026/27 rising to ~£6bn (~$8.0bn) by FY2030/31. The driver is almost entirely online casino — the one vertical growing (+£736m in FY25/26) and the one the Budget targeted (RGD to 40%). The moving parts are: (1) RGD at 40% on ~£5.85bn of online casino + bingo GGY (+~£1.1bn p.a. static); (2) the 25% remote-betting rate from April 2027 on ~£1.6bn of non-horse remote betting GGY (+~£160m); (3) Bingo Duty abolition (−~£24m); (4) the behavioural response — promotional cuts, RTP compression, channel shift to retail or unlicensed sites — which the first quarter of receipts (July RGD ≈£590m, +108%) suggests has so far been small. All bridge figures below are our static estimates on FY25/26 bases unless labelled OBR/HMRC.

- **HMRC betting & gaming receipts FY2025/26 (actual, 21% RGD):** £3.83bn (~$5.1bn) (+5.9% YoY)
- **+ RGD 21%→40% on ~£5.85bn online casino/bingo GGY (est., static):** +~£1.1bn (~$1.5bn) (from 1 Apr 2026)
- **+ Remote betting GBD 15%→25% on ~£1.6bn non-horse GGY (est.); − Bingo Duty abolition (~£24m):** +~£0.14bn net (~$0.19bn) (from 1 Apr 2027 / 1 Apr 2026)
- **OBR forecast FY2026/27 → FY2030/31 (secondary citation):** ~£5.0bn → ~£6.0bn (before any MGD change or behavioural leakage)

Our independent view for the next 12–24 months: **licensed GGY grows 3–5% p.a. (est.), still led by online casino (+8–12% est., decelerating as operators cut bonuses and RTP to protect post-tax margin), with online betting flat-to-down in GGY and retail betting −3–5% p.a.** Operator economics compress sharply — **sector online-casino EBITDA margins fall by high-single-digit points (est.) unless RTP and marketing are cut, which in turn slows GGY growth** — and the long tail consolidates: expect further licence surrenders, white-label exits and asset sales through 2027, and incumbents to re-price affiliates and reduce free-bet intensity ahead of the April 2027 betting-duty rise. **Fiscal risk is still to the upside**: with early receipts up 19% and no visible leakage, the Treasury has every incentive to look at Machine Games Duty and Gaming Duty at the next Budget, and the OBR path (~£5bn FY26/27) already assumes the 2027 betting rate. **Regulatory direction remains tightening but incremental**: the 'aim to permit' consultation and Gambling Impact Assessments will slow AGC and bingo premises growth from 2027; the June 2027 category-B machine standards and any mandatory enhanced financial risk assessments add cost rather than remove revenue; FATF-driven AML supervision will produce larger, more frequent penalties. **Leverage direction for operators: up** — lower cash conversion at 40% RGD will push mid-tier balance sheets toward refinancing or sale. For B2Tech, the base case favours a B2B platform/content entry or an opportunistic acquisition of a distressed licensed brand over a greenfield B2C casino launch; a GB sportsbook-only launch is hard to justify.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- 1 April 2027: 25% remote-betting rate within General Betting Duty takes effect (Finance Act 2026 s.87) — confirm scope, horseracing carve-out mechanics and any further tweaks in the next Budget.
- Next UK Budget: any change to Machine Games Duty (trade-press speculation) or Gaming Duty bands; OBR's updated betting & gaming duty forecast and HMRC's monthly RGD receipts for evidence of volume leakage after 40%.
- DCMS consultation on the Gambling Act's 'aim to permit' principle (expected after MHCLG's 2 Nov 2026 High Street paper) and the first year of Gambling Impact Assessments — the key swing factor for AGC/bingo premises growth.
- Gambling Commission Corporate Strategy 2027–32 (April 2027) and any associated licence-fee consultation; FATF mutual evaluation timetable and resulting AML licence reviews.
- Outcome of the Commission's October 2026 data request on financial vulnerability checks and the enhanced financial risk assessment pilot — whether higher-threshold checks become mandatory.
- Quarterly Industry Statistics (FY2026/27 Q1–Q2) for the first regulator-reported view of online casino GGY, registrations and customer balances after the RGD rise; next annual statistics due autumn 2027.
- Illegal-market enforcement cadence — payment-blocking, advertising/affiliate takedowns, supplier settlements after Evolution — and any Commission estimate of unlicensed market size.
- Category-B machine rules (bulk effective June 2027) and the Premier League front-of-shirt sponsorship ban (2026/27 season) as leading indicators of land-based cost and marketing-channel constraints.
- Further licence surrenders, administrations and M&A in the online long tail (post BresBet/Bet St George) — potential acquisition targets and read-across on post-tax unit economics.
