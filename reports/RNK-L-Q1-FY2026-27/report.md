## 1. Executive Summary

- **Headline in line, no profit disclosure.** Q1 FY27 (1 Jul–30 Sep 2026) group like-for-like NGR grew 5% to £216.1m (~$286.5m); digital +6%, venues +5%. This is a revenue-only RNS: no operating profit, EBITDA, cash flow or balance-sheet data were disclosed, so every profit and leverage read-through below rests on the FY26 prelims (13 Aug 2026) and is labelled as such.
- **Guidance reiterated, not raised — and explicitly conditional.** Management is "confident of delivering Group LFL operating profit in line with market expectations for the current year", unchanged from August, but now framed "based on the current industry tax regime in the UK". No division drove a change; the bridge from FY26's £78.6m (~$104.2m) underlying operating profit is still dominated by the digital tax reset (RGD 21%→40% from 1 Apr 2026: ~£35m (~$46.4m) gross FY27 impact, ~£20m (~$26.5m) targeted mitigation).
- **The in-quarter deceleration is the number management did not highlight.** At the August prelims the first six weeks of FY27 were running +8% group NGR and +10% digital; the full 13-week quarter printed +5% and +6%. Assuming equal weekly weighting, the final seven weeks ran roughly +2.4% group and +2.6% digital [our estimate]. Grosvenor machines (+15% at six weeks, +14% for the quarter) were the exception.
- **Grosvenor machines remain the growth engine.** Grosvenor LFL NGR +6% to £108.1m (~$143.3m), visits +1% and spend per visit +5%; gaming machines +14% (Q4 FY26 +12%, Q1 FY26 +12% — a ~28% two-year stack), table gaming +3%, electronic gaming +5%. The 850-machine rollout across 37 casinos (completed H1 FY26) plus ongoing optimisation is still compounding; at the prelims management flagged room for ~650 further machines. Implied average NGR per week ~£8.3m (~$11.0m) vs £7.6m FY26 average [our arithmetic].
- **Three Grosvenor closures (Swansea, Reading Central, Sunderland)**, judged "no longer economically viable in the medium term", follow nine Mecca closures in FY26. Reported growth will therefore trail LFL: Q1 LFL NGR of £216.1m is only +2.8% against the £210.2m (~$278.7m) reported in Q1 FY26, implying ~£4.4m (~$5.8m) of prior-year revenue has been stripped from the LFL base (Mecca's base alone restates from £35.5m to ~£32.2m).
- **UK digital resilient at +6% in its first full quarter under 40% RGD.** Mecca online +7% (PY +9%); Grosvenor online growth was not disclosed this time (PY +31%); international digital +4% (PY −1%, after Spanish platform capacity issues were fixed with a new bingo platform). Growth was delivered with deliberately reduced above-the-line marketing and, per Shore Capital, growth in first-time depositors — i.e. the mitigation plan is not (yet) visibly costing volume.
- **Mecca flat** at £32.2m (~$42.7m): visits −3%, spend per visit +3%, with the prolonged UK hot summer blamed for footfall. The weakest of the four prints, but on a small profit base (FY26 underlying LFL operating profit £8.9m, ~$11.8m, 6.2% margin) — a 3% visit miss is worth low single-digit £m of NGR, not a thesis change.
- **Enracha +7%** to £10.9m (~$14.5m), driven by electronic gaming +11%; the Spanish venues business continues to compound (FY26 +7% to £45.3m, ~$60.1m) after absorbing the £6.5m (~$8.6m) payment-fraud loss booked as an SDI in FY26.
- **Capital position (30 Jun 2026, last disclosed):** net cash pre-IFRS 16 of £56.8m (~$75.3m); IFRS 16 net debt £147.2m (~$195.2m), down from £154.7m; FY26 net free cash flow £25.5m (~$33.8m) after SDI outflows; ROCE 18.3% (+3.2ppt). FY26 DPS 3.50p (~$0.046), +35%; the 2.5p final is payable 23 Oct 2026 (~£16.5m cash at 3.5p on ~472m shares [estimate]). No buyback in place; net debt/EBITDA not disclosed in Q1 [n/a].
- **Mitigation is a cost-action programme, not a formal transformation plan.** Against the ~£35m FY27 RGD step-up, management targets ~£20m of offsets via sharply lower ATL advertising, modestly higher performance marketing, supplier renegotiation, headcount cuts and operating-model streamlining; FY26 LFL employment costs were held flat at £270.3m (~$358.4m) despite ~4% underlying inflation. Venues are separately absorbing National Living Wage, employer NIC and statutory-levy increases.
- **The MGD overhang dominates everything.** CEO Richard Harris: any increase to Machine Games Duty "will increase costs, reduce venue viability, lead to venue closures and job losses, reduce tax receipts and halt investment". Shore Capital sizes a hypothetical 20%→40% doubling at ~£35m gross (£25m/~$33.1m Grosvenor, £10m/~$13.3m Mecca); Deutsche Bank at ~£35m (~$46.4m) p.a. even after partial mitigation, equal to ~44% of FY28E EBIT and ~17% of near-term EBITDA — DB downgraded to Hold in January 2026 (PT 104p from 163p).
- **The >£100m (~$132.6m) medium-term operating-profit ambition is retained**, having reached £78.6m underlying in FY26 (+21%, margin 9.4% vs 8.1%). On Shore's arithmetic an MGD doubling would delay it materially; Shore nonetheless left forecasts unchanged and argues Budget risk is largely priced in, while Peel Hunt expects the Budget to pass without a negative effect on Rank.
- **Share price:** Shore Capital's note dated 15 Oct recorded the shares at 74p (~$0.98), −14% over one month, −23% over three months and −43% over twelve months — the stock had already de-rated on the June 2026 machine-tax reports (−8% on 30 Jun) and the January DB downgrade. The official close on the day could not be verified from the sources read [unverified].
- **Management & governance:** this is Richard Harris's first Q1 as Chief Executive following John O'Reilly's retirement (announced 6 Jan 2026; Harris confirmed in the role in July 2026). FY26 carried £22.9m (~$30.4m) of separately disclosed items, including a £5.0m (~$6.6m) proposed Gambling Commission settlement in lieu of a penalty for historical Grosvenor venues failings (July 2026) and the £6.5m Spanish fraud loss — statutory operating profit fell 7% to £55.7m (~$73.8m) and basic EPS 22% to 6.4p (~$0.085) despite the underlying beat.
- **Disclosure quality is thin.** No actives, ARPU, customer numbers, marketing spend, London vs provincial split or Grosvenor online growth were given — all of which appeared in prior updates. Management is emphasising machines and digital resilience; it is burying the deceleration, Mecca footfall, the closures and the absence of any profit commentary beyond "in line".
- **Sector read-through:** Entain's Q3 (same morning) showed UK & Ireland online growth, Betfred's FY showed retail shrinking, and Genting has warned on MGD and shut its Coventry venue — the UK land-based estate is already rationalising pre-Budget. Rank, with ~70% of NGR from venues and machines as the growth product, is the most MGD-exposed listed name (DB).
- **Thesis read-through:** operationally the model is working — machine yield, spend per visit and digital retention are all moving the right way and the balance sheet is ungeared ex-leases. But the policy binary dwarfs the operating delta: a benign Budget leaves a business compounding mid-single-digit NGR on an undemanding multiple; an adverse one removes ~45% of FY28E EBIT (DB) and would likely trigger a further wave of closures.
- **Next catalysts:** the Autumn Budget (MGD decision), then H1 FY27 results on 28 Jan 2027 (first disclosure of the post-RGD digital margin).

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Digital (UK: Grosvenor & Mecca online; International: Spain)

*+6% LFL to £64.9m through the first full quarter of 40% RGD — resilient, but decelerating from +12% in Q4 and +10% in the first six weeks.*

### Headline growth

**Digital LFL NGR grew 6% to £64.9m (~$86.0m), resilient through the first full quarter of 40% RGD but decelerating from +12% in Q4.**

- Digital is 30.0% of group revenue and up 1.6% sequentially on Q4 FY26's £63.9m (~$84.7m).
- Growth was running +10% in the first six weeks of FY27 before slowing to +6% for the full quarter.

### UK and Grosvenor online

**Growth was almost entirely UK-driven at +6%, with Mecca online +7% and Grosvenor online growth conspicuously not disclosed.**

- Mecca brand online grew 7% against +9% in the prior year, attributed to the core customer base.
- Grosvenor online was +31% in the comparable quarter a year ago; given the UK aggregate of +6%, it is unlikely to have been meaningfully above that.
- We read the omission as the end of the Grosvenor-online outperformance narrative rather than an oversight.

> “strong performance from our core customer base” — Management

### International digital

**International digital, principally Spain under the Yo/Enracha brands, grew 4%, recovering from −1% a year ago.**

- The prior-year decline reflected platform capacity issues still being worked through and a new bingo platform launch.

### RGD and mitigation

**This is the first full quarter under 40% Remote Gaming Duty (up from 21%), and the ~£20m mitigation plan is not yet costing volume.**

- The duty increase took effect 1 April 2026 following the November 2025 Budget.
- At the August prelims gross annualised impact was sized at ~£46m (~$61m), with ~£35m (~$46.4m) landing incrementally in FY27.
- Mitigation of ~£20m (~$26.5m): significant cut to above-the-line advertising, modest scale-up in performance marketing, renegotiated supplier terms, streamlined operating model, while funding free bets and incentives.
- Shore Capital points to growth in first-time depositors; this is the single most encouraging thing in the release for the digital P&L.
- Marketing spend as a percentage of revenue, monthly actives and ARPU were not disclosed, so growth quality cannot be tested.

> “despite the higher tax regime introduced in April 2026 and reduced above-the-line marketing spend” — Management

### Deceleration

**The deceleration is real and under-discussed: a +6% full quarter after +10% at six weeks implies the back seven weeks averaged roughly +2.6%.**

- UK digital accelerated from +2% in Q3 FY26 to +12% in Q4 FY26; the +2.6% figure is our estimate using equal weekly weighting.
- Causes cannot be determined: tougher comparative (Q1 FY26 was +13%), lagged effect of lower ATL spend, or genuine post-tax softening as operators reset bonusing.
- Management's framing reads to us as an acknowledgement that the market is in flux rather than a statement of confidence.

> “the customer proposition and overall marketing spend balance” — Management

### Profitability outlook

**FY26 digital underlying operating profit was £36.6m (~$48.5m), +10%, a 14.7% margin; management guides profitability will reset in FY27.**

- FY26 total NGR was £249.3m (~$330.6m), with only one quarter of the higher duty absorbed.
- A ~£15m net headwind on a ~£37m base implies a FY27 digital margin of 8–10% unless NGR growth or mitigation outperforms [our estimate].
- No segment profit guidance was given at Q1; H1 FY27 on 28 Jan 2027 will be the first disclosure of the post-reset margin.
- We would treat any Q2 digital growth below ~5% as evidence that the ATL cuts are biting on acquisition.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Grosvenor venues (UK casinos)

*+6% LFL to £108.1m on spend per visit +5% and machines +14%; three closures signal the estate is still being pruned ahead of the Budget.*

### Headline growth

**Grosvenor LFL NGR grew 6% to £108.1m (~$143.3m), with visits up 1% and spend per visit up 5%.**

- A markedly different mix from a year ago, when the quarter delivered +8% on visits +5% and spend +3%.
- Sequentially the business recovered 10% from the Q4 FY26 trough of £98.3m (~$130.3m), held to +3% by Middle East conflict disruption to London international travel.
- Implied average NGR per week is ~£8.3m (~$11.0m) against the FY26 average of £7.6m [our arithmetic].
- The London versus provincial split (London +4%, outside London +10% a year ago) was not repeated; we suspect a machine-yield story rather than Victoria Casino's post-£15m-refurbishment step-up.

### Product mix

**Gaming machines grew 14% on top of 12% a year ago, genuine yield improvement from estate optimisation rather than simple capacity.**

- Q4 FY26 machines +12%, Q1 FY26 +12%; the 850 incremental B1 machines installed across 37 casinos in H1 FY26 have fully annualised.
- Tables +3%, unchanged on the prior-year rate; electronic gaming +5%, down from +11% as the terminal-upgrade tailwind laps.
- At the prelims management flagged room for ~650 additional machines, the principal organic lever behind the >£100m group operating-profit ambition.
- Table gaming remains the laggard and is most exposed to London's international-visitor mix.

> “underlined our confidence in further improvement” — Management

### Closures and MGD

**Three casinos closed in the quarter — Swansea, Reading Central and Sunderland — signalling the marginal estate is on the edge ahead of the Budget.**

- The implied LFL base restates to ~£102.0m from £102.7m reported, so these were small venues.
- Closures were decided before the Budget outcome is known; management's language on MGD (closures, job losses, halted investment) is positioned as a signal to HM Treasury.
- Shore Capital sizes a 20%→40% MGD doubling at ~£25m (~$33.1m) gross for Grosvenor alone.
- FY26 underlying LFL operating profit was £35.5m (~$47.1m), +11%, an 8.9% margin on NGR; that is why the equity trades as it does.

> “no longer economically viable in the medium term” — Management

### Costs and outlook

**No profit, cost or capex data were given; for FY27 we model NGR growth of 4–6% with modest margin expansion, absent an MGD change.**

- FY26: Grosvenor absorbed National Living Wage, employer NIC and statutory-levy increases while growing operating profit 11%.
- Group LFL employment costs were held flat at £270.3m (~$358.4m).
- The £5.0m (~$6.6m) Gambling Commission settlement proposal for historical Grosvenor failings was booked below the underlying line.
- The swing factor is entirely fiscal.
- A 1% visit increase against 5% a year ago means footfall recovery has largely run its course; growth from here is yield, which a machine tax would attack.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Mecca venues (UK bingo)

*Flat LFL NGR at £32.2m as visits fell 3%; weather is the stated excuse, but this is the fourth consecutive quarter of negative footfall.*

### Headline and footfall

**Mecca LFL NGR was flat at £32.2m (~$42.7m) as visits fell 3%, the fourth consecutive quarter of negative footfall.**

- Spend per visit rose 3%; management attributed visit softness largely to hot weather, plausible for a bingo estate in July–September.
- Shore Capital expects trends to improve as weather normalises.
- Visits were also down 1% in the comparable quarter a year ago (offset by spend +6%), so the spend-per-visit cushion has halved.
- NGR fell 9% sequentially from Q4 FY26's £35.4m (~$46.9m), consistent with summer seasonality.
- Prior-year reported £35.5m restates to ~£32.2m LFL, reflecting the nine commercially unviable clubs closed during FY26.

> “the prolonged period of hot weather in the UK” — Management

### Strategic context

**Mecca is run as a right-sized, cash-generative estate; FY26 LFL NGR grew 4% to £143.0m and profit more than doubled to £8.9m.**

- FY26 underlying LFL operating profit of £8.9m (~$11.8m) rose from £4.3m, on investment in gaming-machine areas, external signage and modernisation.
- FY26 LFL NGR was £143.0m (~$189.6m); the main-stage bingo game is described as in good health.
- A 6.2% margin on a flat top line leaves little room for error, but profit at stake is small relative to group.
- A 3% visit shortfall is worth roughly £1m of quarterly NGR [our estimate].

### Fiscal risk

**The bigger risk is fiscal: Shore Capital sizes a 20%→40% MGD doubling at ~£10m (~$13.3m) gross, more than FY26 divisional operating profit.**

- Such a change would render a further tranche of clubs unviable and accelerate the closure programme management has shown it is willing to execute.
- No profit, cost or customer data beyond visits and spend were disclosed for the quarter.
- No comment was made on machine performance within Mecca, which is where the capital has been going.

### Q2 outlook

**In Q2 (October–December) the weather excuse disappears and the comparative is undemanding, so low-single-digit LFL growth is the base case absent a tax shock.**

- Q2 FY26 was described as "slightly softer".
- Another quarter of negative visits with weather normalised would be a structural signal on the core customer base rather than noise.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Enracha venues (Spain)

*+7% LFL to £10.9m on electronic gaming +11%; small, steady, and the only segment outside the UK tax debate.*

### Headline growth

**Enracha LFL NGR grew 7% to £10.9m (~$14.5m), driven by 11% growth in electronic gaming, continuing mid-to-high single-digit growth.**

- Prior run: Q1 FY26 +5%, FY26 +7% to £45.3m (~$60.1m).
- On a reported sterling basis growth is closer to +4.8% against £10.4m (~$13.8m) a year ago; sterling strength against the euro absorbed roughly two points.
- Sequentially NGR is ~3.5% below the ~£11.3m (~$15.0m) we derive for Q4 FY26 [group less disclosed segments], in line with summer seasonality.

### Diversification and fraud

**Enracha is 5% of group NGR and the only segment with no UK RGD or MGD exposure, a diversification value the market does not price.**

- FY26 was marred by the £6.5m (~$8.6m) payment fraud loss in the Spanish business announced December 2025, booked as a separately disclosed item.
- No update on recoveries or control remediation was given at Q1; we expect one at the interims.

## 7. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- **Grosvenor gaming machines +14%** on top of +12% a year ago (~28% two-year stack) — the 850-machine rollout has fully annualised, so this is yield optimisation, with ~650 further machines identified. This is the core of the >£100m (~$132.6m) operating-profit ambition. _(structural)_
- **UK digital +6% in the first full quarter under 40% RGD**, delivered with deliberately reduced above-the-line marketing and growth in first-time depositors (Shore) — early evidence that the ~£20m (~$26.5m) mitigation plan is not costing volume. _(early)_
- **Spend per visit up across the venues estate** — Grosvenor +5%, Mecca +3% — supporting NGR despite flat-to-negative footfall; Grosvenor implied NGR/week ~£8.3m (~$11.0m) vs £7.6m FY26 average [our arithmetic]. _(mixed)_
- **Enracha +7% with electronic gaming +11%** and Spanish digital back to growth (+4% vs −1%) — the Spanish platform is now a net contributor after the FY26 fraud loss and platform issues. _(structural)_
- **Balance sheet is ungeared ex-leases**: net cash pre-IFRS 16 £56.8m (~$75.3m) at Jun-26, IFRS 16 net debt £147.2m (~$195.2m) falling, FY26 net FCF £25.5m (~$33.8m), DPS +35% to 3.50p (~$0.046) — ample capacity to absorb a tax shock without a dilutive response. _(structural)_
- **Guidance held** — FY27 LFL operating profit "in line with market expectations" reiterated, with Shore Capital and Peel Hunt leaving forecasts unchanged. _(watch)_

**Lowlights**

- **Undisclosed in-quarter deceleration**: group NGR was +8% and digital +10% in the first six weeks (Aug prelims) but +5%/+6% for the quarter, implying ~+2.4%/+2.6% over the final seven weeks [our estimate]. Management did not address the slowdown. _(watch)_
- **Mecca visits −3%, NGR flat** — fourth consecutive quarter of negative footfall and the spend-per-visit cushion has halved (+3% vs +6%); weather is the stated cause but the trend predates the summer. _(mixed)_
- **Three Grosvenor closures** (Swansea, Reading Central, Sunderland) after nine Mecca closures in FY26; reported group growth is only +2.8% vs +5% LFL, with ~£4.4m (~$5.8m) of PY revenue stripped from the base. The estate is shrinking before the Budget outcome is known. _(structural)_
- **Machine Games Duty binary**: a 20%→40% doubling would cost ~£35m (~$46.4m) gross p.a. (Shore: £25m Grosvenor, £10m Mecca; DB: ~44% of FY28E EBIT) against FY26 underlying operating profit of £78.6m (~$104.2m); guidance is explicitly conditional on no change. _(governance)_
- **Digital margin reset in FY27**: ~£35m (~$46.4m) incremental RGD less ~£20m mitigation implies a ~£15m net headwind on a £36.6m (~$48.5m) FY26 digital profit base — FY27 digital margin likely 8–10% vs 14.7% [our estimate]. _(structural)_
- **Disclosure has thinned**: no profit, actives, ARPU, marketing %, London/provincial split or Grosvenor online growth (PY +31%) — all previously provided. Statutory FY26 EPS fell 22% to 6.4p (~$0.085) on £22.9m (~$30.4m) of SDIs including the £5.0m (~$6.6m) Gambling Commission settlement. _(governance)_
- **Shares −43% over 12 months to ~74p (~$0.98)** per Shore's 15 Oct note, with two tax-driven sell-offs (Oct-24 reports, Jun-26 machine-tax report, −8%) and a DB downgrade (Jan-26) in the rear-view mirror — the stock is a policy instrument until the Budget clears. _(watch)_

## 8. Forecast & Outlook

There is no numerical guidance change to bridge: in August (FY26 prelims) management guided FY27 group LFL operating profit "in line with market expectations" with a ~£35m (~$46.4m) incremental RGD headwind and ~£20m (~$26.5m) of mitigation, and retained the >£100m (~$132.6m) medium-term ambition; on 15 October that was reiterated verbatim but now conditioned on "the current industry tax regime in the UK". No division drove a change — digital is tracking the mitigation plan, Grosvenor is ahead on machines, Mecca is modestly behind on footfall, Enracha is in line. Company-compiled consensus for FY27 was not disclosed in the Q1 RNS [unverified]; the last published reference point was FY26 consensus of £68.2m (~$90.4m) in July 2026, which the company then beat by delivering £78.6m. The moving parts for FY27 are: (i) the net ~£15m digital tax reset; (ii) Grosvenor operating leverage from machines and closure savings; (iii) wage/NIC/levy inflation in venues; and (iv) the MGD outcome, which sits entirely outside guidance.

- **FY27 guidance — Aug 2026 (prelims):** LFL op. profit "in line with market expectations"; RGD ~£35m gross / ~£20m mitigated (Base)
- **FY27 guidance — 15 Oct 2026 (Q1):** Reiterated, "based on the current industry tax regime in the UK" (Unchanged (new tax caveat))
- **Our FY27E underlying op. profit:** ~£72–76m (~$95–101m) vs FY26 £78.6m (−3% to −8% YoY [estimate])
- **MGD doubling scenario (not in guidance):** −£35m (~$46.4m) gross p.a. (Shore/DB) (~−45% of FY28E EBIT (DB))

**Revenue:** we model FY27 LFL NGR of ~£865–875m (~$1.15–1.16bn), +4–5%, with Q1's £216.1m representing ~26% of the FY26 base. Within that, Grosvenor +4–6% on machine yield (the only product growing double-digit), digital +4–6% as the comparative hardens (Q4 FY26 was +12%) and ATL cuts lag into acquisition, Mecca flat to +2% as weather normalises, Enracha +5–7%. Reported NGR will trail LFL by ~1–2ppt given twelve closures in the last five quarters. **Margin:** FY26 underlying operating margin of 9.4% compresses to ~8.5% [estimate] as the digital reset lands: FY26 £78.6m + ~£8–10m venues operating leverage (machines, closure savings) − ~£15m net RGD − ~£3–5m venues wage/NIC/levy inflation = **~£72–76m (~$95–101m)** underlying operating profit, i.e. a modest decline that we think is broadly what "in line with market expectations" means after the July upgrade cycle. Digital margin falls from 14.7% toward 8–10%; Grosvenor should hold or modestly expand from 8.9%. **Leverage:** with ~£25m of annual net FCF against ~£16.5m of dividends [3.5p × ~472m shares, estimate], pre-IFRS 16 net cash should build from £56.8m toward £60–70m by Jun-27 absent a tax shock; IFRS 16 net debt stays ~£140–150m and there is no case for a buyback until the Budget clears. **Q2 (Oct–Dec):** expect low-to-mid single-digit group LFL growth — Mecca recovering, Grosvenor machines still +10% or better, digital decelerating toward mid-single digits against the +12% Q4 exit rate — but the Q2 print will be irrelevant if MGD doubles. **The swing factor is binary:** an unchanged MGD leaves a business compounding mid-single-digit NGR with an ungeared balance sheet on a distressed multiple; a doubling to 40% removes ~£35m gross (~£20–25m net of mitigation and closures [our estimate]), pushes the >£100m ambition out by three-plus years, and triggers a further closure wave across the marginal Grosvenor and Mecca estate. We would not extrapolate the Q1 revenue beat into FY27 upgrades until the Budget outcome is known.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Autumn Budget: any change to Machine Games Duty (current 20%) — the only variable that matters for the equity; Shore/DB size a doubling at ~£35m gross p.a.
- H1 FY27 results on 28 Jan 2027: first disclosure of the post-RGD digital operating margin and whether the ~£20m mitigation is being delivered; also any update on Spanish fraud recoveries.
- Digital growth trajectory: Q2 UK digital below ~5% would indicate the ATL marketing cuts are biting on acquisition; watch first-time depositor commentary and whether Grosvenor online growth is disclosed again.
- Grosvenor machine yield: whether +14% sustains as the 850-machine comparative is fully lapped, and timing of the ~650 additional machines; London vs provincial split.
- Mecca footfall once the weather excuse lapses — a further quarter of negative visits would be a structural signal; also any further closure announcements across either venues brand.
- Cost inflation: April 2027 National Living Wage and any further employer NIC/statutory levy changes in the Budget, against the flat £270.3m FY26 employment-cost base.
- Regulatory: finalisation of the £5.0m Gambling Commission settlement and any conditions attached to Grosvenor's operating licence.
