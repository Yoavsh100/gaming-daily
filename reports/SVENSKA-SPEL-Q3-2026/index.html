Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

Svenska Spel

Released 22 Oct 2026 · Currency SEK (tables in SEK millions; USD equivalents at USD/SEK 9.91 as of 22 Oct 2026, prefixed "~") · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: n/a — AB Svenska Spel is 100% owned by the Swedish state (2,000 shares, no listed equity or public debt; HQ Visby, org. no. 556460-1812), so there is no market reaction to measure. Fiscal year ends 31 December; the year-end report is due 8 February 2027.

The one-line verdict

A revenue-steady, cost-heavy quarter. Group NGR rose 1.3% to SEK 1,898m (~$192m) as post-World Cup Oddset and casino (Sport & Casino +5.5%) plus Triss/Lotto offset a weaker Eurojackpot and a 14% Vegas decline, but a SEK 69m (+18%) step-up in external costs for the brand relaunch, AI and cloud migration cut operating profit 9% to SEK 656m (~$66m) and the margin by c.375bps to 34.6%. Net profit fell only 3% to SEK 565m (~$57m) because finance income tripled; the business is still well above the owner's 30% margin target, so the debate is about the duration of the spend, not demand.

1Executive summary

  • Headline: top line held, EBIT missed the prior year on self-inflicted cost. Q3 2026 group net gaming revenue (NGR) was SEK 1,898m (~$192m), +SEK 24m / +1.3% YoY (Q3 2025: SEK 1,874m), but operating profit fell SEK 62m / -8.6% to SEK 656m (~$66m) and the operating margin compressed c.375bps to 34.6% (company-rounded 35% vs 38%). Svenska Spel gives no numerical guidance and has no sell-side consensus, so 'vs expectations' is against management's own framing: they describe Q3 as 'growth and good profitability' and say the Vegas decline was 'in line with expectations'.
  • The entire EBIT decline is opex, not demand or tax. Gross profit ('net sales from gaming operations') was flat at SEK 1,320m (~$133m, +0.3%): NGR +SEK 24m and other income +SEK 9m were absorbed by gaming tax +SEK 26m (+6.7%, to 21.8% of NGR from 20.7%) and direct costs +SEK 4m. Below gross profit, other external costs jumped +SEK 69m / +17.6% to SEK 460m (~$46m), lifting total operating costs 10.9% to SEK 663m, or 34.9% of NGR (31.9%). Personnel (SEK 185m, -0.5%) and D&A (SEK 31m) were flat.
  • Management attributes the cost step-up to three 'strategic investments': brand, AI and cloud. They are running what they call the largest renewal of the Svenska Spel brand since the company's formation in 1997 (new visual identity, 'en större känsla' positioning), integrating AI (coding and in-house AI agents for personalised CRM) and migrating the IT estate to cloud-based services. No budget, phasing or end-date is disclosed — the single most important gap in the report for anyone modelling 2027 margins.
  • Sport & Casino was the growth engine, Tur did the heavy lifting on mix, Vegas kept shrinking. Tur (lotteries) NGR SEK 1,316m (~$133m, +0.7%; 69% of group), Sport & Casino SEK 516m (~$52m, +5.5%; 27%), Vegas SEK 67m (~$7m, -14.1%; 4%). On a 9M basis NGR is SEK 5,741m (~$579m, +3.2%): Tur +3.2%, Sport & Casino +6.2%, Vegas -15.7%.
  • World Cup after-glow quantified as far as the company allows. Management says they beat their World Cup sales targets through the closing weeks in July with 'high customer activity all the way to the final', and that Sport & Casino active customers hit record levels in Q3 (no number disclosed). The Q2 report gave the event peak: June Sport & Casino revenue +24% YoY, sports betting +135%, playing customers +36% and new registrations +340% YoY. Sequentially S&C NGR fell 6.5% from Q2's SEK 552m as the tournament ended on 19 July.
  • Channel shift continues: online 67% of NGR, retail still eroding. Online NGR SEK 1,274m (~$129m, +4.1%), of which mobile 59% of group NGR (57%); 9M online +8.4% to SEK 3,837m (~$387m). Retail agents ('Ombud') SEK 558m (~$56m, -1.6%; 9M -3.5%) and Restaurant & Bingo halls (Vegas) SEK 67m (-14.1%).
  • Market share: management claims gains in both the licensed and lottery markets in H1; the regulator's data broadly support it. Spelinspektionen puts the licensed market at SEK 7,380m (~$745m) in Q2 2026, +5.1% YoY, with commercial online & betting +7.1% to SEK 4,960m and public-benefit lotteries +2.0%; Svenska Spel's Q2 NGR grew 6.7%, Sport & Casino +10.8% and Tur +6.6%. Our derived share of the commercial online & betting pool for Sport & Casino is c.11.1% in Q2 2026 (10.8% in Q2 2025) [estimate]; Q3 market data are not yet published.
  • Below the line, finance income flattered the net result. Net financial items were SEK 60m (~$6m) vs SEK 20m: SEK 59m (13) return on financial investments, SEK 36m (-12) discounting effect on long-dated Triss Månadsklöver prize liabilities, and SEK 10m (20) bank interest. Profit after financial items was SEK 716m (~$72m, -3.1%); tax SEK 152m (ETR 21.2%); net profit from continuing operations SEK 565m (~$57m, -3.3%). Strip out the SEK 40m finance swing and the underlying net result is down roughly 9%, in line with EBIT.
  • 9M is still ahead of 2025 on every line. EBIT SEK 1,932m (~$195m, +1.8%), margin 33.7% (34.1%), continuing net profit SEK 1,630m (~$164m, +6.1%) and total net profit SEK 1,918m (~$194m, +33.3%) including a SEK 288m (~$29m) gain on the Q1 sale of the former Casino Cosmopol Stockholm property to Alecta Fastigheter (reported in discontinued operations). The land-based casino exit (April 2025) is now complete: no held-for-sale assets remain and the shell companies are being merged into the parent in Q4 2026.
  • Cost-transformation: there is no formal savings program — the opposite is happening. Headcount is down 3.5% to 825 (855) while external costs rise 18%, i.e. cost is shifting from payroll to consultants/cloud. Vegas is the only area with an explicit efficiency agenda (cost efficiency and 'profitable partners'); the estate is now c.2,700 machines in c.900 venues versus c.3,000/c.1,000 described in the Q2 report. No one-off costs were booked in the quarter (company's own alternative-KPI note).
  • Capital position: net cash, no borrowings, and above the owner's equity band. Cash SEK 2,575m (~$260m), short-term investments SEK 877m (~$89m) and non-current financial assets SEK 1,707m (~$172m) total SEK 5,159m (~$521m) of liquid/financial assets against no interest-bearing debt (only lease liabilities within other liabilities; prize liabilities of SEK 2,258m are operational). Equity SEK 2,837m (~$286m); equity ratio 43% vs the owner's 20–40% full-year target band.
  • Cash flow: Q3 operating cash flow SEK 393m (~$40m) vs SEK 484m; 9M SEK 1,788m (~$180m, +22%). 9M includes SEK 260m from settlement of intra-group loans by the property buyer. 9M capex SEK 91m (~$9m) (SEK 71m intangible — IT/platform modernisation — and SEK 21m tangible), so 9M FCF is c.SEK 1,697m (~$171m) [derived]. SEK 463m (~$47m) was deployed into financial investments. The SEK 1,800m (~$182m) dividend to the Swedish state was paid in Q2 (FY2025: SEK 1,600m), equal to 92% of FY2025 total net profit versus the ≥80% policy.
  • Owner targets: all three met at 9M. Operating margin over time ≥30% (9M 33.7%, Q3 34.6%); equity ratio 20–40% at year-end (43% at Sep, before the Q4 earnings build — implies another above-policy distribution is plausible); dividend ≥80% of net profit after tax.
  • Product news skewed to lotteries and sport. Triss 40th-anniversary jubilee ticket with 40 million-kronor prizes; three new digital lotteries (Summa Summarum, Fruttis Troppix, Kanonkulan); 132 million-kronor wins paid in Q3 (116 lottery, 16 sport) including a c.SEK 200m (~$20m) Vikinglotto win — the largest of the year and ninth largest ever; '99 Royale' multiplayer slot concept (up to 99 players per round) in casino; SHL/SDHL ice-hockey main-sponsorship extended to 2031 and the Swedish Ski Association deal extended/expanded to 2032 (from Q2). Katarina Borstedt joined as Chief People & Culture Officer in September.
  • Regulation is the medium-term swing factor and management is leaning into it. The government appointed an inquiry (Sept 2026) into gambling by children and young adults, reporting February 2028; Spelinspektionen's consultation on new duty-of-care regulations (with specific focus on 18–24s) has closed; a 'participant criterion' to widen enforcement against unlicensed operators is under discussion; and the report flags prediction markets as a new category the 2019 framework was not designed for. Spelpaus self-exclusions were c.138,000 at end-Q2 (-0.7% QoQ). Svenska Spel welcomes the youth inquiry — tighter rules typically favour the incumbent.
  • Responsible-gambling KPI slipped a point. 'Healthy revenue' (NGR from low-risk customers) was 94% vs 95% in Q3 2025 (9M 94% both years) while management says active customers and new-customer offers drove growth and that they see 'no increase in risky gambling'. Worth watching given the 18–24 regulatory focus.
  • Thesis read-through: a defensive, cash-generative state monopoly-plus-licence hybrid whose growth is low-single-digit and whose margin is now a management choice. The lottery franchise (69% of NGR) is stable, online/sport is taking share, and the balance sheet is over-capitalised against the owner's band. The negatives are the opaque, open-ended strategic spend (external costs +18% YoY for a second consecutive quarter of elevated spend), the structural retail/Vegas decline and the fact that disclosure (no actives, ARPU, handle by product, marketing spend, segment profit) is well below listed-peer standard.

3Segment deep-dive: Tur (lotteries) — 69% of group NGR

NGR +0.7% to SEK 1,316m (~$133m): Lotto and a 40th-birthday Triss outran a jackpot-starved Eurojackpot; 9M +3.2% and still taking share in a low-growth lottery market.

Quarterly NGR and drivers

Tur NGR rose 0.7% to SEK 1,316m (~$133m), 69% of group, as Lotto and Triss offset a jackpot-starved Eurojackpot.

  • NGR up SEK 9m YoY and down 1.9% sequentially from a Eurojackpot-boosted Q2 (SEK 1,341m).
  • Lotto continues to benefit from its 2025 relaunch; Triss lifted by 40-year jubilee ticket carrying 40 million-kronor prizes and grocery-retail visibility push.
  • Eurojackpot did not reach last year's customer activity because jackpot levels were lower.
  • Roughly a third of this division's swing factor is a Europe-wide random-number generator rather than anything Visby controls.

Nine-month performance

9M Tur NGR is SEK 3,909m (~$394m), +SEK 120m or +3.2%, with Eurojackpot the growth driver over the period.

  • May 2026 set a monthly revenue record on a high jackpot.
  • Lotto still showing positive relaunch effects over the nine months.

Market share

Tur grew 6.6% in Q2 2026 versus 2.0% for public-benefit lotteries, supporting management's H1 share-gain claim; Q3 likely roughly flat share.

  • Spelinspektionen's 'public-benefit lotteries' category (Postkodlotteriet-type competitors) grew 2.0% in Q2 2026.
  • Q3 market data not yet published; with Tur growth slowing to +0.7% the Q3 read will be less flattering.
  • No actives, ARPU or stakes by game disclosed; only hard engagement datapoints are prize statistics.
  • 116 million-kronor lottery wins paid in Q3, including c.SEK 200m (~$20m) Vikinglotto prize, a Swedish record and ninth-largest in Svenska Spel's history.

Products and channels

Product cadence is steady rather than transformative; retail-agent NGR fell 1.6% to SEK 558m while online grew 4.1% in Q3.

  • Three new digital-only scratch lotteries (Summa Summarum, Fruttis Troppix, Kanonkulan) launched in Q3, after Q2's Triss Sommar, Triss Mors Dag and Triss Semestervinsten.
  • Strategy is to keep Triss relevant on mobile while retail visibility defends the physical ticket.
  • Ombud NGR (overwhelmingly lottery) fell 1.6% to SEK 558m (~$56m) in Q3 and 3.5% to SEK 1,712m over nine months.
  • Online, of which Tur is the largest component, grew 4.1% in Q3 and 8.4% over nine months.
  • Channel migration lowers agent-commission element of direct costs over time: direct costs -1.0% at 9M despite +3.2% NGR.

Profitability and outlook

Segment profit is not disclosed and no guidance is given; Q4 is the seasonally strongest lottery quarter and hinges on Eurojackpot.

  • Company reports revenue by business area only and explicitly declines to prepare IFRS 8 segment reporting, so Tur's share of the SEK 69m external-cost increase cannot be isolated.
  • Our read is that the brand relaunch is group-wide but Triss-40 and grocery-visibility campaigns sit here.
  • Q4 2025 Tur NGR was SEK 1,471m (Christmas Triss and Lotto); Q4 2026 will again hinge on the Eurojackpot roll cycle.

4Segment deep-dive: Sport & Casino (online sportsbook, pools, casino, poker, bingo) — 27% of group NGR

NGR +5.5% to SEK 516m (~$52m) on the World Cup finish and record actives; Oddset and casino are taking share of a +7% commercial online market, but handle, hold and promo intensity remain undisclosed.

Quarterly NGR and drivers

NGR rose 5.5% to SEK 516m (~$52m), 27% of group, on the World Cup finish and record actives, but key metrics are undisclosed.

  • Up SEK 27m YoY; down 6.5% from the SEK 552m World Cup-peak quarter.
  • Strong tournament finish (final on 19 July) drove positive Oddset performance; new-customer offers pushed active customers to record levels; casino showed 'stable growth'.
  • No disclosure of handle, net or structural sports margin, promotional spend as a percentage of stakes, actives or ARPU.
  • Q2 report data: June Sport & Casino revenue +24% YoY, sports betting +135%, playing customers +36%, new registrations +340%; World Cup sales targets exceeded through 6 July.

Nine-month trend

9M NGR is SEK 1,633m (~$165m), +SEK 95m or +6.2%, an underlying mid-single-digit growth business with a tournament overlay.

  • Growth was +2.5% in Q1, +10.8% in Q2 and +5.5% in Q3.

Market share and hold

Implied share rose to c.11.1% in Q2 2026 from 10.8%, but lower hold suggests share is partly bought through price/RTP and marketing.

  • Spelinspektionen's commercial online & betting category grew 7.1% in Q2 2026 to SEK 4,960m (~$500m) while Sport & Casino grew 10.8%.
  • Implied share c.11.1% versus 10.8% in Q2 2025 and Q3 2025 [our estimate; category includes all 70-odd online licensees].
  • Q3 market figures arrive in late November and will show whether post-tournament share held.
  • Group payout ratio rose to 58.5% of stakes (57.5%) with stakes +3.8% versus NGR +1.3%, consistent with faster growth in Oddset-style fixed-odds product.
  • The +18% external-cost line also hints at marketing-driven share gains.

Products and sports rights

Investment is tilted toward engagement features and sports rights, including SHL and SDHL sponsorships extended to 2031.

  • Casino launched '99 Royale', a multiplayer slot format (Ragnarok, Ra the King, Cherry Blossom) where up to 99 players compete in the same round.
  • 'Europatipsmästaren' pools competition over six rounds attracted a little over 12,000 participants and 350 teams; winners donated SEK 200k to grassroots clubs via Gräsroten.
  • Sixteen million-kronor sports wins paid, including one solo 13-correct Stryktipset winner in September.
  • Swedish FA partnership credited by the CEO with strengthening the business during the World Cup; Nations League activations continued in Q3.
  • Became an official WSOP partner in Q2 to seed the poker vertical.

Regulatory risk

Segment profitability and guidance are undisclosed; the regulatory overlay is the main medium-term risk and lands mostly here.

  • Government inquiry into gambling by children and young adults (report due February 2028); Spelinspektionen's draft duty-of-care regulations focus on 18–24-year-olds.
  • Expanded credit ban in force since May 2026; industry debate over prediction markets.
  • Svenska Spel already runs lower deposit limits for young adults; management frames tighter rules as strengthening the licensed channel (2025 channelisation 84%; betting 96%, online casino 81%).
  • The 'healthy revenue' KPI dipping to 94% from 95% in the same quarter actives hit a record is the one number we would press them on.

5Segment deep-dive: Vegas (video lottery terminals in restaurants & bingo halls) — 4% of group NGR

NGR -14% to SEK 67m (~$7m), 'in line with expectations'; the estate has been cut to c.2,700 machines in c.900 venues and the division is in managed decline under a social mandate.

Quarterly NGR and trend

Vegas NGR fell 14.1% to SEK 67m (~$7m), 'in line with expectations', with decline steepening versus Q3 2025's -10%.

  • Down SEK 11m YoY and 2.9% sequentially; nine-month NGR SEK 198m (~$20m), -15.7%.
  • Decline attributed to fewer partner venues and long-run shift of customers to online play; response is cost efficiency and focus on 'profitable business partners'.
  • Multi-year trajectory: SEK 596m in 2021, 537m in 2022, 469m in 2023, 364m in 2024, 310m in 2025; 2026 tracking toward roughly SEK 265m [our arithmetic].
  • In Q3 2025 management blamed the difficult economy for restaurant partners.

Estate and investment

Estate cut c.10% in a single quarter to c.2,700 machines in c.900 venues, with no meaningful capex programme.

  • Q2 report described c.3,000 machines across c.1,000 venues; Q3 describes c.2,700 machines across c.900 restaurants with serving licences and bingo halls.
  • Game refresh continues at low cost: Drakens Tempel Sparky's Riches launched this quarter after Fortune Fever 7s in Q2; core titles remain Big Easy, Loaded 7s and Grodjakten.
  • Group tangible capex was SEK 21m for nine months.

Strategic position

Vegas exists under the owner's social mandate, so an outright exit is not on the table; it is a persistent c.1pt drag on group growth.

  • Owner's mandate requires Svenska Spel to offer value-machine gaming, the only VLT form permitted in Sweden, unlike Casino Cosmopol's exit.
