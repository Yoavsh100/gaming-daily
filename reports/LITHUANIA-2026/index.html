Lithuania

Released 23 Nov 2026 · Currency EUR (tables in € millions; ~USD at EUR/USD 1.094) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Lithuania's regulated gambling market is compounding at mid-teens rates — 9M-26 GGR €229m (~$250.5m), +14% — almost entirely on online slots, with remote now 78% of GGR and retail shrinking 6.5%. But the state is about to take a much larger cut and a much tighter grip: a 22%→36% progressive GGR tax proposed from July 2027, a full advertising ban from January 2028 and a mandatory player card with cashless venues from 2029. The commercial window is real but narrowing, and it belongs to scale incumbents; for a newcomer the only credible routes are acquisition of a licensee or B2B supply.

1Executive summary

  • Market is €229m (~$250.5m) of gambling GGR in 9M-26, +14% YoY (9M-25: €200.5m / ~$219.3m), on top of FY25 GGR €274.1m (~$299.9m, +13%), FY24 €241.9m (~$264.6m, +8.85%) and FY23 €222.2m (~$243.1m). Growth is accelerating, not fading — all figures are regulator (LPT) GGR = stakes minus winnings, before tax, excluding lotteries.
  • Online is the market: remote GGR €179.0m (~$195.8m), +21.8%, 78% of total (73% in 9M-25, 74% FY25, 70% FY24; remote overtook retail in 2020). H1-26 remote was €119.9m (~$131.2m, +24.8%); derived Q3-26 remote €59.1m (~$64.7m, +~16%) shows some deceleration from H1.
  • One product carries everything: remote Category A slots €131.6m (~$144.0m), +~30% (+€30.4m), 57% of all gambling GGR. The slots gain alone exceeded the whole market's net increase (+€28.5m), i.e. every other category combined shrank by ~€1.9m. Online slots+tables+Cat B = €149.9m (~$164.0m), 65% of market GGR.
  • Betting is stalling despite a World Cup summer: remote betting GGR €29.1m (~$31.8m), −2%; retail betting €4.9m (~$5.4m), down. Combined betting is only ~15% of GGR. The 2026 FIFA World Cup (Jun–Jul) sat inside the period and still did not lift the line; LPT does not publish handle, so hold cannot be isolated (n/a).
  • Online table games €17.7m (~$19.4m), +24% (H1 +34%, so Q3 slowed to ~+6% derived); remote Category B machines collapsed −67% to €0.6m (~$0.7m) as play migrates to Cat A; online bingo is dead (€284).
  • Retail is in structural decline: land-based GGR €50.0m (~$54.7m), −6.5% (H1 −5%, derived Q3 −~10%). Split: Cat B slot halls €22.2m (~$24.3m), casino Cat A machines €12.1m (~$13.2m, slightly up), tables €10.8m (~$11.8m, down), betting shops €4.9m. Machines in venues fell to 3,736 from 3,914 (−4.5%).
  • Lottery is a separate, resilient duopoly: ticket sales €131.5m (~$143.9m), +9%; prizes €74.2m (~$81.2m, 56% payout); lottery GGR €57.3m (~$62.7m). FY25 sales were €167.2m (~$182.9m, +12.7%) with prizes €91.9m (~$100.5m). Two private operators as of 30 Jun 2026. Adding lottery GGR, the combined 9M-26 market is ~€286m (~$313m).
  • Fiscal take €74.19m (~$81.2m), +15%: gambling operators ~€50m (~$54.7m), lottery operators €24.2m (~$26.5m). FY25 take was €86.9m (~$95.1m; gambling €57.6m, lottery €29.3m), FY24 €73.9m (~$80.9m). Gambling tax ÷ GGR = 21.8%, which independently corroborates the 22% headline rate; lottery tax ÷ sales = 18.4%, corroborating 18% on turnover.
  • Tax regime (Lottery and Gaming Tax Law, VMI): 22% of GGR on slots, table games, bingo, totalisator, betting and remote gambling since 1 Jan 2025 (20% from 1 Jul 2022), plus a fixed monthly amount per licensed retail device; lotteries 18% of the nominal value of tickets sold (turnover base) since 1 Jan 2023; quarterly tax periods; one-off licence-issuance fee. No player-level withholding at operator level.
  • Tax cliff ahead: the Finance Ministry published amendments on ~10 Nov 2026 raising lottery tax 18%→20% and making gambling tax progressive — 22% on GGR up to €3m (~$3.3m), 29% on €3–5m, 36% above €5m (~$5.5m) — targeted for 1 Jul 2027 (per Casino Guardian/iGamingToday). The sponsor (Democrats' T. Tomilinas) cites +€36m (~$39m) p.a. With all eight operators well above €5m GGR, the effective market rate is ~35%, not 22%.
  • Politics are one-directional. A rival LVŽS bill (lottery 25%, gambling 30%, licence fee doubled to €2m/~$2.2m) received a negative Government opinion in Sept 2026, and the Ministry had resisted a 22–37% progressive bill in 2025 — yet within months the Ministry tabled its own progressive scale to fund the 2027 budget. Every coalition partner is now on record for higher gambling taxes; only the scale is in play.
  • Licensing is closed in practice. LPT (Lošimų priežiūros tarnyba, under the Ministry of Finance) licenses by game type under the Gaming Law IX-325 (2001; remote since 2016). LPT counts 12 licensed gambling companies, of which 8 operate (unchanged YoY; 12 operated in 2022, 12 of 15 licensed in 2020). Remote licences have historically been tied to holding a domestic land-based licence [per the 2016 regime; current text not re-verified].
  • Operator landscape is an oligopoly with no per-operator disclosure. The four-member Responsible Gambling Business Association says it represents ~60% of the 2024 market. Active brands include TOPsport, Betsafe (Betsson), Olybet, 7bet, Uniclub, Cbet and Optibet [brand list from market knowledge, not from the LPT register in this research]; market shares n/a.
  • Player-protection overhaul (Draft Law XVP-1690): submitted 2 Apr 2026, approved by Government in June 2026, notified to the EU TRIS system 18 Aug 2026, Seimas adoption pending. Expanded LPT powers and streamlined compliance from 1 May 2027; a mandatory, ID-linked player card for retail and online plus cashless venues from 1 Jan 2029; affordability checks for higher-spending players. Separately, Law XV-517 (13 Nov 2025) gives the self-exclusion register its own article with a 6-month minimum from 1 Jan 2027.
  • Advertising is being switched off. The Gaming Law already prohibits gambling advertising and promotion (including incentive bonuses) with narrow exceptions; from 1 Jul 2025 outdoor advertising is limited to name/trademark; a complete ban applies from 1 Jan 2028 (per multiple secondary sources). Licensed operators cannot buy market share — a decisive structural advantage for incumbents.
  • Payments are euro, bank-centric and about to go cashless in venues (from 2029, tied to the player card, with a 3-year equipment transition). Detailed online payment-method data is not published (n/a).
  • Enforcement is active but uneven: in 2025 LPT issued 47 warnings to foreign entities and ran 37 illegal-remote investigations; it blocked Polymarket in Sept 2026 as unlicensed betting while Kalshi stayed accessible (LRT, Oct 2026). Licensed operators complained to LPT in Feb 2026 about unlicensed brands advertising in Lithuania. Illegal-market size: n/a.
  • Quarterly cadence: Gaming Intelligence reports Q1-26 €82.7m (~$90.5m, +4%) and a record Q2-26 €109.3m (~$119.6m, +24%); these appear to include lottery GGR (Q1+Q2 = €192.0m ≈ gambling GGR €153.6m + ~€38m lottery GGR). Gambling-only Q3-26 derived from LPT data is €75.4m (~$82.5m), +~9% — the slowest quarter of the year.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: a small (~2.9m people), fast-growing, online-led, high-margin market that is about to become high-tax (~35% effective), ad-dark and card-gated. Greenfield entry is impractical; value lies in (i) acquiring a licensee before the 2027 tax reset is priced in, (ii) supplying slots/live content, platform and compliance/payment tech to the eight incumbents, and (iii) retail consolidation as cashless capex forces marginal halls out.

3Deep-dive: Online casino (remote Category A/B slots and table games)

Online slots are the Lithuanian market: €131.6m (~$144.0m) 9M-26 GGR, +~30%, 57% of all gambling GGR — and the only category that is growing the pie.

Remote slots

Remote Category A slot GGR of €131.6m (~$144.0m) in 9M-26, up roughly a third, is 57% of gambling GGR and ~74% of remote GGR.

  • 9M-25 base €101.2m derived; H1-26 €85.7m (~$93.8m, +~33%), implying derived Q3-26 €45.9m (~$50.2m), still +~25% YoY.
  • +€30.4m YoY gain exceeded the whole market's +€28.5m, so ex-slots the regulated market contracted by ~€1.9m.
  • Analyst: single most important fact in the market; growth is a slots story.
  • Every policy lever being pulled (progressive tax, affordability checks, player-card limits) lands squarely on this category.

Remote tables and Cat B

Remote table games reached €17.7m (~$19.4m), +24% YoY, but derived Q3 growth of ~6% is the first visible deceleration.

  • 9M-25 ≈ €14.3m; H1 €12.3m (~$13.5m, +34%); derived Q3 ~€5.4m (~$5.9m), +~6%.
  • Remote Category B machines fell 67% to €0.61m (~$0.67m) as operators migrate content to Cat A.
  • Online bingo recorded €284 for the period — commercially extinct.

Online casino total

Online casino generated ~€149.9m (~$164.0m) in 9M-26, about 65% of gambling GGR and 84% of remote GGR, tracking toward ~€200m FY26 (estimate).

  • Run-rate FY26 estimate ~€200m (~$219m).
  • Growth achieved with no permitted bonuses or incentive promotions and outdoor advertising restricted since 1 Jul 2025.
  • Growth is organic/product-led, partly a channel shift from retail slot halls (whose GGR fell) and plausibly from offshore sites after LPT blocking and warnings.

Market structure and risks

ARPU and player counts are n/a; the top two or three online operators dominate slots, and the 36% top tax band and 2029 card-linked limits are forward risks.

  • Regulator publishes no active accounts, deposits or RTP by operator.
  • Association of four large licensees claims ~60% of the 2024 market.
  • Supplier demand is for slots content, aggregation and live tables into eight licensed platforms.
  • 36% top tax band is effective ~35% on a scale casino book; Scandinavian precedents suggest card-linked limits can knock double-digit percentages off regulated casino GGR.

4Deep-dive: Sports betting (remote and retail)

Betting is a ~15% side-vertical that went backwards (−2% online) through a World Cup summer; LPT publishes no handle, so hold is n/a.

Betting GGR

Betting is a ~15% side-vertical that went backwards: remote GGR €29.1m (~$31.8m), −2%, and retail €4.9m (~$5.4m), also down.

  • Derived 9M-25 remote base ~€29.7m; prior-year retail figure not disclosed.
  • Combined betting ~€34.0m (~$37.2m) is 14.8% of gambling GGR and 16% of remote GGR.
  • LPT reports GGR only; stakes, hold, promo intensity and structural margin n/a, so results- versus volume-driven decline cannot be determined.

World Cup weakness

A flat-to-down print through the 2026 FIFA World Cup (June–July, Q2/Q3) is unusually weak for a market growing 14% overall.

  • Plausible contributors: ban on incentive promotions (no free bets), pricing in a 22% GGR tax environment, leakage to unlicensed sportsbooks and prediction markets.
  • LPT ordered Polymarket blocked in September 2026 as unlicensed betting while Kalshi remained accessible.
  • Inconsistency criticised publicly by MP M. Lingė (LRT, Oct 2026).

Retail betting

Retail betting infrastructure is thin and shrinking; €4.9m retail betting GGR is under 10% of land-based GGR.

  • LPT's 2020 annual report counted 131 betting points among 349 gambling venues at end-2020; current count n/a.
  • Betting is overwhelmingly a digital product bundled with casino; economics of a sportsbook-led entry are poor.

Tax outlook

At an assumed ~8% hold, a 36% top GGR rate equals ~3% of handle, heavy for a product competing on price with offshore books.

  • Expect incumbents to treat betting as a cross-sell funnel into slots rather than a profit centre.
  • Progressive tax, if adopted, would widen pricing gaps versus unlicensed operators further.

5Deep-dive: Land-based venues (casinos, slot halls, betting shops)

Retail GGR €50.0m (~$54.7m), −6.5% and accelerating downward (derived Q3 −~10%); 3,736 machines and a 2029 cashless mandate point to managed decline and consolidation.

Retail GGR

Land-based GGR fell to €50.0m (~$54.7m) in 9M-26 from €53.5m (~$58.5m), −6.5%, with the decline accelerating.

  • LPT noted lower stakes, prizes and revenue across the board.
  • H1-26 €33.7m (~$36.9m, −5%); derived Q3-26 €16.3m (~$17.8m), down ~10% YoY.
  • Retail share of gambling GGR now 22%, versus 30% in FY24 and parity as recently as 2019–20.

Product mix and venues

Category B slot-hall machines remain the largest retail line at €22.2m (~$24.3m); installed machines fell 4.5% to 3,736.

  • Casino Category A machines €12.1m (~$13.2m, slightly up); table games €10.8m (~$11.8m, down; H1 €7.7m flat, implying weak Q3 ~€3.1m); betting shops €4.9m (~$5.4m).
  • Machines 3,736 at end-September 2026 versus 3,914 a year earlier.
  • LPT 2020 report: 17 casinos, 201 slot halls, 131 betting points; SBC News (May 2026) cited 18 casinos, 50 'arcade halls', 10 online licences — inconsistent, flagged unverified.

Tax and 2029 cashless mandate

Retail carries 22% of GGR plus fixed monthly per-device charges and faces the heaviest reform capex from the 1 Jan 2029 cashless mandate.

  • Per-machine/table fixed amount payable quarterly.
  • From 1 Jan 2029 all venue payments and payouts must be non-cash and tied to the player card; three-year transition for equipment replacement.
  • Combined with the 2028 advertising ban, this will push marginal halls out; falling machine numbers are the leading indicator.

Consolidation window

Land-based assets matter mainly as licence anchors and brand touchpoints, making 2027–29 the likely window for distressed consolidation.

  • Historically a remote licence required the corresponding land-based licence [2016 regime; current text not re-verified].
  • Small retail licensees are potential acquisition vehicles for an online-led entrant.

6Deep-dive: Lotteries

A two-operator segment with €131.5m (~$143.9m) of 9M-26 ticket sales (+9%) and €57.3m (~$62.7m) GGR, taxed at 18% of turnover (≈42% of GGR) and proposed to rise to 20%.

Lottery sales and GGR

9M-26 ticket sales €131.5m (~$143.9m), +9%, with GGR of €57.3m (~$62.7m), up ~8.7%, roughly one-fifth of the combined market.

  • Prizes €74.2m (~$81.2m, +9.3%), a 56.4% payout; derived 9M-25 GGR base ~€52.8m.
  • H1-26 sales €87.73m (~$96.0m, +8.6%).
  • FY25 sales €167.2m (~$182.9m, +12.7% vs FY24 €148.3m), prizes €91.9m (~$100.5m), FY25 lottery GGR ~€75.3m (~$82.4m).
  • Combined gambling-plus-lottery market ~€286m in 9M-26.

Operators

Two private companies organised the large lotteries as of 30 June 2026; the segment is effectively closed.

  • Olifėja is the long-standing national operator (Eurojackpot, Vikinglotto, Teleloto); second operator's identity and share not verified; no per-operator sales published.
  • Lotteries Law obliges statutory support (parama); LPT 2020–22 overview shows €5.2–10.3m (~$5.7–11.3m) p.a.

Lottery tax

Lottery tax is 18% of turnover (≈42% of GGR), proposed to rise to 20% from July 2027, pushing effective tax on GGR toward 45–55%.

  • 18% since 1 Jan 2023 (13% from 1 Jul 2022; lower before); 9M-26 tax €24.2m (~$26.5m), 18.4% of sales.
  • Finance Ministry November 2026 draft: 20%, worth ~+€2.6m (~$2.9m) on 9M-26 base, ~+€3.5m annualised; rival LVŽS bill proposed 25%.
  • Operators have pushed back on payout economics.

Relevance to gambling

Lottery is not an entry route but competes for the same discretionary wallet and serves as a political benchmark.

  • Lawmakers repeatedly pair lottery and gambling tax rises in the same bills.
  • Lottery-tax politics are a leading indicator for gambling-tax outcomes.

7Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime

A closed, game-type licence system under LPT with a 22% GGR tax (plus per-device charges) that is heading to a 22/29/36% progressive scale from July 2027 — an effective ~35% for every incumbent.

Legal framework

A closed, game-type licence system under LPT; 12 licensed gambling companies of which 8 operate, unchanged YoY.

  • Gaming Law (No. IX-325, 2001), Lotteries Law and Lottery and Gaming Tax Law; LPT under the Ministry of Finance; remote gambling licensable domestically since 2016.
  • Licences per game type: table games and Cat A machines, Cat B machines, bingo, totalisator, betting, remote; remote historically required corresponding land-based licence [not re-verified].
  • Down from 12 operating in 2022 and 12 of 15 licensed in 2020; end-2020: 7 remote betting, 5 remote table, 7 remote Cat A, 6 remote Cat B licensees.
  • Minimum gambling age 18 [not re-verified].

Fees and requirements

A one-off licence fee of ~€1m (~$1.1m) is implied by the LVŽS bill to double it to €2m (~$2.2m).

  • Statutory fee schedule not verified.
  • Minimum authorised-capital thresholds apply by licence type (amounts n/a).
  • Local establishment required; server-location and local-ownership rules not verified (n/a).

Current tax

Gambling tax is 22% of GGR since 1 Jan 2025 plus a fixed monthly amount per device; lotteries pay 18% of ticket value.

  • 20% from 1 Jul 2022; 2025 step scored at ~+€4.4m/~$4.8m; tax period is the calendar quarter.
  • Collections corroborate: ~€50m on €229m GGR = 21.8%; lottery €24.2m on €131.5m = 18.4%.
  • FY24 sector data: €32.6m (~$35.7m) other taxes and fees and 1,561 employees.

Progressive tax pipeline

The Finance Ministry draft of 22/29/36% from 1 Jul 2027 is a flat ~35% in practice for every incumbent, removing ~40% of EBITDA before mitigation.

  • Ministry draft (~10 Nov 2026): lottery 18%→20%; gambling 22% ≤€3m, 29% €3–5m, 36% above €5m; +€36m (~$39m) p.a.
  • LVŽS (Girskienė) bill: lottery 25%, gambling 30%, licence fee €2m — negative Government opinion Sept 2026, still before Seimas; Democrats' 2025 bill 22–37% rejected as premature.
  • Example: €60m GGR → €21.0m tax, 35.1%; market gambling tax rises from ~€67m (22% of ~€305m FY26E) to ~€105m.
  • Licensee at 30% EBITDA margin loses ~40% of EBITDA from a 13-point rise; B2B revenue-share contracts will be renegotiated.

8Deep-dive: Player protection, advertising, payments & enforcement

Lithuania is building an EU-first centralised control stack — mandatory ID-linked player card, cashless venues and affordability checks by 2029 — on top of an advertising regime that goes fully dark on 1 Jan 2028.

Player card package

Draft Law XVP-1690 mandates an ID-linked player card, cashless venues and affordability checks from 1 Jan 2029; Seimas adoption still required.

  • Submitted 2 Apr 2026, Government-approved June 2026, notified to EU TRIS 18 Aug 2026; card phased in 2027–28; LPT powers expanded from 1 May 2027.
  • Backed by Finance Minister K. Vaitiekūnas and PM I. Ruginienė; LPT describes the card as the core tool for enforcing pre-set limits.
  • Law XV-517 of 13 Nov 2025 sets a six-month minimum self-exclusion from 1 Jan 2027.
  • LRT (May 2026) reported a proposed €1,000 (~$1,094) per-month loss limit; status unverified.

Advertising ban

Advertising is heavily constrained and goes fully dark on 1 Jan 2028, so acquisition is brand-, SEO- and product-led only.

  • Gaming Law prohibits advertising and incentives such as bonuses; from 1 Jul 2025 outdoor ads may show only name and trademark.
  • Responsible Gambling Business Association (four licensees, ~60% of 2024 market) complained 6 and 26 Feb 2026 about foreign brands advertising; LPT reply (24 Feb 2026, No. 2E-272) said such promotion falls under the prohibition.
  • A newcomer cannot buy awareness.

Enforcement

LPT blacklists and ISP-blocks illegal sites; in 2025 it issued 47 warnings and ran 37 investigations, but no current illegal-market estimate exists.

  • Blacklist updated by director orders (e.g. 24 Apr 2026); 2,160 consultations and AML/CTF training in 2025.
  • Polymarket blocked Sept 2026 while Kalshi remained accessible — inconsistency criticised in the Seimas.
  • Illegal-market estimate n/a; 15min report (Nov 2026) on tax lost could not be read in full.

Payments

Euro bank-rail market; cash stays legal in venues until the 2029 mandate, creating a 2027–29 demand wave for identity and cashless technology.

  • E-wallet and open-banking usage not published (n/a).
  • After 2029 every venue transaction will be identity-linked through the player card.
  • Ministry cited compliance costs in 2025 as a reason to delay tax rises — a reason it has since dropped.

9Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Structural online growth with no sign of saturation: remote GGR +21.8% in 9M-26 (€179.0m / ~$195.8m) after +19% in FY25 and +14.9% in FY24; online casino alone is tracking to ~€200m (~$219m) for FY26 (estimate). Online penetration of 78% already matches Nordic benchmarks. structural
  • A protected oligopoly: 8 operating licensees, no bonus competition, advertising restricted — so incumbent margins are high and customer bases sticky. For an entrant that means acquiring a licensee buys a defensible position, and the 2027 tax reset may create motivated sellers before it is priced in. structural
  • Slots dominance (57% of GGR, +30%) is a direct B2B demand signal for content, aggregation and live-casino supply into eight platforms; growth is product-led because promotions are banned, which rewards suppliers with differentiated titles. structural
  • Retail consolidation window 2027–29: machine count already −4.5% YoY, retail GGR −6.5% and a cashless/player-card capex mandate by 1 Jan 2029 will force marginal slot halls and small licensees to exit — opportunities to acquire licences and footprint cheaply. one-off
  • Fiscal and legal predictability relative to emerging markets: euro, EU member, GGR-based tax (not turnover for gambling), quarterly filing, published regulator statistics, and a regulator that actively blocks and warns offshore competitors (47 warnings, 37 investigations in 2025; Polymarket blocked). structural
  • The player-card/cashless mandate creates a 2027–29 procurement cycle for identity, limit-management, affordability and cashless-payment technology across all licensees — a niche for compliance-tech and platform suppliers. one-off
  • Tax take is growing faster than the market (+15% vs +14% GGR) without yet denting growth — evidence that the regulated channel is still winning share from offshore, at least until the ad ban and card land. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Tax cliff: the Finance Ministry's 22/29/36% progressive scale from 1 Jul 2027 is a flat ~35% for every incumbent (all are >€5m GGR), lifting gambling tax from ~€67m to ~€105m on a ~€305m base. A 30%-margin licensee loses ~40% of EBITDA before mitigation; B2B revenue-share contracts will be repriced. structural
  • Advertising goes fully dark on 1 Jan 2028 on top of an existing promotion/bonus ban — a newcomer cannot build brand, and affiliate/SEO channels will be the only acquisition route. This freezes market shares in favour of TOPsport/Betsafe-tier incumbents. structural
  • Mandatory ID-linked player card, affordability checks and cashless venues from 2029 will bite hardest on the slots category that is 57% of GGR; Scandinavian precedents (Norway's card, Sweden's limits) point to double-digit regulated-GGR losses and offshore leakage, and the Polymarket/Kalshi inconsistency shows enforcement is not airtight. structural
  • Betting is weak: remote betting GGR −2% through a World Cup summer and only ~15% of the market; LPT publishes no handle, so margin trends cannot be diagnosed. A sportsbook-led thesis does not work here. mixed
  • Retail decline is accelerating (−5% H1, −~10% derived Q3) and carries a dual tax burden (22% GGR plus per-device charges) and a cashless capex bill — retail assets are liabilities except as licence anchors. structural
  • Small, concentrated market: ~2.9m people, €274m (~$300m) FY25 GGR, eight operators, two lottery companies and a single regulator whose powers expand from May 2027. Policy risk is binary and idiosyncratic. structural
  • Governance volatility: three competing tax bills in 18 months (Democrats 22–37%, LVŽS 30%/25% with a €2m licence fee, MoF 22/29/36%), with the Ministry reversing its 2025 'not yet' position to fund the 2027 budget — the direction is clear but the final rate, thresholds and timing are not. governance
  • Data gaps: LPT does not publish actives, deposits, handle or per-operator shares, and its portal was not directly retrievable in this research; licence-fee and venue-count figures cited in press are inconsistent and flagged unverified. watch

10Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The regulatory bridge runs from today's comparatively liberal settings — a flat 22% GGR tax, outdoor-only advertising limits and voluntary player limits — to a 2027–29 regime of a 22/29/36% progressive GGR tax (effective ~35%), lottery tax at 20%, a complete advertising ban (1 Jan 2028) and a mandatory ID-linked player card with cashless venues and affordability checks (1 Jan 2029). The driver is fiscal: the Finance Ministry needs 2027 budget revenue and its coalition partners (the Democrats 'Vardan Lietuvos' and LVŽS) have each tabled steeper variants; the player-protection track (Draft Law XVP-1690) was approved by Government in June 2026, cleared EU TRIS notification in August and awaits the Seimas. The moving parts are the thresholds (€3m/€5m), the start date (July 2027 vs earlier), whether the LVŽS €2m licence fee survives, and the affordability thresholds LPT will set in secondary legislation.

Independent view. Revenue: we estimate FY26 gambling GGR of ~€310m (~$339m), +13% (9M actual €229m plus a Q4 of ~€82m assuming +12% on Q4-25's €73.6m), with online share ending the year at ~79%. FY27E +6–9% as the July 2027 tax step is partly passed through via lower RTP and wider betting margins, and 2028 flat to −3% once advertising goes dark; 2029 −5–10% is the realistic downside when the card and affordability checks arrive, with online share nonetheless rising to 83–85% as retail keeps shrinking ~8–10% a year. Tax take: combined collections of ~€100m (~$109m) in FY26E rising to ~€135–145m (~$148–159m) in 2028 if the progressive scale is adopted — the state's revenue grows even as operator EBITDA falls by roughly a third. Margins: incumbents will defend through RTP cuts, cost take-out and renegotiated supplier revenue shares; expect at least one or two of the eight operators to exit or merge, leaving 5–6 by 2029. Leverage/illegal share: offshore leakage rises with each step, and the Polymarket/Kalshi episode suggests enforcement will remain reactive. Our stance: attractive to supply, hazardous to enter greenfield; any acquisition must be underwritten at a 36% top rate and zero advertising.
