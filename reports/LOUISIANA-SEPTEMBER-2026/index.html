The one-line verdict

September 2026 was Louisiana's best handle month of the year at $405.1m (+0.9% YoY), but a soft 10.7% hold cut net proceeds 8.0% to $43.4m and state tax 9.4% to $8.9m — the sportsbook market is now mature, 95% mobile, parlay-dependent (63% of mobile win) and taxed at 21.5%. The land-based base is the growth story for once ($195.5m casino GGR, +11.8% on landside relocations and Caesars New Orleans), while the rule-set is hardening: sweepstakes are banned with racketeering exposure from 1 Aug 2026, and a prop/micro-bet ban was shelved only because it would cost the state >$20m a year.

1Executive summary

  • Best handle month of 2026, weakest hold since February: total sports wagers of $405.1m (+0.9% YoY, +48.9% MoM as football returned) produced net proceeds (GGR after promo deductions) of $43.4m, down 8.0% YoY, because hold fell to 10.72% from 11.76%. Gross win before the $1.3m promo deduction was $44.7m (-7.5%), matching the ~$45m / -7% figure carried by trade press.
  • Mobile is the market: mobile handle $384.5m (+4.6% YoY) is 94.9% of stakes and $39.6m (91%) of net proceeds; retail handle collapsed 39.0% to $20.6m, although an 18.9% retail hold lifted retail win 30.6% to $3.9m. Mobile hold of 10.29% vs 12.03% a year ago explains the entire NP decline.
  • Tax take tracked hold, not rate: $8.88m paid in September (-9.4% YoY) — $8.50m mobile at exactly 21.5% of net proceeds and $0.38m retail at ~10% (9.7% effective after loss carry-forwards). September 2025 already carried the 21.5% rate, so there is no rate tailwind left in the YoY comparison.
  • Fiscal Q1 FY27 (Jul–Sep 2026): handle $956.7m (+6.4%), net proceeds $110.9m (-1.1%), tax $23.0m (+8.3%). The tax line grew only because July 2025 was the last month taxed at 15%; hold compressed to 11.6% from 12.5%.
  • Calendar 2026 to date: 9M handle $2,998m (+1.8% vs 9M-25 $2,945m), net proceeds $357.1m (+3.4%), tax $74.6m, promo deductions $31.4m (1.0% of handle). CY2025 closed at $4,245m handle / $521.5m NP / $92.7m tax — growth has decelerated to low single digits on stakes.
  • FY26 (Jul-25–Jun-26) was the step-change year: handle $4,241m (+5.7%), net proceeds $534.5m (+14.1%), tax $109.6m (+55.7%) as the mobile rate rose from 15% to 21.5% (Act 298 of 2025, effective 1 Aug 2025). Mobile: $3,996m / $505.1m / $106.6m; retail: $244.9m (-13.0%) / $29.4m / $2.9m. FY25 had grown +20.3% on handle, so the market has roughly halved its growth rate in a year.
  • Product mix is parlay-led: parlays generated $24.9m, 63% of September mobile net proceeds; football $5.2m, 'other' (props/tennis/golf/etc.) $6.4m, baseball $2.1m, basketball $0.8m, soccer $0.4m. FY26 parlays were $357.7m of $505.1m mobile NP (71%). This is the economic engine and the political target (see SB 354).
  • Market structure (FY25, regulator-reported): statewide gaming revenue ~$3,625m — riverboats $1,811.0m (50%), video poker net device revenue $757.7m (21%), mobile sports $442.6m (12%), racino slots $317.5m (9%), Caesars New Orleans land-based $270.1m (7%), retail sports $25.1m (1%), DFS net $1.5m. 'Online penetration' is therefore ~12% of gaming revenue and is sports-only: there is no iGaming and no iLottery.
  • Land-based is growing double digits in Sep-26: $195.5m (+11.8%), with the 16 riverboat/land-based casinos at $172.3m (+14%). Lake Charles $69.7m (+9.4%), New Orleans $50.4m (+13.8%; Caesars New Orleans $25.4m, +30.4%), Shreveport/Bossier $47.6m (+15.9%; Live! +31.3%), Baton Rouge $22.3m (+10.7%; Bally's Baton Rouge +607% after its landside move). Racetracks lag: Delta Downs $12.2m (-2.2%), Evangeline Downs $5.5m (-2.8%). Jefferies puts Q3 statewide GGR at +3.5% against an +8.3% comp.
  • Licensing is capped and casino-anchored: the Sports Wagering Act allows up to 20 sports wagering licences, reserved for the 15 riverboat, 4 racino and 1 land-based casino licensees; mobile is offered through platform providers (permit: $100,000 non-refundable application + $250,000 permit fee per five-year term) and service providers ($10,000 + $12,500). The LGCB platform list names 10 sportsbooks — FanDuel, DraftKings, Caesars, BetMGM, bet365, Fanatics, BetRivers, Bally Bet, ESPN Bet and TwinSpires — but several are understood to be inactive [current status unverified]; the regulator publishes no operator-level data, so shares are n/a.
  • Tax regime (base = net proceeds, i.e. GGR less promo wagers, with operator losses carried forward): mobile 21.5%, retail 10%; DFS 8% of net revenue; riverboats pay a 21.5% state fee on net gaming proceeds ($389m on $1,811m in FY25) plus local admission fees; racino slots paid $48m (15% effective state fee) and Caesars New Orleans $65.0m under its operating contract; video poker franchise fees are 22.5% for racetrack/OTB (Type 4) locations and higher for other location types [other rates unverified], $229.6m in FY25 (30% blended).
  • Tax allocation politics: Act 298 earmarks 25% of mobile sports tax to the SPORT Fund for public-university athletics (~$1.7–2m per Division I programme) and 3% to the Postsecondary Inclusive Education Fund, alongside the legacy split (25% early-childhood education, 10% local governments, 3% problem gambling). The original HB 639 draft proposed 32.5% — the legislature has shown both appetite and a ceiling.
  • State take: roughly $802m across all verticals in FY25 (22% blended on $3,625m), of which sports betting was only $70.4m; lifetime sports wagering since launch (retail Oct-21, mobile Jan-22) is $16.2bn handle, $1.85bn net proceeds and $312.6m tax.
  • Enforcement has turned hard-edged: HB 883 (dual-currency sweepstakes defined as illegal 'gambling by computer'; fines up to $100,000 per violation; liability extended to platform providers and payment processors; AG cease-and-desist and injunction powers) and HB 53 (gambling by computer, electronic sweepstakes devices and sports bribery added to the racketeering statute — up to 50 years and $1m) were signed in May 2026 and took effect 1 Aug 2026, after the Governor vetoed a 2025 sweepstakes ban and the LGCB issued 40+ cease-and-desist letters.
  • Prop/micro-bet ban shelved purely on fiscal grounds: SB 354 (ban on all proposition and in-play micro wagers from 1 Aug 2026) was withdrawn on 1 Apr 2026 after the fiscal note showed >$20m of annual General Fund loss (~$40m total tax in some reports); the sponsor has said she will return in 2027. With parlays and props carrying ~70% of mobile win, this is the single biggest product risk in the state.
  • No iGaming, no iLottery, grey-zone prediction markets: no online casino bill advanced in 2026; an iLottery authorisation was prefiled [outcome unverified]; federally-regulated event contracts (Kalshi et al.) remain outside the LGCB framework [enforcement status unverified]. The 'online' TAM is therefore capped at sportsbook NP of ~$500–535m a year.
  • Payments and RG: the regulator publishes no payments data; deposits run on standard US rails (ACH, cards, wallets, casino-cage cash) with no state credit-card ban identified [unverified]. Minimum age 21, parish-level geofencing (55 of 64 parishes opted in), a statewide LGCB self-exclusion list, 3% of sports tax to problem gambling, and an LGCB prohibition on college player props (since 2024) [not re-verified].
  • Read-through for B2Tech: B2C market access requires a skin agreement with one of the 20 casino/racino licensees, a $350k permit outlay and competing for share in a FanDuel/DraftKings-dominated market growing 2–6% on handle and taxed at 21.5% of a promo-deductible base — expensive share-taking, not greenfield. The more realistic angle is B2B (platform/service-provider permits are cheap) and supplying land-based operators, who are the only growing, protected segment; keep any grey-market adjacency (sweeps, prediction markets) out of Louisiana entirely given racketeering exposure.

3Deep-dive: Mobile sports betting (online)

$384.5m handle (+4.6%) but a 10.3% hold took net proceeds down 10.6% to $39.6m; parlays are 63% of win and the whole economic argument.

September handle and hold

Mobile handle of $384.5m rose 4.6% but a 10.3% hold cut net proceeds 10.6% to $39.6m.

  • Wagers up 47.5% on August; strongest month of calendar 2026 and third-highest ever behind October and November 2025 ($430.8m and $414.6m).
  • Win before promo deductions $40.9m; after $1.3m promotional-wager deduction, net proceeds $39.6m; hold 10.3% versus 12.0% a year earlier and 11.9% in August.
  • Football net proceeds $5.2m, 17% below September 2025's $6.2m on larger handle; 'other' bucket fell from $9.9m to $6.4m as favourites covered.
  • Parlay win $24.9m, flat year on year ($25.4m), carried 63% of mobile net proceeds; baseball $2.1m, basketball $0.8m, soccer $0.4m immaterial.

Maturing market

Growth has slowed from 37% in FY24 to 7% in FY26; hold is now the swing variable, with structural net hold modelled at 11.5–12.5%.

  • Mobile handle grew 37% in FY24, 23% in FY25 and 7% in FY26.
  • FY27 Q1 running +9.8% on handle but -0.6% on net proceeds; calendar 9M-2026 handle only +1.8% versus 9M-2025.
  • Trailing-twelve-month hold 12.4% on $4.30bn handle and $533m net proceeds; monthly range 10.3% (September) to 15.6% (December 2025).
  • Model 11.5–12.5% structural net hold with ±2pp monthly noise, not the 14–15% of November–December 2025.

Promotional deductions

Uncapped promo deductions of $34.9m in FY26 (0.9% of handle) can materially move the taxable base and are a likely legislative target.

  • $25.6m of FY26 deductions landed in January–April 2026; $12.0m in January alone, ten times the normal $1.0–1.3m monthly run-rate.
  • Deductions dropped back to $1.1–1.3m from May; cause could be an operator's acquisition push or a reclassification.
  • September's deduction was a modest $1.3m, 0.3% of handle.

Tax and operator roster

Tax of $8.50m equals 21.5% of net proceeds, confirming Act 298 in force since August 2025; operator share cannot be tracked publicly.

  • July 2025 was still taxed at 15.0%; lifetime mobile has produced $14.8bn handle, $1.70bn net proceeds and $297m tax.
  • LGCB platform list names ten brands (FanDuel, DraftKings, Caesars, BetMGM, bet365, Fanatics, BetRivers, Bally Bet, ESPN Bet, TwinSpires) with no operator-level handle.
  • At least two listings understood to be dormant [unverified]; third-party guides count seven live apps.
  • For B2Tech, entry only possible as a second skin on a casino licensee whose first skin is almost certainly a top-four brand.

Guidance

Calendar 2026 should land at $500–525m net proceeds and $4.25–4.35bn handle, flat-to-low-single-digit growth with hold risk to the downside.

  • State FY27 budget built on 21.5% rate and roughly $105–110m sports tax (reading of FY26 $109.6m outturn), requiring ~$500m net proceeds.
  • 9M-2026 net proceeds of $357m against a Q4-2025 comparison of $176m.
  • Guidance is implicit rather than official; figures are estimates.

4Deep-dive: Retail sports betting

Retail handle fell 39% to $20.6m — structural migration to mobile — though an 18.9% hold produced a one-off 31% rise in win.

September retail results

Retail handle fell 39.0% to $20.6m, the lowest September since launch, though an 18.9% hold lifted net proceeds 30.6% to $3.9m.

  • Down from $33.8m a year earlier; retail handle fell 13.0% in FY26 to $244.9m and is down 36.3% in FY27 Q1 at $42.3m.
  • Trajectory is roughly $150–170m annual retail handle by FY27, around 4% of state total versus 10% in FY23.
  • Parlays $2.2m and football $1.8m made up more than the entire win, with baseball, basketball and 'other' negative; not repeatable.
  • Tax paid $0.38m, 9.7% of net proceeds against 10% statutory rate; gap explained by loss carry-forward provision.

Market access role

Retail books are an amenity anchoring the licence rather than a profit centre, with FY26 net proceeds of $29.4m across up to 20 venues.

  • All 20 sports wagering licences held by land-based casinos; statute launched retail October 2021 and mobile three months later, tying skins to properties.
  • Roughly $1.5m per licence before staff, kiosk and platform costs.
  • Several licensees run their retail book through the same platform provider that holds their mobile skin.

Operator implications

The structural shift to mobile is complete and the legislature treats casinos as the constituency to protect.

  • 95.6% of FY27 Q1 handle is mobile; retail is a 2–4% channel still carrying full cash-handling and cage compliance cost.
  • Retail left at 10% tax while mobile raised to 21.5%, plus loss carry-forward, signals protection of casino licence holders.
  • Any future product restriction such as the shelved SB 354 would hit mobile parlays far harder than casino-floor betting.

5Deep-dive: Land-based casinos, racinos & video poker

September casino GGR of $195.5m (+11.8%) is the fastest growth in the state, driven by landside relocations and Caesars New Orleans; racetracks lag and video poker is the quiet $750m+ vertical.

September casino GGR

Statewide gaming revenue of $195.5m rose 11.8%, the fastest growth in the state, driven by landside relocations and Caesars New Orleans.

  • 15 riverboats and the land-based casino $172.3m (+14%); four racino slot floors ~$23m.
  • Lake Charles largest at $69.7m (+9.4%): L'Auberge du Lac $26.7m (+10.6%), Golden Nugget $25.1m (+14.3%), Delta Downs $12.2m (-2.2%).
  • New Orleans +13.8% to $50.4m on Caesars New Orleans $25.4m (+30.4%) lapping $435m rebuild; Treasure Chest $11.9m (-3.4%), Boomtown $7.3m (+2.6%).
  • Shreveport/Bossier +15.9% to $47.6m: Margaritaville $11.9m (+17.8%), Horseshoe $11.1m (+17.2%), Live! $10.5m (+31.3%).
  • Baton Rouge +10.7% to $22.3m as Bally's moved ashore ($4.9m, +607%), masking L'Auberge Baton Rouge (-6.9%) and Queen (-16.4%); Evangeline Downs $5.5m (-2.8%).

Quarterly run-rate

Jefferies reads Q3 statewide GGR at +3.5% against an +8.3% prior-year comp, with Caesars the only operator sustaining momentum.

  • Racetracks were the laggards in September.
  • The Q3 +3.5% is the better run-rate than September's +11.8%.

Annual scale FY2024-25

Riverboat AGR of $1,811.0m (+4.6%) generated over $389m state fees; video poker's $757.7m is the second-largest vertical, 70% larger than mobile sports.

  • Riverboats +$79.7m; Kenner/Harvey/Amelia $298.3m (+$58.4m), Baton Rouge $275.2m (+$12.5m); $270m landside facility opened February 2025.
  • Caesars New Orleans $270.1m (+$29.6m, +12.3%), paid $65.0m under operating contract, ~2,430 employees.
  • Racetrack slots $317.5m, essentially flat, $48m state fees.
  • Video poker: 12,199 devices at 1,386 locations June 2025, 12,482 at 1,376 by October 2025; net device revenue +1.1%, $229.6m franchise fees (30% blended; Type 4 22.5%).
  • Tribal casinos (Coushatta, Paragon, Cypress Bayou, Jena Choctaw Pines) report nothing under IGRA and are excluded.

Market structure

Land-based market is closed to new entrants via statutory licence caps, but it is where capital is being rewarded.

  • Licences change hands only via M&A; incumbents are Bally's, Boyd, Caesars, Penn, Churchill Downs, Cordish/Live!, Golden Nugget/Fertitta.
  • 2018 landside law continues to pull revenue up as boats relocate ashore; riverboats employed ~9,700 people in Q3 2025.
  • Technology openings: cashless and cage integration, retail sportsbook kiosks, platform supply to licensees with unfilled mobile skins.
  • For B2C sportsbooks, casinos are the gatekeepers and will price a second skin accordingly.

Cautions

September's +11.8% is flattered by easy comps, and the state's ~$730m dependence on land-based fees explains policy protecting incumbents.

  • Bally's Baton Rouge was mid-transition a year ago; hurricane/weather effects in 2025 not disclosed in sources read.
  • FY25 fees ~$730m from riverboats, Caesars, racinos and video poker versus $70m from sports.
  • Every 2025–26 policy move has protected land-based incumbents while tightening the screws on online verticals.

6Deep-dive: Licensing, tax & enforcement

Casino-anchored licensing (20 licences, $350k platform permits), a 21.5% mobile tax on a promo-deductible base, and a 2026 session that criminalised sweepstakes while shelving a prop-bet ban for fiscal reasons.

Regulatory framework

The five-member LGCB, with a $1.0m FY25 budget, delegates enforcement to State Police; sports wagering is legal in 55 of 64 parishes.

  • Enforcement, audit and revenue reporting delegated to Louisiana State Police Gaming Enforcement Division; budget funded from riverboat, sports-wagering and pari-mutuel enforcement funds.
  • Statutes include the Riverboat Act, Economic Development and Gaming Corporation Act, Video Draw Poker law, Pari-mutuel Act, Fantasy Sports Contests Act and Sports Wagering Act (2021).
  • Nine parishes that declined in November 2020 are geofenced out, slightly reducing addressable population versus 4.6m residents.

Licence structure

Up to 20 casino-anchored sports wagering licences and cheap permits ($100,000 plus $250,000) make the casino deal the binding constraint.

  • Only riverboat, slots-at-the-track and land-based casino licensees were eligible initially; each may run its own book or contract a platform provider.
  • Platform provider permit: $100,000 application fee plus $250,000 for five years, renewable; service provider permit $10,000 plus $12,500 for five years.
  • Entry cost a fraction of New York's or Pennsylvania's, but ten platforms against 20 licences means skins are concentrated among largest brands.
  • House rules and new wager types must be submitted to the Sports Wagering Catalog (version 78, 2 Sep 2026).

Tax rates and dedications

Mobile pays 21.5% on promo-deductible net proceeds under Act 298; new SPORT Fund dedications create a durable constituency for the tax.

  • Act 298 of 2025 (HB 639) signed June 2025, effective 1 Aug 2025; first draft proposed 32.5%. Retail pays 10%; DFS 8% of net revenue.
  • Riverboats pay 21.5% state fee plus admission fees; racinos ~15% of slot AGR in FY25; Caesars New Orleans paid $65.0m; video poker 22.5% for Type 4 [non-Type-4 rates not verified].
  • Statutory loss carry-forward means monthly tax can deviate from headline rate.
  • 25% of mobile tax to SPORT Fund for eleven NCAA Division I programmes (roughly $1.7–2m each), 3% to Postsecondary Inclusive Education Fund, plus legacy 25% early-childhood, 10% local, 3% problem gambling.

2026 enforcement session

HB 883 and HB 53 created the toughest enforcement regime of any US state, criminalising sweepstakes with fines up to $100,000 and racketeering exposure.

  • HB 883 (House 98-0, Senate 35-0) covers dual-currency games, extends liability to platform and financial-transaction providers, raises fines from $20,000 to $100,000, gives AG injunctive powers.
  • HB 53 (House 86-11, Senate 27-9) adds gambling by computer and sweepstakes devices to racketeering: up to 50 years' hard labour, $1m fines, five-year minimum above $10,000.
  • Both signed May 2026, effective 1 Aug 2026, after Governor Landry vetoed SB 181 in 2025; LGCB then issued 40+ cease-and-desist letters.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

A conventional US payments stack with no regulator data, a modest RG framework (3% of sports tax, statewide self-exclusion, 21+, parish geofencing) and advertising rules that lean on the Board's catalog and wager approvals rather than hard caps.

Payments

Louisiana publishes no payments data; HB 883 extends liability to financial-transaction providers, eliminating grey-market processing opportunity.

  • Licensed books run the standard US stack: ACH, cards, PayPal/Venmo-type wallets, Apple Pay, PayNearMe vouchers and cash at the casino cage.
  • No credit-card deposit ban legislated [unverified].
  • From 1 Aug 2026 acquirers and processors will tighten merchant-category screening; raises compliance bar for B2B payments products.

Responsible gambling

RG framework is statutory but thin: 21+, parish geofencing, statewide self-exclusion and 3% of sports tax (roughly $3.3m a year).

  • Geolocation enforces wagering within the 55 opted-in parishes; self-exclusion applies across riverboats, Caesars, racinos, video poker and sportsbooks.
  • 3% of FY26 sports tax of $109.6m funds problem-gambling programmes via Office of Behavioral Health and 1-877-770-STOP helpline.
  • No statutory deposit limits, affordability checks or mandatory loss limits; operators must offer self-set limits and cooling-off tools.
  • Board prohibited prop wagers on individual college athletes in 2024 [widely reported; not re-verified], significant in an LSU-dominated market.

Advertising and outlook

Advertising rules rely on licence conditions with no bonus cap; tighter in-play, prop and promo rules are likely within two years.

  • No marketing to under-21s or on campuses, no 'risk-free' framing, mandatory RG messaging; no whistle-to-whistle restriction or affiliate licensing of note.
  • Promo deduction makes bonusing tax-efficient, visible in the January–April 2026 surge to $25.6m of deductions.
  • SB 354 debate framed around in-play micro-betting harm to college-age bettors; sponsor returning in 2027 with AG racketeering tool available.
  • Operators should assume tighter in-play and prop rules and possibly a cap on promo deductions.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Mobile handle is still growing in a mature market — +4.6% YoY in September, +9.8% in fiscal Q1 and 95% of all stakes — on a $4.3bn trailing-twelve-month base that yields ~$530m of net proceeds; the addressable online pool is real even if growth is now mid-single-digit. structural
  • Parlay-led economics: parlays delivered $24.9m (63%) of September mobile win and 71% of FY26 mobile net proceeds; with the prop/micro-bet ban (SB 354) withdrawn, the high-margin product set stays intact through at least the 2026-27 season. mixed
  • Tax base relief is built into the statute: promotional wagers are deductible and operator losses carry forward, so the 21.5% headline rate applies to a net base — FY26 promo deductions of $34.9m were worth ~$7.5m of tax at the mobile rate. structural
  • Land-based momentum: casino GGR +11.8% in September (16 casinos +14%), with landside relocations (Bally's Baton Rouge +607%, Shreveport/Bossier +15.9%) and Caesars New Orleans (+30.4%) proving that capex is being rewarded in a protected, capped licence environment. mixed
  • The sweepstakes ban (HB 883/HB 53, effective 1 Aug 2026) removes an untaxed online-casino substitute that had been competing with licensed sportsbooks for the same wallets; licensed operators are the direct beneficiaries of the AG's new enforcement toolkit. structural
  • Entry costs for suppliers are low: a platform provider permit is $350k per five years and a service provider permit $22.5k, so a B2B route (platform, risk, payments, kiosks, cashless) into the 20 casino licensees and ~12,500-device video poker estate is cheap to secure relative to the state's ~$3.6bn gaming revenue. structural
  • Enforcement credibility is high and public: the LGCB's 40+ cease-and-desist letters and the annual report's explicit claim of having stopped multiple illegal operators give licensed participants a cleaner competitive field than most US states. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • Tax risk is live, not theoretical: the mobile rate rose 43% (15%→21.5%) in August 2025, the opening bid was 32.5%, and 28% of the take is now earmarked to college athletics and inclusive education — constituencies that will lobby for more, not less; a promo-deduction cap is the obvious next lever. structural
  • Hold compression hit the P&L: September mobile hold of 10.3% (vs 12.0%) turned +4.6% handle growth into -10.6% net proceeds; monthly hold has ranged 10.3–15.6% over the past year, so quarterly earnings from Louisiana are results-driven and volatile. one-off
  • Retail is in structural collapse — handle -39% in September, -36% in fiscal Q1, -13% in FY26 — leaving casino-floor books as a compliance cost that anchors the licence rather than a profit centre. structural
  • Market access is gated: 20 licences held exclusively by casinos/racinos, ten platforms already listed, no operator-level data published, nine parishes geofenced out, and no iGaming or iLottery — a new B2C entrant must buy a second skin from an incumbent and take share from FanDuel/DraftKings in a market growing ~2% on calendar handle. structural
  • Product restriction is a 2027 risk: SB 354's prop and micro-bet ban died only on a >$20m fiscal note, and the sponsor has committed to refile; with parlays and 'other' wagers carrying ~79% of mobile win, even a partial in-play restriction would cut net proceeds and tax materially. watch
  • Racketeering exposure for anything adjacent: HB 53 makes 'gambling by computer' a RICO predicate (up to 50 years, $1m) and HB 883 reaches platform and payment providers; prediction-market and sweepstakes adjacencies carry criminal, not just civil, risk in Louisiana from 1 Aug 2026. governance
  • Data gaps limit diligence: the regulator reports no operator shares, no actives/ARPU, no payments data, and tribal casino revenue is undisclosed; September video poker and lottery figures were not available for this brief, so the total-market picture rests on FY25 annual data. watch

9Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. A market has no guidance, so the bridge tracks how the rule-set has moved from the FY25 baseline (15% mobile tax, sweepstakes in a grey zone, props unrestricted, no online casino) to the regime in force for FY27. The tax step-up came from the legislature's 2025 budget-gap search and the college-athletics lobby; the enforcement step-up came from the LGCB and the Attorney General rather than new operators; and the one measure that would have hit the parlay-led mobile model — SB 354 — was stopped by the Legislative Fiscal Office's arithmetic, not by principle. Net effect for the September data: the same handle generates ~43% more tax per dollar of net proceeds than two years ago, while the grey-market competitors that were absorbing wallet share are being criminalised out of the state.

Independent view. Our independent view is that Louisiana is a mature, mobile-only sportsbook market growing 2–5% on handle with a land-based base growing faster for the next two to three quarters as landside relocations annualise. For calendar 2026 we model (estimates) handle of $4.25–4.35bn, net proceeds of $500–525m (assuming Q4 hold reverts to 12–13% against Q4-25's exceptional $176m) and sports tax of ~$105–110m; for FY27 we expect handle +3–5%, net proceeds flat to +5% and mobile share above 95%, with retail handle falling a further 25–35%. Trailing hold should normalise to ~12%, and parlay share will stay above 60% of mobile win unless product rules change. On rules, we see no change to the 21.5% rate before the FY28 budget cycle, but a better-than-even chance of a promo-deduction cap or an in-play/prop restriction being filed in the 2027 regular session; the fiscal note on SB 354 shows the state values the product at >$20m of General Fund a year, which is also the price at which it would trade it away. Enforcement will intensify rather than relax — the AG now has racketeering tools and payment-processor liability — and we expect the first HB 883/HB 53 actions, and probably a Louisiana move against sports event contracts, before mid-2027. No iGaming before 2028 at the earliest: the casino and video poker constituencies (~$730m of state fees) have no incentive to cannibalise themselves, and the legislature's 2025–26 record is one of protecting them. For B2Tech, the implication is that Louisiana is a B2B and land-based supply opportunity, not a B2C entry market: platform, risk, kiosk, cashless and compliance-payments products sold to the 20 licensees and the video poker route, with no exposure to any grey-market adjacency.
