## 3. Segment deep-dive — Marketing Services (73% of revenue) — owned brands, Grandstand Partners, Rollcard

*Revenue stabilised at -1% YoY (+11% QoQ) on North American partner, iGaming and prediction-market growth, but the SEO-to-partner mix shift has cut gross margin 1,230bps YoY to 79% and international owned brands are in free-fall.*

### Revenue and mix

**Marketing revenue stabilised at $29.5m, down 1% YoY but up 11% QoQ from $26.5m, versus -10% YoY in Q2.**

- Performance marketing (CPA, revenue share, hybrid, ticketing) rose 7% to $25.6m, 64% of group revenue; advertising and other fell 33% to $3.9m.
- Casino declined 11% to $20.5m (51% of group vs 59%); sports grew 20% to $17.9m (45%); 'other' more than doubled to $1.8m.
- Non-SEO channels roughly two-thirds of marketing revenue, SEO roughly one-third; recurring revenue ~50% of group disclosed in Q2, not updated.
- North American growth led by Grandstand Partners, iGaming, prediction markets and RotoWire cross-selling; NDCs up QoQ and YoY but no count disclosed.
- Marketing contribution margin improved versus Q2, without quantification.

### Cost of sales and margin

**Cost of sales rose 148% YoY and 42% QoQ to $8.4m, cutting group gross margin to 79.0% from 91.3% in Q3-25.**

- Cost of sales is 21% of group revenue against 9% a year ago; CFO conceded it landed roughly $1m above internal forecast on stronger partner revenue.
- Gross margin was 84.3% in Q2.
- Sales and marketing expense fell 13% YoY to $13.9m, 34% of revenue (41% in Q3-25, 43% in Q2-26), on lower people costs after May restructuring.
- We read this as a permanent re-basing of gross margin into the high-70s/low-80s rather than a transition cost.
- The bull case has to rest on opex leverage and B2B data mix, not on gross-margin recovery.

> “have a different cost structure versus SEO” — CEO

### Demand: US vs international

**US demand is 'really positive' despite sportsbook pullback, while international faces SEO erosion, lower casino LTVs and unstable legacy revenue share.**

- US online sportsbooks are 'pulling back a little bit' on marketing through NFL season amid prediction-market uncertainty; prediction markets and iGaming largely fill the gap.
- Internationally, SEO erosion is multiplied by lower casino player lifetime values after tax increases, also hitting legacy revenue-share arrangements.
- Macquarie flagged the UK sports-betting duty increase due in 2027; management says sports is 'a little more stable' and casino 'gets hit a little harder'.
- CEO said international net new business is still a positive contribution and not loss-making; CFO expects improved international owned-brand contribution margins in 2027.
- Management is not expecting SEO growth and guided marketing revenue 'more or less flat' from 2026 to 2027.

> “not been as stable as we've historically seen” — Management

### Restructuring and Rollcard

**A second restructuring targets unquantified 2027 cost savings, while Rollcard is an immaterial consumer-fintech bet until mid-2027.**

- Plan streamlines brand-portfolio management toward multi-channel and retention strategies across fewer brands, repositioning resources primarily within marketing.
- Rollcard, the high-roller debit card launched in August, uses cash-back on spend rather than upfront bonus; acquisition via owned brands and Partners network.
- Deposits to operators growing ~100% month-on-month; monthly interchange ~$411 per customer, heavily skewed to high-rollers.
- 'More meaningful' revenue expected from mid-2027; immaterial to FY26/27 and we would want customer counts before ascribing value.

### Jackpot variance

**A single tagged-player jackpot cut October revenue share by ~$0.5m, already embedded in the Q4 guide.**

- Revenue-share income carries operator-side variance.
- The impact is already embedded in the Q4 guide.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Data Services (27% of revenue) — OpticOdds (enterprise), OddsJam & RotoWire (consumer)

*Enterprise data (OpticOdds) is compounding at >50% with expanding deal sizes and a growing market-maker channel, but total data revenue still fell 4% QoQ because OddsJam consumer subscriptions are shrinking faster than the headline suggests.*

### Revenue and OpticOdds

**Data revenue rose 17% YoY to $10.8m, with OpticOdds enterprise growing 'well above 50%' and deal sizes up 25% sequentially.**

- Growth accelerated from +12% in Q2; data is 27% of group revenue from 24%; nine-month revenue grew 13% to $33.2m.
- Enterprise sports-data services represented a 'large majority' of data revenue.
- Total customer count grew 11% sequentially; international partners ~41% of new deals; market makers ~30% of new OpticOdds deals but smaller share of revenue.
- In the 2027 framing, essentially all group growth is expected to come from data.

> “a long runway and multiple opportunities for product expansion” — Management

### Consumer subscription decline

**Three consecutive quarters of flat-to-down data revenue imply consumer subscriptions are declining at a rate well into double digits.**

- Data revenue was ~$12.0m in Q4-25 (26% of $46.2m, +29% QoQ), $11.2m in Q1-26, $11.2m in Q2-26 and $10.8m in Q3-26.
- OddsJam faces 'a lot of increased competition from cheaper but worse alternatives' and is repositioned upmarket; subscribers down YoY while ARPU up 85%.
- RotoWire subscribers up 17% YoY but shifting to annual plans, which depressed Q3 and should lower churn in 2027.
- Enterprise/consumer split, subscriber counts and ARPU are undisclosed; this is the single most useful disclosure they could add.

### Strategy and risks

**OpticOdds justifies the 'intelligence layer' positioning and is most likely to attract strategic interest, but carries young-vendor risks.**

- Customer set spans traditional sportsbooks and market makers; deal-size expansion suggests pricing power within the installed base.
- Risks: undisclosed customer concentration, prediction-market regulatory overhang on a channel ~30% of new bookings, and incumbents (Sportradar, Genius) competing on pricing tools.
- Layering prediction-market monetisation onto OddsJam users is sensible but defensive, arresting a declining base rather than a growth story.

### Modelling view

**We would model data services at mid-teens growth for FY27 with mix moving further toward enterprise.**

- Any quarter where total data revenue falls sequentially again signals consumer decay is outrunning OpticOdds.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Geographic — North America vs International (UK&I, Other Europe, Rest of World)

*North America +54% to $30.4m (76% of group) on partners, iGaming, prediction markets and OpticOdds; international -49% to $9.8m as SEO, casino LTVs and legacy revenue share all erode at once.*

### North America

**North America rose 54% YoY and 16% QoQ to $30.4m, 76% of group revenue, the one unambiguously good operating data point.**

- Share of group up from 51% a year ago and 70% in Q2; nine-month revenue grew 39% to $83.3m.
- Growth from Grandstand Partners, iGaming and prediction-market referrals, RotoWire cross-selling and OpticOdds, with NDCs up on both comparisons.
- Partner-led portion carries cost of sales SEO never did, so growth is margin-dilutive at the gross level.
- Q3-25 base already included a full quarter of OddsJam/OpticOdds (acquired January 2025), so growth is largely organic.
- Some North American SEO assets reported holding up 'pretty well', unlike the international portfolio.

### International decline

**International revenue fell 49% YoY and 14% QoQ to $9.8m, 24% of group against 49% a year ago.**

- UK & Ireland -45% YoY, -17% QoQ to $5.3m (13% vs 25%); Other Europe -51% YoY, -5% QoQ to $3.4m (8% vs 18%).

- Drivers: SEO volatility and AI-search disruption, lower casino LTVs after UK remote-gaming tax step-up, 2027 sports duty rise, and erosion of legacy revenue share.
- Macquarie's Chad Beynon asked whether UK&I deceleration was SEO or tax-driven; the answer was effectively both, multiplied together.

### Strategy and modelling

**Management will manage international brands 'for what they are now' and we assume 30–40% YoY declines through H1-27.**

- 'Multiple ways to win' in North America versus 'fewer ways we monetise those audiences' internationally.
- Not exiting: net new international business is a positive contribution and the legacy revenue-share annuity remains cash-generative as it decays.
- Plan: consolidate brand portfolio, shrink team sizes, lift contribution margins in 2027 rather than invest for growth.
- North America must carry the entire top line; blended gross margin keeps drifting toward North American partner mix.

## 6. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Sequential inflection delivered as promised: revenue +7% QoQ to $40.3m, adj. EBITDA +31% QoQ to $10.0m, margin +460bps to 25%, with ~$3.25m of May-restructuring savings realised in the quarter against a ~$13m annualised target; opex ex-FV -5% YoY and S&M down to 34% of revenue from 41%. _(structural)_
- OpticOdds enterprise data growing >50% YoY with average deal size +25% QoQ, customer count +11% QoQ, 41% of new deals international and ~30% from prediction-market makers — a genuine B2B compounding asset inside a shrinking affiliate. _(structural)_
- North America +54% YoY to $30.4m (76% of group), largely organic on a full-period OddsJam base; NDCs up QoQ and YoY; iGaming, prediction markets and Partners offsetting OSB operator budget cuts. _(structural)_
- Cash conversion intact: adj. FCF $8.0m at 80% of adj. EBITDA (9M $21.5m, 81%); ~$30m FY26 adj. FCF reiterated — roughly twice the post-print market capitalisation. _(structural)_
- Vertical mix moving toward sports (+20% YoY, 45% of revenue) and away from casino (-11%), which management says is more resilient to tax changes; performance marketing +7% YoY. _(mixed)_
- IFRS profitability restored — operating profit $3.8m and net income $1.0m vs losses a year ago — though largely because the $7.5m Q3-25 contingent-consideration FV charge did not recur. _(one-off)_
- Rollcard early KPIs (deposits +100% MoM, ~$411 monthly interchange per customer) suggest the high-roller fintech niche has product-market fit, with a low-cost, performance-based acquisition model. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- FY26 guidance cut to ~$157m / ~$37m from $165–170m / $45–50m (-6% / -22% vs midpoints) three months after being reiterated; implied Q4-26 revenue ~$38.5m is -17% YoY and -4% QoQ in the seasonally strongest quarter, and implied Q4 adj. EBITDA ~$10.3m is -33% YoY. Second consecutive November guidance cut (FY25 was also adjusted in Nov-25). _(structural)_
- International revenue -49% YoY / -14% QoQ to $9.8m (UK&I -45%, Other Europe -51%, RoW -57%), now 24% of group vs 49%; SEO/AI-search disruption compounded by UK tax-driven casino LTV compression that is also eroding legacy revenue share. Management expects no SEO growth and flat marketing revenue in 2027. _(structural)_
- Gross margin -1,230bps YoY to 79% (-530bps QoQ) as cost of sales rose 148% to $8.4m, ~$1m above internal forecast; the partner/paid traffic model is structurally lower-margin than SEO and adj. EBITDA margin (25%) is unlikely to return to the mid-30s of FY25. _(structural)_
- Data revenue fell 4% QoQ to $10.8m and is below the ~$12m Q4-25 level despite OpticOdds >50% — OddsJam consumer subscriptions are shrinking (subs down YoY, ARPU +85%), and the enterprise/consumer split is not disclosed. _(watch)_
- Balance sheet is the binding constraint: $120.1m borrowings (due 2028) plus $27.0m OddsJam deferred consideration (April 2027) against $8.1m cash; net debt 2.7x LTM adj. EBITDA ex-deferred, 3.3x including it, rising to ~3.0x/3.8x on FY26E; finance expense +52% YoY to $3.8m. Stock settlement of the deferred payment at ~$0.44 would imply ~170% dilution — hence the Jefferies refinancing mandate. _(governance)_

- US OSB operators are cutting marketing spend through the NFL season; advertising & other revenue -33% YoY; a $0.5m October jackpot hit to revenue share is already in the Q4 guide. _(mixed)_

## 7. Forecast & Outlook

Guidance moved from FY26 revenue of $165–170m and adj. EBITDA of $45–50m (set March 2026, reiterated in May and August) to approximately $157m and $37m. The ~$10.5m midpoint revenue cut flows through almost dollar-for-dollar to EBITDA, which tells you the shortfall is in the highest-margin lines: international owned-brand affiliate revenue (SEO and casino revenue share, which carry negligible cost of sales) and OddsJam consumer subscriptions. The B2B lines — OpticOdds enterprise data and Grandstand Partners — are described as on plan, but they arrive at ~79% gross margin rather than ~91%. The other moving parts are unchanged: ~$6.5m of H2-26 fixed-cost savings from the May restructuring (~$13m annualised), initial Rollcard revenue and costs for Aug–Dec, and an average EUR/USD of 1.15 (from 1.16). Management did not give Q4 guidance explicitly, but 9M actuals imply Q4-26 revenue of ~$38.5m (-17% YoY vs the record $46.2m Q4-25; -4% QoQ) and adj. EBITDA of ~$10.3m (~27% margin; -33% YoY vs $15.5m). ~$30m of FY26 adj. FCF was reiterated at 70–80% conversion.

- **FY26 revenue guide:** $165–170m → ~$157m (-$10.5m vs midpoint (-6%); -5% vs FY25 $165.4m)
- **FY26 adj. EBITDA guide:** $45–50m → ~$37m (-$10.5m vs midpoint (-22%); -36% vs FY25 $58.1m; margin 28%→24%)
- **Implied Q4-26 (our calc from 9M):** Rev ~$38.5m / adj. EBITDA ~$10.3m (~27%) (-17% / -33% YoY; -4% / +3% QoQ)
- **FY26 adj. FCF:** ~$30m (reiterated) (Unchanged; 70–80% conversion; ~2x post-print market cap)

**Q4-26: achievable but with downside skew.** The implied ~$38.5m already embeds the $0.5m October jackpot hit and the OSB spend pullback; the risk is that international (-14% QoQ in Q3) decays faster than North American partners and prediction markets can offset, and that cost of sales again overshoots. Adj. EBITDA of ~$10.3m requires a full quarter of restructuring run-rate (~$3.25m/qtr) and S&M held near 34% of revenue — plausible. We would pencil in Q4 revenue of $37.5–39.0m and adj. EBITDA of $9.5–10.5m (estimate). **FY27 (our estimate, not guidance):** marketing flat at ~$112–115m per management, data +15–18% to ~$50–52m, giving revenue of ~$160–167m (+2–6%); gross margin drifting to ~78–80%; a full year of the ~$13m fixed-cost programme (~$6.5m incremental vs FY26) plus an unquantified second marketing restructuring; adj. EBITDA ~$42–47m (26–28% margin) and adj. FCF ~$30–35m. That is growth off a depressed base, not a recovery to FY25's $58m. **Leverage direction:** net debt/adj. EBITDA peaks around 3.0x (3.8x incl. deferred) at FY26 and should fall toward ~2.3–2.5x by end-27 if FCF is applied to debt — but the $27m April-27 deferred payment absorbs nearly a year of free cash flow, which is why the refinancing is non-optional. **Valuation framing:** at ~$0.44 the market cap is ~$16m and EV ~$155m, i.e. ~4.2x FY26E / ~3.5x FY27E adj. EBITDA and ~0.5x FY26 adj. FCF on the equity — the equity trades as a stub behind the credit. Plausible review outcomes range from (i) a term-out of the Wells Fargo facility plus a cash/renegotiated settlement of the deferred consideration (equity survives as a levered option on OpticOdds), to (ii) a sale of the group or of OpticOdds alone to a data or affiliate consolidator, to (iii) a dilutive stock settlement. **Swing factors:** terms and timing of the refinancing; whether international stabilises below $9m/qtr; Q4 gross margin; prediction-market regulation hitting the market-maker channel; and Nasdaq $1 minimum-bid compliance with the stock at ~$0.44.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Refinancing terms (coupon, maturity, covenants) and how the $27m April-27 OddsJam deferred consideration is settled — cash, stock, or renegotiated; any stock component at sub-$1 prices is extremely dilutive.
- Q4-26 actuals vs the implied ~$38.5m revenue / ~$10.3m adj. EBITDA, and specifically whether gross margin holds ~79% or slips further as partner mix rises.
- International run-rate: does the -14% QoQ decline in Q3 (to $9.8m) moderate, and what does the UK 2027 sports-betting duty increase do to revenue share?
- Sequential data revenue — a second QoQ decline would confirm OddsJam consumer decay is outrunning OpticOdds; push for disclosure of the enterprise/consumer split, customer concentration and subscriber counts.
- US OSB operator marketing budgets into Q1-27 and the regulatory trajectory of prediction markets (CFTC/state actions), which affect both NDC referrals and ~30% of new OpticOdds bookings.
- Nasdaq minimum-bid-price ($1.00) compliance — the shares have been below $1 since well before the print; a deficiency notice or reverse split would be a governance event.
- Nature and size of the new 'litigation costs' adjusting item ($0.8m in Q3) and any further restructuring charges from the 2027 brand-portfolio consolidation.
- Rollcard customer counts, interchange economics and any credit/fraud losses as it scales toward 'meaningful' revenue in mid-2027.
