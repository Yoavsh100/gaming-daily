# Latin America () — 2030 FORECAST Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $bn / $m). Markets reporting locally (Brazil BRL, Colombia COP, Chile CLP) shown in local currency with ~USD alongside. · Results released 23 Oct 2026 (lead article; GVR poker databook published 13 May 2025; H2 regional update 30 Apr 2026; GVR LatAm online databook 28 Apr 2026)**

> Latin America's 2030 story has split in two. The forecast houses still print a region of $51bn total GGR by 2030 (H2 Gambling Capital, Apr 2026) and $12.3bn of regulated online GGR by 2028 (Vixio), with a $467.6m online-poker niche (GVR) — but every one of those numbers pre-dates Brazil's MP 1.394/2026, which extinguished all 85 federal fixed-odds authorisations from 6 Oct 2026 and removed roughly half of the region's regulated online GGR (R$36.9bn / ~$7.2bn in 2025) at a stroke. Until Congress and the courts settle Brazil's fate, the investable regulated pool is Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Argentina's provinces and (eventually) Chile — a ~$5–7bn market with rising tax takes — while Brazilian demand migrates offshore.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Two-speed region.** H2 Gambling Capital's 30 Apr 2026 update (IMF-linked, constant-FX, nominal) forecasts Latin America & Caribbean total gambling GGR — all channels, onshore plus offshore — rising from $29bn in 2024 to $51bn in 2030 (~9.9% CAGR), making LatAm and Africa the fastest-growing regions globally, 'driven primarily by online channel expansion'. Global GGR crosses $1,031bn in 2030 with online at $530bn (51%).
- **Regulated online was the engine.** Vixio's Latin America Outlook (Dec 2024) projected regulated online GGR growing five-fold from $2.5bn (2024) to $12.3bn (2028), with Brazil $2.9bn in 2025 (47% of the region) rising to $6.3bn (>50%) by 2028; Mexico $1.3bn→>$2bn, Colombia >$1bn, Peru $436m→>$850m. Vixio's Americas total (US, Canada, LatAm) goes from $22.3bn (2023) to $56.3bn (2028), 20.3% CAGR.
- **GVR sizes LatAm online at $7.24bn in 2025** (8.3% of global online), growing 10.7% CAGR 2026–33 to $16.2bn — implying ~$12.0bn in 2030 (our arithmetic). Sports betting is both the largest and fastest-growing product; Brazil the highest-CAGR country. Note the definitional gap: Brazil's regulated GGR alone (R$36.9bn, ~$7.2bn) roughly equals GVR's entire regional figure.
- **Brazil beat every forecast — then banned itself.** SPA-MF reported regulated fixed-odds GGR of R$36.9bn (~$7.2bn) in the first year of regulation (2025), >2x Vixio's $2.9bn forecast; H1 2026 GGR was R$20.07bn (~$3.9bn), +15.3% YoY (~R$3.3bn/month), with 15.1m unique CPFs betting in Q1 2026 (GGR R$7.6bn, ~$1.5bn).
- **MP 1.394/2026 (25 Sep 2026) is the single largest swing factor for 2030.** The provisional measure ends the exploitation, offer, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting — sports and online games, physical and virtual — including state/DF authorisations. New deposits banned from 25 Sep; sites and apps offline from 6 Oct; balances returned via banks 9–14 Oct, then Caixa. No compensation for lost profits, investment or the R$30m (~$5.9m) outorgas — 85 outorgas totalling R$2.55bn (~$0.5bn).
- **Enforcement is being hard-wired.** Fines up to R$2bn (~$0.39bn), domain blocking, app removal, payment-rail restriction and account blocking; a Casa Civil-led inter-ministerial committee; a companion bill creating five crimes (4–6 years for operating fixed-odds betting; 2–4 years for advertising, data-driven acquisition, financial facilitation or hosting). Anatel had already blocked 15,463 sites (Oct 2024–Jun 2025) and ~25,000 by end-2025; 37 fintechs were notified in Jul 2026 for moving illegal-bet money.
- **Brazil's pre-ban tax regime** was 12% of GGR (Law 14.790/2023) plus ordinary corporate taxes; the Jun 2025 MP 1.303 to lift the rate to 18% collapsed in Oct 2025 after a retroactive-tax rewrite. Treasury now loses ~R$800m (~$157m) per month of bet-related revenue, per press estimates — a fiscal incentive to re-regulate at a higher rate rather than prohibit indefinitely.
- **Political direction: prohibition framed as public health.** Government cites +140% growth in SUS gambling-related care (2018–25), a projected +104% by 2028 and an IEPS study putting the annual social cost at R$38.8bn (~$7.6bn), R$30.6bn of it health-related. Congress has 120 days to vote the MP; trade-press headlines report Lula's re-election in Oct 2026, so a reversal by a new administration is off the table — the courts (STF) and Congress are the only routes back [post-25 Sep status unverified in this session].
- **Grey market absorbs the demand.** Brazil's Central Bank estimated up to R$30bn (~$5.9bn) wagered per month in Q1 2025 across licensed and unlicensed sites; Blask tracked 523 Brazil-facing brands in Aug 2026. H2 counts offshore GGR in its totals, so the $51bn regional number is less affected than the onshore share — which we expect to fall from ~45% globally-equivalent levels to well below 40% in LatAm.
- **Colombia — mature, over-taxed.** Coljuegos reported 14 authorised online operators at end-2025 (down from high-teens); exploitation rights 15% of GGR (17% if RTP <83%) [not re-verified], plus a 19% VAT on online gambling deposits imposed Feb 2025 under an economic-emergency decree; Fecoljuegos says online income fell 32%, while Coljuegos credits online VAT with ~COP 900bn (~$239m) for the Catatumbo region by Nov 2025. Vixio still sees >$1bn GGR by 2028.
- **Peru — the cleanest licensing template.** Law 31557 (Aug 2022, amended by Law 31806) makes MINCETUR the licensing, supervisory and sanctioning authority; tax is 12% on net income (SUNAT); 145 applications became 118 licences for 63 operators (Dec 2024) and >60 authorised companies by Apr 2026; crypto payments are prohibited. Vixio: $436m (2025) → >$850m (2028).
- **Mexico — large but legally fragile.** Vixio: $1.3bn (2024) → >$2bn (2028). SEGOB permits under the 1947 Federal Gaming Law; disputes over land-based permit renewals and online restrictions persist; a federal IEPS increase on gaming for 2026 was reported [unverified in this session].
- **Argentina & Chile — patchwork and pending.** Argentina licenses online province-by-province (CABA, Buenos Aires Province and ~10 others) with a federal online-betting tax layered on provincial GGR levies [rates n/a]; Vixio flags 'potential advertising bans'. Chile has no licensing regime: a Jul 2026 study puts 2025 online GGR above $460m, with the licensing bill in the Senate since Dec 2023 [status unverified].
- **Online poker — the lead release.** GVR (research commissioned by Ignition Casino, an offshore brand) sizes LatAm online poker at $261.6m (2024), 6.8% of global, rising to $467.6m by 2030 (10.2% CAGR); desktop is the largest and fastest-growing device segment. Global poker goes $3.9bn → $6.9bn. The sponsor is itself a tell: LatAm poker liquidity sits offshore.
- **Payments are the regulatory lever.** Pix is 'part of the product' in Brazil (Blask) and the MP explicitly targets payment rails; Peru's law bars cryptocurrency; Colombia taxes the deposit, not the GGR. Any LatAm stack must support instant-payment rails plus regulator-grade transaction reporting (Brazil's SIGAP precedent).
- **Advertising and RG tightening everywhere.** Brazil: PL 2.985/2023 (Senate-approved 28 May 2025) would restrict ad timing and sports-broadcast exposure — now overtaken by a blanket advertising/sponsorship ban under the MP, extending to placements abroad; a federal self-exclusion platform exists; health services will actively contact bettors. Peru mandates deposit limits and self-exclusion; Argentina's ad-restriction bill is pending.
- **Forecast dispersion is wide — use the right definition.** H2 ($51bn total 2030), Vixio ($12.3bn regulated online 2028), GVR ($7.24bn online 2025), IMGL (>$37bn total 2025; $10–12bn online 2028) and Bradesco BBI ($17bn Brazil 2025, which BNLData says overstates SPA data 2.5x) measure different perimeters. We anchor on H2 for total GGR and Vixio/SPA for regulated online.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Do not underwrite Brazilian regulated revenue until the MP's fate is decided; prioritise Peru, Colombia (post-VAT economics), Mexico and Argentine provinces; model GGR-equivalent tax of 12–30%+ with upward drift; build for instant-payment rails and regulator reporting; keep B2B (platform/content/payments) optionality that survives jurisdictional shocks.
- **Our 2030 view (estimates).** Ban case: LatAm regulated online GGR ~$6–8bn in 2030 with total GGR near H2's $51bn but onshore share <40%. Reversal case (Brazil re-licensed by 2027 at ≥15–18% GGR tax): regulated online ~$17–20bn. The probability-weighted outcome sits well below the pre-ban consensus.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Regional outlook to 2030 — forecast houses, definitions and scenarios

*Every published 2030 number pre-dates Brazil's prohibition; the honest range for regulated online in 2030 is now ~$6–8bn (ban case) to ~$17–20bn (reversal case).*

### H2 total GGR series

**H2 Gambling Capital's 30 April 2026 update puts LatAm & Caribbean GGR at $29bn in 2024 and $51bn in 2030, roughly 9.9% compound.**

- Only regional series covering every channel and both onshore and offshore supply; folds in the IMF's April 2026 World Economic Outlook.
- Names LatAm and Africa as the two fastest-growing regions, with growth 'driven primarily by online channel expansion'.
- Forecast in local currency at constant FX, so $51bn does not absorb future BRL, ARS or COP devaluation; current-FX USD figures would be lower.
- Series is nominal; in high-inflation Argentina a material share of growth is price, not volume.

### Vixio regulated online

**Vixio's December 2024 outlook had regulated online rising from $2.5bn in 2024 to $12.3bn in 2028, but the region ran well ahead before the ban.**

- Brazil forecast at $2.9bn in 2025 (47% of region) and $6.3bn in 2028 (over half).
- Mexico $1.3bn (2024) rising above $2bn; Colombia above $1bn by 2028; Peru from $436m (2025) to more than $850m (2028).
- May 2025 update: combined US, Canada and LatAm regulated online GGR $56.3bn by 2028 (20.3% CAGR from $22.3bn in 2023), drawing level with Europe.
- Brazil's actual 2025 regulated GGR of R$36.9bn (~$7.2bn) was more than double Vixio's $2.9bn; regional 2025 total probably $10–11bn (our estimate), not ~$6bn.

### Other forecast references

**Grand View Research's $7.24bn 2025 figure cannot be reconciled with SPA-MF data and should be read as a lower-bound, revenue-to-operator definition.**

- GVR (28 April 2026): LatAm online $7.24bn in 2025, 8.3% of global, growing 10.7% a year from 2026 to $16.2bn in 2033, implying ~$12.0bn in 2030.
- GVR sees sports betting as largest and fastest-growing product and Brazil the highest-CAGR country; Brazil's regulated GGR alone roughly equals GVR's regional total.
- IMGL: combined LatAm gambling revenue above $37bn in 2025; online $10–12bn by 2028.
- Bradesco BBI's $17bn Brazil 2025 estimate overstates SPA-reported GGR by ~2.5x by counting grey-market flow, per BNLData.
- For planning we anchor total GGR on H2 and regulated online on regulators' own series plus Vixio.

### 2030 scenarios

**No model incorporates MP 1.394/2026, so 2030 regulated online is framed as ~$6–8bn (ban case) to ~$17–20bn (reversal case).**

- Brazil was roughly half of regulated LatAm online GGR and the majority of its forecast growth.
- Ban case: ex-Brazil regulated online compounds from ~$3.5–4bn 2025 base to roughly $6–8bn by 2030; Chile adds perhaps $0.3–0.5bn only if its bill passes by 2028.
- Ban case offshore Brazil GGR of $6–9bn in 2030 is plausible, given up to R$30bn (~$5.9bn) monthly wagering in Q1 2025 and 523 Brazil-facing brands in August 2026.
- Reversal case: Brazil re-licenses during 2027 at 15–18% of GGR plus ad limits; Brazil R$55–65bn (~$11–13bn constant FX) and region ~$17–20bn by 2030.
- We weight the ban case for 2027–28 but see partial re-opening as more probable by 2030, given fiscal loss of ~R$800m (~$157m) per month.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Brazil — from flagship to prohibition

*R$36.9bn (~$7.2bn) of regulated GGR in year one, +15.3% in H1 2026, then a presidential provisional measure that switched the market off on 6 Oct 2026 without compensation.*

### Framework and uptake

**Regulated GGR reached R$36.9bn (~$7.2bn) in 2025 and R$20.07bn (~$3.9bn) in H1 2026, up 15.3% year on year, exceeding every forecast.**

- Law 13.756/2018 and Law 14.790/2023: 12% levy on GGR, R$30m (~$5.9m) five-year fee for up to three brands, SPA-MF as regulator; live 1 January 2025.
- H1 2025 GGR R$17.4bn (~$3.4bn); H1 2026 averaged ~R$3.3bn (~$0.65bn) per month (Sigma reports R$20.28bn and +6.2% on a different baseline).
- Q1 2026: 15.1m unique CPFs bet, generating R$7.6bn (~$1.5bn), roughly R$168 (~$33) of GGR per active bettor per quarter.
- Authorised companies grew from 82 (March 2026) to 85 outorgas by September, roughly 186–188 brands; fees paid totalled R$2.55bn (~$0.5bn).

### Tax and enforcement

**Tax was in flux before the ban, with the 12%→18% hike collapsing, while domain blocking and payment enforcement escalated effectively.**

- 12% GGR contribution sits on top of PIS/COFINS, ISS and corporate taxes; players pay income tax on net winnings [rates not re-verified].
- MP 1.303/2025 to lift GGR rate to 18% collapsed without a vote in October 2025; a glide-path to 15% by 2028 is reported [unverified].
- Anatel blocked 15,463 unlicensed domains (October 2024–June 2025) and about 25,000 by end-2025; 229 illegal influencer posts removed in 2025.
- July 2026: government notified 37 fintechs for processing illegal-bet money; PL 2.985/2023 (Senate, 28 May 2025) would bar ads during live sports broadcasts.

### MP 1.394/2026 prohibition

**On 25 September 2026 Lula signed MP 1.394/2026, ending fixed-odds betting on public-health grounds with sites offline on 6 October and no compensation.**

- Covers sports betting and online games, physical and virtual channels; extinguishes state, Federal District and all 85 federal authorisations.
- Deposits banned from publication; withdrawals until 23:59 on 5 October; balances reported 7–8 October; bank refunds 9–14 October, Caixa as fallback.
- No indemnity for lost profits, sunk investment or outorga fees; fines up to R$2bn (~$0.39bn); Casa Civil committee drives domain blocking, payment restriction and account freezes.
- Companion bill creates five crimes: 4–6 years for exploiting betting, 2–4 years plus fines for advertising, acquiring bettors, facilitating payments or hosting.
- Evidence base: 140% rise in SUS gambling-related care 2018–2025, projected further 104% by 2028, IEPS social cost R$38.8bn (~$7.6bn) a year, R$30.6bn health-related.
- Congress must convert the MP within 120 days (by late January 2027); criminalisation bill needs separate passage.

### Operator implications

**Roughly half of LatAm regulated online GGR is now at best a 2027–28 re-entry option, and committed capital is impaired.**

- Licence fees, Brazilian entities, SIGAP integrations, Pix acquiring and sponsorships are impaired; refusal of compensation makes recovery a judicial question.
- Lula's re-election in October 2026 means a change of administration is not a path to reversal.
- Live routes: congressional rejection or amendment, STF suspension (2024 PGR action shows the Court is seised), or re-regulation at a materially higher tax rate once the ~R$800m (~$157m) monthly fiscal hole bites.
- Model Brazil as an offshore market with aggressive payment and domain enforcement; assess Brazil-facing activity against proposed criminal exposure, including extraterritorial advertising ban.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Colombia, Peru, Chile & Argentina — the Andean and Southern Cone regulated pool

*Colombia's VAT and Peru's licensing wave define the ex-Brazil opportunity; Chile is still unlicensed and Argentina is a provincial patchwork with an ad-ban overhang.*

### Colombia

**Colombia is the clearest case of fiscal over-reach, with 19% VAT on deposits cutting licensees to 14 and online income reportedly down 32%.**

- Coljuegos has licensed internet operators since 2016 under Law 643/2001; 14 authorised online operators at end-2025, down from high-teens in 2023–24.
- February 2025 economic-emergency decree (Catatumbo crisis) imposed 19% VAT on deposits, on top of exploitation rights of 15% of GGR (17% where RTP below 83%) [not re-verified].
- Coljuegos credits online games with roughly COP 900bn (~$239m) of VAT plus COP 46bn (~$12.2m) of exploitation rights by November 2025; VAT survival depends on Constitutional Court and Congress [unverified].
- Vixio still projects GGR above $1bn by 2028; deposit-based VAT punishes high-turnover sportsbook and makes bonus-led acquisition uneconomic against BetPlay, Wplay, Rushbet and Codere/Betsson/Stake incumbents.

### Peru

**Peru is open, competitive and reasonably taxed at 12%, with the main risk being crowding of more than 60 licensees in a sub-$1bn market.**

- Law 31557 (August 2022), amended by Law 31806, operational February 2024; MINCETUR regulates, SUNAT administers 12% tax on net income; cryptocurrencies excluded.
- 145 applications in first window; 118 licences to 63 operators by December 2024; more than 60 authorised companies in April 2026.
- Vixio forecasts $436m GGR in 2025 rising above $850m by 2028; deposit limits and self-exclusion mandatory.
- A selective-consumption tax on each wager reported from 2025 [rate unverified] would be an incremental turnover-based drag.

### Chile

**Chile has demand of over $460m of unlicensed online GGR in 2025 but no regime; we would not plan for licensed revenue before 2028.**

- Fiscal leakage from unauthorised play put at ~CLP 86bn (~$92.6m) per a July 2026 study reported by Yogonet.
- Bill cleared the Chamber in December 2023 and has sat in the Senate since; envisages GGR tax, SCJ licensing and a cooling-off period for grey-market operators [unverified].
- Vixio expected a framework 'by 2025', which has not happened.
- Grey-market exposure is a licensing liability given the bill's blackout provisions and the SCJ's litigation record against offshore brands.

### Argentina

**Argentina is a provincial patchwork with an advertising-ban overhang, best approached as a B2B supplier with ARS-denominated terms.**

- Buenos Aires City (LOTBA) and Province (IPLyC) launched in 2021–22, joined by roughly ten other provinces [unverified], each with own tax base and rate.
- Federal indirect tax applies to online bets with a surcharge for operators without local presence [rates n/a].
- Vixio warns of 'potential advertising bans'; a national bill restricting betting advertising has been advancing [status unverified].
- Hyper-inflation and currency controls make USD forecasts unreliable; no clean Argentine GGR line from Vixio or H2.
- Work through established licensees: Boldt, Codere, Betsson, bet365 and the Buenos Aires casino groups.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Mexico, Central America & the Caribbean

*Mexico is the second-largest regulated pool (>$2bn by 2028) but runs on a 1947 statute and discretionary permits; the smaller Central American and Caribbean regimes are niche licensing bases rather than demand markets.*

### Mexico

**Mexico is the second-largest regulated pool, $1.3bn in 2024 rising above $2bn by 2028, but runs on a 1947 statute and discretionary permits.**

- Operators work under the 1947 Federal Law on Games and Raffles and its 2004 regulation, policed by SEGOB; online typically runs under a land-based permit-holder's authorisation [not re-verified].
- Vixio flags 'regulatory challenges tied to land-based operator licenses and disputes over online gaming restrictions', referencing the November 2023 decree curbing slots and new permits.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax matrix — how the rules compare

*GGR-based levies of 12–15% were the regional norm; the trend since 2025 is to add turnover- or deposit-based layers (Colombia VAT, Peru ISC) or to prohibit outright (Brazil).*

### Tax architecture

**GGR levies of 12–15% were the norm; since 2025 the trend is to add turnover- or deposit-based layers or prohibit outright.**

- Brazil: R$30m (~$5.9m) for five years and up to three brands, 12% of GGR; Peru 12% on net income; Colombia 15% of GGR (17% below 83% RTP) [not re-verified].
- Mexico IEPS 30% on gross receipts [2026 increase unverified]; Argentina stacks provincial GGR taxes on a federal online-bet tax [rates n/a].
- Upward moves: Colombia's 19% VAT on deposits, Peru's reported wager-level ISC, Brazil's failed 12%→18% hike and reported 15%-by-2028 glide path, Mexican IEPS proposal.
- Colombia's 14 licensees and reported 32% fall in online income show deposit- or turnover-based taxes bite far harder than headline rates, because they tax stake recycling.

### Licence durability

**Licence availability is not the constraint; durability is, after Brazil extinguished 85 authorisations without refunding R$2.55bn (~$0.5bn) of fees.**

- Brazil: 114 applications (late 2024) to 85 companies and ~188 brands by September 2026, then zero; Peru: 145 applications to 118 licences for 63 operators; Colombia drifted to 14.
- Brazil's extinguishment is the most severe regulatory expropriation in a licensed online market anywhere and will be priced into every LatAm licence by lenders and boards.
- Keep technology, content and payments contracts separable from the licensed entity and avoid front-loading multi-year fees; now a governance requirement.

## 8. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & enforcement

*Instant-payment rails made LatAm online gambling scale; regulators have now learned to use the same rails — and advertising law — as the enforcement perimeter.*

### Payment rails

**Instant rails like Pix drove scale and are now the enforcement perimeter, so platforms need regulator-grade reporting and rapid rail switching.**

- Pix enabled the 15.1m-bettor quarter in Q1 2026 and up-to-R$30bn (~$5.9bn) monthly wagering measured in Q1 2025.
- MP 1.394/2026 directs ministries to restrict payment methods, block accounts and trace flows; 37 fintechs notified in July 2026; Pix transactions declined nationwide before the ban.
- Peru excludes cryptocurrency; Colombia collects VAT at deposit, making the processor the tax-collection point; Mexico uses OXXO vouchers and SPEI, Argentina bank transfers and Mercado Pago [not re-verified].
- Brazil's SIGAP daily feed is the precedent for transaction reporting.

### Responsible gambling

**Responsible-gambling obligations have hardened into public-health policy, making operators' affordability data a political asset or liability.**

- Brazil's pre-ban regime required a federal self-exclusion platform, ad rules barring appeals to minors, and a credit-card funding ban [not re-verified].
- MP cites +140% SUS gambling-related care (2018–25) and IEPS social cost of R$38.8bn (~$7.6bn) a year; health system to actively seek out former bettors.
- Peru requires deposit limits and self-exclusion; Colombia and Argentina's provincial lotteries run self-exclusion registers [not re-verified].

### Advertising and enforcement

**Affiliate-, influencer- and sponsorship-led growth models that built Brazil's R$36.9bn year are the most exposed to the next regulatory wave.**

- PL 2.985/2023 (Senate, 28 May 2025) would bar ads during live sports; the MP overtakes it with a complete ban including placements abroad and 2–4 years' imprisonment proposed.
- Argentina's national advertising bill and Vixio's 'potential advertising bans' warning show the same direction; Colombia and Peru restrict content and bonus messaging.
- Anatel blocked 15,463 sites in the first nine months and ~25,000 by end-2025, removed 229 influencer posts in 2025; Coljuegos has blocked domains since 2017; Chile's SCJ pursues offshore operators.
- Media-rights and club-sponsorship commitments should carry regulatory-change termination rights.

## 10. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Structural demand is proven, not theoretical: Brazil generated R$36.9bn (~$7.2bn) of regulated GGR in its first year — more than double Vixio's forecast — with 15.1m unique bettors in a single quarter; that demand persists whatever the licensing status, and H2 still models LatAm total GGR rising to $51bn by 2030. _(structural)_
- Peru offers a clean, open licensing template: MINCETUR has authorised >60 companies, tax is 12% of net income, and Vixio sees GGR doubling to >$850m by 2028 — a low-friction base for a B2B platform/content play. _(structural)_
- Mexico is the largest regulated pool unaffected by new legislation (>$2bn online GGR by 2028, Vixio), with a mature cash-voucher and bank-transfer payment stack; permit partnerships with incumbents are the entry route. _(structural)_
- Chile's >$460m of unlicensed online GGR (2025) is an identifiable licensing prize once the Senate bill passes; early compliance positioning (no grey-market exposure) is the way to be eligible on day one. _(early)_
- Brazil's fiscal hole (~R$800m / ~$157m per month of lost federal revenue) and the enforcement cost of a wholly offshore market create a credible path to re-regulation at a higher tax rate by 2027–28 — a re-entry option for operators who preserve Brazilian entities, data and relationships. _(mixed)_
- Online poker and other liquidity products ($467.6m by 2030, GVR) favour network operators; a B2B supplier positioned across Argentine provinces, Mexico and Peru can aggregate liquidity that single-market licensees cannot. _(structural)_
- Regulators across the region (SPA-MF's SIGAP, Coljuegos, MINCETUR) now demand daily transaction-level reporting — a compliance-technology moat for suppliers who already run it. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Brazil's MP 1.394/2026 is the most severe regulatory expropriation in any licensed online market: 85 authorisations extinguished from 6 Oct 2026, R$2.55bn (~$0.5bn) of fees forfeited, no compensation for sunk investment, fines up to R$2bn (~$0.39bn) and proposed 4–6-year prison terms for operators — roughly half of regional regulated online GGR removed. _(structural)_
- Tax drift is upward and shifting to turnover/deposit bases: Colombia's 19% VAT on deposits coincided with a reported 32% fall in online income and a licensee count down to 14; Peru added a wager-level ISC [unverified]; Brazil attempted 12%→18%; Mexico's IEPS rise was reported for 2026 [unverified]. _(structural)_
- Every published 2030 forecast (H2 $51bn total; Vixio $12.3bn regulated online 2028; GVR $7.24bn→$16.2bn) pre-dates the Brazilian ban and the models disagree on perimeter by a factor of 2–7x; capital plans built on these numbers are mis-specified. _(watch)_
- Advertising prohibition is spreading: Brazil's MP bans all betting advertising and sponsorship (even placed abroad) with criminal penalties proposed; PL 2.985/2023 and Argentina's national bill show the same direction — affiliate-, influencer- and sponsorship-led growth models are the most exposed. _(structural)_
- Payment rails are now an enforcement perimeter: Brazil is restricting payment methods and freezing accounts, notified 37 fintechs in Jul 2026, and Pix volumes were already falling; PSP and acquiring partners carry legal exposure for any Brazil-facing flows. _(governance)_
- Political timing is unfavourable to reversal: Congress has only 120 days to vote the MP, trade-press headlines report Lula's re-election in Oct 2026, and the public-health framing (R$38.8bn / ~$7.6bn social cost; +140% SUS care) gives prohibition broad cover; STF litigation is slow and uncertain [post-25 Sep status unverified]. _(watch)_
- FX and inflation erode USD economics: H2's forecasts are constant-FX, and Argentina's and Brazil's currencies have a record of devaluation; USD-reported growth will lag local-currency growth, and licence fees paid in local currency (R$30m) are hard to recover. _(mixed)_

## 11. Forecast & Outlook

The pre-ban consensus had Latin America as the world's fastest-growing regulated region: H2 Gambling Capital's April 2026 update takes total GGR from $29bn (2024) to $51bn (2030), Vixio takes regulated online from $2.5bn (2024) to $12.3bn (2028), and GVR's online series implies ~$12bn by 2030 with poker at $467.6m. Brazil drove the bridge in every model — Vixio had it at 47% of regulated online in 2025 rising to over half by 2028, and actual SPA-MF data (R$36.9bn / ~$7.2bn in 2025; R$20.07bn / ~$3.9bn in H1 2026, +15.3%) ran more than twice ahead of that path. MP 1.394/2026 reverses the bridge: from 6 Oct 2026 Brazil contributes zero regulated GGR, and the moving parts for 2030 are (i) whether Congress converts, amends or lets the MP lapse within 120 days, (ii) whether the STF suspends it, (iii) the tax rate and advertising regime attached to any re-licensing, and (iv) how much Brazilian demand the offshore market retains under payment-rail enforcement. Outside Brazil the bridge is intact but slower: Colombia's VAT has cut licensee numbers to 14, Peru is crowding, Mexico's permit regime is discretionary, Chile's bill remains unpassed and Argentina faces an advertising bill.

- **H2 GC — LatAm & Caribbean total GGR 2030F (all channels, pre-ban):** $51bn (from $29bn in 2024 (~9.9% CAGR))
- **Vixio — LatAm regulated online GGR 2028F (pre-ban):** $12.3bn (from $2.5bn in 2024; Brazil $6.3bn (>50%) now removed)
- **Brazil regulated GGR annualised run-rate (H1-26):** R$40.1bn (~$7.9bn) (→ 0 regulated from 6 Oct 2026 under MP 1.394/2026)
- **B2Tech 2030 regulated online — ban case / reversal case (est.):** ~$6–8bn / ~$17–20bn (vs ~$10–11bn actual regulated base in 2025 (est.))

Our independent view is that **the 2030 regulated market will be materially smaller than the published consensus and more fragmented**. Base case (ban persists through 2027, partial Brazilian re-licensing from 2028 at a 15–18% GGR levy with a blanket sports-sponsorship ban): LatAm regulated online GGR of roughly **$10–13bn in 2030** (estimate), ex-Brazil regulated online compounding at ~12–15% a year to ~$6–8bn, Brazil regulated contributing ~$4–5bn in a first partial year, and offshore Brazilian GGR of $5–8bn persisting despite enforcement. H2's $51bn total-GGR figure remains plausible because it includes offshore and land-based (lotteries, Mexican and Argentine casinos), but the onshore online share of LatAm GGR falls rather than rises. **Margins compress**: effective tax on GGR-equivalent terms moves from ~12–15% to 20–30%+ across Colombia (VAT), Mexico (IEPS) and a re-licensed Brazil, and acquisition costs rise as advertising windows close — expect EBITDA margins for licensed sportsbook operators in the low-to-mid teens, with casino content and B2B suppliers retaining better economics. **Leverage and capital intensity rise**: licence fees become sunk and non-recoverable risks, so boards will demand shorter payback, local-currency debt and separable B2B structures. Swing factors, in order of importance: the fate of MP 1.394/2026 in Congress (by late Jan 2027) and at the STF; Colombia's Constitutional Court/Congress decision on the permanence of the 19% VAT; passage of Chile's bill; Mexico's 2026 IEPS treatment; and Argentina's advertising bill. Upside to our base case comes from a quick Brazilian reversal; downside from Mexico or Colombia following Brazil's public-health framing.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Brazil: Congress vote on MP 1.394/2026 within 120 days (by ~late Jan 2027); STF actions on the MP and the 2024 PGR challenge to Law 14.790; progress of the five-crime bill; any Finance Ministry signal on re-licensing at 15–18% of GGR.
- Brazil: Central Bank Pix volume data and Anatel blocking statistics as a proxy for offshore migration; monthly federal revenue shortfall (~R$800m / ~$157m) in Receita Federal releases.
- Colombia: Constitutional Court and Congress decisions on the 19% VAT on online gambling deposits; Coljuegos licensee count (14 at end-2025) and quarterly exploitation-rights collections.
- Peru: MINCETUR licence renewals/revocations among >60 authorised companies; SUNAT collections under the 12% regime and the reported wager-level ISC.
- Chile: Senate committee timetable for the online gambling bill; SCJ litigation against offshore brands; whether the 2025 >$460m unlicensed GGR estimate is adopted in the fiscal case for passage.
- Mexico: 2026 IEPS treatment of gaming and SEGOB permit renewals; any move to a standalone online licence.
- Argentina: national advertising-restriction bill in the Senate; provincial licence rounds (CABA, PBA renewals); ARS devaluation impact on USD-reported GGR.
- Forecast-house revisions: H2's next IMF-linked update and Vixio's 2026 LatAm Outlook are the first to incorporate the Brazilian ban — compare their onshore/offshore split for Brazil against SPA-MF's final H1-26 series.
