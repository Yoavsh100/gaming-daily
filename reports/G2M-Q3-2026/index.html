Gentoo Media

G2M · Released 25 Nov 2026 · Currency EUR (tables in millions; USD equivalents at EUR/USD ~1.094 shown as ~$) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

A quarter that confirms the August diagnosis rather than changes it: the cost base and 41% EBITDA margin are intact and the December bond cliff is gone, but the revenue engine is still shrinking (€20.8m/~$22.7m, -9% YoY, seventh consecutive decline) and FY26 guidance was cut for the third time in three months because operators resisted the commercial re-terms meant to convert record deposits into revenue. The price of the balance-sheet fix is control: the Juroszek family now owns ~56–59% of the equity and holds all of the residual ~€57.5m (~$62.9m) of debt at EURIBOR+7.50%.

1Executive summary

  • Seventh consecutive quarter of revenue decline, and a miss against a plan that had already been cut twice. Q3-26 revenue €20.8m (~$22.7m), -9% YoY (€22.7m/~$24.8m) and -9% QoQ (€22.9m/~$25.1m); 9M €67.7m (~$74.0m), -7.5% vs restated €73.1m (~$80.0m). The 1 Oct trading update had already flagged Q3 'below expectations', so the print confirmed rather than surprised.
  • EBITDA before special items €8.5m (~$9.3m), -9% YoY, margin 40.9% (41.1% in Q3-25; 38.9% in Q2-26). Profitability was held entirely by cost: total opex €12.3m (~$13.5m), -€1.1m (~$1.2m) YoY; marketing €5.1m (~$5.6m), -14% YoY and -25% QoQ (25% of revenue vs 26%/30%); employee costs €4.2m (~$4.6m), -24% YoY. Other opex rose 54% YoY to €2.9m (~$3.2m), partly offsetting the staff savings — not called out by management.
  • Third guidance cut in three months, and the biggest in cash-flow terms. FY26 now: revenue €87–88m (~$95.2–96.3m) vs €92m (1 Oct), €97–100m (26 Aug) and €105–115m (original); EBITDA before special items €35–36m (~$38.3–39.4m) vs €40m / €44–47m / €49–54m; operating cash flow ~€24m (~$26.3m) vs €30m / €32–36m / €37–41m. At midpoints the cumulative cuts are c.-20% to revenue and c.-31% to EBITDA; FY26 now implies revenue -11% and EBITDA -14% on FY25 (€98.7m/€41.3m; ~$108.0m/~$45.2m).
  • Which division drove it: Publishing's revenue-share line, not Brazil. Brazil (~€4.1m/~$4.5m of 9M revenue, ~6% of group) had only a 'limited' Q3 impact and was already stripped out on 1 Oct. The incremental cut is the slower-than-planned commercial optimisation programme — operators responded 'more negatively than anticipated' to proposed Q4 term changes and Gentoo chose to honour existing deals — plus weaker sports margins late in Q3 and a soft Paid Media quarter (revenue €3.4m/~$3.7m, -21% YoY). Group revenue share income -14% YoY, CPA -21%, listing fees/other +13%; mix now 60/11/29.
  • Implied Q4-26 is ugly for a seasonally strong quarter. Guidance arithmetic: Q4 revenue €19.3–20.3m (~$21.1–22.2m), i.e. -20% to -24% YoY vs Q4-25 €25.6m (~$28.0m) and down sequentially; Q4 EBITDA before special items €7.2–8.2m (~$7.9–9.0m), a 36–41% margin; Q4 operating cash flow only ~€3.8m (~$4.2m) vs €14.0m (~$15.3m) in Q4-25. Management confirmed on the call that Q4 revenue is expected to decline from Q3.
  • The core problem is monetisation, not traffic. Value of deposits €202m (~$221m), +4% YoY, the fourth straight quarter above €200m; yet revenue per euro of deposits fell to 10.3% from 11.6% in Q3-25 and 11.1% in Q2-26. FTDs 92,256, -15% YoY / -9% QoQ, split Publishing 53.9k (+3% YoY, +3% QoQ) and Paid Media 38.4k (-32% YoY, -23% QoQ). CEO Warrer on the call: increased player activity is not translating into revenue, 'rather the opposite'.

Quarterly segment EBITDA is not disclosed; Publishing is effectively the whole profit pool.

Capex €1.4m (~$1.6m, capitalised development); FCF before financing €4.0m (~$4.4m) after €0.9m deferred consideration; after €2.6m (~$2.8m) interest and €0.4m leases, ~€1.9m (~$2.1m). Note 7 concludes no material going-concern uncertainty only because of the post-period refinancing.
  • Refinancing completed 2 Nov — €100m (~$109m) from the controlling family. (i) €50m (~$54.7m) senior secured loan from Fundacja Zbigniewa Juroszka at 3M EURIBOR +7.50% (2.00% floor), maturing 18 Dec 2029, amortising €10m (~$10.9m) on each of the first two anniversaries, no fee/warrants, prepayable; (ii) €50m (~$54.7m) directed issue of 91,928,663 unlisted Class Z shares at SEK 6.1633 (€0.5439/~$0.60) — the 12-month VWAP and a 58% premium to the SEK 3.90 close on 30 Sep — taken entirely by two Juroszek foundations. Dilution 40.6%; shares in issue 226.6m; the underwriters hold ~56.5% (report) and the family ~59.3% in total (next.io). Proceeds: €91.0m bond redemption, €2.0m unsecured facility, €6.5m of the €14.0m secured shareholder facility. Pro forma borrowings ~€57.5m (~$62.9m), leverage ~1.6x at year-end.
  • Interest burden roughly halves — the one clean positive for 2027 FCF. Our estimate: ~€5.5m (~$6.0m) annual cash interest pro forma (€50m at ~9.6% + €7.5m at ~9.3%) versus a ~€10m (~$10.9m) current run-rate (bond interest €2.2m/quarter plus shareholder loans) — a €4–5m swing, worth ~12–14% of guided FY26 EBITDA [analyst estimate].
  • Governance has changed materially. The loan, underwriting and issue are related-party transactions; directors Mateusz and Tomasz Juroszek abstained, an independent Refinancing Committee ran the process (>100 providers approached, several firm offers), and Deloitte Malta gave a fairness opinion. But all residual debt is now owed to the majority holder, Class Z shares are unlisted and convertible 1:1 after 12 months, and the February 2026 attempt at a €120m bond via ABG/Pareto failed. The Oslo listing was dropped earlier in 2026; Nasdaq Stockholm (G2M) is the sole listing.
  • Commercial optimisation: real but slow, and now a 2027 story. Improved agreements were secured 'with a number of operators across multiple markets'; the ambition is a 'double-digit' revenue uplift via fewer, deeper partnerships, re-weighting from listing fees toward rev-share/CPA (accepting short-term revenue pressure) and data-led traffic allocation by market/asset/placement. From January 2027 management says it regains 'far more flexibility' on which operators it promotes. Q4 is showing 'early signs'; benefits 'build progressively into 2027'.
  • Product/tech delivered the quarter's genuine operating wins. Casinomeister migrated to the proprietary WordPress platform in July (largest migration to date): organic search clicks +85% QoQ and >4x Q3-25, clicks to operators >3x QoQ, FTDs >2x QoQ, with dedicated sales resource added in August. AskGamblers embedded its complaints data (~40,000 disputes, >$93m recovered since 2009) across reviews and launched a public Complaints Intelligence Dashboard post-quarter. AI football predictions now cover 30 leagues in 8 languages; a 'smart offers engine' is in early development. AI-driven discovery traffic remains 'very, very modest'; a Google core update was rolling out at the time of the call. FTEs 277 across Malta, Copenhagen and Belgrade.
  • Brazil: an investment market closed before it paid back. The 25 Sep Provisional Measure banned fixed-odds betting and online gaming with immediate effect (Congress ratification pending, 120-day window); Gentoo ceased activity and redirected marketing. Brazil's EBITDA/cash contribution was proportionately lower than its ~6% revenue share, so the exit is revenue-negative but near-EBITDA-neutral; the full quarterly impact lands in Q4 and guidance assumes zero Brazil revenue. ABG notes Gentoo's exposure was roughly half Better Collective's. Any return would be judged on earnings, not top line.
  • Management is buying, modestly. CEO Jonas Warrer bought 100,000 shares at SEK 3.98–3.99 (~€35.5k/~$39k) around the print, taking holdings with close associates to ~1.3m shares plus 2.0m options — still outside the top-20 register.
  • Quality-of-reporting flags. 2025 comparatives are restated for material errors identified in Q3-25 (H1-25 revenue +€1.2m, EBITDA +€1.5m, unrecorded domain amortisation €1.4m, Brazilian tax reclassified against revenue). Q1-26 carried a €2.5m goodwill/intangible impairment on the Norwich closure. Secondary coverage contains errors: next.io attributes the €4.1m Brazil figure to Q3 alone (it is 9M) and gamblingnews says 'sixth' quarter above €200m deposits (company: fourth).
  • Thesis read-through. The equity is now a 2027 revenue-recovery option on a ~41%-margin, deleveraged, majority-controlled affiliate with a ~€87m (~$95m) revenue base that the cost line can no longer rescue. Q3 confirmed the market's August verdict — the problem sits in revenue share yield per player, not the P&L beneath it. For minorities, the 58%-premium equity raise was better than a market-priced rights issue, but the counterweight is that the controller is also the senior lender, and a take-private at depressed levels cannot be excluded.
  • Share price: muted, because the bad news had been pre-released. ~-4% to ~SEK 3.95 (~$0.38) on the day per Investing.com, versus -26% on Q2 day (26 Aug); 52-week high SEK 8.66 (~$0.84); the stock trades ~36% below the SEK 6.16 issue price the family just paid.

3Segment deep-dive: Publishing (owned affiliate sites: AskGamblers, Casinomeister, Time2Play, CasinoTopsOnline, WSN; 65+ sites)

The whole profit pool — 55% 9M margin on a cost base cut 21% — but revenue is still falling 6–8% and the rev-share yield per player keeps sliding.

Revenue and mix

Q3 revenue estimated at €17.4m (~$19.0m), roughly -6% year-on-year and -6% quarter-on-quarter; 9M revenue €55.0m (~$60.2m), -8% versus restated €59.6m (~$65.2m).

  • Q3 figure derived as group revenue of €20.8m (~$22.7m) less disclosed €3.4m (~$3.7m) Paid Media; comparatives ~€18.4m (~$20.1m) Q3-25 and ~€18.5m (~$20.2m) Q2.
  • 9M revenue share down 10% to €31.0m (~$33.9m), CPA down 15% to €6.0m (~$6.6m), listing fees/other up 1% to €18.0m (~$19.7m).
  • Listing fees now 33% of Publishing revenue versus 30% a year ago; management wants to rebalance toward rev-share and CPA, accepting near-term revenue pressure.
  • Q3 decline attributed to a strong Q3-25 North America comparative with strong CPA earnings, plus weaker sports margins compressing rev-share in the back half.
  • No geographic split beyond Brazil (€4.1m/~$4.5m over nine months, ~6% of group, exited end-September); UK, Nordics and other European exposure cannot be quantified.

Profitability

9M EBITDA before special items rose 7% to €30.2m (~$33.0m), a 55% margin against 47%, as the cost base fell 21% to €24.9m (~$27.2m).

  • Cost reduction came from portfolio simplification, the Norwich closure and headcount reduction.
  • Special items of €3.0m (~$3.3m) left reported EBITDA of €27.1m (~$29.7m) at a 49% margin (43%).
  • Quarterly segment EBITDA not disclosed; with group EBITDA before special items of €8.5m (~$9.3m) and Paid Media loss-making, Publishing likely earned north of €9m (~$9.8m) in Q3 [estimate].
  • Cost lever now largely exhausted: 9M cost base runs at ~€8.3m (~$9.1m) a quarter and further cuts would hit product and SEO investment management says it needs.

Player activity and yield

FTDs grew to 53.9k (+3% quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year) but derived revenue per FTD fell to ~€322 (~$352) from ~€354 (~$387) in Q2.

  • FTDs were 52.2k in Q2 and 52.5k in Q3-25; implied Publishing deposits ~€121.8m (~$133m), group €202m (~$221m) less Paid Media's €80.2m (~$87.7m).
  • Revenue per FTD was ~€350 (~$383) a year ago; group revenue per euro of deposits dropped to 10.3% from 11.6%.
  • This is a yield problem (operator terms, sports margins, cohort maturity, listing-fee weighting) rather than a traffic problem.
  • Company retains position as one of the larger European casino affiliates, but no market-share or ranking data is disclosed; we would not take 'leading affiliate' language at face value.
“rather the opposite”Warrer

Product and technology

A genuinely productive quarter: Casinomeister's July platform migration lifted organic search clicks 85% quarter-on-quarter and more than four times Q3-25.

  • Casinomeister migration to proprietary WordPress-based platform was the group's largest to date; clicks to operators more than tripled, FTDs more than doubled, dedicated sales resource assigned in August.
  • AskGamblers expanded use of complaints data (roughly 40,000 disputes processed, more than $93m recovered since 2009) and launched a public Complaints Intelligence Dashboard after the quarter.
  • AI football-prediction products give daily predictions across 30 leagues in eight languages, being developed into an interactive tips platform to reduce reliance on organic search; 'smart offers engine' at early stage.
  • Traffic from AI-driven discovery remains 'very, very modest'; a Google core update was rolling out during the call — the perennial affiliate risk.
“very, very modest”Management

Commercial optimisation

The programme is where the quarter was lost; its output is now unambiguously a 2027 variable and guidance implies Publishing revenue falls again sequentially in Q4.

  • Improved agreements secured with a number of operators across multiple markets; operator performance now evaluated at market, asset and placement level.
  • Some partners responded more negatively than expected to proposed Q4 changes; Gentoo honoured existing arrangements as 'a long-term partner'.
  • Flexibility to decide who is promoted on which pages only arrives in January 2027.
  • Warrer floated working with fewer partners — some sites run hundreds — for deeper relationships, with an ambition of a double-digit revenue uplift.
  • Credit for transparency, but this is the third consecutive quarter in which converting activity into revenue has been 'the priority'.

4Segment deep-dive: Paid Media (performance marketing: SEM, social, programmatic)

A deposit-volume engine with negative EBITDA: 40% of group deposits, 16% of revenue, and a 9M loss that widened to -€2.3m after the World Cup push.

Revenue

Q3 revenue was €3.4m (~$3.7m), -23% quarter-on-quarter and -21% year-on-year; 9M revenue €12.6m (~$13.8m), -7% versus €13.5m (~$14.8m).

  • Comparatives: €4.4m (~$4.8m) in Q2 and ~€4.3m (~$4.7m) in Q3-25.
  • Model is almost entirely performance-based — 76% revenue share, 18% CPA, 6% listing fees over nine months — so revenue follows the acquisition curve with a lag.
  • After the deliberate World Cup push in Q2 (Paid FTDs +46% quarter-on-quarter then), Q3 pivoted to acquisition efficiency and profitability.
  • Weaker sports margins limited rev-share from World Cup cohorts, the same luck/margin exposure that hit Publishing, amplified by Paid's heavier sports mix.
  • Brazil exit removes a growth bet contributing revenue and deposits but little or no earnings.

Volume and economics

FTDs fell to 38.4k (-23% quarter-on-quarter, -32% year-on-year) while deposits stayed broadly stable at €80.2m (~$87.7m), -2% from ~€81.8m (~$89.5m).

  • FTDs were 49.7k in Q2 and 56.6k in Q3-25; budgets cut and redirected from Brazil toward 'markets and channels offering the strongest returns'.
  • Stable deposits indicate the existing cohort base is still active.
  • Paid Media produced roughly 42% of group FTDs and ~40% of deposits but only ~16% of revenue; revenue per euro of deposits ~4.2% (Q2 ~5.4%) versus ~14% in Publishing.
  • Revenue per FTD flat at ~€89 (~$97), up from ~€76 (~$83) a year ago, so the mix bought has improved as volume shrank.
  • Operator bonuses and acquisition incentives reduce initial revenue from new players, so returns are back-end loaded — the recurring theme.

Profitability

9M EBITDA before special items was a €2.3m (~-$2.6m) loss, a -19% margin, versus a €1.6m (~-$1.8m) loss (-12%) a year earlier.

  • Segment costs were €14.9m (~$16.4m), -1%, so the loss widened because revenue fell faster than media spend.
  • Special items of €0.8m (~$0.8m) took reported EBITDA to -€3.1m (~-$3.4m).
