Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

Genting Singapore

G13.SI · Released 12 Nov 2026 (after SGX close) · Currency SGD (tables in S$ millions; ~USD at S$1 = US$0.777) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Shares closed at S$0.61 (~US$0.47) on 12 Nov 2026 (-0.8%) with the overview released after the SGX close, then fell ~6.6% to S$0.57 (~US$0.44) on 13 Nov, the first session after the release. The stock was -16.4% YTD before the print and ~28% below its year-ago level of ~S$0.79 (~US$0.61); market cap ~S$6.9bn (~$5.4bn).

The one-line verdict

A volume-and-luck double hit: Q3-26 gaming revenue fell 22% YoY to S$315.0m (~$245m) on softer premium play plus a September hold miss that management sizes at ~S$36m (~$28m) of adj. EBITDA, dragging adj. EBITDA -27% to S$161.8m (~$126m) and NPAT -42% to S$54.7m (~$42.5m). Non-gaming (+1% to S$250.9m/~$195m) and the S$2.9bn (~$2.25bn) net cash pile are the cushions, but management has now stretched RWS capacity constraints "through 2027", so the Q2 "recovery" narrative is dead for at least four more quarters.

1Executive summary

  • Headline miss on both volume and luck. Q3-26 (three months to 30 Sep; FY-end 31 Dec) revenue fell 12.9% YoY to S$566.1m (~$440m), adj. EBITDA -27.4% to S$161.8m (~$126m) and net profit after tax -42.2% to S$54.7m (~$42.5m), -39.8% QoQ from S$90.9m (~$70.6m) in Q2. Sell-side consensus could not be verified, but the Q2 'turn' (adj. EBITDA +18% QoQ) had set a higher bar, and the -6.6% share price reaction the next day says the print was below the bar.
  • Gaming did all the damage. Gaming revenue -21.7% YoY and -21.4% QoQ to S$315.0m (~$245m) — against S$402.3m (~$313m) in Q3-25, S$403.4m (~$313m) in Q1-26 and ~S$401m (~$312m) in Q2-26 (derived). Non-gaming was +1% YoY. There is only one reportable business (Resorts World Sentosa, RWS), so the 'division' that drove the miss is the casino, specifically the premium/VIP segment.
  • Management gave two reasons, in a deliberate order. (i) 'Softer premium gaming activity… consistent with a trend observed in regional key markets' — a demand/volume problem; (ii) 'an unfavourable rolling win rate in September' — luck. The company quantifies only the second: adj. EBITDA would have been ~S$36m (~$28m) higher at normalised rolling hold. The remaining ~S$25m (~$19m) of the S$61m (~$47m) YoY EBITDA decline is volume/mix.
  • Hold-normalised, the quarter was still down double digits. Adding back S$36m gives adj. EBITDA of ~S$198m (~$154m): -11% YoY and -6% QoQ at a ~35% margin (derived). Q3-25 was itself a strong VIP quarter (adj. EBITDA +36% YoY on rolling volume and hold), so the comp was tough — but premium volumes clearly went backwards after Q2's +21% QoQ rolling-chip rebound flagged by DBS.
  • Non-gaming held, and is now 44% of revenue. Non-gaming revenue +1% YoY (+1.4% derived) to S$250.9m (~$195m), +29% QoQ on school-holiday seasonality, despite fewer hotel rooms in service. Its share of group revenue rose to 44.3% from 38.1% a year ago and 32.7% in Q2 — partly structural (Singapore Oceanarium, WEAVE, The Laurus), mostly arithmetic from the gaming collapse.
  • Negative operating leverage: margin -570bps YoY to 28.6%. Adj. EBITDA margin was 34.3% in Q3-25 and 35.4% in Q2-26 (-680bps QoQ). Opex ex-D&A (revenue less adj. EBITDA, derived) was S$404m (~$314m), -5% YoY but +5% QoQ; 9M opex is flat YoY at ~S$1,218m (~$946m) while 9M revenue is -5%. The cost base is largely fixed outside gaming duty and rebates.
  • Below the line, the cash pile is earning less. Net interest income -37% YoY to S$11.3m (~$8.8m) as cash is deployed into RWS 2.0 and rates fall (1H-26 interest income was -55%). PBT -40% to S$76.0m (~$59m); tax S$21.3m (~$16.6m) for a 28.0% effective rate (Q3-25: 25.0%). Reported EBITDA S$157.7m (~$123m), -22.8%; the adjusted-vs-reported gap narrowed to S$4.1m from S$18.5m, so there is little 'one-off' cushioning in the adjusted number.
  • 9M-26 (derived: 1H reported + Q3): revenue S$1,769.7m (~$1.38bn), ~-5.1% YoY; gaming S$1,119.4m (~$870m), ~-9.7%; non-gaming S$649.7m (~$505m), ~+4.2%; adj. EBITDA S$551.6m (~$429m), ~-14.7% (margin 31.2% vs 34.7%); NPAT S$210.8m (~$164m), -36.0%; basic EPS ~1.74 S¢ (~US¢1.35) vs ~2.72 S¢.
  • No numeric guidance — but the qualitative guide deteriorated. RWS is 'entering the next phase of its renewal' (upgrading/repositioning the hotel portfolio plus enhancements to gaming areas, lounges, dining and guest spaces) and these works 'will temporarily constrain operating capacity through 2027'. At 1H (Aug) the message was Q2 'good progress', RWS 2.0 'on track for completion in 2030' and disruption framed around current hotel closures. 'Through 2027' is an extension of the trough, and it now explicitly touches the gaming floor.
  • Q4 messaging is seasonal, not structural. Management is 'focused on capturing year-end travel and festive demand', and continues to 'strengthen its premium customer strategy' via business-development and marketing capability and WEAVE programming. Maybank had noted in May that key BD hires would only join in 2H-26; Q3 shows no traction yet, and Maybank's post-result headline was 'no meaningful recovery yet'.
  • Market-wide, not idiosyncratic — mostly. Marina Bay Sands' Q3-26 adjusted property EBITDA fell 20.6% YoY to US$590m, corroborating a regional premium slowdown. RWS fell 27% reported / ~11% hold-adjusted. MBS still generates ~4.7x RWS's EBITDA (~US$126m); analysts cited in the Singapore press put MBS at 60-65% of Singapore GGR and RWS 'above 30%'. GENS does not disclose share, rolling volume or hold, so share gain/loss in Q3 is unknowable from the filing.
  • Balance sheet remains the anchor. At 30 Jun-26: cash and equivalents S$2.9bn (~$2.25bn), no borrowings beyond lease liabilities, total equity S$8.1bn (~$6.3bn). The Q3 quarterly overview discloses no cash, capex, operating cash flow or borrowings, so Q3 cash burn is unknown. Third-party estimates put annual net cash burn (capex + dividends less operating cash) at S$500-700m (~$390-545m) — not company-disclosed.
  • Dividend arithmetic is getting tighter. The 4.0 S¢ (~US¢3.1) FY payout (2.0 S¢ interim paid 22 Sep-26, 2.0 S¢ final in FY25) costs ~S$484m (~$376m) p.a. against FY26E NPAT of ~S$270-285m (~$210-221m) (our estimate) — a >170% payout, fundable from cash but not from earnings. Parent Genting Berhad (c.52% holder) was downgraded by Fitch over heavy capex and needs the upstream cash, which argues for the dividend being held rather than cut.
  • RWS 2.0 is the reason to own it and the reason earnings are down. S$6.8bn (~$5.3bn) programme to 2030, aligned with the Greater Sentosa Master Plan. Delivered so far: Singapore Oceanarium, WEAVE lifestyle enclave, The Laurus (Luxury Collection all-suites, Oct-25), Four Points by Sheraton Jurong rebrand (2Q26); Hard Rock Hotel closed for renovation/rebranding. The P&L cost is visible: 1H-26 D&A +25% to S$200.6m (~$156m), plus 'asset refresh' expenses. Morgan Stanley has flagged possible debt funding for the back half of the programme.
  • Disclosure quality is a lowlight in its own right. The quarterly business overview is an unaudited management-accounts summary not reviewed by auditors: no VIP rolling volume, hold %, mass GGR, visitation, hotel occupancy/ADR, marketing spend, capex or cash. Investors are pricing a S$6.9bn (~$5.4bn) company off a half-page P&L for two of four quarters. Full financials come only with 1H and FY (next: FY26 in Feb-27).
  • Management and governance. New gaming management installed in 1H-26 to run a retention/reactivation/acquisition strategy with renovated gaming areas and an expanded BD team; RWS appointed a new COO in late Oct/early Nov 2026 (SGX announcement referenced by TipRanks; name not verified here). Chairman remains Tan Sri Lim Kok Thay; CEO Tan Hee Teck.
  • Shares. Closed S$0.61 (~US$0.47) on 12 Nov (-0.8%) with results released after the close; fell ~6.6% to S$0.57 (~US$0.44) on 13 Nov. YTD -16.4% pre-release and ~28% below the year-ago S$0.79 (~US$0.61). Market cap ~S$6.9bn (~$5.4bn) on ~12.1bn shares; trailing yield ~7% at 4.0 S¢.
  • Thesis read-through. GENS is a 2028-30 RWS 2.0 option paying a balance-sheet-funded ~7% yield while it waits. Q3 says (a) the bridge period is longer (through 2027), (b) the premium book is more fragile than the Q2 bounce implied and more regionally correlated than management had conceded, and (c) non-gaming, while on plan, cannot fill an S$85m+ (~$66m) quarterly gaming hole. The debate for Q4 is volume, not luck.

3Segment deep-dive: Resorts World Sentosa — Gaming

Gaming revenue -22% YoY to S$315.0m (~$245m): premium volumes softened regionally and a September hold miss cost ~S$36m (~$28m) of EBITDA; hold-adjusted the segment is still down ~9-11%.

Revenue and hold

Gaming revenue fell 21.7% YoY to S$315.0m (~$245m), weakest print in the series; hold-adjusted it is still down ~9-11%.

  • Down 21.4% QoQ; Q1-26 S$403.4m (~$313m), Q2-26 roughly S$401m (~$312m), Q3-25 S$402.3m (~$313m).
  • Management cited 'softer premium gaming activity' consistent with regional key markets, plus an unfavourable rolling win rate in September.
  • Only luck quantified: adj. EBITDA would have been approximately S$36m (~$28m) higher at normalised rolling hold.
  • At ~65-70% flow-through, hold shortfall implies ~S$50-55m (~$39-43m) of revenue; hold-adjusted ~S$365-370m (~$285m), ~9% lower YoY, ~8% lower QoQ.
  • Our estimate, not a company figure; rolling volumes went backwards after the +21% QoQ VIP rolling chip rebound DBS highlighted for Q2-26.
“softer premium gaming activity”Management

Market and MBS comparison

Marina Bay Sands' Q3-26 adjusted property EBITDA fell 20.6% YoY to US$590m, so the premium slowdown is market-wide, not RWS share loss.

  • RWS adj. EBITDA ~US$126m is about 18% of the two properties' combined EBITDA (derived).
  • Singapore press analysts expect MBS to hold 60-65% of GGR with RWS 'above 30%'.
  • GENS discloses no GGR share, rolling volume, hold percentage or mass drop, so no share conclusion is possible.
  • Hold-adjusted RWS ~-11% EBITDA screens better than MBS's -21%, but MBS's hold variance is unknown; we would not read share gain into it.
  • First time in 2026 management leaned on a regional narrative rather than the Q1 'improving momentum' language.

Premium-skewed mix

Mass GGR was only 26% of gaming revenue in Q1-26, so roughly three-quarters of RWS gaming revenue is premium/VIP, driving sensitivity.

  • Materially more premium-skewed than MBS's mass-led floor (per AGB citing Maybank).
  • Tiered duty: 8%/12% on premium GGR, 18%/22% on mass, higher rates above S$2.4bn and S$3.1bn thresholds respectively.
  • Premium carries lower duty but higher rebates and commissions, and is most exposed to regional credit, Chinese and Southeast Asian high-end travel and hold.
  • Premium customer strategy: new gaming management team installed 1H-26, expanded BD team with key hires arriving 2H-26, renovated gaming areas and lounges.
  • Right medicine but administered while the patient is on the operating table; enhancements will constrain operating capacity through 2027.

Costs and leverage

Operating leverage is punishing: opex ex-D&A of S$404m (~$314m) fell 5% YoY but rose 5% sequentially on a 21% sequential gaming decline.

  • Nine-month opex flat at ~S$1,218m (~$946m) against revenue down 5%.
  • Marketing and promotional spend and gaming expense split are not disclosed, so rising reinvestment in the premium book cannot be confirmed.
  • Sequential opex increase suggests some incremental spend went in without a return in the quarter.

Q4 and FY26 outlook

We model Q4-26 gaming revenue of S$345-370m (~$268-288m), putting FY26E at ~S$1.46-1.49bn (~$1.14-1.16bn), down 8-10% on FY25.

  • Only guidance is seasonal: 'capturing year-end travel and festive demand'.
  • Q4 implies +10-17% QoQ on normalised hold and festive mass, but roughly flat to -5% against Q4-25 base of ~S$363m (~$282m, derived).
  • FY25 gaming ~S$1.60bn (~$1.25bn, derived); FY26E well short of the 'flat' base case Fitch assumed for 2026.
  • Swing factors: hold (two standard deviations comfortably S$30-40m of EBITDA either way), Chinese and Indonesian premium travel over Lunar New Year 2027 (Q1 event), and BD team conversion.

4Segment deep-dive: Resorts World Sentosa — Non-gaming, and Group corporate/treasury

Non-gaming +1% YoY to S$250.9m (~$195m) despite fewer hotel rooms; the cash pile (S$2.9bn/~$2.25bn at 30 Jun) is earning 37% less interest as RWS 2.0 absorbs it.

Non-gaming revenue

Non-gaming revenue rose 1% YoY to S$250.9m (~$195m) and 29% sequentially despite fewer hotel rooms, reaching 44.3% of group revenue.

  • +1.4% on our arithmetic against Q3-25 S$247.5m (~$192m); Q2-26 ~S$194.7m (~$151m, derived); Q3 is the June-September school-holiday peak.
  • Nine months S$649.7m (~$505m), roughly +4% YoY; share of group revenue up from 38.1% a year earlier.
  • Growth came 'despite a reduced number of hotel rooms': Hard Rock Hotel closed for renovation and rebranding, broader portfolio being repositioned.
  • Room revenue S$107.3m (~$83m) in 1H-26, +9%, held back by inventory rather than demand; other non-gaming S$73.1m (~$57m), +12%.
  • Attractions supported by Universal Studios Singapore, Singapore Oceanarium (opened 2025), Adventure Cove and season passes; no Q3 split given.
“despite a reduced number of hotel rooms”Management

Growth quality

With group adj. EBITDA margin at 28.6% and new assets still ramping, we would not assume non-gaming is margin-accretive yet.

  • GENS discloses no segment EBITDA, occupancy, ADR, attendance or per-cap spend.
  • Management macro commentary cautious through 2026: elevated airfares, higher energy, freight and logistics costs, moderating arrivals, 'more measured consumer spending'.
  • New assets (The Laurus since Oct-25, WEAVE, Four Points by Sheraton Jurong since 2Q26) depreciated and staffed ahead of stabilised revenue.
  • 1H D&A +25% to S$200.6m (~$156m) with explicit 'asset refresh' costs; Q3 implied below-EBITDA charges ~S$93m (~$72m).

Cash and treasury

Net interest income fell 37% YoY to S$11.3m (~$8.8m) as the S$2.9bn (~$2.25bn) cash pile is drawn down for RWS 2.0.

  • 1H interest income -55% to S$22.8m (~$17.7m); Singapore-dollar rates falling.
  • At 30 Jun-26: S$2.9bn cash, no borrowings other than leases, S$8.1bn (~$6.3bn) equity; no 30 Sep cash, capex or operating cash flow disclosed.
  • RWS 2.0 is S$6.8bn (~$5.3bn) through 2030; dividend costs ~S$484m (~$376m) a year at 4.0 S¢; parent Genting Berhad downgraded by Fitch.
  • All three claims on cash are rising while operating cash generation falls; we see debt funding for later phases as the base case for 2028-29, not a risk.

Q4 outlook

We model Q4 non-gaming revenue of S$225-240m (~$175-186m), giving FY26E of ~S$875-890m (~$680-690m), +3-4%.

  • Down 5-10% sequentially from the Q3 peak, roughly flat to +5% YoY against Q4-25 ~S$227m (~$176m, derived).
  • Segment is doing what it was built to do; the problem is scale.
  • Even at 44% of revenue, a S$85m+ (~$66m) quarterly gaming hole cannot be filled by low-single-digit non-gaming growth.
  • Capacity constraint 'through 2027' caps the hotel contribution for another five quarters.

5Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Non-gaming revenue grew +1% YoY to S$250.9m (~$195m) and +4% over 9M to S$649.7m (~$505m) despite reduced hotel room inventory — the Oceanarium/WEAVE/Laurus pipeline is converting, and non-gaming is now 44% of revenue. structural
  • The September hold miss is worth ~S$36m (~$28m) of adj. EBITDA by the company's own estimate; hold-normalised adj. EBITDA of ~S$198m (~$154m) would have been -11% YoY rather than -27%, with a ~35% margin. one-off
  • Balance sheet is unlevered: S$2.9bn (~$2.25bn) cash, no borrowings beyond leases and S$8.1bn (~$6.3bn) equity at 30 Jun-26; the 2.0 S¢ (~US¢1.55) interim was held and paid 22 Sep. structural
  • Premium weakness is regional, not RWS-specific: MBS Q3-26 adjusted property EBITDA fell 20.6% YoY to US$590m. Hold-adjusted, RWS's decline screens no worse than its larger neighbour's. mixed
  • Opex ex-D&A (derived) fell 5% YoY to S$404m (~$314m) and is flat over 9M at ~S$1,218m (~$946m) — some cost discipline is visible even if operating leverage is negative at these revenue levels. mixed
  • The Q2-26 rebound (VIP rolling chip +21% QoQ, adj. EBITDA +18% QoQ) proves the premium book can re-rate quickly when demand and hold cooperate; the new BD team is only fully staffed from 2H-26. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Gaming revenue -21.7% YoY and -21.4% QoQ to S$315.0m (~$245m); even stripping out the hold miss, premium volumes fell ~8-9% sequentially after Q2's rebound — the volume problem is real and management now calls it regional. structural
  • Adj. EBITDA margin -570bps YoY and -680bps QoQ to 28.6%; NPAT margin 9.7%. With ~75% of gaming revenue premium-skewed, RWS has the most hold- and VIP-sensitive P&L in the Singapore duopoly. structural

Leverage direction is clear even if the pace is not: net cash of S$2.9bn (~$2.25bn) at June is being consumed by RWS 2.0 capex and a ~S$484m (~$376m) dividend that earnings no longer cover; we assume year-end cash of ~S$2.5-2.7bn (~$1.9-2.1bn) and treat debt funding for the 2028-30 phases as the base case. The swing factors are Q4 hold (±S$30-40m / ±$23-31m of EBITDA), Chinese and Indonesian premium travel into Lunar New Year 2027 (a Q1 event), and whether the new BD team shows up in Q1-27 rolling volume. The stock is a 2028-30 option with a balance-sheet-funded ~7% carry; Q3 lengthened the bridge and reminded holders how volatile the carry's source is.
