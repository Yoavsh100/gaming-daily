SEPTEMBER 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Arizona

Released 19 Nov 2026 (ADG release; press coverage 20 Nov 2026) · Currency USD (market reports in USD; tables in $ millions unless stated) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Arizona's sixth NFL September delivered a handle of $836.7m (-1.7% YoY) but a far better revenue print: gross receipts +18.5% to $65.2m on a 7.8% hold, promo deductions down to 49% of gross, taxable receipts +68% to $33.0m and state fees +70% to $3.3m. The market is a 99.7%-mobile, FanDuel/DraftKings duopoly (61.5% of handle) operating under one of the lowest effective tax regimes in the US (~5% of gross) — which is precisely why the Governor's 45% tier for ≥$75m-handle operators and the criminal case against Kalshi make the next 12 months a regulatory, not a demand, story.

1Executive summary

  • Handle slipped for the second straight month into the NFL season — the Arizona Department of Gaming (ADG) reports September 2026 event-wagering handle of $836.7m, -1.7% YoY versus $851.3m in September 2025, though +43.6% MoM from $582.8m in August (itself -4.6% YoY). With April -1.2%, February +8.7% and June +22.3%, Arizona is oscillating around flat in year six — a maturing market rather than a growth one.
  • Revenue went the other way: gross receipts +18.5% — payouts of $769.5m left adjusted gross receipts (before promotional deductions) of $65.2m, a 7.8% hold versus $55.0m / 6.5% a year earlier, when Arizona posted the lowest collective win rate (6.7%) of 32 reporting states. Hold normalisation, not volume, drove the print.
  • Promo deductions fell and the taxable base jumped 68% — operators deducted $32.2m in free bets/promotional credits (49% of gross; 3.9% of handle) against $35.4m (64% of gross) in September 2025, leaving $33.0m subject to privilege fees versus $19.6m, which had been the lowest monthly taxable total since July 2022.
  • State take $3.3m (+70% YoY) but still only ~5% of gross — the 10% mobile / 8% retail privilege fee is levied on the post-promo base, so the effective rate is 5.1% of gross receipts and 0.39% of handle. FY2027 year-to-date (Jul–Sep) fees are $14.8m from event wagering plus $349,935 from fantasy sports ($15.2m combined).
  • Mobile is 99.7% of the market — $833.9m online against $2.8m retail (retail -36% YoY from $4.4m; mobile -1.5%). Retail sportsbooks at tribal casinos and stadium districts are marketing assets, not revenue lines; a new entrant needs no physical footprint beyond what its licence partner already owns.
  • A duopoly with a shrinking third — FanDuel led mobile handle at $270.8m (32.5% share, +6.4% YoY), DraftKings $242.0m (29.0%, -9.8% vs $268.2m), BetMGM $77.3m (9.3%, -21.0% vs $97.8m). Top-2 = 61.5%, top-3 = 70.8%; the remaining ~$244m (29.2%, +7.6% YoY) is spread across the long tail (Caesars, Fanatics, bet365, Hard Rock, BetRivers, Bally Bet, theScore Bet, Golden Nugget, TwinSpires, tribal brands). The tail is the only part of the market gaining share.
  • Fantasy contests are a $13.6m-entry-fee niche taxed at 5% — adjusted revenue $2.2m (16.2% take), fees $111,221; PrizePicks $1.12m (~51% of adjusted revenue) and DraftKings $544k. Entry fees +66% MoM on the NFL start. Pick'em-style products are licensed and regulated here, unlike in several other states.
  • Lifetime: ~$38bn wagered, $205.4m in privilege fees since the 9 Sept 2021 launch — a cumulative yield of 0.54% of handle, which is the number Arizona's Governor is now explicitly targeting as too low.
  • Legal framework — HB 2772 (signed April 2021) and the concurrently amended Tribal-State Gaming Compacts created A.R.S. Title 5 event-wagering (§5-1301 et seq.) and fantasy-sports (§5-1201 et seq.) regimes with ADG as sole licensing, rules and enforcement body; the compacts also expanded tribal Class III casino offerings in exchange for tribal participation in sports betting.
  • Licensing is capped and access-gated — statute reserves up to 10 event-wagering operator licences for tribes with compacts and up to 10 for professional sports franchises/organisations (Cardinals–BetMGM, Suns–FanDuel, Diamondbacks–Caesars, TPC Scottsdale/PGA Tour–DraftKings, Phoenix Raceway–Penn, among others), each carrying one online brand plus retail at the venue or on tribal land, with up to 10 'limited' retail licences for racetracks/OTBs. No licence exists without a tribal or franchise partner. A new operator licence was issued in early August 2026 via the San Juan Southern Paiute Tribe (launch targeted Q1 2027; operator identity not confirmed in sources read) and ADG approved nine additional in-person wagering locations in September 2026 (press reports; not read in full).
  • Licence fees [schedule from ADG rules as recalled, not re-verified this session] — application fee $100,000, initial operator licence fee $750,000, annual renewal $150,000; management-services-provider and supplier licences carry separate, lower fees. Treat as ~$1m upfront plus partner revenue share.
  • Tax regime: 10%/8% on a promo-shielded base, with a 45% tier on the table — privilege fee of 10% (mobile) / 8% (retail) of adjusted gross event-wagering receipts after winnings, federal excise (0.25% of handle) and free-bet/promo deductions capped as a sliding share of handle in the operator's first five years [cap schedule not re-verified]. Governor Hobbs' FY2027 executive budget (January 2026) proposed 45% for 'large operators' with ≥$75m monthly handle; the measure was not enacted and iGB confirms all books still pay 10%, the fifth-lowest rate in the US. Expect a reprise in the January 2027 session.
  • Enforcement has gone criminal on prediction markets — Arizona filed 20 criminal counts against Kalshi in 2026 alleging unlawful acceptance of wagers on political contests, college sports and individual player performances. The outcome (and the parallel federal CFTC-preemption litigation) will determine whether exchange-listed event contracts can keep siphoning NFL-season volume from licensed books — a plausible contributor to Arizona's flat handle.
  • Responsible gambling and product limits — minimum age 21, statewide ADG self-exclusion list covering event wagering and fantasy, 1-800-NEXT-STEP helpline, operator-funded problem-gambling obligations under HB 2772; individual collegiate-athlete props and political events sit outside the permitted catalogue, while in-play, props, pools and exchange wagering are allowed per ADG's Catalog of Approved Wagers.
  • Payments are standard US-digital — account-based, KYC'd wagering with in-state geolocation; funding via ACH/online banking, debit (and, by issuer discretion, credit) cards, PayPal/Venmo/Apple Pay, prepaid Play+ and cash at tribal casino cages; operators may not extend credit to patrons [statutory detail not re-verified]. No mobile-money or withholding-tax friction of the kind seen in African markets.
  • No iGaming and no commercial casinos — tribal exclusivity for casino-style gaming under the 2021 compacts (16 tribes, ~26 casinos, approximate) makes online casino politically unavailable without tribal consent; the Arizona Lottery is a separate state agency. Sports betting and DFS are the only commercially accessible verticals.
  • Data caveat — ADG monthly figures are operator self-reported and unaudited; fee totals are on a collections basis so FY-to-date ($14.8m) and lifetime ($205.4m vs $202.5m after August) totals do not reconcile exactly to monthly sums. The ADG FY2026 Annual Report (published October 2026) could not be opened this session.
  • Read-through for B2Tech — entry requires a tribal/franchise partner and ~$1m upfront; the economics are a 7–8% hold, promo intensity of ~50% of gross, a top-2 share above 60%, flat handle and a credible tax cliff at $75m monthly handle. A sub-scale B2C entrant pays 10% today and would stay sub-threshold under the Hobbs tier — a perverse advantage — but share gains have to come from the ~29% long tail. A B2B platform/supplier route (licensed management-services provider to a tribal or franchise licensee) carries the lower regulatory and tax beta.

3Deep-dive: Online sports betting (mobile event wagering)

99.7% of a flat $837m September handle; hold recovery to 7.8% and lighter promos lifted taxable receipts 68% — FanDuel extended its lead while DraftKings and BetMGM lost share.

Mobile handle

Mobile handle of $833.9m in September 2026 was 1.5% below September 2025's $847.0m, a market at maturity with probable leakage to event contracts.

  • Handle was 43.5% above August's $581m, following normal NFL/college football seasonality.
  • Decline is notable because September 2025 had grown 16.3%; negative YoY months were August (-4.6%), September (-1.7%), April (-1.2%); positive February +8.7%, June +22.3%.
  • No new large-brand launches in the period; some probable leakage to CFTC-listed sports event contracts.
  • Arizona remains a top-5/7 US state: September 2025's $851m ranked fifth behind New York, Illinois, New Jersey and Ohio.

Gross receipts and hold

Gross receipts before promotional deductions rose 18.5% to $65.2m on a 7.8% hold versus 6.5% a year earlier.

  • September 2025's 6.7% collective win rate was the lowest of 32 reporting states.
  • August 2026 derived hold was 8.7% ($582.8m handle less $532m payouts).
  • Structural hold trending up as parlay/SGP mix and pricing discipline improve; 7.5–8.5% band is base case for NFL months.
  • Favourite-heavy NFL weeks drive month-to-month swings of ±1.5pp.

Promotional deductions

Promotional deductions of $32.2m (49.4% of gross, 3.9% of handle) halve the taxable base to $33.0m, making the 10% rate effectively 5.1% of gross.

  • Down from $35.4m (64.4% of gross) a year earlier; up sharply from derived ~$14.8m (29% of gross) in August as operators front-loaded NFL acquisition spend.
  • In September 2025 bet365 ($60.3m handle, 4.8% hold) and Fanatics ($73.1m handle, 3.4% win rate) reported zero adjusted revenue; ADG caps deductions at 100% of gross.
  • Headline 10% rate converts to 5.1% of gross and 0.39% of handle.
  • For a new entrant this is the single most valuable feature of the regime: acquisition spend is effectively tax-deductible in launch years.

Operator shares

FanDuel led at $270.8m (32.5% share, +6.4% YoY) while DraftKings ($242.0m, -9.8%) and BetMGM ($77.3m, -21.0%) lost share.

  • FanDuel grew from $254.5m; in September 2025 it also led adjusted revenue with $8.5m on a 3.34% net hold.
  • DraftKings 29.0% share, down from $268.2m per InGame (iGB cited $270.2m — minor source discrepancy), surrendering the handle lead.
  • BetMGM share fell to 9.3% from 11.5%.
  • Residual ~$243.8m (29.2%, +7.6% YoY) spread across Caesars, Fanatics, bet365, Hard Rock Bet, BetRivers, Bally Bet, theScore Bet, Golden Nugget, TwinSpires and tribal brands; none posted six-figure adjusted revenue in September 2025.
  • Challenger share gains must come from the long tail or BetMGM's eroding third position; the duopoly's combined 61.5% share is stable-to-rising.

Approved wagers and controls

ADG's catalogue offers product breadth comparable to New Jersey, including exchange wagering, but excludes collegiate-athlete props and political events.

  • Permits single-game, parlays, teasers, props, pools, in-play/in-game, over-unders, money lines and exchange wagering.
  • Exclusion of individual collegiate-athlete props and political events matters for the Kalshi case.
  • Operators must geolocate to Arizona, verify age 21+, use ADG-approved KYC; statewide self-exclusion applies across all licensed apps.

Forward view

Estimates: handle flat to +3%, gross receipts +10–15%, taxable receipts +20–30% over twelve months; the swing factor is regulatory.

  • Handle flat to +3% absent a new large brand; gross receipts up on hold normalisation; taxable receipts up as launch-year promo caps age out and acquisition spend is cut.
  • A 45% tier on the three ≥$75m-handle operators would lift state fees from $3.3m to an illustrative ~$11.5m a month (estimate, assuming top-3 generate ~71% of taxable receipts).
  • The tier would push those operators to cut promos and worsen pricing, which historically transfers share to sub-threshold competitors.

4Deep-dive: Retail event wagering, tribal casinos and fantasy contests

Retail is a $2.8m rounding error (0.3% of handle, -36% YoY) attached to tribal exclusivity; fantasy is a $13.6m-entry-fee niche taxed at 5% where PrizePicks holds half the revenue.

Retail handle

Retail handle was $2.8m in September 2026, 0.33% of total and down 36.4% YoY, functioning mainly as brand presence for paired apps.

  • Year-earlier $4.4m included DraftKings' TPC Scottsdale book ($3.0m) and Caesars' American Wagering ($810,080); up 75% from August's $1.6m.
  • Taxed at 8% versus 10% for mobile, but immaterial: only $79,416 of $1.94m in September 2025 fees came from retail.
  • Retail books sit at tribal casinos (Desert Diamond, Gila River, Casino Arizona/Talking Stick, Harrah's Ak-Chin) and franchise venues (State Farm Stadium, Footprint Center, Chase Field, TPC Scottsdale, Phoenix Raceway).
  • ADG approved nine additional in-person locations in September 2026 (articles not read in full), consistent with the limited retail licence class capped at 10 statewide.

Tribal compact

Tribal exclusivity over Class III gaming, locked in by the April 2021 compact amendments, means online casino is not legislatable without tribal consent.

  • Arizona has no commercial casinos; 16 tribes operate roughly 26 casinos (approximate, not re-verified this session).
  • Tribes accepted off-reservation commercial sports betting via franchise licences in exchange for more slots, craps, roulette, baccarat and ten event-wagering licences, mostly leased to national brands.
  • Tribal contributions to the Arizona Benefits Fund are reported separately and excluded from event-wagering fee figures (FY figures n/a this session).
  • Tribes have shown no appetite to cede exclusivity.

Fantasy contests

Fantasy entry fees were $13.6m in September 2026 (+66% MoM) with 5% fee on $2.2m adjusted revenue; PrizePicks holds ~51%.

  • Adjusted revenue $2.2m (16.2% take, +38% MoM); fees $111,221 (5.06% effective); entry fees up from $8.2m.
  • PrizePicks $1.12m adjusted revenue (~51%), DraftKings $544k (~25%); remainder Underdog, Sleeper, Betr and smaller operators.
  • FY2027 YTD fantasy fees $349,935 versus $14.8m from event wagering; fantasy is roughly 2.3% of combined take.
  • Licensing pick'em products as fantasy is a competitive overlap: PrizePicks' $1.12m exceeds any sportsbook outside the top two in September 2025.

B2Tech read-through

Retail and DFS are adjacencies, not entry points, but a fantasy licence is the cheapest regulated presence available in Arizona.

  • Retail licence derives from the operator licence or a limited racetrack licence with no online skin; DFS licence confers no sports-betting rights.
  • The 5% fee and absence of the tribal/franchise gate make fantasy a potential low-cost brand-building beachhead ahead of a sportsbook partnership.

5Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime

A capped, partner-gated licence structure (10 tribal + 10 franchise + 10 limited retail) and a 10%/8% fee on a promo-shielded base — effective ~5% of gross — now facing a proposed 45% tier for operators above $75m monthly handle.

Legal framework

HB 2772 (signed 15 April 2021) caps operator licences at 20 (10 tribal, 10 franchise) plus 10 limited retail, with ADG as sole regulator.

  • Codified at A.R.S. Title 5 (event wagering §5-1301 et seq.; fantasy §5-1201 et seq.); market launched 9 September 2021.
  • ADG also regulates tribal compliance, racing/pari-mutuel, unarmed combat sports and houses the Division of Problem Gambling.
  • Each operator licence supports one online brand plus retail at the tribal casino or franchise venue; franchises include NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, PGA Tour, NASCAR.
  • Management services providers, suppliers and key employees are separately licensed.

Fees and partner access

State fees are modest ($750,000 initial licence); the real cost is partner economics, and licence exhaustion makes entrants dependent on partner swaps.

  • $100,000 application fee, $750,000 initial operator fee, $150,000 annual renewal (as recalled, not re-verified); provider and supplier fees materially lower.
  • Tribes and franchises typically extract an upfront payment plus revenue share.
  • Entry requires a partner swapping brands (Coyotes/SaharaBets, Phoenix Rising/Fubo have cycled) or a tribe with an unactivated licence.
  • August 2026 licence with the San Juan Southern Paiute Tribe, launch targeted Q1 2027, shows the route remains open; operator identity unconfirmed.

Privilege fee

The 10% mobile / 8% retail fee applies to a promo-shielded base, giving an effective 5.1% of gross in September 2026 and 3.5% in September 2025.

  • Adjusted receipts equal handle minus winnings, federal excise tax (0.25% of handle) and free bets/promotional credits.
  • Promo deduction capped on a sliding scale over five years (20% → 15% → 10% → 5%, as recalled, not re-verified) and cannot push monthly receipts below zero.
  • Promo deductions ran 29–64% of gross in the past year; fantasy operators pay 5% of adjusted revenue.
  • Fees go to the event wagering fund, ADG costs first, balance to the general fund (not re-verified); lifetime fees $205.4m on ~$38bn handle equal 0.54% yield.

Proposed 45% tier

Governor Hobbs' proposed 45% rate on operators handling ≥$75m a month was not enacted; a 15–25% compromise on all operators looks more probable.

  • Operators still pay 10%, the fifth-lowest US rate per iGB; the FY2027 budget did not enact the tier.
  • On September 2026 figures it would capture FanDuel ($270.8m), DraftKings ($242.0m) and BetMGM ($77.3m, marginally above the line), sparing Caesars, Fanatics, bet365 and the tail.
  • Handle-based threshold creates a cliff-edge incentivising BetMGM and growing challengers to cap volume; carve-out favours sub-threshold brands, unlike Illinois, Maryland, New Jersey, Louisiana hikes.
  • FY2028 budget cycle (January 2027 session) makes a reprise likely; compromise or narrowed promo deduction more probable than 45% as drafted (judgement, not sourced).

B2Tech routes

No direct B2C path exists without a tribal or franchise partner; B2B supply is the route insulated from the tax debate.

  • Routes: (i) management-services agreement with a tribe holding an unactivated or lapsed licence, (ii) acquiring an exiting brand's partnership, (iii) B2B supply requiring only a supplier licence.
  • Routes (i)–(ii) must be underwritten at a 20%+ tax rate, not 10%.

6Deep-dive: Enforcement, prediction markets, responsible gambling & payments

Arizona has moved from cease-and-desist to 20 criminal counts against Kalshi; RG rules are mainstream-US (21+, statewide self-exclusion, 1-800-NEXT-STEP) and payments are frictionless — the regulatory risk is political, not operational.

Kalshi prosecution

Arizona's 20 criminal counts against Kalshi are the sharpest test yet of whether federal law pre-empts state gambling law for event contracts.

  • Counts allege unlawful wagers on political contests, college sports and individual player performances, all outside Arizona's catalogue; most states used civil cease-and-desist.
  • September handle softness of -1.7% during NFL and college seasons is consistent with (not proof of) migration to zero-tax, zero-licence venues.
  • If Arizona prevails, licensed books regain the protection their $750k fee was meant to buy; if pre-emption is upheld, the 45% debate becomes academic as the base erodes.

Conventional enforcement

Beyond prediction markets, ADG polices licensed compliance, offshore and sweepstakes products, and HB 2772 integrity obligations.

  • Covers advertising, self-exclusion, wager catalogue adherence, illegal offshore market and unlicensed skill/sweepstakes products.
  • Integrity rules: official-league data for in-play tier-2 wagers, suspicious activity reporting, wagering bans on athletes, officials and insiders.
  • In-state collegiate player props and political/non-sporting events are prohibited, which is why the Kalshi counts cite those categories.

Responsible gambling

RG rules are mainstream-US (21+, statewide self-exclusion, 1-800-NEXT-STEP) with promotions largely unconstrained, keeping promo intensity at ~50% of gross.

  • Providers must fund and display problem-gambling resources, honour the ADG self-exclusion list across event wagering and fantasy, and offer deposit/time/wager limits.
  • Advertising must not target under-21s or self-excluded persons and must carry RG messaging.
  • No affordability-check or bonus-restriction regimes as in Great Britain or the Netherlands.
  • No state restriction on credit-card funding (unlike Massachusetts, Tennessee, Iowa), though issuers often block the merchant code; operators may not extend credit [not re-verified].

Payments

Payments are a commodity US-digital stack with no structural friction; the differentiator is geolocation/KYC cost and chargeback management.

  • Deposits via ACH/online banking (Trustly/Plaid), debit card, PayPal, Venmo, Apple Pay, Play+ prepaid, PayNearMe vouchers and casino cage cash; withdrawals mainly ACH, PayPal, Play+.
  • Account-based wagering with ADG-approved KYC and continuous geolocation; no state-level withholding on winnings (federal W-2G thresholds apply).
  • Contrasts with the mobile-money-dominated African markets B2Tech operates in.

Direction of travel

Fiscal pressure and hostility to unlicensed prediction markets are rising, while compact-locked market structure is not moving.

7Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Lowest-cost tax regime among large US states: 10% mobile / 8% retail on a base that deducts winnings, federal excise and capped free bets — effective 5.1% of gross receipts and 0.39% of handle in September 2026. Launch-year acquisition spend is effectively tax-deductible. structural
  • Scale and digital purity: $836.7m September handle (top-5/7 US state), ~$7.7bn calendar-2025 handle (secondary source), 99.7% mobile — no retail capex, no cage logistics, standard US payment rails. structural
  • Contestable long tail: 29.2% of mobile handle (~$244m/month) sits outside the top three and grew +7.6% YoY while BetMGM lost 21% — the #3 slot and the tail are where a challenger can take share. mixed
  • Hold is normalising upward: 7.8% gross hold vs 6.5% a year earlier and the lowest-in-nation 6.7% of September 2025; taxable receipts +68% YoY on lighter promos — margin structure improving for incumbents and entrants alike. early
  • Licences are still being issued: a new tribal-partner operator licence (San Juan Southern Paiute Tribe, August 2026, launch Q1 2027) and nine new in-person wagering sites approved in September 2026 demonstrate that the partner route remains open. one-off
  • Regulated fantasy/pick'em at a 5% fee: a licensed, low-tax adjacency without the tribal/franchise gate — PrizePicks' $1.12m of September adjusted revenue exceeded every sportsbook outside the top two in September 2025. structural
  • Broad product catalogue including exchange wagering, in-play and props — product differentiation (not just price) is available as a competitive lever. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • Tax-hike overhang: the Governor's proposed 45% tier for operators with ≥$75m monthly handle was not enacted in FY2027 but is likely to resurface in the January 2027 session; any underwriting must assume ≥20% and a possible narrowing of promo deductions. structural
  • Handle has stalled: -1.7% in September, -4.6% in August, -1.2% in April against a national market still growing — maturity plus probable leakage to unlicensed prediction markets. Growth must come from share, not market expansion. watch
  • Access is gated and exhausted: 20 operator licences tied to tribes and franchises, all effectively allocated; entry requires a partner swap or an unactivated tribal licence, plus partner revenue share on top of ~$1m in state fees. structural
  • Duopoly economics: FanDuel + DraftKings hold 61.5% of handle and, in September 2025, essentially all of the market's six-figure adjusted revenue; sub-scale brands (bet365, Fanatics) reported zero taxable revenue after promos — the tail is share-rich but profit-poor. structural
  • Promo intensity: deductions of 49–64% of gross over the past year mean customer acquisition costs are brutal; the sliding promo-deduction cap also steps down with licence age, so the tax shield fades precisely as a new entrant scales. structural
  • Legal uncertainty on prediction markets: if federal pre-emption is upheld in the Kalshi litigation, licensed operators face a zero-tax, zero-licence competitor in exactly the NFL/college volume that drives Arizona's season. watch
  • No iGaming and no path to it: tribal Class III exclusivity is compact-locked; the only commercially accessible verticals are sports betting and DFS. Data is self-reported/unaudited and FY fee totals do not reconcile to monthly sums. governance

8Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The regulatory bridge from a year ago is a shift from 'low-tax, promo-friendly, volume-growing' to 'low-tax-for-now, promo-moderating, volume-flat, enforcement-hardening'. The taxable base moved from $19.6m (Sept 2025) to $33.0m (Sept 2026) almost entirely on hold and promo mix rather than handle, and the state's take rose 70% without any rate change — which cuts both ways: it strengthens the Governor's argument that the base can bear more tax, and it weakens the operators' argument that Arizona is already yielding its fair share. The Kalshi criminal case and the Hobbs 45% tier are the two regulatory events that determine whether 2027 is a repricing year.

Independent view. Our independent view (estimates, not sourced): handle flat to +3% over the next twelve months absent a new large-brand launch, with the Q1 2027 tribal-partner entrant adding at most low-single-digit share; gross hold settles in a 7.5–8.5% band, lifting gross receipts +10–15%; promo deductions drift toward 35–45% of gross as the market ages out of launch-year caps and operators pre-emptively cut spend under tax-hike threat, so taxable receipts grow +20–30% and FY2027 event-wagering fees land at roughly $50–60m at the current 10% rate (Q1 FY27 collections of $14.8m annualise to ~$59m but include NFL-season months). Regulatory leverage is rising, not falling: we assign a meaningful probability that the January 2027 session produces a rate increase — more likely a uniform 15–25% or a tightened promo deduction than the 45% tier as drafted — and a lower but non-trivial probability of no change for another year. The cliff-edge design of the $75m-handle threshold would, if enacted, hand sub-scale brands a structural tax advantage; for B2Tech that argues for a B2B/management-services or fantasy-licence entry first, with B2C underwritten at ≥20% tax and a 7.5% hold, and for treating the Kalshi ruling as the gating event for any capital commitment.
