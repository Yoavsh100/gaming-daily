A record Q3 top line (€83.1m / ~$91.2m, +6% organic) and a 39% North American EBITDA margin are genuine, but they were earned while absorbing a €6.5m (~$7.1m) sports-win-margin shortfall, a one-third collapse in Europe & RoW EBITDA, and a Brazilian ban that removes ~€30m (~$33m) of annual revenue from Q4 onward with no 2027 framework to replace it. 3Segment deep-dive: North America

Revenue +28% and EBITDA tripled to a 39% margin on sponsorship, prediction-market CPA and maturing revenue-share cohorts — now 47% of group EBITDA, but Q4-25's derived 56% margin is the comp to beat.

Revenue growth

North American revenue rose 28% to €26.6m (~$29.2m), up 10% sequentially and 24% year-to-date to €76.7m (~$84.1m).

  • Trailing-twelve-month revenue is €108m (~$118m), up 19% reported and 26% in constant currency after a ~€5.5m FX translation headwind from the weaker dollar.
  • Sponsorship more than doubled to €9.2m (34% of regional revenue versus 21%) on Playmaker HQ, the Roommates Show and the new Yahoo Sports distribution partnership.
  • CPA rose 39% to €5.5m on prediction-market partners whose deals are structured on upfront CPA terms; CPM grew 28% to €2.9m.
  • Revenue share fell 18% to €5.8m as prior-year hybrid agreements with upfront CPA components converted to pure revenue share.
  • Subscriptions slipped 9% to €2.9m, though management flagged a strong September rebound in Action Network's products with the NFL season start.

EBITDA and costs

EBITDA before special items reached €10.3m (~$11.4m) versus €3.3m, lifting the margin to 38.9% from 16.0%, a 2,290bps expansion.

  • Margin was 25.9% in Q2 and roughly 31% in Q1 (derived).
  • Regional costs fell 7% to €16.2m, or 61% of revenue versus 84%, on maturing revenue-share cohorts, prediction-market and Playbook contributions, and premium distribution leverage.
  • Reconciliation wrinkle for IR: Publishing reports costs up 16% 'driven entirely by higher direct costs associated with the expansion of North American activities', yet NA segment costs fell 7%.
  • Either allocations differ materially or talent-led content costs are partly capitalised (9M intangible additions €32.6m versus €1.8m), flattering the 39% EBITDA margin relative to EBITA.

Volumes and products

CPA-based NDC share rose on North American prediction-market demand, with NA CPA rising every quarter this year, though we see the stream as still CPA-weighted.

  • NA CPA: Q1 €5.2m, Q2 €5.1m, Q3 €5.5m (derived); volume disclosure is not regionalised.
  • Playbook won an Affiliate Product Innovation award and integrated Kalshi and then Novig.
  • Prediction Pro launched 19 October (after quarter-end) with Kalshi and Polymarket pricing, forecasting tools and a 2026 Midterms hub, standalone or as an Action Pro add-on.
  • The Roommates Show's outdoor live podcast sold more than 10,000 tickets in New York.
  • CPA is less recurring than revenue share and more exposed to partner marketing budgets and state-level pushback on sports event contracts.

Q4 outlook

No regional guidance, but Q4-25 delivered €31.3m revenue and €17.5m EBITDA bsi (56% margin, derived), so 39% is neither ceiling nor peak comp.

  • North America is now 32% of group revenue and 47% of EBITDA bsi; the group result is effectively this region offsetting Europe.
  • Swing factors: NFL-season subscription and revenue-share conversion, direct-sold sponsorship substituting for programmatic at higher yield, and prediction-market CPA pricing as operator competition intensifies.
  • FX: EUR/USD around 1.10 versus ~1.16 a year ago continues to cost translation growth.

4Segment deep-dive: Europe & Rest of World

Flat revenue, EBITDA down a third and margin -970bps to 20% as UK duty, Brazil and a €6.5m sports-margin shortfall meet +12% cost growth; the Brazil hole opens fully in Q4.

Revenue mix

Revenue fell 1.5% to €56.5m (~$62.0m), down 13% sequentially and only 0.7% higher year-to-date at €181.9m (~$199.6m).

  • Region is now 68% of group revenue versus 73% a year ago.
  • Revenue share fell 5% to €29.7m, absorbing the €6.5m sports-win-margin shortfall, ~€2m UK Remote Gaming Duty effect (21% to 40% from 1 April) and ~€1m Brazil impact.
  • CPA edged up 2% to €14.9m; sponsorship rose 29% to €6.7m on HLTV's Esports World Cup traffic and European talent-led formats.
  • Subscriptions doubled to €1.5m on Mindway AI; CPM collapsed 35% to €3.5m as FUTBIN monetisation softened ahead of EA FC 27 and direct sales cannibalised programmatic.

Profitability

EBITDA before special items fell 33% to €11.6m (~$12.7m), taking the margin to 20.4% from 30.1% a year ago and 31.9% in Q2.

  • Regional costs rose 12% to €45.0m, 80% of revenue versus 70%, reflecting the group's €4m increase in direct costs around the World Cup, talent-led media and Paid Media.
  • Of the €5.7m EBITDA decline, roughly €3m is UK duty and Brazil; the balance is cost growth against a flat top line.
  • Sports margin was neutral year-on-year but, had it been normal, the regional margin would have been closer to 29%.
  • Structural damage from tax and regulation is running at €3-4m a quarter, with luck doubling the optical hit this quarter.

Brazil

Brazil revenue is now expected at ~€30m (~$32.9m) for 2026 versus ~€45m planned, with ~€15m (~$16.5m) disappearing in Q4 after the betting ban.

  • Revenue peaked above €70m in 2024 and fell to €49m in 2025 after the 1 January regulation and new tax regime.
  • Provisional Measure 1,394/2026 of 25 September banned fixed-odds betting, online casino and related marketing; operators required to cease by 6 October. Q3 impact ~€1m plus softer NDC intake.
  • Management plans on zero Brazilian betting and casino revenue, has started cost adjustments, and declined to quantify the residual cost base (Nordea's analyst referenced ~€10m).
  • Brazil-related domains and customer accounts carry €50-70m of value to be impaired if the MP becomes law; Congress granted a 60-day extension on 9 November, deliberation deadline 4 March 2027.
  • The CEO's letter is unusually blunt in calling the ban counter-productive.

UK duty

The UK is the second regulatory drag, with operators passing the duty increase through via tighter CPA and revenue-share terms.

  • Paid Media's UK casino book is the most exposed.

Disclosure and Q4 setup

Thin regional disclosure hides whether the ex-Brazil European business is growing at all, and Q4-26 must absorb the full Brazil loss against a 31% Q4-25 comp.

  • No split of UK & Ireland, the Nordics, Southern Europe or LatAm ex-Brazil, a material gap given regulatory flux.

5Segment deep-dive: Business-model segments: Publishing, Paid Media, Esports

Publishing (+16%) carries the group; Paid Media (-12% revenue, -31% EBITDA) is where UK tax and Brazil bite; Esports' 73% margin is event-timing.

Publishing

Publishing grew revenue 16% to €53.9m (~$59.1m), 65% of the group, with EBITDA bsi up 19% to €13.4m (~$14.7m) at a 24.8% margin.

  • Organic growth 16% against -11% a year ago; sponsorship rose 79% to €12.3m; CPA nearly doubled to €7.0m on prediction-market acquisition.
  • Subscriptions rose 12% to €4.4m; revenue share slipped 5% to €24.3m on hybrid conversion; CPM fell 12% to €5.4m as direct sales displaced programmatic.
  • Costs rose 16% to €40.5m, attributed entirely to direct costs of North American expansion; most group support-function costs are booked here.
  • Margin up 50bps year-on-year but down from 29.3% in Q2 and well short of the 32% FY25 margin, mostly due to the sports-margin shortfall.
  • Publishing contributed 61% of group EBITDA bsi versus 54%.

Paid Media

Paid Media revenue fell 12% to €24.2m (~$26.6m) and EBITDA bsi fell 31% to €4.9m (~$5.3m), with margin down 560bps to 20.1%.

  • Segment is 29% of group versus 35%; CPA down 10% to €13.4m, revenue share down 15% to €10.8m; runs at ~80% cost-to-revenue.
  • Management cites the UK casino taxation increase, Brazil's late-quarter developments and the sports margin, which hurts Paid Media's revenue-share book.
  • Costs cut 6% to €19.4m through campaign ROI discipline; Q2 margin was 26.2%; year-to-date revenue +1% and EBITDA -3%.
  • Most exposed segment to the Brazil marketing ban and UK operator term renegotiation; clearest evidence the legacy affiliate model is under structural price pressure.

Esports

Esports revenue grew 14% to €5.0m (~$5.5m) and EBITDA bsi jumped 58% to €3.7m (~$4.0m) at a 73% margin driven by event-cost timing.

  • Sponsorship rose 32% to €3.5m on HLTV's record Esports World Cup traffic; revenue share doubled to €0.5m; CPM fell 33% to €1.0m on FUTBIN headwinds ahead of EA FC 27.
  • Costs dropped 36% to €1.3m on Counter-Strike event promotional phasing; margin was 53% a year ago and 63% in Q2.
  • Year-to-date margin is an unchanged 66%, the better guide; Q4 will carry the deferred event costs.
  • Esports is 17% of group EBITDA bsi on 6% of revenue; FC 27 partnership and HLTV Brain launch are real engagement assets.

Mix shift

Recurring revenue fell to 56% of the total from 64%, while CPA and sponsorship rose to 43% from 36%; we see the annuity shrinking.

  • Management frames this as diversification and improving advertising yield.
  • Revenue share fell 8%, and Brazil will cut it further, while direct-sold advertising, contract-based, cyclical and budget-sensitive, fills the gap.

Reporting points

The FY26 annual report will merge sponsorship and CPM into one 'Advertising' line, making the programmatic decline invisible; EBITA and EBIT bsi give a fuller read.

  • 9M intangible additions were €32.6m (versus €1.8m) as media-partnership and development projects are capitalised.
  • Resulting amortisation (€11.6m in Q3, +54%) sits below EBITDA bsi; EBITA €20.4m (+7%) and EBIT bsi €8.7m (-24%) better reflect talent-led media economics than the 26% EBITDA margin.

7Forecast & outlook

Guidance bridge. FY26 guidance is unchanged from the 27 September revision (regulatory release 85/2026), which cut organic revenue growth to 3-8% from 7-12% and EBITDA bsi growth to -7%/+3% from +8%/+18% (both constant currency), withdrew the 2027-28 targets (which had included a 35-40% EBITDA bsi margin ambition for 2027) and discontinued the €40m FY26 buyback after €23m. The entire downgrade is Brazil — i.e. Europe & RoW, split across Publishing and Paid Media — with FY26 Brazil revenue now ~€30m (~$32.9m) versus ~€45m and a ~€15m hit concentrated in Q4; the UK duty rise (~€2m/quarter) was already in the original plan. The net-debt/EBITDA target of <3x is retained. With 9M organic growth at 8% cc and EBITDA bsi +9% cc, the guidance implies Q4 revenue of €85-102m versus €94.3m in Q4-25 and Q4 EBITDA bsi of €24-34m versus €36.9m — a year-on-year profit decline in every scenario, which is what the equity is reacting to.

Independent view. Q4-26E (our estimate): revenue ~€90-93m (~$99-102m), -1% to -4% YoY, as the ~€15m Brazil loss is partly offset by NFL-driven North America, Esports deferred activity and some sports-margin normalisation; EBITDA bsi ~€27-30m (~$30-33m), a 29-32% margin versus 39% in Q4-25, assuming Brazil drops through at ~70% before cost actions. That lands FY26E revenue ~€349-352m (+4-5% organic cc) and EBITDA bsi ~€101-104m (-1% to +2%), ~29% margin — low-to-mid guidance on revenue, upper half on EBITDA given 9M is already €74m. 2027E: the arithmetic starts with a ~€30m revenue hole (~9% of the base) at 60-70% drop-through (-€18-21m EBITDA pre-mitigation), offset by removal of a ~€10m Brazil cost base and by North America compounding at 15-20% on a €108m TTM base (+€16-22m revenue at ~40% incremental margin, +€6-9m EBITDA); net, we see 2027E EBITDA bsi roughly flat to -10% versus FY26E (~€90-105m) before any sports-margin normalisation (worth +€6-7m if Q3-type shortfalls do not recur) and before any Brazil reopening option. Leverage: with buybacks halted and FCF (our definition) of ~€35-40m a year, NIBD should drift to ~€230-240m by end-2027, keeping NIBD/EBITDA at ~2.2-2.6x on that EBITDA range; the 3x ceiling is only threatened if EBITDA bsi falls below ~€80m. The €50-70m impairment is non-cash, outside the EBITDA-based metric, and would cut equity to ~€570-590m. Margin direction is down into 2027 at group level (Europe & RoW drag) but up in North America; the stock debate is whether NA's 39% is a run-rate or a seasonal/capitalisation-assisted peak — Q4-25's derived 56% NA margin suggests Q4 can be strong, but Q1-27 will be the first clean read.
