Q1 FY2027 · results · analyst report

The Star Entertainment Group

SGR.AX · Released 27 Oct 2026 · Currency AUD (A$), tables in millions; ~USD equivalents at AUD/USD 0.702 (27 Oct 2026) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Shares +13.0% to A$0.13 (~$0.09) on 27 Oct 2026 from a A$0.115 prior close, having traded as high as A$0.138 (+20%) intraday; the stock had fallen >10% on 25 Sep when the NICC extended the Sydney licence suspension, and rallied again on 29 Oct after the A$260m AUSTRAC penalty landed in line with provisions.

The one-line verdict

A genuine operational inflection: first positive group EBITDA (A$12m / ~$8m) since quarterly reporting began and the first YoY revenue growth at the two owned properties, delivered by EGM-led growth at Gold Coast and Sydney plus a 29% cut in unallocated corporate costs. But the equity remains hostage to a still-suspended Sydney licence, a Gold Coast suspension deferral expiring 31 Mar 2027, a A$260m (~$183m) AUSTRAC penalty payable over 19 months against A$239m (~$168m) of cash, and a going-concern uncertainty the company itself re-flagged. 1Executive summary

  • Back in the black at EBITDA level. Q1 FY27 (3 months to 30 Sep 2026) group EBITDA before significant items of A$12m (~$8m) vs a A$13m loss in the pcp and a A$9m loss in Q4 FY26 — a A$25m YoY swing and the first positive quarter since ASX-mandated quarterly reporting began in the Sep-24 quarter. Group EBITDA margin 4.2% vs −4.6% pcp (+880bps) and −3.4% QoQ (+770bps).
  • Versus expectations: no meaningful published consensus, but the tape says 'beat'. SGR closed +13.0% at A$0.13 (~$0.09) from A$0.115, having traded as high as A$0.138 (+20%) intraday. Context matters: the stock had lost >10% on 25 Sep when the NICC extended the Sydney licence suspension, so part of the move is relief positioning rather than re-rating.
  • Revenue has inflected, but only at the owned properties. Group revenue A$284m (~$199m), +2% YoY and +8% QoQ. The Star Sydney + The Star Gold Coast combined A$279m (~$196m), +5% YoY / +9% QoQ — management stresses this is the first YoY quarterly growth for the pair since Q1 FY25. The gap between +5% owned and +2% group is entirely the A$9m YoY cut in the Brisbane operator fee.
  • Gold Coast drove the quarter; Sydney drove the cost side. Gold Coast revenue +12% YoY to A$117m (~$82m) and EBITDA +92% to A$20m (~$14m). Sydney revenue only +1% to A$162m (~$114m) but EBITDA rose A$12m to A$15m (~$11m) as opex fell 13%. Corporate costs added a further A$10m; Brisbane's fee reset subtracted A$6m. Bridge: −13 +12 (Syd) +9 (GC) +10 (corp) −6 (Bris) = +12.
  • Growth is EGM-led and promo-funded — quality is mixed. Both properties cite 'strong electronic gaming machine growth' from 'revenue and marketing initiatives'; Sydney tables and non-gaming were soft, with non-gaming hit by 'increased complimentary investment' and the Sep-25 sale of the Event Centre. No EGM/table/non-gaming revenue split, win rates, visitation, actives or market shares are disclosed in the quarterly.
  • Cost-out is real but unquantified as a program. Group opex −12% YoY to A$202m (~$142m), 71.1% of revenue vs 81.8% pcp. Unallocated corporate A$23m (~$16m), −29% YoY. The Chairman says 'tens of millions' of corporate bloat have been removed and decentralisation of operating control to the properties was completed in October; management expects further corporate reductions to fund marketing. There is no stated savings target or timeline — a disclosure gap vs listed peers.
  • Gaming-tax leverage is working against the P&L. Gaming taxes & levies +10% YoY to A$70m (~$49m) on +2% revenue — 24.6% of revenue vs 22.9% pcp (Sydney 29.6% vs 28.0%; Gold Coast 18.8% vs 18.1%), consistent with the EGM-heavy mix and scheduled NSW duty step-ups. EGM-led growth carries a structurally higher duty take than the tables business it is replacing.
  • Brisbane is a record for the asset, not for shareholders. The Star Brisbane (managed under the amended CMA after Stage 1 of the DBC unwind closed in Mar-26) produced EBITDAM of A$37m (~$26m), +193% YoY on revenue of A$136m (~$95m), +14%; margin 27.2%. The Chairman says EBITDAM now annualises to >A$150m vs ~A$50m at takeover. But Star's operator fee is now A$4.5m/qtr (vs A$13.8m pcp) against A$5m of directly attributable cost, so segment EBITDA is nil (vs A$6m pcp). The upside accrues to the DBC owners.
  • Reported operating cash flow was negative; 'underlying' positive. Appendix 4C net operating cash flow −A$15.5m (~−$11m), which includes a one-off payment of historical underpaid NSW casino duties (in item 1.2(b), amount not disclosed) and +A$41.5m (~$29m) of one-off receipts (ATO settlement on junket GST/withholding tax, plus returned rebate duty). Management says operating free cash flow excluding one-offs was positive; by arithmetic that implies the duty catch-up payment exceeded ~A$63m [analyst inference, unverified]. No cash interest was paid — the first year of WhiteHawk interest to May 2027 is funded from a A$48m Interest Reserve Account.
  • Liquidity is shrinking at the margin. Cash & equivalents A$239.4m (~$168m) vs A$267.1m at 30 Jun (−A$27.6m); restricted deposits A$80m (~$56m) vs A$101m; total cash & deposits A$319m (~$224m) vs A$368m (−A$49m). Composition deteriorated more than the headline: bank balances −A$42m to A$186m while cage cash +A$15m to A$32m. The Chairman told the AGM cash is 'approximately $200 million' — which reconciles to cash ex-cage (A$207m) or signals further October outflow; unverified either way.
  • Debt and leverage. Drawn facilities A$570.7m (~$401m): the US$390m WhiteHawk facility (A$559.1m at 30 Sep; three-year term; minimum-liquidity covenant A$50m for the first 12 months rising to A$75m thereafter) plus a A$11.6m 9% PIK liability to Bally's/Investment Holdings now being converted to 105.9m shares. Analyst-computed net debt ~A$252m (~$177m) including restricted deposits, ~A$331m (~$233m) excluding. Net debt/EBITDA is not meaningful on LTM EBITDA near zero; on Q1 annualised EBITDA of A$48m it is >5x and rising once AUSTRAC instalments are paid.
  • AUSTRAC resolved at A$260m (~$183m), payable in four instalments over 19 months. Federal Court judgment 28 Oct; Star says the quantum is 'materially in line' with the FY26 provision and does not impair going concern. The penalty terms constitute a 'review event' under the WhiteHawk facility; management is 'engaging' and 'confident' the facility remains available — the waiver terms (fees, pricing, covenants) are the near-term credit event to watch.
  • Going concern still formally flagged. The report repeats that sufficient liquidity is 'subject to and dependent on the outcome of a number of material uncertainties' (Note G of the FY26 accounts), to be reassessed at the H1 FY27 filing (Feb 2027). Appendix 4C shows 15.9 quarters of funding on the reported (one-off-affected) burn.
  • Licences remain on probation into 2027. Sydney: licence suspended, NSW Manager's appointment extended to 30 Jun 2027 unless terminated earlier by the NICC (the Sep-25 extension cited unresolved governance concerns, per press). Gold Coast: suspension deferral and Special Manager to 31 Mar 2027. Brisbane: External Adviser to 31 Mar 2027. Management says >85% of remediation-plan milestones are complete and decentralisation is 'a critical regulatory milestone'.
  • Guidance is directional, not numerical. FY27 is 'expected to be the first time The Star will generate net annual operating cash inflows, before one-off items, in more than two years'; management 'now expect[s] to start building cash reserves in FY27, before non-operating items'; October momentum 'has continued'. Nothing on FY27 revenue, EBITDA or capex.
  • Governance and management. All AGM resolutions passed on a poll, including five director elections and the PIK share settlement (Bally's 62.57m / Investment Holdings 43.35m shares). New property CEOs: John Koster (Sydney) and Ameet Patel (Gold Coast & Brisbane); NEDs Brooke Lindsay and Grant Bowie joined 1 Sep. Chairman Soo Kim opened by noting that 'if the reporting is to be believed' this is his 'first and last' AGM — a succession signal the market should not ignore given Bally's ~38% holding.
  • Capital allocation is survival-mode. Capex A$5.7m (~$4m), 2.0% of revenue — well below maintenance levels for three integrated resorts. A$5.1m of loans and A$1.0m of PP&E payments went to the Destination Gold Coast Consortium JV (Tower 2 delivered; Andaz hotel opening Nov-26). No dividend or buyback, obviously.
  • Accounting presentation changed. From FY27 corporate shared-service costs are directly attributed to properties rather than allocated by fixed percentage; FY26 property figures are restated and are not comparable with previously published segment EBITDA. Group EBITDA is unaffected. Treasury Brisbane remains discontinued (nil EBITDA).
  • FY26 base for context. Statutory NPAT −A$307.3m (~−$216m), normalised revenue A$1,101m (~$773m, −2%), normalised EBITDA −A$16.1m (~−$11m). Q1 FY27 annualises to ~A$48m EBITDA before seasonality and further cost-out — small against A$571m gross debt, A$260m of penalties and ~A$70m+/yr of cash interest once the reserve account is exhausted.
  • Thesis read-through. The operating turnaround is credible and now visible in three consecutive data points (Mar-26 monthly inflection, Jul-26 +6%, Q1 +5% owned). What it does not yet do is fund the liabilities: equity value hinges on (i) suitability outcomes by Mar/Jun 2027, (ii) WhiteHawk's treatment of the AUSTRAC review event, and (iii) whether ~A$60–90m of FY27E EBITDA [our estimate] can carry AUSTRAC instalments plus post-May-2027 cash interest without another equity call from Bally's/Mathieson.

3Segment deep-dive: The Star Sydney (owned)

Revenue +1% YoY to A$162m but EBITDA +A$12m to A$15m on a 13% opex cut — the margin story, not the growth story; licence still suspended to at least 30 Jun 2027.

Revenue and mix

Sydney revenue of A$162m (~$114m) rose 1% on the pcp and 8% on Q4 FY26, driven by electronic gaming machines offset by softer tables and non-gaming.

  • Growth came from 'strong year on year electronic gaming machines growth', partially offset by softness in table games and non-gaming.
  • Non-gaming was hit by 'increased complimentary investment' (comps netted against revenue) and loss of events and catering revenue after the Event Centre sale completed in September 2025.
  • The 1% headline understates gaming growth and overstates the health of the broader resort; the premium/tables business, the pre-2022 profit engine, continues to shrink.
  • No EGM/table split, win rates, visitation, actives or NSW market shares are disclosed, so the quality of gaming growth cannot be independently assessed.

Gaming taxes

Gaming taxes and levies rose 7% to A$48m (~$34m), 29.6% of revenue versus 28.0% in the pcp, so the state took more than the incremental revenue.

  • This reflects an EGM-led mix in NSW, where machine duty exceeds table duty and the duty schedule steps up over time.
  • Sydney's revenue growth will convert to EBITDA at a lower rate than Gold Coast's for as long as tables remain soft.

Costs and EBITDA

Operating expenses fell 13% YoY (−A$14m) and 7% QoQ (−A$7m) to A$99m, lifting segment EBITDA to A$15m from A$3m in the pcp.

  • Opex was 61.1% of revenue against 70.2% in the pcp and 70.7% in Q4.
  • Segment EBITDA of A$15m (~$11m) versus A$3m pcp and a A$1m loss in Q4; 9.3% margin, +740bps YoY and +1,000bps QoQ.
  • FY26 comparatives restated to the new direct-attribution methodology for corporate shared services, so part of the improvement reflects a lower allocated shared-service base rather than property-level cost-out.
  • Management concedes the property only 'stabilised' revenue; a 9% margin on A$650m annualised revenue leaves Sydney the lowest-return owned asset by a wide margin.

Regulatory status

The NICC advised the licence suspension 'will remain in effect at this time' and the Manager's appointment runs to 30 June 2027 unless terminated earlier.

  • Press coverage of the September decision cited unresolved governance and leadership concerns.
  • New property CEO John Koster is in place; decentralisation of operating control to the property was completed in October, which management calls 'a critical regulatory milestone'.
  • The Chairman says the group has 'embraced' NSW and QLD AML/KYC/safer-gambling standards.
  • The AUSTRAC judgment on 28 October (A$260m, four instalments over 19 months) removes the largest Sydney-related legal overhang at a cost in line with provisions.
“a critical regulatory milestone”Management

Analyst view

Sydney is the swing asset for the equity: roughly 57% of group revenue, with comps-funded EGM growth masking a structurally smaller premium business.

  • It is the only property with no suitability horizon shorter than mid-2027.
  • Sustaining the 13% opex reduction while 'improving service standards' (the CEO's stated priority) and funding higher marketing is a real tension.
  • We would want tables to stop declining and non-gaming to stabilise before treating the Q1 margin as a base rather than a high-water mark.
  • No segment guidance was given.

4Segment deep-dive: The Star Gold Coast (owned)

The growth engine: revenue +12% to A$117m, EBITDA +92% to A$20m, margin 17.1% (+660bps) on marketing-led EGM growth — but the licence suspension deferral expires 31 Mar 2027.

Revenue and taxes

Gold Coast revenue rose 12% on the pcp and 9% on Q4 FY26 to A$117m (~$82m), driven by marketing-led electronic gaming machine growth.

  • Management attributes growth to 'strong growth in electronic gaming machines, driven by increased marketing investment and promotional initiatives'.
  • This is the one property where both revenue and EBITDA 'increased substantially'; it is now the group's highest-margin owned asset.
  • Gaming taxes rose 17% to A$22m (~$15m), 18.8% of revenue versus 18.1% in the pcp; the Queensland duty take is materially lower than NSW's.
  • Lower duty is why Gold Coast converts revenue growth to EBITDA far more efficiently than Sydney.

Costs and EBITDA

Segment EBITDA was A$20m (~$14m), up A$9m or 92% YoY and 18% QoQ, for a 17.1% margin (+660bps YoY, +120bps QoQ).

  • Opex flat YoY at A$75m (~$53m) but up 8% (A$6m) QoQ as marketing stepped up; 64.1% of revenue versus 71.4% pcp and 64.5% in Q4.
  • The QoQ trade of A$6m extra opex for A$10m extra revenue and A$3m extra EBITDA is the right one at this stage of the turnaround.
  • It is the clearest evidence that management's 'marketing to reinvigorate growth' thesis works when the duty rate allows it.

Property footprint

Through the Brisbane unwind Star acquired the JV partners' interests in the Gold Coast tower assets; Tower 2 is delivered and the Andaz hotel opens November 2026.

  • Tower assets are held via the Destination Gold Coast Consortium.
  • Star put A$5.1m of loans and A$1.0m of PP&E funding into the DGCC JV in the quarter (A$6.1m of related-party investing payments).
  • Should lift non-gaming revenue and room inventory from Q2 FY27, though it adds cost and the quarterly gives no view on hotel ramp economics.
  • Ameet Patel has been appointed property CEO for both Gold Coast and Brisbane.

Regulatory horizon

The Queensland Government and Commissioner for Liquor and Gaming deferred the licence suspension and Special Manager's appointment only to 31 March 2027, the shortest horizon in the group.

  • A 17% margin property growing double digits is what the regulator needs to see to extend, but the decision is binary and five months away.
  • Medium-term cannibalisation risk: The Star Brisbane's ramp (EBITDAM +193% YoY) draws on the same south-east Queensland catchment, though both properties are growing together for now.
  • No segment guidance was given beyond the group-level comment that October momentum continued.

5Segment deep-dive: The Star Brisbane (managed under CMA; DBC-owned)

Record EBITDAM of A$37m (+193% YoY, 27% margin) at the asset — but Star's operator fee has reset to A$4.5m/qtr and segment EBITDA is nil, so the record is a credential, not cash.

Operator fee reset

Star's operator fee fell 67% to A$4.5m (~$3.2m) from A$13.8m, leaving segment EBITDA at nil versus A$6m in the pcp.

  • Star operates Brisbane under the amended Casino Management Agreement with Destination Brisbane Consortium following Stage 1 of the JVP transaction in March 2026.
  • Star transferred its interest in Brisbane (and Treasury) to the Hong Kong partners for their Gold Coast JV interests, release from a A$700m debt guarantee and the revised CMA.
  • The A$4.5m fee is set against A$5m of directly attributable overhead.
  • The A$6m swing is the only negative item in the group EBITDA bridge; the A$9m fee cut is why group revenue grew 2% rather than 5%.

Asset performance

Managed-property revenue rose 14% YoY and 12% QoQ to A$136m (~$95m), with record EBITDAM of A$37m (~$26m), up 193% YoY and 43% QoQ.

  • EBITDAM margin of 27.2% versus 10.9% in the pcp; EBITDAM is before Star's management fee and excludes DBC's owner-level costs.
  • Management calls it 'an all-time record'; the Chairman says operating profit annualises to 'more than $150 million' against 'just $50 million annualised when we took over'.
  • That is already comparable to the combined EBITDA of the two owned properties (A$35m this quarter).
“an all-time record”Management

Who captures value

The release does not disclose any performance-linked fee component, so how much further EBITDAM growth accrues to Star cannot be determined.

  • The economics visible in the quarterly are a flat-looking A$4.5m fee.
  • Treasury Brisbane remains classified as discontinued with nil EBITDA.
  • The External Adviser's appointment for Brisbane has been extended to 31 March 2027, matching the Gold Coast horizon.

Analyst view

The A$37m record is a credential for regulators, not cash; on disclosed numbers it contributes nothing to group EBITDA.

  • Management leads with the record in the highlights while the fee reset sits lower down.
  • The record demonstrates operator capability to two regulators and keeps the Brisbane relationship and Star's role in Queensland's flagship asset intact.
  • Investors should not capitalise it unless and until Star discloses a participating fee.

6Segment deep-dive: Corporate, cash flow & liquidity

Corporate costs −29% and 'underlying' operating FCF positive, but reported cash fell A$28m (total cash & deposits −A$49m), interest is being paid from a reserve that runs out in May 2027, and A$260m of AUSTRAC instalments start inside the next 19 months.

Corporate costs

Unallocated corporate costs fell 29% or A$10m on the pcp to A$23m (~$16m), 8.1% of revenue from 11.8%, with further falls expected.

  • Down 4% on Q4; the reduction coincides with the switch to direct attribution of shared-service costs (FY26 restated, no group impact), so splits are not comparable with older disclosures.
  • Group total opex decline of 12% YoY is unambiguous.
  • Decentralisation of customer-facing functions completed in October; corporate retains remediation, legal/compliance, technology, audit, tax and consolidated reporting.
  • Management expects corporate costs to fall further 'as this decentralisation model takes shape' and says cost-out will fund marketing; no savings target or timeline given.

Operating cash flow

Reported net operating cash flow was −A$15.5m (~−$11m), distorted by an unquantified NSW duty catch-up and A$41.5m of one-off receipts.

  • Receipts of A$334.4m are grossed up by the at-cost DBC labour recharge, so not comparable to revenue.
  • Historical underpaid NSW casino duties, agreed with NSW Treasury, sit unquantified inside A$178.1m of 'product manufacturing and operating costs'.
  • A$41.5m (~$29m) of one-off receipts (ATO settlement on junket GST/withholding tax and returned rebate duty) sit in 'Other'; management says operating FCF excluding one-offs was positive, implying after A$5.7m capex the duty catch-up exceeded roughly A$63m [analyst inference].
  • Cash marketing spend A$10.4m (3.7% of revenue), staff costs A$177.5m, admin/corporate A$25.8m.
  • No cash interest was paid because year-one WhiteHawk interest is funded from a A$48m Interest Reserve Account, so operating cash flatters true free cash flow.

Cash position

Cash and equivalents ended at A$239.4m (~$168m) versus A$267.1m at 30 June (−A$27.6m), with total cash and deposits down A$49m to A$319m.

  • Bank balances fell A$42m to A$185.7m while cage cash rose A$15m to A$32.2m; call deposits were A$21.6m; FX impact −A$1.7m.
  • Restricted deposits fell A$21m to A$80m (~$56m), consistent with the reserve account funding interest.
  • The Chairman told the AGM 'cash is now building, with our cash balance approximately $200 million'; this reconciles to cash excluding cage cash (A$207m) but could imply further October outflow; unverified and we want it clarified.
  • The 4C's mechanical 15.9 quarters of funding is computed on one-off-distorted burn and should not be read as a runway.

Debt and AUSTRAC

Drawn facilities total A$570.7m (~$401m); estimated net debt is ~A$252m including restricted deposits or ~A$331m excluding them.

  • US$390m WhiteHawk term facility (A$559.1m at 30 Sep; three-year term, minimum liquidity covenant A$50m for first 12 months rising to A$75m thereafter).
  • A$11.6m 9% PIK liability to Bally's and Investment Holdings, approved for conversion into 105.9m shares (62.57m/43.35m) at the AGM; company did not state net debt.
  • Interest rate undisclosed; the A$48m reserve covering roughly eight months to May 2027 implies ~A$6m/month, ~A$70m+/yr (~13% on A$559m) [analyst estimate].
  • The A$260m AUSTRAC penalty, four instalments over 19 months, is a review event; Star is 'engaging with WhiteHawk and is confident that the facility will remain available', penalty in line with FY26 provision and no going-concern impact.

Analyst view

The quarter was operationally cash-positive but structurally cash-negative; the balance sheet does not self-fund through FY28 absent asset sales or further Bally's/Mathieson support.

  • Even on a generous ~A$60–90m FY27E EBITDA [our estimate], the group faces AUSTRAC instalments averaging ~A$65m each if evenly spread [assumption].
  • ~A$70m+/yr of cash interest from mid-2027, a covenant step-up to A$75m minimum liquidity, and sub-maintenance capex of ~A$23m annualised.
  • Going-concern language is repeated verbatim and will be re-tested at the H1 FY27 accounts in February 2027.

Independent view. Q2 FY27 (Dec quarter) should be seasonally stronger (school holidays, Christmas/NYE, Andaz opening in November) and the group is lapping a weak Q2 FY26. We pencil in revenue of A$290–305m (~$204–214m) and group EBITDA of A$18–28m (~$13–20m) [our estimates], assuming owned-property growth of 5–8% YoY, Gold Coast marketing intensity maintained, corporate costs ≤A$23m and the Brisbane fee flat at A$4.5m. For FY27 we estimate revenue of ~A$1.13–1.16bn (~$0.79–0.81bn) and EBITDA of A$60–90m (~$42–63m), a 5–8% margin — a swing of ~A$75–105m from FY26's normalised −A$16.1m, of which roughly A$40m is corporate/opex already in the run-rate and the balance requires continued EGM growth. Risks to that range are two-sided: the mix of comps-funded EGM growth and rising duty take caps conversion, while any tables/non-gaming stabilisation in Sydney would be pure upside. Leverage direction is up, not down. Our ~A$252m estimated net debt (incl. restricted deposits) will rise as AUSTRAC instalments (A$260m over 19 months; ~A$65m each if evenly spread [assumption]) and, from May 2027, ~A$70m+/yr of cash interest [est.] are paid out of an operating cash inflow we estimate at A$40–70m for FY27 before one-offs. On those numbers total cash & deposits trend toward the A$75m minimum-liquidity covenant during FY28 absent asset sales, a CMA renegotiation or further equity from Bally's/Investment Holdings — which is why we read the Chairman's succession hint and the PIK-to-equity conversions as the more telling capital signals. The FX exposure on the USD-denominated facility (A$559m at 30 Sep) is unhedged as far as the quarterly discloses; every 1c move in AUD/USD is ~A$8m of principal. The swing factors that matter more than any of this arithmetic are regulatory: the Gold Coast deferral (31 Mar 2027) and the Sydney Manager term (30 Jun 2027).
