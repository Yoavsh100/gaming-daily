# Alberta () — FIRST THREE MONTHS OF REGULATED MARKET Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: CAD (tables in C$ millions unless stated; ~USD alongside at USD/CAD 1.40) · Results released 23 Oct 2026**

> Alberta's open-licence online market generated C$122.1m (~$87.2m) of non-adjusted GGR on C$2,885m (~$2.06bn) of wagers in its first 80 days (13 Jul–30 Sep 2026), exiting September at C$56.6m (~$40.4m), +30.7% MoM, with only 24 operators live against 51 registered brands. It is a casino-led (78% of NAGGR), thin-hold (4.2%) land-grab with Ontario-parity economics (20% GGR share + 3% earmarks), but the regulator has not yet disclosed operator shares, the adjusted-GGR levy base or any channelization measurement — the headline is strong, the invoice is still open.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Primary release.** The Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC) published its first Market Performance Report on 23 Oct 2026, covering 13 Jul–30 Sep 2026 (80 days). Data are operator-submitted, unaudited, CAD, online-only; AiGC says results reflect gradual operator onboarding and are 'not a full representation of the total iGaming market'.
- **Market size.** Non-adjusted gross gaming revenue (NAGGR = wagers less winnings, before any promotional adjustment) was C$122.1m (~$87.2m) on wagers of C$2,885.1m (~$2.06bn) and winnings of C$2,762.9m (~$1.97bn): a blended hold of 4.23%, with 95.6–95.9% of wagers returned to players each month.
- **Trajectory.** NAGGR ran C$22.2m (~$15.9m) in July (19 trading days), C$43.3m (~$30.9m) in August (+95%) and C$56.6m (~$40.4m) in September (+30.7%). On a per-day basis the market moved from C$1.17m to C$1.40m to C$1.89m (+61% Jul→Sep), so growth is not simply a calendar effect.
- **Product mix is casino-heavy.** Launch-to-date NAGGR: RNG casino C$95.5m (~$68.2m, 78.2%), sports & event betting C$20.1m (~$14.4m, 16.5%), live casino C$6.6m (~$4.7m, 5.4%). September split: 75.6% / 19.5% / 4.9%. No fantasy or P2P (poker) revenue was reported — those verticals had no monthly activity in the period.
- **Betting inflected in September.** Betting NAGGR more than doubled to C$11.1m (~$7.9m, +113% MoM) on wagers of C$152.7m (~$109.1m, +36%), as hold jumped to 7.3% from 4.6% in August — a mix of NFL/NHL season start and operator-favourable results. Casino NAGGR grew a steadier +19.9% to C$42.8m (~$30.6m) at a 4.3% hold.
- **Online vs retail.** This report captures only the privately-operated online market conducted and managed by AiGC. AGLC's own Play Alberta platform, the land-based casinos, VLTs and lottery are reported separately by AGLC and are not in these figures; online penetration of Alberta's total gambling spend is therefore n/a from this release.
- **Players.** Active accounts rose from 226,959 (Jul) to 314,091 (Aug) to 388,585 (Sep, +23.7%); AiGC stresses these are accounts, not unique players (multi-operator sign-ups inflate the count). ARPPA rose from C$98 to C$138 to C$146 (~$104); wagers per active account were C$3,287 (~$2,348) in September.
- **Operator count is still ramping.** 15 operators were live at launch (16 active in July), 20 in August and 24 at 30 Sep; AiGC expects 'several more' by end-2026. The AiGC registered-site directory listed 51 brands on 10 Nov 2026 (28 had been approved pre-launch and 22 sites went live on day one per trade press). AiGC publishes no operator-level revenue or share data.
- **Legal framework.** The iGaming Alberta Act, SA 2025 c I-0.2 (Bill 48: introduced 26 Mar 2025, passed 15 May 2025, AiGC-establishing sections proclaimed by Order in Council 4 Jun 2025) sits alongside the Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act, RSA 2000. AGLC is the regulator (registration, Standards & Requirements for Internet Gaming (SRIG), centralized self-exclusion); AiGC is the Crown corporation that 'conducts and manages' the lottery schemes under Criminal Code s.207 and holds the operating agreements, AML oversight, complaints and financial reporting — a replica of Ontario's AGCO/iGO split.
- **Licensing regime.** Registration opened 13 Jan 2026. Operators need AGLC registration plus an AiGC operating agreement; eligibility requires a minimum three years' operating history in a regulated North American jurisdiction and a comprehensive financial audit; RG Check accreditation and self-exclusion API integration are preconditions; AiGC guides 3–4 months end-to-end. Fees per law-firm guides: C$50,000 (~$35.7k) one-time application fee and C$150,000 (~$107k) annual registration fee per operator [AGLC fee schedule not read directly]. No cap on licences.
- **Levy is a revenue share, not a tax.** AiGC retains a 20% 'provincial share' of gross gaming revenue, plus 3% of gross revenue guaranteed for Indigenous Relations/First Nations (2%) and Social Responsibility (1%) — an effective 23% of GGR, with operators retaining 80% of the headline base. Whether the base is NAGGR or an 'adjusted' GGR net of bonuses/promos is not disclosed in the report; the 'non-adjusted' label implies an adjusted figure exists [unverified].
- **Implied public take (estimate).** If levied on NAGGR, the 20% share would be C$24.4m (~$17.4m) and the full 23% C$28.1m (~$20.1m) for the 80 days; September alone implies C$11.3m (~$8.1m) at 20%, or ~C$136m (~$97m) annualised — already well above the C$75m (~$53.6m) year-one iGaming revenue line reported from Budget 2026 (secondary) and the ~C$100m (~$71m) annual figure government cited pre-launch.
- **Channelization.** Government data (per AiGC) put ~70% of Alberta's pre-launch iGaming activity with unregulated operators; targets are 70% channelization after year one and 75% by year two. Unregulated operators had to cease Alberta betting and return balances by 13 Jul 2026 but could keep marketing and pre-registering. No channelization measurement has yet been published.
- **Payments.** Fiat only through recognized compliant financial institutions; cryptocurrency is expressly prohibited under provincial/federal AML rules. No requirement for an Alberta office or Alberta-located servers (data must meet Canadian/Albertan privacy rules per SRIG s.5); English is the only mandated language; poker/P2P liquidity must be ring-fenced to players physically in Alberta.
- **Responsible gambling & advertising.** 18+ with mandatory verification; RG Check accreditation; AGLC centralized self-exclusion with three options (iGaming only, land-based only, both); auto-play and simultaneous multi-screen play prohibited; bonuses cannot be marketed as 'free' if the player must risk money; advertising may not target minors or use active professional athletes except for RG messaging. SRIG was issued 14 Jan 2026 and its advertising section updated 18 Jun 2026.
- **Ontario benchmark.** Ontario took C$4.08bn (~$2.91bn) of wagers in its first 88 days (Apr–Jun 2022) with roughly three times Alberta's population; Alberta's C$2.89bn in 80 days equates to ~C$590 per resident versus ~C$270 for Ontario's launch quarter (population-based estimate) — Alberta is wealthier, younger and had a larger pre-existing grey market.
- **Scale vs projections.** Pre-launch estimates put mature annual GGR at C$750–900m (~$536–643m) with ~C$200m (~$143m) flowing to the province (secondary). September ×12 is C$679m (~$485m) with fewer than half of registered brands live, so those projections look conservative rather than aggressive.
- **Enforcement/political direction.** The UCP government framed Bill 48 as consumer protection and revenue repatriation rather than expansion; critics (CBC, Apr 2026) argued protections are too light, and the June advertising update shows AGLC is willing to tighten mid-flight. The near-term direction is more operators, more data transparency (monthly reports) and tighter marketing — not higher levies.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Entry cost is modest (≈C$200k year-one fees, 23% of GGR, RG Check/SOC 2-type compliance) and the market is growing >20% MoM, but it is a casino-led, 4%-hold, promotion-intensive fight against 24 live incumbents including FanDuel, bet365, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars, Penn/theScore and the Super Group/Apricot stable. A sportsbook-led entrant is competing for C$11m/month of betting NAGGR; a casino-led entrant for C$43m/month, with live dealer (C$2.8m/month) the least contested niche.
- **Data caveats.** Trade-press figures conflict: casino.org reported C$2.76bn and Soloazar C$2.81bn of wagers against the regulator's C$2,885.1m; CTV described 'C$56m since launch' (September alone). We use AiGC's Excel throughout. All figures are unaudited and 'may be subject to revision'.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online casino (RNG + live dealer)

*Casino is 78% of launch-to-date NAGGR at a 4.2% hold; live dealer is a 5% niche with a 2.3% hold — the Alberta market is a slots market first.*

### RNG casino

**RNG casino produced C$95.5m (~$68.2m) of NAGGR on C$2,275.2m (~$1.63bn) of wagers, 78.2% of regulated revenue and 78.9% of handle.**

- Covers slots, progressive jackpots, RNG table games, poker and keno under AiGC's definition, across the 80-day period.
- Monthly NAGGR: C$17.0m (~$12.1m) in July's 19 days, C$35.7m (~$25.5m) in August (+110%), C$42.8m (~$30.6m) in September (+19.9%).
- Hold crept up each month — 4.02%, 4.20%, 4.27% — consistent with a slot-dominated mix with theoretical RTP of 95–96%.
- No sign of operators buying share through loosened game maths.

### Casino share and growth

**Casino's NAGGR share peaked at 82.3% in August, fell to 75.6% in September as football returned; September growth came from new accounts and operators.**

- Absolute casino number still grew nearly 20% in September while active accounts rose 23.7%, so casino revenue per account softened slightly.
- Four new operators joined during August–September; growth was not from deeper monetisation of existing players.

### Live casino

**Live casino is small: C$6.6m (~$4.7m) NAGGR on C$284.1m (~$203m) wagers, 5.4% of revenue but 9.8% of handle at ~2.3% hold.**

- Hold remarkably stable by month: 2.35%, 2.33%, 2.30% for live-dealer blackjack/baccarat/game shows.
- Share of NAGGR fell each month (6.3% → 5.6% → 4.9%) as RNG slots and betting scaled faster.
- Supply is not the constraint given Evolution-style studios serving Ontario from Canadian studios; demand among a slots-oriented Albertan base is.
- For an operator, live is a retention and VIP tool rather than a revenue engine here.

### Combined casino mix

**Casino plus live was C$102.1m (~$72.9m), 83.6% of NAGGR — materially above Ontario's typical casino weight, signalling a casino-first grey market.**

- Many day-one brands are pure casino: the Apricot/Casino Rewards stable (Captain Cooks, Casino Classic, Golden Tiger, Grand Mondial, Luxury Casino, Yukon Gold, Zodiac).
- Also Jackpot City, Royal Vegas, Ruby Fortune, Spin Casino, PlayOJO, Slots Magic, LuckyDays, CasinoDays, Delta, Betty, PureCasino.ca.
- Caesars Palace/Horseshoe/Golden Nugget/Stardust/Hollywood casino-only skins add to the mix, explaining it as much as player preference.

### Fantasy and poker

**No fantasy or peer-to-peer (poker) revenue was reported for any month; these verticals were either not yet launched or immaterial.**

- AiGC reports products only where there is monthly activity.
- Poker is handicapped by ring-fencing all P2P liquidity to players physically in Alberta (~4.9m population), ruling out shared-liquidity networks.
- Poker will stay marginal unless an interprovincial compact emerges.

### Commercial implications

**A casino entrant competes for roughly C$43m (~$31m) a month of RNG revenue growing ~20% MoM against at least 20 casino-capable live brands.**

- Bonus economics are tightened by the 'free' advertising rule and uncertainty over whether promotional spend is deductible from the 23% levy base.
- The 'non-adjusted' GGR label implies an adjusted post-promo figure exists for settlement, but AiGC has not published it.
- Promo intensity cannot yet be inferred from the public data.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Sports & event betting

*Betting NAGGR doubled to C$11.1m in September on a 7.3% hold as the NFL/NHL calendar arrived; it is 16.5% of the market to date and the swing factor for Q4.*

### Betting volumes

**Betting generated C$20.1m (~$14.4m) NAGGR on C$325.8m (~$233m) handle over 80 days — 16.5% of revenue on 11.3% of handle.**

- Covers traditional sports, esports and novelty markets; singles, live, pools and parlays.
- Monthly NAGGR: C$3.8m (Jul, 19 days), C$5.2m (Aug), C$11.1m (Sep).
- Handle rose C$60.9m → C$112.2m → C$152.7m (~$109m), +36% in September against +20% for the market overall.
- Per calendar day, handle went from C$3.2m to C$3.6m to C$5.1m.

### Hold volatility

**Hold swung 6.24% (Jul), 4.63% (Aug), 7.27% (Sep); volatility, not handle, explains most of September's +113% NAGGR jump.**

- At August's hold, September's handle would have yielded ~C$7.1m, so roughly C$4m of the increase was results-driven.
- A 6–7% blended hold is consistent with a parlay-heavy North American product set rather than a European low-margin singles book.
- FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars, bet365, theScore Bet, BetRivers, Bally Bet, DAZN Bet and Sports Interaction are all live.

### Q4 outlook

**Betting's share troughed at 12.1% in August and recovered to 19.5% in September; we expect Q4 handle well above C$200m (~$143m) a month [estimate].**

- August was the MLB-only window for North American operators; October brings full NFL, NHL and NBA openers plus MLB post-season, pushing share higher.
- AiGC does not disclose promo spend, so true net margin after free bets and odds boosts cannot be derived.
- A 7.3% 'structural' hold on a non-adjusted basis probably translates to a mid-single-digit net margin after promotions [estimate].

### Product rules

**Product rules are permissive by Canadian standards, though peer-to-peer exchanges ring-fenced to Alberta players are commercially unviable at this population size.**

- Single-event betting, live/in-play, esports and novelty markets are all within AiGC's 'Eligible iGames' definition.
- No restriction on betting on Canadian professional or amateur leagues disclosed in the AiGC material.

### Entrant implications

**A sportsbook-led entrant addresses ~C$11m (~$8m) a month of gross win, dominated by brands with Ontario and US databases and national media deals.**

- Alberta-specific hook: Calgary/Edmonton NHL and CFL fandom plus land-based crossover; PureCasino.ca is the only Alberta land-based operator with a registered online brand so far.
- Advertising constraints bite hardest in sports — no active professional athletes in creative, no inducement-style 'free bet' claims — blunting the US-style acquisition playbook.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, levy & legal framework

*An Ontario-clone 'conduct and manage' model: AGLC registers, AiGC contracts; C$200k of year-one fees and a 20% GGR share plus 3% earmarks, with no licence cap and no local-presence requirement.*

### Enabling statute

**The iGaming Alberta Act (Bill 48) establishes an Ontario-clone 'conduct and manage' model under Criminal Code s.207, carrying the same residual federal-law risk as Ontario.**

- SA 2025 c I-0.2, introduced by Minister Dale Nally on 26 Mar 2025, passed 15 May 2025; AiGC sections proclaimed in force 4 Jun 2025.
- Operates alongside the Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act, RSA 2000.
- Private operators supply under operating agreements, the same architecture as iGaming Ontario, whose legality has been tested in Ontario litigation [outcome not verified this session].

### Regulator roles

**AGLC registers and regulates; AiGC, a Crown corporation led by CEO Dan Keene, contracts, collects the provincial share and reports — while AGLC also runs Play Alberta.**

- AGLC issues the SRIG (published 14 Jan 2026, advertising section updated 18 Jun 2026), certifies technology via accredited test facilities and runs centralized Self-Exclusion.
- AiGC executes operating agreements, oversees AML, handles public complaints and publishes the monthly market report.
- The Crown is simultaneously regulator, conductor and competitor.

### Registration process

**Registration opened 13 Jan 2026 with no operator cap; AiGC guides 3–4 months from complete application to execution.**

- Three-pronged AGLC process: due diligence (suitability), compliance (technical certification, SOC-type security attestation, go-live checks) and self-exclusion integration, then AiGC negotiates the agreement.
- Eligibility requires active AGLC registration, three years' operating history in a regulated North American jurisdiction and a comprehensive financial audit — effectively excluding grey-market-only brands.
- Unregulated operators had to stop accepting Alberta wagers and refund balances by 13 Jul 2026, but could keep marketing and pre-registering accounts.

### Fees and requirements

**Fees are a one-time C$50,000 (~$35.7k) application fee and a C$150,000 (~$107k) annual registration fee per operator, with no local-presence requirement.**

- Per Gowling WLG and Mondaq guides, consistent with Blakes and Canadian Gaming Business coverage of the January AGLC bulletin.
- Treatment of multiple skins under one registration should be confirmed with AGLC; suppliers pay a separate (lower) fee [amount not verified].
- No local-office, local-ownership or Alberta-server requirement; data must satisfy Canadian and Albertan privacy rules (SRIG s.5); English is the only mandatory language.

### Levy structure

**AiGC retains 20% of GGR plus 3% earmarks (2% Indigenous Relations/First Nations, 1% Social Responsibility), an all-in 23% public take; model 23% on NAGGR as conservative.**

- Levy is contractual, not statutory; operators retain 80% of the headline base before the 3%.
- Compares with Ontario's 20% of promo-adjusted gaming revenue plus separate First Nations arrangements.
- The 'non-adjusted' label implies settlement on an adjusted base, but neither AiGC nor AGLC has published adjustment rules.
- Alberta's 8% corporate income tax (Canada's lowest) and no separate gaming excise are genuine advantages versus most US states and the UK's 21% remote gaming duty.

### Public finance

**The September run-rate of ~C$11.3m (~$8.1m) a month at 20% is already ahead of Budget 2026's C$75m (~$53.6m) year-one target; no levy increase expected in 12–24 months.**

- Government talked pre-launch of ~C$100m (~$71m) a year; earmarks add ~C$1.7m a month.
- Fiscal goodwill rather than pressure to raise the rate; we see no proposal and no mechanism for a levy increase.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

*Fiat-only with crypto banned, mandatory ID verification, RG Check and centralized self-exclusion, no autoplay, and an advertising code that was tightened on 18 Jun 2026 — before launch.*

### Payments and AML

**Payments are fiat-only through recognized, compliant financial institutions, with cryptocurrency 'strictly restricted' — binding crypto-native Stake to a fiat-only basis.**

- AiGC carries the AML oversight role; operators are reporting entities under Canada's PCMLTFA/FINTRAC regime.
- No method-level data published; Interac e-Transfer, debit/credit cards and bank transfers dominate Canadian regulated markets [general market knowledge, not from the report].
- No deposit-limit cap or affordability threshold mandated beyond the operator's obligation to offer limit-setting tools.

> “strictly restricted” — AiGC

### Responsible gambling

**Every player is identity- and age-verified (18+), every operator needs RG Check accreditation and Self-Exclusion API integration before signing its operating agreement.**

- RG Check is the Responsible Gambling Council's standard also used in Ontario.
- Self-exclusion is fully digital with three choices — all registered iGaming, all land-based casinos and racing entertainment centres, or both — linking online and land-based unlike Ontario's BetGuard at launch.
- Game-design rules prohibit auto-play and simultaneous multi-screen slot play; offers may not be called 'free' if the player must risk money or incur a loss.
- 1% of gross revenue is earmarked for social-responsibility programmes covering mental health, addiction and research.

> “built in from day one” — AiGC

### Advertising rules

**SRIG section 4.1 (issued 14 Jan 2026, updated 18 Jun 2026) bans targeting minors and active professional athletes, pushing inducement advertising into owned/logged-in channels.**

- Active athletes permitted only for social-responsibility messaging.
- Online, print and billboard advertising permitted but content, endorsers, targeting and public communication of bonuses/free bets are restricted; RG messaging required; operators fully responsible for affiliate content.
- Close to Ontario's AGCO Standard 2.03 regime; removes most of the US-style sign-up-offer blitz from mass media.

### Operational implications

**Rules favour operators with mature Ontario compliance stacks, which is why the day-one roster was Ontario-heavy.**

- Same RG Check accreditation, similar advertising constraints, comparable self-exclusion API and broadly parallel technical standards let Ontario operators port most of their programme.
- For a non-Ontario entrant such as B2Tech, RG Check accreditation (typically multi-month), SOC-type security attestation, test-lab certification of every game and affiliate-liability provisions are the critical-path items rather than fees.

### Regulatory direction

**Direction of travel is towards tighter marketing, not looser; we expect further restrictions on celebrity/influencer use, bonus communication and volume/placement before any loosening.**

- AGLC amended advertising standards before a single regulated bet was taken.
- Opposition and public-health critics argued protections are insufficient (CBC, Apr 2026); parallel federal pressure on sports-betting advertising.
- We expect AiGC to begin publishing self-exclusion enrolment and RG-tool usage statistics as part of its transparency agenda.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Growth is real, not calendar-driven: NAGGR per day rose 61% from July (C$1.17m) to September (C$1.89m, ~$1.35m) and active accounts +23.7% MoM in September, with fewer than half of the 51 registered brands trading — the market is still in its onboarding phase. _(structural)_
- Ontario-parity economics with a lighter total burden than most mature markets: 20% GGR share + 3% earmarks, C$200k (~$143k) of year-one fees, Alberta's 8% corporate tax, no gaming excise, no local office/server requirement and English-only obligations keep fixed entry cost low. _(structural)_
- A large, identified channelization pool: government estimates ~70% of pre-launch activity was unregulated, operators were forced to cease and refund by 13 Jul 2026, and the policy target is 70% regulated after year one / 75% by year two — the regulated pie should keep expanding independent of underlying demand growth. _(structural)_
- Betting is under-penetrated at 16.5% of NAGGR launch-to-date versus 19.5% in September alone; Q4 NFL/NHL/NBA seasonality and the 7.3% September hold point to betting share climbing into the mid-20s, which favours sportsbook-capable entrants. _(early)_
- Live dealer is the least contested vertical — C$2.8m (~$2.0m) of September NAGGR, 4.9% share and a flat 2.3% hold — leaving white space for a live-casino-led proposition rather than another slots skin. _(early)_
- Unusual transparency: AiGC publishes a monthly Excel with wagers, winnings, NAGGR, accounts and product splits within three weeks of month-end, enabling precise market-growth tracking (though not share). _(structural)_
- The public purse is already ahead of plan — a September run-rate of ~C$11.3m (~$8.1m) a month at 20% annualises near C$136m (~$97m) against a reported C$75m (~$53.6m) budget line — which removes near-term fiscal pressure to raise the levy. _(one-off)_

**Lowlights**

- Casino concentration (78% of NAGGR, 84% with live) makes the market politically exposed: critics already call the protections inadequate, and any tightening of slot rules (stake limits, session caps, bonus curbs) would hit the bulk of revenue. _(structural)_
- The levy base is undisclosed: reported revenue is explicitly 'non-adjusted', implying settlement on an adjusted figure whose rules neither AiGC nor AGLC has published — promo deductibility, and therefore the true effective rate, cannot be modelled from public data. _(watch)_
- No operator-level disclosure: shares, concentration and competitor promo intensity are n/a, so an entrant cannot size the attainable share from regulator data and must rely on supplier/affiliate triangulation. _(structural)_
- Crowded and Ontario-seasoned: 24 live operators (51 brands) including FanDuel, bet365, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars, Penn and Super Group arrived with Canadian databases and mature compliance; the day-one acquisition window has closed and the 3–4 month onboarding means a new applicant lands in Q1–Q2 2027 at the earliest. _(structural)_
- Advertising is being tightened, not loosened: SRIG's advertising section was amended on 18 Jun 2026 before launch, 'free' bonus claims and active-athlete creative are barred, operators carry affiliate liability, and federal pressure on sports-betting advertising persists — the US acquisition playbook does not transfer. _(watch)_
- Thin, promo-sensitive economics: a 4.2% blended hold and C$146 (~$104) monthly ARPPA on non-unique accounts leave little room once a 23% levy, platform/content fees and bonus costs are deducted; betting hold swung 4.6%→7.3% in one month, so monthly results will be volatile. _(mixed)_
- Data and enforcement gaps: figures are unaudited and revisable, accounts are not unique players, channelization is unmeasured and AiGC's anti-offshore toolkit appears limited to education and eligibility rules rather than payment or ISP blocking; the Criminal Code s.207 'conduct and manage' model also carries the same residual legal-challenge risk as Ontario's. _(governance)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

There is no formal guidance to bridge — AiGC's first report simply sets the base. The moving parts over the next 12 months are (i) operator onboarding: 24 active at 30 Sep against 51 registered brands, with 'several more' agreements expected by end-2026 and Ontario-licensed applicants completing AGLC's 3–4 month process through H1 2027; (ii) seasonality: betting's share should rise from 19.5% in September through the NFL/NHL/NBA calendar, lifting both handle and blended hold; (iii) channelization: the 70% year-one target has no published measurement yet, and AiGC's October report (due third week of November) will show whether the +20–30% monthly growth persists once launch promotions fade; (iv) rules: SRIG advertising standards were tightened on 18 Jun 2026 and further marketing restrictions are more likely than any levy change; and (v) disclosure: the adjusted-GGR settlement base and operator-level data remain unpublished, which matters more to an entrant's P&L than any headline growth number.

- **September 2026 NAGGR run-rate:** C$56.6m/month (~$40.4m) (×12 = C$679m (~$485m) annualised; +30.7% MoM, +61% per-day vs July)
- **Operator onboarding still to come:** 24 active vs 51 registered brands (AiGC expects 'several more' by YE2026; further Ontario-licensed entrants H1 2027)
- **Implied provincial share at Sept run-rate (est.):** ~C$11.3m/month at 20% (~$8.1m) (≈C$136m annualised vs reported Budget 2026 year-one line of C$75m (~$53.6m))
- **Channelization target:** 70% after year one, 75% by year two (From ~70% unregulated pre-launch; no measurement published yet)

Our independent view: **first-12-month NAGGR (13 Jul 2026–12 Jul 2027) of C$700–800m (~$500–571m) [estimate]**, assuming monthly NAGGR rises to C$65–75m by Q1 2027 as onboarding completes and betting seasonality kicks in, then plateaus as launch promotions normalise; that already reaches the lower end of the C$750–900m pre-launch 'mature market' projections, which we now regard as conservative for year two. **Mix shifts modestly toward betting (20–25% of NAGGR in Q4/Q1) while casino + live stays above 75%**; blended hold should hold at 4.2–4.6%. **Public take at 23% on NAGGR would be ~C$160–185m (~$114–132m) in year one [estimate]**, comfortably above budget, so we assign a low probability to any levy increase in the next 24 months and a higher probability to tighter advertising, bonus and affiliate rules plus eventual publication of RG metrics. **For B2Tech, the attainable prize for a 2027 entrant is a 1–3% share of a ~C$800m market [estimate] — C$8–24m of NAGGR — unless it brings a differentiated live-dealer or land-based/First Nations-partnered proposition; a me-too sportsbook or slots skin will struggle against 24 incumbents under advertising constraints.** Key swing factors: whether the adjusted-GGR base allows meaningful promo deductions (upside to net margins), whether AiGC adds payment- or ISP-blocking against offshore sites (upside to channelization), and any federal advertising legislation (downside to acquisition).
