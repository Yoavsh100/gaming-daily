# Tennessee () — SEPTEMBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (market reports in USD; tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released 23 Oct 2026 (SWC "Monthly Sports Gaming Report: September 2026"; trade coverage 23–28 Oct 2026)**

> Tennessee's mobile-only sportsbook market took $591.9m of gross wagers in September 2026 (+4.7% YoY; taxable gross handle $588.6m, +4.9%) — the best month of 2026 but still short of the $598.6m booked in November 2024 — and yielded $10.88m of privilege tax under the only handle-based regime in the US (1.85% of gross handle). Growth has decelerated hard from the World Cup-inflated summer (+30–38% YoY in June/July) to mid-single digits, the licensed field has thinned to nine operators, and the regulatory story is now enforcement-led (a Sixth Circuit ruling that Tennessee's Sports Gaming Act reaches Kalshi's sports contracts, and a run of offshore cease-and-desists) rather than tax or licensing change. For an entrant, Tennessee is cheap to enter but structurally rewards scale and high-hold product; it penalises promo-heavy or low-margin models because free bets are taxed at face value.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Single-product, mobile-only market.** Tennessee permits online sports wagering and regulated paid fantasy only — no commercial or tribal casinos, no retail sportsbooks, no iGaming; the Tennessee Education Lottery and limited charitable gaming complete the legal perimeter. Online penetration of sports betting is therefore 100% by construction. Regulator: the Tennessee Sports Wagering Council (SWC), independent since 1 Jan 2022 (Public Chapter 593) and responsible for fantasy sports since 1 Jul 2023.
- **September 2026 print: $591.9m gross wagers (+4.7% YoY vs $565.3m), gross handle $588.6m (+4.9%), privilege tax $10.88m (+4.9% vs $10.4m).** Adjustments (voids, cancellations, negative resettlements) were ≈$3.3m, 0.56% of wagers. Month-on-month, wagers jumped +42.8% as NFL and college football restarted.
- **Best month of 2026 — but the peak has plateaued.** September's $591.9m remains below the $598.6m of gross wagers recorded in November 2024 (SWC), and December 2024 was $572.7m; football-season months have sat in a ~$550–600m band for two years. The 2022–26 September CAGR of 15.1% ($336.9m → $591.9m) flatters a market whose recent organic growth is mid-single digit.
- **Sharp deceleration from a World Cup summer.** Gross handle grew +30% in June 2026 ($456.1m, World Cup opening month), +38% in July ($412.1m vs $298.0m) and +10.7% in August ($412.0m vs $372.3m) before slowing to +4.9% in September. Q3-26 gross wagers ≈$1.42bn vs ≈$1.24bn in Q3-25 (+~15%), roughly two-thirds of it tournament-driven.
- **Annual scale: CY2025 handle $5.87bn (+10.8%), tax $107.6m; FY2026 (Jul-25–Jun-26) handle ≈$6.0bn [secondary]; 2026 YTD Jan–Sep ≈$4.37bn.** That is ≈$1,100 of handle per 21+ adult per year (approx.), in line with mature US online-only states.
- **No GGR is published — and the 'hold' figures in circulation are artefacts.** Since 1 Jul 2023 the SWC reports only gross wagers, adjustments, gross handle and tax (its FAQ states payouts/AGI are no longer collected for tax purposes). Trade trackers' Tennessee 'AGI' and 'hold' (e.g. $37.9m / 9.19% for July 2026) are simply tax ÷ 20% and 1.85% ÷ 20%, not operator data. Independent GGR estimates for 2025 range from $0.62bn (Track360) to $700.2m (AGA, which applies a Tennessee hold premium); the last actual disclosures were Sept–Nov 2022 holds of 12.9%, 8.6% and 10.3% under the old 10%-minimum-hold regime.
- **Tax regime: 1.85% privilege tax on gross handle, with the 0.25% federal excise deductible (PC 450, effective 1 Jul 2023).** Gross handle = gross wagers including promotional wagers, less cancelled/voided wagers and negative resettlements — there is no promo deduction, so free bets are taxed at face value. It replaced a 20%-of-AGI tax plus a 10% minimum-hold rule (operators under 10% hold paid the shortfall or a $25,000 fine). Effective burden ≈20.6% of GGR at 9% hold, 26.4% at 7%, 14.2% at 13%. Sept-26 implied rate on gross handle: 1.85%.
- **Tax distribution is politically entrenched:** 80% to the Lottery for Education Account, 15% to local governments, 5% to the Department of Mental Health for problem-gambling services (≈$5.4m in CY2025). This makes a rate cut improbable and a rate rise a live, if unproposed, option as other states (NJ, IL, MD, LA) have raised sports-betting taxes in 2025–26.
- **Licensing: uncapped licences, no casino tether, $50,000 non-refundable application fee and a $750,000 annual fee for operators with ≥$100m gross wagers in the prior 12 months** (lower tier for smaller books — reportedly $375,000 [unverified]). PC 450 also created vendor registration and capped administrative fines at $25,000 per violation. Any suitable operator can apply directly — unusual in the US, where most states require a land-based partner.
- **Operator landscape is consolidating: 12 licensed sportsbooks at end-2024 → 10 at end-2025 (AGA) → 9 on the SWC register at 23 Sep 2026; 16 registered fantasy operators.** The SWC publishes no operator-level handle, so Tennessee market shares are n/a; national patterns imply a FanDuel/DraftKings duopoly of ~70%+ of online handle [unverified for TN].
- **Fixed-cost squeeze explains the exits.** A $750k annual fee is 8.3% of GGR for a $100m-handle book at 9% hold but 0.8% for a $1bn book; add 1.85% handle tax (20.6% of GGR) and 0.25% federal excise (2.8%), and all-in government take runs ≈26% of GGR for a $300m-handle operator versus ≈24% for a $1bn one — and 34%+ for a low-hold entrant at 7%.
- **Enforcement has become the regulator's headline activity.** The SWC reported on 9 Sep 2026 that a cease-and-desist had shut another illegal sportsbook, and on 30 Sep issued C&Ds to Sportbet and FullStarSportsbook (announced 7 Oct), framing the eradication of illegal books as a core mission.
- **Prediction markets: on 25 Sep 2026 the Sixth Circuit held that Tennessee's sports gaming law can be applied to Kalshi's sports event contracts and vacated the district court's preliminary injunction** (the injunction stays in force until the mandate issues). This is the most important regulatory development of the period for licensed operators' channelisation; it remains subject to en banc/certiorari risk.
- **Payments are standard US rails** (debit, ACH/online banking, e-wallets, prepaid), with credit-card funding prohibited in Tennessee [statutory basis to confirm]; no mobile-money dimension; 21+ with geolocation/KYC. Tennessee levies no state income tax on winnings (Hall tax fully repealed 2021), but the federal OBBBA caps gambling-loss deductions at 90% from tax year 2026 — a headwind for high-volume bettors that could push sharp handle offshore or into prediction markets.
- **Responsible gambling and advertising rules are mid-pack, not outliers.** Statutory self-exclusion for sports wagering and fantasy (weekly licensee list transmission, monthly reporting), 5% of tax ring-fenced for treatment, TN REDLINE helpline, and advertising standards under SWC Rule 1350-01 (RG messaging, no targeting of under-21s) [detail not re-verified]. No 2026 Tennessee advertising-restriction legislation was identified.
- **Direction of travel: enforcement-led, fiscally stable, structurally closed to casino/iGaming.** No proposal to change the 1.85% rate, fee tiers or licence structure was identified in 2026; the 2027 General Assembly (convenes January) is the watch point for prediction-market, sweepstakes and any tax tinkering. Tennessee's handle tax is being showcased in-state as a stability model (September: TN tax +4.9% while New Jersey's fell 17.7% on low hold).
- **Fiscal outlook:** FY2027 Q1 (Jul–Sep 2026) privilege tax ≈$26.1m (+~14% vs $22.8m; August estimated) and CY2026E tax ≈$113–115m (+5–7%) — estimates. Absent a World Cup, 2027 handle growth should revert to +4–7% unless prediction-market enforcement or OBBBA behaviour shifts materially change channelisation.
- **Read-through for B2Tech:** Tennessee is a low-friction, low-fixed-cost licence (≈$0.8m year-1 fees) with no gatekeeper partner, but the handle tax structurally favours a scaled, high-hold, parlay-led product and penalises a promo-led land-grab (bonus handle taxed at 1.85%). A realistic challenger share of 2–5% equates to ≈$120–300m annual handle, or ≈$11–27m GGR at 9% hold (illustrative), against ≈$3.0–7.3m of tax, excise and fees. Entry only makes sense as part of a multi-state US footprint with existing content, trading and risk infrastructure.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online sports betting — volume, seasonality and trend

*September's $591.9m is the best month of 2026, but growth has slowed to +4.9% and peak-season handle has plateaued below the Nov-2024 high.*

### September 2026 volumes

**September 2026 gross wagers of $591.9m were the best month of 2026, but YoY growth has slowed to +4.9% on the taxable base.**

- $591.9m gross wagers, ≈$3.3m adjustments (0.56% of wagers), $588.6m gross handle, $10.88m privilege tax assessed.
- Versus September 2025 ($565.3m wagers, $10.4m tax): +4.7% wagers, +4.9% taxable base, +4.9% tax — levy is a flat percentage of handle.
- Month-on-month wagers rose 42.8% from August's $414.5m as NFL and SEC football resumed.
- September is typically the third- or fourth-largest month of the Tennessee year behind November, December and January.

### Peak-month plateau

**Peak months have stopped growing: the record remains November 2024 at $598.6m, while off-peak months grow faster, pointing to product-led gains near saturation.**

- September wagers rose from $336.9m (2022) to $445.2m-era levels in late 2023, $565.3m (2025) and $591.9m (2026) — a 15.1% four-year CAGR.
- Highest published month is November 2024 at $598.6m, with December 2024 at $572.7m.
- March 2026 wagers of $562.6m were +18.8% on March 2024 ($473.6m) — off-peak growing faster than the football peak.
- Consistent with same-game parlays, in-play and basketball/baseball depth rather than new-bettor adoption, near saturation in a state of ~5.4m adults.

**Summer 2026 was World Cup-inflated; Q3-26 wagers of ≈$1.42bn (+~15%) overstate an underlying mid-to-high single-digit run-rate.**

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Tax regime and the state's fiscal take

*A flat 1.85% of handle (≈20.6% of GGR at 9% hold) that shifts all hold risk to operators, taxes promotional wagers at face value, and has been stable since July 2023.*

### Tax structure

**PC 450 (2023), effective 1 Jul 2023, replaced a 20% AGI tax with a flat 1.85% privilege tax on gross handle, stable since.**

- Fiscal Review Committee memorandum records the bill proposed 2% and was enacted at 1.85%; licensees may deduct federal excise tax paid (0.25% of wagers).
- Gross handle is gross wagers — including promotional or free-bet wagers — less cancelled and voided wagers and negative resettlements.
- September 2026's $10.88m on $588.6m is 1.85% of gross handle, consistent with the formula.

### Previous regime

**The old 10% minimum-hold regime was 'mathematically equivalent' to about 2% of handle, so 1.85% was broadly revenue-neutral for the state.**

- Operators with monthly hold below 10% paid tax as if at 10% or a $25,000 penalty.
- Switch removed the hold floor and the burden of auditing payouts.
- Comptroller's May 2024 performance audit confirmed privilege-tax funds must be held in a segregated account pending monthly transfer.

> “mathematically equivalent” — SWC FAQ

### Fiscal receipts

**Collections are growing steadily: CY2025 $107.6m on $5.87bn handle, and FY2027 Q1 ≈$26.1m (+~14%).**

- CY2025 collections were 1.83% of wagers; FY2026 Q1 (Jul–Sep 2025) was $22.8m versus $21.0m a year earlier.
- FY2027 Q1 (Jul–Sep 2026) ≈$26.1m: $7.59m July, ≈$7.6m August (estimated), $10.88m September.
- Proceeds split 80% Lottery for Education Account, 15% local governments, 5% Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services.
- In-state commentary contrasts Tennessee's +4.9% September tax with New Jersey's −17.7% on a low-hold month, entrenching the handle tax politically.

### Operator incidence

**The hold-agnostic tax equals 20.6% of GGR at 9% hold, penalises promotions and low-margin pricing, and is hostile to challengers.**

- GGR-equivalent rate: 26.4% at 7% hold, 20.6% at 9%, 16.8% at 11%, 14.2% at 13%.
- Add 0.25% federal excise (2.8% of GGR at 9% hold) and $750,000 annual fee (2.8% of GGR on $300m handle; 8.3% on $100m).
- All-in government take ≈26% of GGR for a $300m-handle book, ≈24% for $1bn, ≈34% for a low-hold $300m entrant.
- No promo deduction: a $100 free bet costs $1.85 of tax before any customer value, unlike Michigan, Pennsylvania or Colorado.
- Sharp-friendly, low-margin pricing is penalised rather than rewarded.

### Peer context

**At ~20% of GGR-equivalent Tennessee sits between New Jersey/Ohio (~20%) and Pennsylvania/New York (36%/51%), with a 2.0% rate a non-trivial tail risk.**

- Rate has not moved while New Jersey, Illinois, Maryland and Louisiana raised rates in 2025–26.
- A hypothetical 2.0% of handle — originally tabled in 2023 — would add ≈$9m a year at current volumes.
- No such proposal identified in 2026, but national direction makes it a tail risk for 2027 budget season.

### Modelling guidance

**Model 1.85% of gross handle + 0.25% of wagers (federal) + $0.75m fixed, with promotional handle fully taxable.**

- For a 9%-hold product this is ≈23.4% of GGR before the fee; for a 12%-hold parlay-led product ≈17.5%.
- The structural advantage of the national leaders' product mix is amplified by the tax base.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, operator landscape and the wider gambling perimeter

*Uncapped, untethered licences at $50k + $750k a year — cheap to enter, but a shrinking field (12 → 10 → 9) shows sub-scale economics do not work under a handle tax.*

### Legal framework

**The 2019 Sports Gaming Act made Tennessee the first US state to legalise sports betting with no pre-existing casino industry; live since 1 Nov 2020.**

- PC 593 created the independent Sports Wagering Council from 1 Jan 2022; PC 450 (2023) rewrote tax and fees; Fantasy Sports Act transferred to SWC 1 Jul 2023.
- Operating rules sit in Chapter 1350-01; the monthly report, published around the third week of the following month, is the only official statistic.
- From June 2026 files carry a '(UA)' suffix, read as unaudited/subject to revision [interpretation].

### Licence economics

**Licences are uncapped and untethered — no land-based partner needed — at a $50,000 application fee plus $750,000 a year for larger operators.**

- No cap on operator licences; no casinos or racetracks means no market-access partner, which elsewhere costs a revenue share or seven-figure fee.
- Annual fee $750,000 for ≥$100m gross wagers in preceding 12 months; lower tier for smaller operators (reportedly $375,000) [unverified].
- Before July 2023 every operator paid a flat $750,000 ($50k + $700k renewal).
- PC 450 introduced vendor registration; key-personnel licensing continues; administrative fines capped at $25,000 per violation.

### Operator consolidation

**The field has shrunk from 12 (end-2024) to 10 (end-2025) to 9 (23 Sep 2026), as sub-scale economics fail under a handle tax.**

- SWC publishes no operator-level handle or tax, so Tennessee shares cannot be verified.
- National data suggest FanDuel and DraftKings hold ~70%+ of US online handle; BetMGM, Caesars, Fanatics, bet365 and Hard Rock Bet form the next tier [unverified for TN].
- Exits came from regional and start-up brands for whom a $750k fee plus margin-blind tax pushed government take above 30% of GGR.

### Compliance architecture

**Requirements per Rule 1350-01 and the Comptroller's audit are standard: monthly tax transfer and reporting, segregated accounts, self-exclusion reporting, official league data for in-play.**

- Monthly privilege-tax transfer and report of gross wagers, deductions and gross handle by the fifth business day.
- Weekly transmission of licensee-specific self-exclusion list and a monthly self-exclusion report; key-personnel suitability.
- Tennessee was first state to mandate official league data for Tier 2 in-play wagers [current rule wording to confirm].
- Minimum age 21; geolocation/KYC standard.

### Wider perimeter

**The gambling perimeter is narrow and likely to stay so: no casinos, retail sportsbooks or iGaming, with casino gambling requiring constitutional change.**

- Only other legal verticals are the Tennessee Education Lottery and limited charitable gaming.
- 16 fantasy operators (DFS and pick'em) are registered with the SWC.
- Prediction markets are the contested frontier.

### B2Tech entry maths

**Year-one government fees ≈$0.8m with no partner tax, but a challenger needs ≥$100–150m annual handle quickly to keep fixed costs under 10% of GGR.**

- Nine-operator field with an exiting tail, but a tax base that makes bonus-led acquisition expensive.
- Target share from the exiting tail and channelised offshore/prediction-market volume rather than from the duopoly.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Enforcement, prediction markets and political direction

*The regulator's energy is on channelisation: a Sixth Circuit win over Kalshi's sports contracts and three offshore cease-and-desists in September alone; no tax or licensing change is on the table.*

### Offshore enforcement

**September 2026 was the SWC's most active public enforcement month on record, with three offshore cease-and-desists.**

- 9 Sep: a C&D demand forced another unlicensed sportsbook to stop serving Tennessee.
- 30 Sep: C&Ds issued to Sportbet and FullStarSportsbook (announced 7 Oct).
- Stated mission now explicitly includes eradicating illegal sportsbooks and their ecosystem — payment processors, affiliates, advertisers — where pressure is likely to fall next.

### Prediction markets

**The Sixth Circuit's 25 Sep 2026 ruling against Kalshi's sports contracts is the single most material development of the period for licensed operators.**

- Court held Tennessee's sports gaming law applies to Kalshi's sports event contracts and vacated the preliminary injunction; it remains in force only until the mandate issues.
- Absent en banc rehearing or a Supreme Court stay, the SWC gains a clear path against Kalshi and, by extension, Robinhood, Crypto.com and other CFTC-registered venues.
- Sports contracts carry no state tax, no $750k fee and no 21+ or RG regime, and were the fastest-growing untaxed competitor through 2025–26.

### Fiscal politics

**The 1.85% rate is stable with no 2026 bill identified, but national tax rises give future budget-writers a template.**

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling and advertising

*Standard US rails with a credit-card ban, a mid-pack RG regime funded by 5% of tax, and player-level headwinds that are federal (OBBBA) rather than Tennessee-specific.*

### Payments

**Standard US payment rails with a credit-card funding ban; processing costs are industry-normal 1.5–3% of deposits.**

- Deposits via debit cards, ACH/online banking, PayPal/Venmo-type wallets and prepaid cards; withdrawals via ACH and wallet.
- Credit-card ban shared with a handful of states (Iowa, Massachusetts) [statutory/rule basis to confirm].
- No mobile-money dimension and no cash-at-retail network comparable to casino-state cages (third-party cash-voucher services not verified).
- Credit ban trims deposit conversion slightly but lowers chargeback and AML exposure.

### Responsible gambling

**A mid-pack statutory RG regime funded by 5% of privilege tax (≈$5.4m in CY2025, ≈$0.54m in September 2026).**

- Statewide self-exclusion covers sports wagering and, since 2023, fantasy sports; weekly list transmission and monthly report (Rule 1350-01-.07).
- Funds ring-fenced for Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services problem-gambling programmes; TN REDLINE is the mandated referral point.
- Deposit, wager and time limits and cooling-off tools are standard across licensees.

### Advertising

**Advertising is governed by Rule 1350-01 content standards rather than volume or time-of-day restrictions; no 2026 state legislation identified.**

- Standards: responsible-gambling messaging, no targeting under-21s, no misleading inducement language [rule detail not re-verified].
- Swing factor is federal (periodic SAFE Bet Act-style proposals) rather than state.

### Player taxation

**Tennessee has no personal income tax on winnings, but the federal OBBBA caps gambling-loss deductions at 90% from tax year 2026.**

- Hall tax fully repealed from 2021 — a mild positive versus high-income-tax states.
- A break-even high-volume bettor incurs a tax bill on phantom income.
- Risk that sharp and high-frequency handle migrates offshore or to prediction markets — the leakage the SWC push and Sixth Circuit ruling aim to close.

### Commercial implication

**Tennessee's RG and payments rules are not a cost outlier; compliance lift for a multi-state operator is incremental.**

- No special product constraints beyond the credit ban.
- 2026–27 shifts in player economics are federal; model as a modest drag on high-value-segment handle, not a Tennessee-specific risk.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale with a stable, predictable government take: $5.87bn of handle in 2025 (+10.8%) and ≈$4.37bn Jan–Sep 2026; the 1.85% handle tax has been unchanged since July 2023 while NJ, IL, MD and LA raised rates — Tennessee's fiscal model is being showcased in-state, lowering near-term rate-hike risk. _(structural)_
- Open, untethered licensing: no cap on licences and no casino market-access partner required (none exist), so entry costs are ≈$0.8m of fees in year one with no revenue-share to a land-based gatekeeper — rare in the US. _(structural)_
- A thinning field creates share to capture: 12 operators at end-2024 → 10 at end-2025 → 9 in Sept 2026; the exiting tail's handle is up for grabs by a well-capitalised challenger without the market having to grow. _(structural)_
- Enforcement tailwind for licensed books: the Sixth Circuit's 25 Sep 2026 ruling that Tennessee's law reaches Kalshi's sports contracts (PI vacated pending mandate) plus three offshore cease-and-desists in September point to meaningful channelisation of untaxed volume back to licensees in 2027. _(early)_
- Handle-based tax rewards high-hold product: a parlay/SGP-led book at 12% hold pays ≈15.4% of GGR in state tax versus ≈26.4% for a 7%-hold book — a structural edge for operators with strong parlay pricing and risk management. _(structural)_
- 100% digital market with mature per-capita engagement (≈$1,100 handle per 21+ adult per year): no retail capex, no cannibalisation debates, and off-peak months (March 2026 +18.8% vs March 2024) still growing on product depth. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- The handle tax penalises entrants: promotional wagers are taxed at face value (no promo deduction), so a bonus-led acquisition campaign carries a 1.85% levy on every free-bet dollar before any customer value is earned; low-margin or sharp-friendly pricing is punished rather than rewarded. _(structural)_
- Growth has decelerated to mid-single digits: +4.9% in September after World Cup-inflated +30–38% in June/July, and peak-season handle has plateaued below the $598.6m of Nov-2024 for two years — this is a share-capture market, not an expansion market. _(mixed)_
- Opaque market data: the SWC publishes no GGR, no hold, no operator-level handle and no promo spend; trade-tracker 'hold' figures (~9.2%) are tax ÷ 20% artefacts. An entrant cannot benchmark margins or size the duopoly's share from official data. _(governance)_
- Fixed-cost burden favours incumbents: the $750k annual fee is 8.3% of GGR for a $100m-handle book at 9% hold vs 0.8% for a $1bn book; all-in government take exceeds 30% of GGR for a sub-scale, low-hold entrant — the exact profile that has been exiting. _(structural)_
- Prediction-market competition is not yet closed off: Kalshi's injunction stays in force until the Sixth Circuit mandate issues, with en banc/certiorari risk and an unresolved federal CFTC pre-emption question; untaxed sports contracts keep siphoning volume in the interim. _(watch)_
- Federal OBBBA caps gambling-loss deductions at 90% from tax year 2026, creating phantom taxable income for high-volume bettors — a leakage risk to offshore books and prediction markets concentrated in the highest-value segment. _(early)_
- Tax-rate tail risk in the 2027 session: a 2% rate was already tabled in 2023, 80% of proceeds fund education, and the national direction is upward; a move to 2% would add ≈$9m a year to the state and ≈8% to operators' state-tax bill. _(watch)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

Nothing in the rulebook changed in September 2026 — the 1.85% handle tax, $50k/$750k fee structure and nine-operator field are as they were — but two things moved that matter more: the Sixth Circuit's 25 Sep ruling against Kalshi's sports contracts and the SWC's escalating offshore cease-and-desist campaign, both of which push untaxed volume back toward licensed books once the mandate issues. On the numbers, the tax take bridge from CY2025 to CY2026 is volume-driven: $107.6m in 2025 rises on ≈$4.37bn of Jan–Sep wagers (≈$80.6m assessed, derived) plus an estimated ≈$33m for Q4-26 on ~$1.8bn of handle (+~5% YoY), giving ≈$113–115m (+5–7%). Roughly $2.5m of the uplift is World Cup (June–July) and will not recur in 2027; the rest is underlying mid-single-digit growth.

- **CY2025 privilege tax (actual):** $107.6m (on $5.87bn handle (+10.8%))
- **Jan–Sep 2026 assessed (derived):** ≈$80.6m (≈$4.37bn gross wagers; Sep $10.88m, Q3 ≈$26.1m (+~14%))
- **Q4-26E (Oct–Dec):** ≈$33m (est.) (assumes ~$1.8bn handle, +~5% YoY vs Q4-25)
- **CY2026E privilege tax:** ≈$113–115m (est.) (+5–7% YoY; ≈$2.5m World Cup non-recurring)

**Handle: +4–7% in 2027 (estimate), with downside in June–July as World Cup comparatives roll off and upside of 2–4 points if the Kalshi mandate issues and the SWC enforces against prediction-market sports contracts in H1 2027.** We expect November 2026 to test the $598.6m Nov-2024 record for the first time; failure to clear it would confirm peak-season saturation. **Margin: unobservable from official data — model 9–11% hold with the tax at 1.85% of handle (≈17–21% of GGR) plus 0.25% federal excise and $0.75m fixed; all-in ≈24–27% of GGR for a $300m–$1bn book.** **Rules: the base case (≈70%) is no change to rate, fees or licensing in the 2027 session; the main tail risk (≈20–25%) is a rate increase toward 2.0% framed as education funding, worth ≈$9m a year; the probability of casino or iGaming legalisation is negligible.** **Operator count: more likely to fall to 8 than rise to 10 in the next 12 months absent a new national entrant; the SWC's register, not handle growth, is the best leading indicator of competitive intensity.** **State fiscal take: FY2027 (Jul-26–Jun-27) privilege tax ≈$112–116m (estimate), +3–5% on FY2026's ≈$111m-equivalent, with Q1 already +~14%.**

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Issuance of the Sixth Circuit mandate in Kalshi v. Tennessee and any en banc or Supreme Court stay petition; first SWC enforcement step against Kalshi/Robinhood/Crypto.com sports contracts thereafter.
- October–December 2026 SWC monthly reports: whether YoY handle growth holds ≥5% through the NFL season and whether November clears the $598.6m Nov-2024 record.
- 2027 General Assembly (from January): any bill touching the 1.85% privilege tax, fee tiers, prediction markets, sweepstakes casinos or advertising; and the post-election governor's first budget.

- Offshore enforcement escalation toward payment processors, affiliates and advertisers supporting unlicensed books, following the September C&Ds.
- Early-2027 behavioural evidence of OBBBA's 90% loss-deduction cap on high-volume handle (Q1-27 YoY in the sharp-heavy March basketball window).
