OCTOBER 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Wyoming

Released c. 19 Nov 2026 (Wyoming Gaming Commission, October 2026 Combined Wagering Activity Report) · Currency USD (market reports in USD; tables in $ as stated — no FX conversion required) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

October 2026 confirms Wyoming as a small, mature, DraftKings-led, mobile-only sportsbook market whose growth has stalled: handle fell 7.3% YoY to $22.6m, GGR fell 12.4% to $2.47m and state tax fell 9.0% to $177,882, even as lifetime handle crossed $1bn and 2026 YTD remains +6.9%. The regime is among the cheapest and simplest in the US (untethered $100k five-year permit, 10% of net proceeds with promo deductions), but the political direction is prohibitionist on casino-style product: the Select Committee on Gaming has sent a felony ban on online casino and sweepstakes operation to the 2027 session rather than a regulatory framework.

1Executive summary

  • Headline: Wyoming online sports wagering (OSW) handle fell 7.3% YoY to $22,629,217 in October 2026 (Oct-25: ~$24.4m), the second consecutive YoY decline after September's −14.2%, and −3.4% month-on-month versus September's $23,417,556. All five permit holders reported lower handle than a year earlier.
  • Revenue fell faster than volume: GGR −12.4% YoY to $2,471,163 (hold 10.92%), taxable net proceeds −14.4% to $1,671,443 and state tax −9.0% to $177,882. Promotional/non-cash deductions implied at ~$0.80m were 32% of GGR and 3.5% of handle — Wyoming taxes after these deductions, so effective tax was 7.2% of GGR and 0.79% of handle.
  • 2026 remains on course for a record year despite the autumn softness: Jan–Oct handle $200.0m (+6.9% vs $187.1m), YTD tax $1.50m (+10.0%). The state needs only $34.5m in Nov–Dec to beat 2025's $234.5m; Nov–Dec 2025 produced $47.4m. Lifetime handle since the 1 Sept 2021 launch passed $1.0bn in October — the first $500m took three years, the second two.
  • Market structure is 100% online for sports: there has never been a retail sportsbook counter; the five mobile permit holders are DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Fanatics and Caesars. Wyoming has no commercial casinos; land-based gaming is historic horse racing (HHR) terminals at pari-mutuel off-track sites, skill-based amusement games (SBAG), tribal casinos on the Wind River Reservation and the Wyoming Lottery.
  • OSW is a sideshow to HHR in dollar terms: the WGC Executive Director told the Select Committee that ~$2.7bn was wagered across regulated activities in CY2025, of which HHR was ~$2.49bn, and projected >$3bn in 2026. OSW's $234.5m 2025 handle is ~9% of regulated wagering; HHR took $240m in September 2026 alone — more than ten times sports.
  • Concentration is extreme and widening at the top: DraftKings took $11.69m (51.7% of handle), ~$1.40m GGR (~57% of market GGR) and paid $102,753 (57.8%) of tax; FanDuel $6.11m (27.0%) with the best hold (13.4%); BetMGM $2.74m (12.1%) but a 0.69% hold and negative taxable revenue (−$107,377); Fanatics $1.40m (6.2%); Caesars $0.70m (3.1%). DK+FD = 79% of handle.
  • Licensing regime (HB 133, 2021; W.S. 9-24): online-only, untethered (no land-based partner required), uncapped above a statutory minimum of five permits; $100,000 initial permit fee for five years, $50,000 renewal; applicant must already operate online sports wagering in at least three other US states; minimum age 18. Regulator is the nine-member Wyoming Gaming Commission (WGC), Casper.
  • Tax regime: 10% of net sports wagering proceeds (gross receipts less winnings and the non-cash/promotional deductions state law allows), assessed operator-by-operator with no cross-operator offset — in October the state's $177,882 equalled 10% of the $1,778,820 earned by the four profitable books, i.e. 10.6% of the net market figure because BetMGM's loss did not reduce it. CY2025 tax was $1,852,855 on $25.94m GGR (7.1% effective).
  • Promo intensity is the swing factor for state receipts, not handle: in September non-cash payouts were cut 32.4% YoY to $780,786, lifting tax +40.2% on −14.2% handle; in October deductions were ~$0.80m, broadly flat MoM, so tax rose +21% MoM on slightly lower handle. FanDuel's taxable revenue rose 67.7% YoY to $598,064 on 5.1% less handle.
  • Political direction is restrictive on casino-style product: on 5–6 October 2026 the Legislature's Select Committee on Gaming voted 6–0 to forward 27LSO-0098 ('Online casinos and sweepstakes prohibited'), which adds operating an online casino or sweepstakes game to the felony 'professional gambling' definition (up to three years' prison), and declined to advance the VGW-drafted regulatory alternative (27LSO-0200). The Committee's 9 Nov 2026 letter asks Management Council to sponsor it for the General Session opening January 2027.
  • Regulated iGaming is off the table: HB 0162 (2025) to legalise online casinos never left committee and was not revived in the 2026 budget session; the interim work has moved toward prohibition, not authorisation. There is no iCasino upside to underwrite a Wyoming sportsbook entry.
  • No OSW tax change was proposed in the 2026 interim: the Committee benchmarked other states' pari-mutuel, OSW and SBAG tax rates and distributions but tabled nothing on the 10% OSW rate. A draft to raise combined HHR fees from 1.9% to 2% of handle (27LSO-0199) died for lack of a second; a minimum horse-racing purse contribution (27LSO-0054) was not recommended.
  • Responsible-gambling funding is the live levy risk: 27LSO-0096 (RG and problem-gambling prevention funding, draft v0.5) — presented in July as drawing on OSW, pari-mutuel and SBAG revenues — received no final action and was flagged for further study. Today PG funding relies on limited statutory allocations from OSW tax and lottery; the Director called problem gaming 'a growing concern'.
  • Product rules tightened in the period: the WGC's catalog of events bans proposition bets on individual college athletes from 1 October 2026; geolocation, age verification and gaming on tribal lands were discussed by the Committee in the context of the sweepstakes ban.
  • Land-based expansion is being localised: 2026 legislation requires city, town or county approval for off-track HHR sites, applying to existing sites from July 2027 — a constraint on the HHR operators (Wyoming Horse Racing, Wyoming Downs, 307 Horse Racing) that dominate Wyoming wagering.
  • Regulator capacity and governance: WGC oversight costs exceed $1m; the Committee considered but did not recommend governance-structure changes; 27LSO-0201 (6–0) would cut fingerprint/background checks from annual to once at initial application. Enforcement ambiguity between gambling statutes and consumer-protection law on sweepstakes is the stated driver of the ban bill.
  • Payments: standard US card/ACH/e-wallet rails; mobile money is irrelevant. Wyoming's OSW statute is cited as uniquely permitting digital assets as a funding method [unverified — from background knowledge, not a source read for this brief].
  • Read-through for B2Tech: Wyoming is a cheap, stable, untethered licence with a 7% effective GGR tax — but the prize is ~$26m annual GGR (~$18m after promos) split five ways, growth has stalled at low single digits, the three-state prerequisite excludes new US entrants, and casino-style product is heading toward criminalisation. A #3 slot is contestable given BetMGM's 0.69% hold, but only as a marginal add-on to an existing multi-state US footprint, not a standalone entry.

3Deep-dive: Online sports wagering — market, operators and October 2026 performance

A $22.6m-a-month, five-operator mobile market in which DraftKings and FanDuel take 79% of stakes and BetMGM is effectively paying to play.

October 2026 market totals

Online sports stakes were $22,629,217 in October 2026, down 7.3% from roughly $24.4m in October 2025 and 3.4% below September's $23,417,556.

  • Gross gaming revenue was $2,471,163, a 10.92% hold, down 12.4% year on year from an implied ~$2.82m (a secondary outlet reported ~$2.9m; regulator-derived figure used).
  • Taxable net proceeds after non-cash and promotional deductions were $1,671,443 (−14.4% YoY, +13.9% MoM); the state collected $177,882 (−9.0% YoY, +21.3% MoM).
  • First time in 2026 that handle has fallen year on year in two consecutive months; March set the all-time monthly record at $28.5m and July was +45.5%.
  • Jan–Oct handle is $200.0m, +6.9% on $187.1m, with YTD tax up 10.0% at $1.50m.

Operator performance

Performance diverged sharply: DraftKings and FanDuel take 79% of stakes while BetMGM's GGR collapsed to $18,818 and it paid no tax.

  • DraftKings: $11,688,315 handle (51.7%, roughly −1% YoY against $11.8m), ~$1.40m GGR (~12.0% hold, ~57% of market GGR), taxable $1,027,530, tax $102,753 — 57.8% of the state's take.
  • DraftKings' handle share swung from 57.8% (Sept-25) to 47.3% (Sept-26) and back to 51.7%, so October is a recovery rather than a new high.
  • FanDuel: ~$6.11m handle (27.0%, −5.1% YoY from ~$6.43m), 13.4% hold (best of the big three), taxable revenue +67.7% to $598,064, tax $59,806 from $35,653.
  • FanDuel now remits about 58 cents for every dollar DraftKings pays on half the volume.
  • BetMGM: handle −25.1% to $2,735,592 (from ~$3.65m), GGR $18,818 (0.69% hold, versus $359,650), taxable −$107,377, tax nil — a ~$341k negative swing in taxable revenue YoY.

Fanatics and Caesars

The two smallest brands contributed modest taxable revenue, with Fanatics recovering from a September in which it paid just $279.

  • Fanatics handled $1,396,453 (9.6% hold, GGR $134,375, taxable $78,046, tax $7,805) after paying just $279 on $1.8m of stakes in September.
  • Caesars handled $703,639 (14.7% hold, GGR $103,743, taxable $75,180, tax $7,518).

Promotional spend

Promotional spend, not volume, is the main driver of state receipts and operator economics in this market.

  • Implied deductions were ~$0.80m in October (32% of GGR, 3.5% of handle); DraftKings and FanDuel each deducted ~26–27% of GGR, Caesars 28%, Fanatics 42%, BetMGM more than 100%.
  • In September the market cut non-cash payouts 32.4% YoY to $780,786, enough to raise tax 40.2% on 14.2% less handle.
  • Wyoming's tax base rewards promotional reinvestment (every bonus dollar is tax-deductible) but the small pool makes promotional share wars zero-sum.
  • The whole market's taxable revenue is ~$1.7m a month; the three sub-scale brands shared ~$46k of taxable revenue between them in October.

Scale constraint

Scale is the binding constraint: a mature market tracking +3–5% in 2026 against a population under 600,000.

  • Calendar 2025 produced $234.5m of handle (+11.9%), $25.9m of GGR at an 11.06% hold and $1.85m of tax; 2026 run-rate is ~$240–247m of handle and roughly flat GGR.
  • Handle grew +19% in 2023, +22% in 2024, +12% in 2025 and is tracking +3–5% in 2026; Gaming Intelligence notes all five brands reported lower October handle.
  • Lifetime, the regulator-reported series shows >$1.0bn of handle, ~$96m+ of GGR and ~$6m+ of tax since launch.
  • A 10% handle share — the whole of BetMGM's current position — is worth ~$2.3m of stakes and ~$0.25m of GGR a month before promotions, which does not fund a state-specific marketing programme.
  • Wyoming only makes sense as a near-zero-incremental-cost extension of a national platform, which the three-other-states eligibility test presumes.

Guidance and estimates

No regulator guidance exists; our estimate is CY2026 handle of ~$241–247m (+3–5%), GGR ~$25–27m and tax ~$1.95–2.0m (+5–8%).

  • GGR estimate is hold-dependent.
  • A record year is contingent on Nov–Dec delivering at least $34.5m against last year's $47.4m.
  • These are estimates, not reported figures.

4Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime — online sports wagering

One of the cheapest, simplest US sportsbook regimes: untethered $100k/5-year permit, 10% of net proceeds with promo deductions, but entry is reserved for operators already live in three other states.

Statutory framework

Created by House Bill 133 (signed 5 April 2021, first bets 1 September 2021), the regime is online-only, untethered and sets a floor of five permits with no ceiling.

  • Regulator is the Wyoming Gaming Commission, a nine-member body in Casper that began life in 1967 as the Pari-Mutuel Commission; it also oversees pari-mutuel/HHR, skill-based amusement games and charitable gaming.
  • No retail sportsbook channel; no land-based casino or racetrack partner needed.
  • Eligibility is the gate: applicants must already offer online sports wagering in at least three other US states, confining the market to national brands and excluding de novo entrants.

Fees and tax

Fees are nominal ($100,000 initial five-year permit, $50,000 renewal) and tax is 10% of net proceeds, running at ~7% effective on GGR.

  • Compares with seven- and eight-figure entry costs in Pennsylvania, Illinois or New York.
  • Net proceeds are gross receipts less winnings paid and statutory non-cash/promotional deductions, assessed per operator.
  • No cross-operator or cross-month offset: BetMGM's −$107,377 produced zero tax but did not reduce the others' take, so the state's $177,882 was 10.6% of the net market figure.
  • Deductions are uncapped, so the effective rate on GGR has run at ~7% (7.2% in October, 7.1% for CY2025) rather than the headline 10%.
  • Whether losses can be carried forward within an operator's own subsequent months is not stated in the sources read [unverified].

Revenue allocation

OSW tax of $1.85m in 2025 is immaterial to the state budget and far below HHR distributions, so legislative tax work focuses on HHR.

  • May 2026 Select Committee minutes record problem-gaming programmes funded from 'limited, statutorily defined revenue streams' from OSW and lottery allocations, regarded as inadequate by the Committee and WGC Director.
  • Our background understanding is a fixed first tranche of OSW tax goes to the Department of Health for problem-gambling treatment with the balance to the General Fund [unverified — amount not confirmed].
  • The 2026 interim work focused on HHR (a 1.9%→2% of handle proposal that died for lack of a second) and comparative benchmarking rather than the OSW rate.

Commercial implication

The regime is cheap to enter and operate but offers no shelter: margin is lost in marketing, not to the state.

  • With a $100k permit and ~7% effective tax, marketing in a five-brand market with ~$1.7m of monthly taxable revenue is where margin is lost.
  • No sign of the tax-rate escalation seen in Illinois, Ohio or New Jersey; the Committee reviewed other states' rates and recommended nothing on OSW.
  • Nearest live risk is 27LSO-0096, a responsible-gaming funding draft drawing on OSW, pari-mutuel and SBAG revenues; not forwarded in October but explicitly flagged for further study.

5Deep-dive: Land-based & other verticals — historic horse racing, skill games, lottery, tribal

HHR terminals are the real Wyoming gaming market — ~$2.49bn of 2025 handle, >10x sports — and they, not sportsbooks, drive the state's tax and political agenda.

Market structure and HHR

HHR terminals are the real Wyoming gaming market at ~$2.49bn of ~$2.7bn regulated CY2025 wagering, more than 10x sports.

  • Wyoming has no commercial casinos; the land-based market is HHR terminals at off-track sites, live racing, SBAG in bars and convenience stores, two tribal Class III casinos and a draw-only lottery.
  • WGC Executive Director projected more than $3bn in 2026; distributions go to the breeders' award fund, the WGC, the Legislative Stabilization Reserve Account, counties and municipalities.
  • HHR handle was $217.8m in October 2025 — Wyoming Horse Racing $82.3m, Wyoming Downs $79.4m, 307 Horse Racing $56.2m — and $240m in September 2026, versus $23.4m of sports stakes.
  • A secondary market-research estimate puts CY2025 HHR GGR at ~$211.8m (~90% of state gaming revenue) and SBAG handle at ~$30.6m; these are not regulator-reported.

HHR fee regime

HHR is taxed on handle at 1.9%, making state receipts structurally larger and steadier than OSW's and HHR permit holders the incumbent lobby.

  • The Committee considered lifting fees to 2% (27LSO-0199) alongside a minimum purse contribution (27LSO-0054); neither was recommended in the 9 November letter.
  • This is why OSW tax policy attracts little legislative attention.
  • The 2026 session's requirement that off-track HHR sites obtain city, town or county approval, applying to existing sites from July 2027, is the one material constraint on HHR growth and may trigger local votes through 2027.

Implications for online operators

There is no retail sportsbook channel, so acquisition is entirely digital, and HHR incumbents' position is being protected rather than opened.

  • No retail or on-premise sportsbook channel to tether to or compete against; acquisition is national-brand-driven.
  • HHR operators are natural proponents of any future iGaming or 'online HHR' authorisation and natural opponents of online casino competition.
  • Regulated iGaming is dead (HB 162, 2025) and the sweepstakes ban is advancing.
  • Tribal casinos (Northern Arapaho and Eastern Shoshone) operate under compacts outside WGC reporting; revenues and any on-reservation sports betting are not disclosed [n/a].

Lottery

Lottery transfers are a statutory problem-gambling funding source alongside OSW tax; sales and transfer figures were not in sources read.

  • The Wyoming Lottery Corporation testified to the Committee on responsible-gaming funding.
  • Sales and transfer figures not available for this brief [n/a].

6Deep-dive: Online casino, sweepstakes & the 2027 legislative pipeline

The Legislature's interim committee chose prohibition over regulation: a felony ban on online casino and sweepstakes operation goes to the January 2027 session.

Sweepstakes prohibition vote

The Select Committee approved 27LSO-0098, a felony online casino and sweepstakes ban, by 6–0 and did not advance the regulatory alternative 27LSO-0200.

  • The Committee, created in 2025 and reauthorised on 1 April 2026, met 5–6 October in Rock Springs to review the two opposed drafts.
  • 27LSO-0098 expands 'professional gambling' to include operating an online casino or sweepstakes game for another person, targeting dual-coin social casino systems; professional gambling is already a felony carrying up to three years.
  • 27LSO-0200 would have authorised online sweepstakes under a regulatory framework, drafted from revisions submitted by Virtual Gaming Worlds.
  • Executive Director Nicholas Larramendy testified that complaints cycle between the Commission and consumer protection because neither statute clearly applies.
  • Trade reports of 8–9 October said no vote was taken; the official minutes and 9 November letter confirm the 6–0 vote.

9 November letter

Two drafts go to Management Council for the January 2027 General Session, where the calendar is favourable to passage.

  • Forwarded: 27LSO-0098 and 27LSO-0201 (gaming background checks, reducing fingerprinting from annually to once at initial application or as the Commission requires).
  • A committee-sponsored bill typically needs a two-thirds introduction vote in a budget session but not in a general session.
  • Not recommended: HHR fee increase, purse minimum, WGC governance restructuring, responsible-gaming funding draft; charitable-raffle net-proceeds minimum and progressive-raffle regulation discussed for further work.

iGaming outlook

Regulated iGaming has no path in this cycle; the only legal online casino-style exposure in Wyoming is nil.

  • HB 0162 (2025) never left committee, did not return in the 2026 budget session, and the interim's output points the other way.
  • Wyoming would join a growing list of states criminalising sweepstakes casinos rather than taxing them, protecting HHR permit holders.
  • Any sweepstakes or social-casino product marketed into the state faces felony exposure from mid-2027 if the bill passes.

Regulatory perimeter

No OSW-specific change emerged, but the trajectory of more enforcement and RG funding attention could see a 2027–28 OSW levy re-emerge.

  • Discussion covered age verification, geolocation and gaming on tribal lands.
  • A draft on non-compliant skill-based amusement games (27LSO-0202) and comparative research on OSW tax rates in other states.
  • Trajectory: more enforcement, clearer criminal definitions, more attention to problem-gambling funding.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & product rules

Light-touch on paper, tightening in practice: an 18+ market with a new college-prop ban, under-funded problem-gambling provision and a legislature looking for a levy to fund it.

Age and product rules

An 18+ market with a new college-prop ban from 1 October 2026 that contributed to autumn hold compression.

  • Minimum age of 18 is lower than the 21 in most US sportsbook states, modestly widening the base but raising regulator sensitivity to youth harm.
  • Account opening requires identity and age verification and in-state geolocation; these controls are being re-examined as the sweepstakes ban is drafted.
  • WGC Catalog of Events prohibits proposition bets on individual college athletes from 1 October 2026, aligning with NCAA-requested restrictions.
  • Commercial effect is small but removes a higher-margin same-game-parlay leg during college football and basketball season.

Payments

Payments run on standard US rails with no retail cash channel; crypto is reportedly permitted by statute but not built.

  • Debit/credit cards, ACH, PayPal-type e-wallets and prepaid cards.
  • Statute widely cited as the only US OSW law expressly permitting digital assets as a funding method, though operators have not built crypto cashiers [unverified — background knowledge].
  • Mobile money is not a factor; no regulator statistics on deposit methods, limits or withdrawal speeds are published.

Responsible gambling

RG architecture is thin and under-funded, and 27LSO-0096 would amount to an incremental levy or earmark on the 10% tax.

  • May 2026 minutes record funding from a fixed OSW tax allocation and lottery transfers, with county amounts too small to be effective.
  • WGC Director testified problem gaming is a growing concern requiring expanded funding, education and treatment access.
  • 27LSO-0096 would broaden funding to OSW, pari-mutuel and SBAG revenue; held for further study, but the Committee commits to continued work with the WGC, Lottery Corporation and Department of Health.
  • Operators must offer self-exclusion and RG tools under WGC OSW Rules; self-exclusion counts and helpline statistics are not published [n/a].

Advertising

Advertising rules are permissive with no ad cap, watershed or sponsorship ban, but the direction of travel is toward restriction.

  • Promotional credits are tax-deductible, making promotional intensity the main lever for operators' taxable margin and state receipts.
  • College-prop ban, felony sweepstakes draft, WGC oversight costs above $1m and interest in a dedicated RG funding stream point to more constraints in 2027–28.
  • The headline 10% rate is expected to remain untouched.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Cheapest untethered mobile licence in the US: $100,000 for five years, $50,000 renewal, no land-based partner, no cap — and a 10% tax on net proceeds with uncapped promo deductions that has delivered an effective ~7% of GGR (7.2% Oct-26, 7.1% CY2025). structural
  • Stable fiscal regime: the 2026 interim benchmarked other states' OSW tax rates and recommended no change; the only tax-rate draft (HHR 1.9%→2%) died for lack of a second. No Illinois/Ohio-style escalation is in the pipeline. structural
  • Still a growth market on a full-year basis: handle +19% (2023), +22% (2024), +12% (2025) and +6.9% YTD 2026 to $200.0m; lifetime handle crossed $1bn in October, the second $500m taking two years versus three for the first. mixed
  • A contestable #3 slot: BetMGM holds 12.1% of handle but posted a 0.69% hold and −$107k taxable revenue in October; Fanatics (6.2%) and Caesars (3.1%) are sub-scale. A disciplined entrant with national brand reach could take 10–15% of stakes without displacing the leaders. mixed
  • 18+ minimum age and 100% digital channel: wider addressable base than 21+ states and no retail incumbents to tether to or compete against; all acquisition is app-based. structural
  • Hold is healthy: 10.9% in October and 11.06% for CY2025, above the ~10% national average, reflecting parlay-heavy recreational play — supportive of GGR per dollar of handle for a well-priced book. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • The prize is tiny: ~$26m annual GGR (~$18m after promos) across five brands; October's whole market produced $1.67m of taxable revenue. State-specific marketing cannot be justified — Wyoming only works as a zero-marginal-cost extension of a national platform. structural
  • Entry barrier by design: an applicant must already operate OSW in at least three other US states, which excludes a new-to-US operator outright and makes Wyoming a late-stage, not early-stage, US market. structural
  • Growth has stalled: two consecutive YoY handle declines (Sept −14.2%, Oct −7.3%), GGR −12.4% and tax −9.0% in October; 2026 is tracking +3–5% after three years of double-digit growth, and all five brands shrank in October. watch
  • Casino-style product is heading toward criminalisation, not regulation: the Select Committee voted 6–0 to send a felony ban on online casino and sweepstakes operation to the 2027 session and rejected the regulatory alternative; regulated iGaming (HB 162) is dead. No cross-sell upside. structural
  • A responsible-gaming levy is on the table: 27LSO-0096 would draw on OSW revenue to fund problem-gambling programmes that the WGC Director calls under-resourced; held for study in October, but the Committee has committed to keep working on it with the Department of Health and the Lottery. watch
  • Per-operator tax with no cross-offset and promo-driven volatility: taxable revenue swings with bonus intensity (Sept tax +40% on −14% handle), and a losing month yields zero relief against other months' tax [carry-forward unverified]; DK+FD control 79% of stakes and set the promotional tempo. structural
  • HHR incumbents own the political agenda: ~$2.49bn of HHR handle versus $234.5m of sports means the Legislature's attention, tax design and lobbying weight sit with the three pari-mutuel permit holders; OSW has little voice when rules change. structural

Independent view. Rules: we expect 27LSO-0098 (felony prohibition of online casino and sweepstakes operation) to pass the 2027 General Session in some form — it has unanimous committee backing, regulator support and no organised in-state opposition beyond VGW's regulatory alternative — making Wyoming a closed market for casino-style online product through at least 2028. OSW tax: the 10% headline rate is secure for 2027; the live risk is an earmark or surcharge via a revived responsible-gaming funding bill (27LSO-0096) in 2027–28, which we would size at low single-digit percentage points of net proceeds if adopted — an estimate, not a proposal on the table. Product/marketing: expect continued tightening of the Catalog of Events (college props banned from 1 Oct 2026) and, if PG funding advances, RG-messaging and promotional conditions attached to it. Market: handle growth of 0–5% a year from here, GGR of ~$25–28m, DK/FD duopoly above 75% of stakes, and a sub-scale tail where at least one of BetMGM, Fanatics or Caesars is a candidate to exit or go passive if US-wide consolidation continues. Leverage for an entrant: nil fiscal headwind, nil structural tailwind; Wyoming is a licence to hold for national-footprint completeness, not a market to invest in.

What to watch next quarter

  • Management Council sponsorship decision and the 2027 General Session (opens January 2027) for 27LSO-0098 (online casino/sweepstakes felony ban) and 27LSO-0201 (background checks); watch for a floor amendment reviving the VGW regulatory model.
  • Any 2027 bill descended from 27LSO-0096 that earmarks or surcharges OSW net proceeds for problem-gambling funding — the only realistic change to the 10% tax base.
  • November–December 2026 WGC reports: Wyoming needs $34.5m to beat 2025's $234.5m record; a third consecutive YoY decline would confirm the market has plateaued.
  • BetMGM's position: a second month of negative taxable revenue or a fall below 10% handle share would signal retrenchment and open the #3 slot; DraftKings' share oscillation (57.8%→47.3%→51.7%) indicates promotional tempo.
  • Local-approval votes for existing off-track HHR sites ahead of the July 2027 application date, and any return of the 1.9%→2% HHR fee proposal — the state's dominant gaming revenue stream.
  • Prediction-market (event-contract) competition: a national legal battle the GI weekly flags; in a 600k-population state with 18+ age limits, unregulated sports event contracts are a direct substitute for licensed books [no Wyoming-specific enforcement action found in sources read].
