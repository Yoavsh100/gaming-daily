Allwyn International

Released 19 Nov 2026 · Currency EUR (tables in € millions; USD equivalents at EUR/USD 1.10 shown as ~$) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: ALWN (Euronext Athens) +0.7% intraday to €9.63 (~$10.6) at 15:55 Athens time versus a €9.56 prior close (day range €9.52–9.71; investing.com quoted +1.6% to €9.71 pre-market), one day after printing a 52-week low of €9.43 on 18 Nov; the shares are -50% from the €19.03 high of 2 Jan 2026 and -21.5% over the past month following the Brazil closure. The one-line verdict

Diversification carried the top line, mix ate the margin: Q3-26 net revenue +19% to €1,220m (~$1.34bn) but only +4% on a comparable basis, adj. EBITDA €396m (~$436m) +6% reported / +2% comparable, and margin -410bps to 32.5% as an 18%-margin PrizePicks, €9m of LottoItalia amortisation and a €12m jump in corporate costs diluted a record-jackpot comp. The real news is in the guidance wording — the "~37%" FY adj. EBITDA margin target of Q1/Q2 has quietly become "within consensus" (€1,769–1,891m / ~$1.95–2.08bn, i.e. ~35.6% at the midpoint) — while Brazil's closure, a UK National Lottery still shrinking online and 3.5x leverage leave the shares at a 52-week low despite a reaffirmed €1.00/share (~$1.10) distribution floor.

  • Headline: revenue +19%, adj. EBITDA +6%, margin -410bps. EBITDA €396m (~$436m) vs €374m, +2% comparable; margin 32.5% vs 36.6%. Investing.com cites revenue consensus of ~€1.23bn, so the top line was in line; management did not publish a quarterly EBITDA consensus, only a FY range.
  • Operating EBITDA +13% to €341m (~$375m) as add-backs fall away. EBITDA adjustments dropped to €55m (~$61m) from €73m (Q1-26 peak ~€107m): UK transition costs now nil, PrizePicks transaction/retention €9m, IWG acquisition accounting €3m — but the 'Allwyn brand initiative' is now €32m/quarter and the single largest add-back, which looks recurring in all but name.

EBITDA margin' language in the Q1 and Q2 releases has been replaced by 'within the range of consensus expectations' (company-compiled €1,769–1,891m / ~$1.95–2.08bn as of 13 Nov). At the midpoint (€1,830m) on €5.14bn revenue (+25%) that is ~35.6% — a ~140bps walk-down. Hitting 37% would need Q4 adj. EBITDA of ~€605m (+22% YoY), above the top of the consensus range.

EBITDA +26% pro forma to €43m (~$47m), but at a 17.8% margin; consolidating an 18%-margin DFS business against a 44%-margin European lottery base is mechanically dilutive.   • Thesis read-through. Diversification is delivering top-line resilience, but the earnings algorithm is migrating to a lower-margin, more acquisitive model (DFS, in-sourced tech, minority buy-ins) while the two cash engines that fund the €1/share floor — Betano and the UK — face regulatory and execution risk respectively. FY26 margin is heading to ~35.5%, leverage is stuck around 3.5x, and the equity is pricing the Brazil downside; the Q4 lottery seasonality and PrizePicks' NFL/NBA quarter are the near-term support.

3Segment deep-dive: Continental Europe (Austria, Czech Republic, Greece & Cyprus, Italy, Germany, Slovakia)

+3% net revenue against a +10% GGR comp; EBITDA flat ex-LottoItalia; iGaming +26% and online +8% carry a -8% lottery line.

Revenue and GGR

Net revenue grew 3% to €754m (~$829m) against a +10% GGR comp; Continental Europe remains the earnings engine at 62% of group revenue.

  • Adj. EBITDA €328m (~$361m) is 83% of group; net revenue +3% reported and constant currency.
  • Comp included segment GGR +10% and a record Tzoker jackpot in Greece/Cyprus in August 2025.
  • Austria led with +10%, Czech Republic +4%, Greece & Cyprus broadly stable.
  • GGR €1,161m (~$1.28bn), +1%; gaming taxes and good-cause contributions €462m (~$508m) absorbed 40% of GGR (+2%).
  • Non-gaming revenue +38% to €55m (~$61m); NGR only +1% at €699m (~$769m). Sequentially +3.1% versus Q2's €731m.

Product mix

iGaming +26% to €151m (~$166m) carried a -8% lottery line; sports betting +1% year-on-year but -10% quarter-on-quarter on weak margins.

  • iGaming +2.7% quarter-on-quarter; sports betting net revenue €130m (~$143m).
  • Stakes higher but win margins materially lower: strong July World Cup final margins offset by customer-friendly August and September; Q3-25 comp also weak.
  • Lottery net revenue -8% to €276m (~$304m) on Tzoker jackpot cycle; VLTs & casinos +2% at €142m (~$156m).
  • CFO: Czech Republic and Austria run roughly €450m iGaming against ~€30m sports revenue versus a 'good proposition' norm of 50/50.
  • This is the real strategic rationale for the Kambi deal and implies sportsbook is a long way from scale in these markets.

Digital and innovation

Online NGR rose 8% to €226m (~$249m), 32% of segment NGR versus 30% last year and 41% of online-eligible NGR.

  • Eurojackpot online launched in Greece in August, reaching one in three online players in its first month.
  • Greece's first Eurojackpot jackpot winner (€38m) drawn this week, which should help Q4 awareness.
  • Smaller innovations continue: Keno upgrade in Czech Republic, gold-prize scratchcard in Austria.
  • Next Lotto (German online lottery reseller) moved to 64.53% controlling stake in October for ~€10m (~$11m) after €3m contribution lifted interest to 46.29%; consolidates from Q4.

Profitability and costs

Adj. EBITDA fell 3% to €328m, 43.5% margin versus 46.2%; entire decline is the €11m fall in equity-method profit, mainly LottoItalia amortisation.

  • Margin -270bps year-on-year but +340bps quarter-on-quarter on seasonality and Q2 tax drag.
  • Share of profit of equity-method investees €8m vs €19m; €9m is higher LottoItalia (32.5% owned) licence-fee amortisation under licence renewed 30 November 2025, ~€36m annual drag.
  • CFO stressed LottoItalia will keep distributing ~100% of free cash flow, about €100m a year net to Allwyn; ex-LottoItalia aggregate EBITDA was flat.
  • Marketing +21% to €80m (~$88m), 10.6% of net revenue versus 9.1%; agents' commissions and materials +3%, personnel/other -2%.
  • No country-level EBITDA breakout; Greece & Cyprus most hit by margins. CAPEX €15m (~$17m), -17%; adj. EBITDA less CAPEX €313m (~$344m), -2%.

Portfolio and regulation

Australian casinos sold for AUD 103m with €3m gain; Austrian lottery licence extended to end-2028 with tender expected in 2027.

  • Australian casino sale in August for AUD 103m (€63m / ~$69m); Brussels casino concession awarded to another operator from July 2027 (less than 1% of segment EBITDA in 2025).
  • Austrian tender carries ~€40m (~$44m) fee on award for 15-year term, bid alongside 30%+ partner ÖBAG; Greece's legacy-games licence falls due in 2030.
  • FY26 carries c.€60m (~$66m) of previously flagged one-off revenue impacts (c.-2%).
  • Our read: mid-single-digit NGR grower with lottery cycling tough jackpot comps into seasonally strong Q4.
  • Margin ambition should be reset to the low-40s rather than mid-40s given LottoItalia amortisation and rising marketing intensity.

4Segment deep-dive: North America (PrizePicks, Allwyn LS Group/Illinois, IWG) — presented on 100% basis

PrizePicks re-accelerated to +29% cFX with EBITDA +26% at a 17.8% margin; Packs adds a non-sports vertical; Q3 is the seasonal trough ahead of the NFL/NBA quarter.

Revenue re-acceleration

Net revenue rose 21% cFX to €242m (~$266m), with PrizePicks +29% cFX to €183m (~$201m), a sharp re-acceleration from Q2's +3%.

  • Reported growth 20% segment, 24% PrizePicks; Q2 had been depressed by sports outcomes.
  • Customer activity (DFS entry fees plus real cash in prediction-market contracts, not notional volume) grew more than 30% year-on-year; no material sports-outcome effect.
  • World Cup boost in July offset by later NFL start.
  • Segment GGR €190m (~$209m), +30% cFX; non-gaming revenue €59m (~$65m), +7%. Illinois fee and allowance are pass-throughs depressing margin ratio, not absolute EBITDA.
  • Sequentially net revenue fell 18% from Q2's €294m on the DFS calendar.

EBITDA and costs

Adj. EBITDA rose 26% to €43m (~$47m) at a 17.8% margin, driven by operating leverage on marketing; Q3 is structurally the DFS low season.

  • Margin +100bps year-on-year but -1,760bps versus Q2's €104m / 35.4% on seasonality; pro forma prior year €34m.
  • Marketing services +14% to €75m (~$83m), 31% of net revenue versus 33%; materials/consumables +12% to €74m; personnel/other -2% at €65m.
  • Operating EBITDA €30m versus €5m as adjustments halved to €13m (PrizePicks transaction/retention €9m, IWG put-option €3m, litigation €1m) from €29m.
  • CFO conceded a 'significant part' of Q1–Q3 non-marketing cost dynamics were acquisition-related one-offs.
  • CAPEX €13m (~$14m), +44%; adj. EBITDA less CAPEX €30m (~$33m), +20%. Nine-month: €775m (~$853m) revenue, €222m (~$244m) adj. EBITDA, 28.6% margin.
“differentiated customer acquisition and retention model”Management

Products and trading

DFS remains the biggest line; Packs collectible cards launched in October add a year-round non-sports vertical, with first contribution in Q4.

  • Prediction markets reported inside DFS; PrizePicks acts as broker earning commission with no sports-outcome margin volatility, likened by CFO to fee income.
  • Fully blended line-ups (player, team, culture picks) launched ahead of NFL; Packs has physically deliverable graded cards with 'grail' inventory up to $8m (a ~$3m Dylan Harper card from a $25 pack).
  • Packs already shows majority of loyal DFS customers participating, dormant reactivation and new-audience acquisition; logic is better retention, lower marketing per active, recycled winnings.
  • Monthly actives grew sequentially from August low; October saw record activity with NFL/NHL/NBA live; November month-to-date actives up double digits year-on-year.
  • No disclosure of actives, ARPU, handle or promotional intensity; 'leading position in DFS' claim rests on 24-month app-download data.

M&A and outlook

Quarter de-risks the 'PrizePicks stalled' worry; 18% margin is a trough, but Q4 marketing step-up is the swing factor for FY EBITDA.

  • Remaining 30% of IWG bought in October for USD 122m (€107m / ~$118m), payable Q1-27 after put-option exercise.
  • Kambi deal: perpetual global sportsbook source-code licence for €50m (~$55m) over three years to 2029, team transferring; gives optionality for a US sportsbook 'if we strategically decide'.

  • Gap between GGR and net-revenue declines reflects licence incentive model, including recovery since Q2 of part of the ~£450m technology-transformation investment.
