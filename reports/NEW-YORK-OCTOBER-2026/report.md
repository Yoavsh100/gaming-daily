# New York () — OCTOBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in millions unless stated) · Results released 6–9 Nov 2026 (NYSGC statewide monthly reports for October 2026)**

> October 2026 was a record volume month for New York's mobile sportsbooks ($2.79bn handle, GGR $258.1m, 51% tax = $131.7m) and the first October in which Resorts World NYC reported as a full commercial casino, lifting statewide casino GGR 248% to $194.9m — but the underlying story is a high-tax, slow-to-liberalise market: FYTD mobile GGR is down ~3% on weaker hold, VLT net win is being cannibalised by RWNYC's conversion, iGaming is dead until at least 2027, and the state is simultaneously fighting prediction markets in court and its own flagship casino over $80.6m of unpaid racing-support levies. For an operator, New York is scale without margin: the largest US sports-betting pool, an eight-operator oligopoly where FanDuel/DraftKings hold 71% of handle, and a 51% GGR tax that leaves little room for a new entrant.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Regulator and framework.** The New York State Gaming Commission (NYSGC; chair Brian O'Dwyer, executive director Robert Williams) regulates commercial casinos (Racing, Pari-Mutuel Wagering and Breeding Law, Art. 13, authorised by the 2013 constitutional amendment permitting up to seven commercial casinos), mobile sports wagering (2021 budget law; live since 8 Jan 2022), video lottery gaming (Tax Law §1612), the NY Lottery, pari-mutuel racing/ADW and charitable gaming. Tribal Class III gaming (Seneca, Oneida, St Regis Mohawk) sits under federal compacts and publishes no GGR — treat as n/a.
- **Market size, October 2026.** Reported regulated win across the three NYSGC-published verticals was ~$585m (est., sum of parts): mobile sports GGR $258.1m, commercial casino GGR $194.9m (incl. $0.6m retail sports) and VLT net win $132.1m. The comparable October 2025 figure was ~$505m (est.), i.e. +16% YoY, but ~two-thirds of the increase is the reclassification of Resorts World NYC (RWNYC) from VLT to commercial casino plus its new live table games, not organic growth.
- **Mobile sports wagering set a US record.** Handle of $2,787,474,078 (+5.5% YoY vs $2.64bn in Oct-25, the prior record; +16.1% MoM vs $2.40bn) with GGR of $258,123,128 (+8.1% YoY), the third-highest month since launch. Hold was 9.26% vs 9.04% a year ago and only 6.6% in September. Net revenue to education (the 51% tax plus $33.6k of prior-period adjustments) was $131,676,346, up from $121.8m in Oct-25.
- **But fiscal-year revenue is going backwards on hold.** April–October 2026 handle of $15.35bn is +6.7% YoY ($14.39bn), yet GGR of $1.37bn is ~3.5% below the $1.41bn of the same months in 2025 because hold slipped to 8.90% from 9.83%. Fiscal tax receipts are $25.1m behind the FY2025/26 pace at the half-year, even though calendar-year tax has already topped $1bn for the third straight year.
- **Operator landscape — a two-firm market with Fanatics as the only mover.** Eight licensees: FanDuel $1,022.7m handle (36.7% share; GGR $113.9m, 44.1% of GGR, 11.14% hold), DraftKings $948.7m (34.0%; GGR $81.9m, -1.2%; 8.63% hold), Fanatics $321.4m (+56.3% YoY, 11.5% share; GGR $24.0m), BetMGM $218.2m (+16.4%; $15.6m), Caesars $175.6m (flat; $13.1m), BetRivers $50.5m (-4.8%; $4.5m), theScore Bet $35.9m (-40.5% vs ESPN Bet's Oct-25; $3.6m) and Bally Bet $14.5m (-12.3%; $1.5m). FanDuel+DraftKings = 70.7% of handle (73.6% a year ago); Fanatics took ~80% of the market's $144.8m YoY handle increase.
- **Commercial casino GGR trebled on RWNYC's conversion.** Five commercial casinos won $194.9m (+248% vs $56.0m; +5.8% vs $184.3m in September): slots/ETG $135.3m (+214.1%), table games $58.3m (+399.6%), poker $0.74m, retail sports $0.59m. RWNYC alone took $134.3m (69% of the total; slots/ETG $89.3m, tables $45.0m on an average 275 tables vs 242 in September). The four upstate casinos won $60.6m (+8.2% YoY): Rivers Schenectady $20.2m (+14.2%), Resorts World Catskills $15.7m (+10.6%), del Lago $15.0m (+3.9%), Tioga Downs $9.7m (+0.5%).
- **Casino tax take $78.8m — and our bottom-up reconciliation matches exactly.** Applying RWNYC's bid rates (56% slots / 30% tables) and the upstate rates (30% slots/ETG, 10% tables, poker and sports) to October's product split yields $63.5m + $15.3m = $78.8m, the NYSGC-reported figure. Effective statewide rate 40.4% of GGR; RWNYC's effective rate ~47%.
- **RWNYC is tracking well below its bid.** Since reporting as a commercial casino in April, RWNYC has generated $794.3m GGR through October (monthly range $122–136m since May); its application projected $2.2bn of GGR by 2027 versus a current ~$1.5–1.6bn run-rate (est.). Between May and October it lifted average daily slots/ETGs 61% to 4,024 but machine revenue fell 9.5% and win per unit per day dropped from $1,275 to $716 — classic floor-expansion dilution. Through 15 Nov the property had 4,137 machines and 345 tables.
- **Downstate licensing is done, but two of three resorts are years away.** The NYSGC unanimously awarded all three downstate licences on 15 Dec 2025: RWNYC (Genting; $5.5bn expansion), Metropolitan Park (Hard Rock/Steve Cohen, Willets Point; $8.1bn) and Bally's Bronx (Ferry Point; $4bn) — $17.6bn of committed investment and a $500m minimum licence fee each. Metropolitan Park and Bally's target 2030 openings; the board's consultants projected $5.5bn of combined annual GGR by 2033 and ~$7bn of incremental gaming tax over 2027–36. NYC's Public Design Commission has twice deferred its design vote on Metropolitan Park (late Oct, 23 Nov); a third attempt is set for 30 Nov, and a residents' lawsuit against the licence (filed 20 Mar 2026) is pending.
- **Tax regime by vertical.** Mobile sports: 51% of GGR (cash basis), $25m one-off licence fee per operator, 10-year term [licence fee/term from the enabling statute, not re-verified this session]. Retail sports at casinos: 10% of GGR. Upstate casinos: 30% slots/ETG (all four petitioned down from 37–45% under the 2021 amendment), 10% tables; 80% of tax to education/property-tax relief, 10% host municipality/county, 10% regional counties. Downstate: statutory minimum 25% slots / 10% tables — RWNYC bid 56%/30%, Metropolitan Park 25%/10%, Bally's 30%/10%, with downstate proceeds split between education and the MTA. VLTs: tiered statutory split of net win (Empire City: education 54–60%, agent commission 26–32%, 10% marketing allowance, 4% racing support).
- **The racing-support dispute is the live fiscal risk.** Downstate licensees must fund >$150m a year of horse-racing support payments to NYRA; because its rivals will not open until ~2030, RWNYC bears the whole obligation (>$500m cumulatively). Genting argues the levy sits inside its 56% slot rate and has been netting it against tax, leaving $80.6m of arrears through October, rising ~$13m a month and potentially >$180m by June 2027; paying both would push its effective slot burden to ~72%. An October interim agreement has RWNYC paying NYRA directly, but the state has not conceded the deficiency, the MTA stands to lose up to ~$150m, and RWNYC has threatened to shelve its $3.3bn expansion. The deal lapses at the end of the June 2027 legislative session.
- **VLT net win fell 37% to $132.1m on the RWNYC reclassification; +4% like-for-like** across 11,373 machines at the eight remaining facilities (+5.8% MoM). Empire City (MGM, Yonkers) led with $52.26m (+2.5% YoY, +7.6% MoM; 4,516 VGMs at $373 win/unit/day; $26.4m to education, $20.6m agent commission), Jake's 58 (Suffolk OTB) $26.0m; Saratoga ran ~$13.6m in September. Empire City did not bid for a downstate licence and remains a VLT facility.
- **Lottery.** NY Lottery sales were $565.5m for the four weeks to 31 October, including $307.5m of instant tickets; the Lottery remains the largest US state lottery and all VLT education contributions flow through it. Full-FY sales: n/a in this review.
- **iGaming is off the table until 2027 at the earliest.** Sen. Addabbo's S2614 (online slots/table games, NYSGC licensing, in-state live-dealer studios, ~$475m/yr projected tax) was shelved before the 4 June 2026 session close after Gov. Hochul declined to engage; her office said only that she would 'review any legislation that passes'. No regulated online casino exists; NYSGC licensing covers land-based casinos, VLTs, racing, lottery and mobile sports only.
- **Prediction markets are the state's enforcement front.** NYSGC issued a cease-and-desist to Kalshi on 24 Oct 2025; a federal judge denied Kalshi's emergency injunction; the Attorney General and Governor sued Kalshi in state court on 31 Jul 2026 (injunction, disgorgement, treble fines — press tallies ≥$36bn incl. $100k per illegal bet), the AG sued Coinbase and Gemini on 21 Apr 2026 and has moved against Polymarket. On 23 Nov 2026 Justice Melissa Crane denied the AG's TRO, leaving sports contracts live in New York pending a January 2027 hearing. S9414 (ban on sports/election contracts) cleared committee 6–0 on 20 May 2026 but never reached the floor.
- **Other direction of travel.** New York has enacted a ban on online sweepstakes casinos (new RPWBL §912) [enacted late 2025; not re-verified this session]; Hochul backed 2026 measures for a gambling mental-health institute and to keep minors off sports-betting apps; the 18+ age for VLTs vs 21+ for casinos/mobile remains a legislative target. Advertising rules bar targeting under-21s and 'risk-free' language [NYSGC 2024 regulations; not re-verified]. Credit-card funding of mobile accounts remains permitted, with recurring bills to prohibit it [unverified].
- **Payments reality.** This is a card/ACH/wallet market (debit, ACH, PayPal/Venmo-type wallets, PayNearMe/cash at casino cages); there is no mobile-money dimension. The 51% tax makes payment-processing cost a material share of operator contribution, which is why promo spend and deposit incentives in NY are thin relative to lower-tax states.
- **Read-through for an operator.** New York is the deepest US sports pool ($100bn cumulative mobile handle passed in July 2026; $9.1bn cumulative GGR; $4.65bn to education) but a closed one: no new mobile licence window exists, the 51% rate and $25m fee were set via a 'highest-tax-wins' RFA, and sub-scale books (BetRivers, theScore, Bally Bet: 3.6% of handle combined) are shrinking. Realistic entry routes are B2B supply (platform, trading, content) to the eight incumbents, retail/sportsbook partnerships with the three downstate licensees, or waiting for an iGaming bill — which would most likely allocate skins to existing casino, racino, tribal and mobile licensees rather than new entrants.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Mobile sports wagering

*Record $2.79bn handle and $258.1m GGR; FanDuel/DraftKings 71% of handle, Fanatics the only share gainer; FYTD GGR down ~3.5% on hold.*

### Volume and revenue

**October mobile handle of $2,787,474,078 was the highest monthly figure recorded by any US state; GGR $258,123,128 rose 8.1% YoY.**

- Handle up 5.5% on October 2025's prior record of ~$2.64bn and 16.1% on September's $2.40bn.
- GGR up 61.9% MoM, third-best month since January 2022 launch; jump almost entirely hold normalisation (9.26% vs 6.6% in September).
- In September six of eight books earned less than a year earlier.
- Weekly data: $619.4m/$49.2m (w/e 4 Oct, 7.9% hold), $615.1m/$63.3m (w/e 18 Oct), fiscal-year-high $656.2m/$79.4m (w/e 25 Oct, 12.1% hold).
- Including $5.0m retail handle and $0.6m retail GGR at upstate casino books, total October sports wagering was $2,792.4m handle and $258.7m GGR.

### Tax

**October 'net revenue to education' was $131,676,346 at the 51% rate, but FY2026/27 receipts are $25.1m behind last year's pace on compressed hold.**

- 51% on mobile GGR, cash basis, no promotional deductions; October figure is 51% × GGR + $33,551 prior-period adjustment, versus $121.8m in October 2025.
- Implied October tax by operator (est.): FanDuel $58.1m, DraftKings $41.8m, Fanatics $12.2m, BetMGM $8.0m, Caesars $6.7m, three small books $4.9m combined.
- Calendar-2026 tax has passed $1bn for a third consecutive year.
- April–October GGR $1.37bn on $15.35bn handle (8.90%) versus $1.41bn on $14.39bn (9.83%) a year earlier.
- Handle growth of ~7% is not translating into revenue for the state; at 51%, every point of hold lost is borne roughly half by Albany.

### Market shares

**FanDuel and DraftKings hold 71% of handle; Fanatics was the only share gainer, adding $115.7m of the market's $144.8m YoY handle increase.**

- FanDuel $1,022.7m (+1.7% YoY), only book ever to clear $1bn monthly in New York; handle share slipped 38.1% to 36.7%, but 11.14% hold gave 44.1% of GGR ($113.9m, +13.3%).
- DraftKings record $948.7m (+1.1%; 34.0% share) but 8.63% hold cut GGR 1.2% to $81.9m.
- Fanatics $321.4m (+56.3%) for 11.5% share; 7.48% hold (9.08% a year ago) limited GGR growth to +28.8% ($24.0m).
- BetMGM +16.4% to $218.2m (GGR $15.6m, 7.1% hold); Caesars flat at $175.6m (GGR $13.1m, -0.6%); BetRivers $50.5m (-4.8%; GGR $4.5m); theScore Bet $35.9m (-40.5% vs ESPN Bet; GGR $3.6m, +5.7%, 10% hold); Bally Bet $14.5m (-12.3%; GGR $1.5m, +27.4%).
- Three sub-scale books hold 3.6% of handle, confirming the 51% rate and no promo deductions make buying share nearly impossible; Fanatics is the exception, funding share from group-level balance sheet.

### Licence structure

**Nine platform licences were awarded via the 2021 RFA; eight live skins remain and no mechanism exists for additional licences absent new legislation.**

- The RFA scored bids on tax rate, producing the 51% outcome.
- WynnBET and Resorts World Bet have exited; ESPN Bet was rebranded to theScore Bet.
- Each licensee paid a $25m fee for a 10-year term from January 2022 [statute, not re-verified this session].
- Cumulative mobile handle crossed $100bn in July 2026 with $9.12bn GGR and $4.65bn remitted to education (secondary compilation of NYSGC data).

### Product and content

**Same-game parlays are the hold engine: FanDuel's 11%+ hold versus Caesars/BetMGM/Fanatics at ~7% is almost entirely mix.**

- Pre-match, in-play and parlay betting permitted on professional and out-of-state college sports; in-state college teams and college player props prohibited.
- Official-league-data requirements apply to in-play markets [statutory framework, not re-verified].
- Retail sportsbooks at the four upstate casinos (10% tax) are immaterial at $0.6m GGR.

### Commercial implication

**New York is a volume-not-margin market where a top-two operator earns a thin positive margin and everyone below #3 is near or below break-even.**

- Contribution math: 9% blended hold, 51% tax, ~1.5–2% of handle in payment/processing and platform fees, modest promo.
- B2Tech angle (i): B2B trading/risk, pricing and SGP content licensed to incumbents.
- B2Tech angle (ii): retail sportsbook or kiosk partnership with a downstate licensee once Metropolitan Park and Bally's open.
- B2Tech angle (iii): positioning for any future iGaming skin allocation, which Addabbo's S2614 would have tied to existing licensees.

### Segment outlook

**November and December should be the year's two highest handle months; if hold reverts to 9–10%, Q4 FY2026/27 tax should recover the $25m shortfall.**

- NFL/NBA/college overlap drives the seasonal peak.
- Structural growth is now mid-single-digit handle with hold-driven GGR volatility.
- Main swing factor is the Kalshi litigation: if sports event contracts remain untaxed and unlicensed through 2027, handle leakage from regulated books is a real, if unquantified, risk.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Commercial casinos (downstate & upstate)

*RWNYC's first October as a full casino lifts statewide GGR to $194.9m and tax to $78.8m; the property is scaling floor faster than win, and owes the state $80.6m in disputed racing levies.*

### Statewide result

**Five commercial casinos reported $194.9m GGR in October, up 248% from $56.0m, with gaming tax of ~$78.8m at an effective 40.4%.**

- GGR up 5.8% from $184.3m in September.
- Slots/ETG $135.3m (+214.1% vs $43.1m), live tables $58.3m (+399.6% vs $11.7m), poker $740,441, retail sports wagering $593,224.
- Bottom-up reconstruction: 56%/30% on RWNYC's $89.3m slots and $45.0m tables = $63.5m; 30%/10% on upstate $46.0m slots, $13.3m tables, $1.3m poker/sports = $15.3m.
- Reconstruction matches the Commission's figure to the decimal, confirming both rate structure and product split.

### Resorts World NYC

**RWNYC's October GGR was $134.3m (+6.0% MoM), but the floor is scaling faster than win, with win per unit per day down from $1,275 to $716.**

- Began reporting as a commercial casino in April 2026 after 15 Dec 2025 licence award; $89.3m slots/ETG and $45.0m tables on an average 275 tables (242 in September).
- Cumulative commercial GGR $794.3m through October (average $113.5m/month, range $122–136m since May) and $859.6m through 15 November, with 4,137 machines and 345 tables.
- Machines up 61% May–October to 4,024 average daily units while machine revenue fell 9.5%.
- Run-rate ~$1.5–1.6bn (est.) against bid projection of $2.2bn GGR by 2027.
- Implied October 2025 VLT net win ~$83m (est.); conversion plus tables lifted site win ~60%, short of the step-change the state's $5.5bn-by-2033 downstate forecast requires.

### Racing-support dispute

**RWNYC is $80.6m in arrears on disputed racing levies, growing ~$13m a month and projected to exceed $180m by June 2027.**

- Downstate licensees must fund >$150m a year to NYRA; RWNYC carries the entire obligation (cumulatively >$500m) until Metropolitan Park and Bally's open (~2030).
- Genting says its 56% slot bid included racing support and payments are deductible from gaming tax; the state says they are separate and both are required by law.
- RWNYC says stacking the levy would take its effective slot tax to ~72%; Genting has signalled it could abandon the $3.3bn phase-two expansion.
- Spring 2026 legislation let NYSGC pay NYRA from RWNYC's tax receipts; October 2026 interim agreement has RWNYC paying NYRA directly while remitting only part of its tax, with the Governor's office reserving the deficiency claim.
- The MTA stands to lose up to ~$150m; the interim deal expires at end of the June 2027 session, making this a 2027 budget/legislative item.

### Upstate casinos

**The four upstate properties won $60.6m, +8.2% YoY, suggesting no cannibalisation from RWNYC yet.**

- Rivers Schenectady $20.2m (+14.2%), Resorts World Catskills $15.7m (+10.6%), del Lago $15.0m (+3.9%), Tioga Downs $9.7m (+0.5%).
- All pay 30% on slots/ETG (reduced from 37–45% via petitions under the 2021 law) and 10% on tables and sports; tax allocated 80% education/property-tax relief, 10% host municipality/county, 10% regional counties.
- A 2025 study projected three downstate casinos could cut upstate casino revenue by up to 76%.
- RWNYC's catchment is NYC proper; Resorts World Catskills is the most exposed once Metropolitan Park opens.

### Pipeline

**Metropolitan Park ($8.1bn) and Bally's Bronx ($4bn) target 2030 openings; downstate tax policy is the single most consequential open item in NY land-based gaming.**

- Metropolitan Park (Hard Rock/Cohen; ~50 acres of Citi Field parking) licensed at 25%/10%; Bally's Bronx (Ferry Point) at 30%/10%; each paid the $500m minimum fee.
- Metropolitan Park's Public Design Commission review deferred on 23 Nov for the second time after five hours of testimony; vote set for 30 Nov; the PDC cannot revoke the state licence.
- A Queens residents' suit against the licence award (20 Mar 2026) is pending.
- RWNYC's supplemental application asks that its 56%/30% rates be equalised downward once lower-taxed competitors open; the state has not granted it.

### Commercial implication

**Downstate is a licensed three-player oligopoly for 15–25 years; entry is via supply or partnering with licensees on sportsbook/retail.**

- Supply routes: systems, EGMs, ETGs, cashless/payments, loyalty.
- RWNYC data caution anyone modelling NYC: floor expansion runs ahead of demand at ~$716 win/unit/day.
- State forecasts assume a tripling of downstate GGR that the first eight months do not yet support.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*Closed licence stacks in every vertical; the highest headline sports-betting tax in the US (51%), bespoke bid-based casino rates (25–56% slots), and statutory revenue splits that make Albany the senior partner in every operator's P&L.*

### Constitutional base

**Gambling is constitutionally prohibited except as expressly authorised; online casino gaming has no authorisation while online lottery and ADW are permitted.**

- Authorised: Lottery (1966), pari-mutuel racing, charitable gaming, and via the 2013 amendment up to seven commercial casinos 'as prescribed by the legislature'.
- Upstate NY Gaming Economic Development Act (2013) created four upstate licences; FY2023 budget (April 2022) released the remaining three downstate.
- Mobile sports wagering authorised in the FY2022 budget (April 2021) as an extension of casino sports wagering with servers at licensed casinos.

### Mobile sports wagering

**The 51% GGR rate, set by the November 2021 competitive RFA, has survived every budget cycle since 2022 despite operator lobbying for promotional deductions.**

- Cash basis, no promo deduction, no carry-forward of negative months other than prior-period adjustments; $25m fee per platform for a 10-year term [not re-verified this session].
- Nine licences issued; eight remain active; no provision for additional mobile licences.
- Commission publishes weekly and monthly handle, GGR and 'net revenue to education' by operator, unusually transparent by US standards.
- Retail sportsbooks at the four upstate casinos pay 10% of GGR.

### Commercial casinos

**Upstate casinos pay 30%/10% after petitions; downstate rates were locked by bid at RWNYC 56%/30%, Bally's 30%/10%, Metropolitan Park 25%/10%.**

- Upstate statutory slot rates of 37–45% made reducible to 30% by petition in the 2021 amendment; allocated 80/10/10 to education-property tax relief, host locality, regional counties.
- Downstate: $500m minimum licence fee, minimum 25% slots / 10% tables; December 2025 awards fixed rates for the licence term (15–25 years per trade reporting; [term not re-verified]).
- Downstate tax split between education and the MTA.
- Racing-support obligation (>$150m/year, borne solely by RWNYC until ~2030) sits on top and is in dispute; RWNYC's request to harmonise rates downward has not been granted.

### Video lottery

**VLT operators are effectively commissioned agents of the Lottery under a statutory tiered schedule with no bid-based tax.**

- Facility licences issued to racetracks and OTB corporations.
- Empire City split: education 54–60%, agent commission 26–32%, 10% marketing allowance, 4% racing support/purses; $19.6m/year host aid to Yonkers.
- Minimum age 18.

### Tribal gaming

**Seneca, Oneida and St Regis Mohawk nations operate Class III casinos under federal compacts; any iGaming bill must navigate tribal exclusivity.**

- Compacts include exclusivity zones and revenue-share payments to the state.
- GGR is not disclosed (n/a).

### Sweepstakes and unlicensed

**New York's sweepstakes prohibition (RPWBL §912) and prediction-market litigation channel real-money play into licensed products, but with no iGaming product to channel it into.**

- Enacted late 2025 [not re-verified this session], joining states closing the dual-currency loophole.
- Licensed operators and the Lottery's own promotions are carved out.

### Commercial implication

**The regime rewards incumbency and balance sheet; B2Tech should model New York as a supply and partnership market.**

- Entry fees are high ($25m mobile, $500m downstate casino); tax rates set by auction, so late entrants inherit rates optimised by winners.
- The 2027 session (racing-support fix, possible downstate rate harmonisation, iGaming reboot, prediction-market bill) is the next window in which the rules can move.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Regulatory direction: iGaming, prediction markets, responsible gambling & payments

*Expansion is stalled (no iGaming before 2027), enforcement is escalating (Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase/Gemini, sweepstakes), and the fiscal fight over downstate levies will dominate the 2027 session.*

### iGaming

**Addabbo's S2614 died without a hearing or vote before 4 June 2026; New York stays the largest US state without online casino through at least FY2027/28.**

- Bill covered online slots, blackjack, roulette, baccarat, live dealer (in-state studios), NYSGC licensing, skins for casinos, racinos, tribes and mobile operators; sponsor projected ~$475m/year of tax.
- Addabbo shelved it after Gov. Hochul declined to engage; he intends to reintroduce in 2027.
- Governor instead backed a gambling mental-health institute and measures to keep minors off sports-betting apps.
- iGaming realistically requires executive sponsorship in an Executive Budget.

> “will review any legislation that passes” — Gov. Hochul

### Prediction markets

**On 23 Nov 2026 Justice Melissa Crane denied the state's TRO against Kalshi; the case proceeds to a January 2027 hearing.**

- Sequence: NYSGC cease-and-desist to Kalshi (24 Oct 2025); Kalshi sues in SDNY, emergency injunction denied; AG James sues Coinbase and Gemini (21 Apr 2026); S9414 advances 6–0 in committee (20 May 2026) but stalls.
- Governor and AG sue Kalshi in state court (31 Jul 2026) seeking injunction, disgorgement, restitution and treble fines (press estimates ≥$36bn including $100,000 per unlawful wager); Kalshi removes to federal court.
- July 2026 federal ruling holds the state can reach sports contracts; AG also moves against Polymarket; AG refiles TRO to bar contracts 'within or from New York' and to under-21s.
- Kalshi and Polymarket are headquartered in New York, so a 'from New York' injunction would have national reach.
- Untaxed, 18+ sports event contracts compete directly with a product taxed at 51% and restricted to 21+.

### Responsible gambling

**New York's RG regime is light on affordability checks and heavy on advertising-content rules and age-gating compared with the UK or Spain.**

- Advertising rules prohibit targeting under-21s (including campuses), bar 'risk-free'/'free' bet language, require problem-gambling messaging and HOPEline number [2024 rules; not re-verified].
- Operators fund problem-gambling services via tax allocation and must offer deposit, wager and time limits plus statewide self-exclusion.
- 2026 session produced the mental-health-institute initiative; raising VLT age from 18 to 21 and affordability-style measures remain live proposals.

### Payments

**At a 51% tax, processing costs of 1.5–3% of deposits are a material share of operator margin, so NY books push ACH and wallets.**

- Funding methods: debit cards, ACH/online banking, PayPal/Venmo-type wallets, prepaid Play+ cards, cash at casino cages (PayNearMe).
- Credit cards not statutorily barred for mobile wagering; bills to prohibit credit-card funding introduced but not enacted [unverified].
- No mobile-money layer; cashless gaming permitted on casino floors and being deployed at RWNYC [unverified detail].

### Fiscal politics

**Albany will protect education/MTA receipts first: a lower RWNYC tax rate is low probability, a legislated NYRA-payment fix is moderate probability.**

- 2027 session item (i): RWNYC racing-support settlement, with interim agreement expiring and arrears ~$180m.
- Item (ii): RWNYC's request to harmonise downstate rates downward, which would cut receipts from the only operating downstate casino.
- Item (iii): Executive Budget treatment of gaming revenue after FYTD mobile tax slipped $25m behind plan.
- The moderate-probability fix would let the Commission pay NYRA from the commercial gaming revenue fund, as RWNYC proposed in June 2026.

### Enforcement posture

**The state is actively defending the regulated perimeter, but slow court action means leakage persists for at least one more NFL season.**

- Commission tools: cease-and-desist letters, licence conditions, referral to the AG.
- AG has become the primary enforcer against unlicensed online activity (prediction markets, crypto exchanges, offshore).
- 14 months from C&D to a January 2027 hearing.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale: October's $2.79bn mobile handle is a US state record and the FYTD pool is $15.35bn (+6.7%); cumulative mobile handle passed $100bn in July 2026. Any B2B product that improves hold or trading efficiency for the eight incumbents is addressing the deepest sportsbook revenue pool in the country. _(structural)_
- Downstate casino build-out is now licensed and funded: $17.6bn of committed investment (Metropolitan Park $8.1bn, RWNYC $5.5bn, Bally's $4bn), $1.5bn of licence fees already paid, and the state's own consultants modelling $5.5bn of downstate GGR by 2033 — a decade-long supply, systems and partnership pipeline. _(structural)_
- RWNYC's live tables are working: $45.0m of table GGR in October (23% of its total) from a standing start in April, with 345 tables by mid-November — evidence that NYC's table demand was unmet under the VLT-only regime. _(early)_
- Fanatics proves share can still move: +56% YoY handle to an 11.5% share, capturing ~80% of market growth, shows that distribution (retail/merch base) can dent the duopoly even at a 51% tax — relevant to any entrant with an existing NY customer base. _(mixed)_
- The state is actively defending the licensed perimeter: sweepstakes banned, AG litigation against Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and Gemini, and a Commission cease-and-desist regime — a tailwind for licensed operators if the January 2027 hearing goes the state's way. _(watch)_
- Upstate casinos grew 8.2% YoY with no cannibalisation yet from RWNYC, and Empire City VLT win rose 2.5% despite a full casino 15 miles away — the downstate conversion appears, so far, to be expanding the market rather than redistributing it. _(early)_
- Unusually transparent data: weekly operator-level handle, GGR and tax disclosure allows precise market-share and hold benchmarking that most jurisdictions (and all African markets in our coverage) do not offer. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- The 51% mobile tax with no promotional deduction leaves no profitable path for a challenger: the three sub-scale books hold 3.6% of handle and are shrinking (theScore -40.5%, Bally Bet -12.3%, BetRivers -4.8%); two of nine original licensees have already exited. _(structural)_
- Hold compression is eroding the state's take: FYTD GGR -3.5% on +6.7% handle (hold 8.90% vs 9.83%), tax $25.1m behind plan — a revenue-hungry Albany is more likely to tighten (e.g., prediction-market bans, VLT age rise) than to cut rates or allow promo deductions. _(watch)_
- RWNYC's $80.6m racing-support arrears (rising ~$13m/month, >$180m by June 2027) and its 72%-effective-rate argument show the downstate tax stack is unresolved; the outcome will set the economics for all three downstate licensees and the MTA/education revenue they fund. _(governance)_
- Downstate ramp is below bid: RWNYC's ~$1.5–1.6bn run-rate (est.) vs a $2.2bn 2027 projection, with win/unit/day down 44% (to $716) as the floor expanded 61% — a warning for the state's $5.5bn-by-2033 downstate forecast and for suppliers sizing orders on bid numbers. _(mixed)_
- No iGaming before 2027 at the earliest and no executive sponsorship; the largest US state market by sports handle has zero regulated online casino GGR, and any eventual bill is likely to allocate skins to incumbents. _(structural)_
- Prediction markets remain live and untaxed in New York after the 23 Nov TRO denial; with Kalshi and Polymarket both headquartered in the state, the January 2027 hearing is binary for both leakage risk and the precedent for other states. _(watch)_
- Two of the three downstate resorts face local friction: Metropolitan Park's design vote has been deferred twice and a residents' lawsuit is pending, pushing 2030 opening risk to the right and extending RWNYC's solo liability for racing support. _(one-off)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The October print moves our FY2026/27 view in two directions: mobile handle growth is running ahead of our prior mid-single-digit assumption (+6.7% FYTD) but GGR is below it because hold has fallen ~90bp, so the state's mobile tax line will finish close to flat on FY2025/26 (~$1.1bn) rather than up. On land-based, RWNYC's conversion lifts statewide commercial casino GGR to a ~$2.2–2.3bn annualised run-rate (est.) from ~$0.7bn in FY2025/26, with upstate flat-to-up mid-single digits and VLT net win settling at ~$1.5–1.6bn annualised (est.) ex-RWNYC. The moving parts are hold normalisation in Q4, RWNYC's table ramp versus slot dilution, and — most importantly — the legislative resolution of the racing-support levy, which determines whether downstate gaming tax lands near 47% or 56%+ of RWNYC's slot win.

- **FYTD mobile GGR (Apr–Oct 26):** $1.37bn (-3.5% YoY; hold 8.90% vs 9.83% — tax $25.1m behind plan)
- **Commercial casino GGR run-rate:** ~$2.2–2.3bn annualised (est.) (from ~$0.7bn FY25/26 — RWNYC conversion + tables)
- **RWNYC racing-support arrears:** $80.6m through Oct (+~$13m/month → >$180m by Jun-27; interim deal expires)
- **Prediction-market enforcement:** TRO denied 23 Nov 2026 (January 2027 hearing; sports contracts still live in NY)

**Market.** We expect calendar-2026 mobile handle of ~$26–27bn (est.) and GGR of ~$2.3–2.4bn (est.), with Q4 hold reverting toward 9–10% on NFL/NBA parlay mix; structural handle growth slows to 3–6% a year from 2027 as the market matures and prediction-market substitution bites at the margin. FanDuel/DraftKings' combined handle share drifts toward ~68% as Fanatics approaches 13–15%; expect at least one more exit among the three smallest books before licence renewal. **Land-based.** RWNYC should stabilise at $1.5–1.7bn GGR in 2027 (est.) — below its $2.2bn bid — with the slot floor consolidating and tables carrying growth; the upstate four grow low-single digits until Metropolitan Park opens (~2030), after which Resorts World Catskills and Empire City face material cannibalisation. **Rules.** Base case for the 2027 session: (1) a legislated racing-support fix that routes NYRA payments through the commercial gaming revenue fund with partial credit to RWNYC — **no cut to the 56%/30% headline rates**; (2) no iGaming unless the Executive Budget sponsors it (we put the probability at ~25%), and if it comes, skins tied to existing casino/racino/mobile/tribal licensees at a 30%+ tax; (3) a statutory ban on sports event contracts (S9414-type) if the January court outcome is unfavourable to the state, otherwise the AG litigation continues through 2027; (4) VLT age raised to 21 and credit-card funding restrictions as RG add-ons. **Leverage/fiscal direction.** The state's gaming tax base rises in FY2026/27 only because of RWNYC; with mobile tax flat and the MTA short up to ~$150m from the racing dispute, the fiscal bias is toward **extracting more from existing licensees, not toward liberalising entry or rates.** For B2Tech the implication is to prioritise B2B supply and downstate partnerships over any direct-licence ambition in New York over the next 24 months.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- 30 Nov 2026 — NYC Public Design Commission vote on Metropolitan Park; and the pending Queens residents' suit against the licence award (timing risk for the 2030 opening).
- January 2027 — Kalshi hearing before Justice Crane on the AG's injunction; a 'within or from New York' order would hit Kalshi and Polymarket at HQ level and set national precedent.
- January 2027 — FY2028 Executive Budget: any iGaming authorisation, mobile tax/promo-deduction change, prediction-market language, or racing-support restructuring.
- June 2027 — expiry of the RWNYC interim racing-support agreement; arrears ~$180m; watch for a legislated fix vs. litigation and for Genting's decision on the $3.3bn phase-two expansion.
- Monthly NYSGC reports — mobile hold vs the 8.9% FYTD average; whether FY2026/27 tax closes the $25.1m gap by March 2027.
- RWNYC KPIs — table count (345 at 15 Nov), win/unit/day (now $716) and whether monthly GGR breaks out of the $122–136m band.
- Mobile licence tenure — initial 10-year terms run to early 2032 [statute, not re-verified]; any renewal/re-bid framework would be the first opening for new entrants or rate changes.
- Bally's Bronx — financing and groundbreaking milestones for a 2030 opening; Bally's balance sheet is the weakest of the three licensees.
- Empire City (MGM) — whether it seeks table games or sportsbook rights via legislation now that it is the only large downstate VLT property without a casino licence.
