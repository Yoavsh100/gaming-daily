Maine

Released 23 Oct 2026 (GCU casino revenue files "as of 20 Oct 2026"; sports wagering files week-ending 30 Sep 2026) · Currency USD (tables in $ millions unless stated) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Maine is a small, two-casino, tribal-exclusive-mobile market growing low-single-digits: 9M 2026 land-based GGR was $129.4m (+2.3%), carried entirely by slots (+4.5%) while tables fell 10%, and sports handle rose 10% to $450.2m with September hold normalising to 10.4%. The state keeps ~42% of casino GGR and 10% of sports AGR, and the only route to the online casino upside is through the Wabanaki Nations — whose iGaming bill (LD 1164) remains the single swing factor for the next 12-24 months.

ACH/online banking, debit and wallet rails via the DraftKings/Caesars platforms; the sports framework restricts credit-card funding of accounts [not re-verified today]. Minimum age 21 for casino and sports; a GCU-administered statewide self-exclusion list; a dedicated Gambling Addiction Prevention and Treatment Fund funded from both verticals; Chapter 41 sports rules restrict college-campus advertising, under-21 imagery and 'risk-free' promotional language [rule detail not re-verified today].

Governor Mills is term-limited and Maine votes on 3 Nov 2026 — the next administration decides whether online casino arrives and who controls it.

  • Read-through for B2Tech: a B2B/partner market, not a licence market. There is no direct route to a mobile licence (tribal exclusivity), the two casinos are vertically owned by PENN and Churchill Downs, and retail sports is sub-1% of AGR. Realistic entries are (i) platform/content supply to the tribes' current or future partners, (ii) positioning for a tribal iGaming skin if LD 1164 or a successor passes, or (iii) ETG/cabinet supply to Oxford, which began reporting electronic table game win in June 2026.

3Deep-dive: Land-based casinos (slots & table games)

Slots +4.5% carry a +2.3% market; tables -10% with hold compression at both properties; Hollywood outgrows Oxford.

Aggregate GGR

9M 2026 GGR of $129.40m (+2.3%): slots +4.5% to $112.44m carry the market while tables fell 10.1% to $16.96m.

  • H1 essentially flat at +1.0% to $82.4m; January -3.4% and May -0.6% offset February's +9.1%.
  • Q3 did all the work: +4.8% to $47.0m (July +3.5%, August +3.6%, September +7.5%).
  • First 20 days of October added a further $9.2m.
  • All figures gross of tax and before promotional allowances, which Maine does not net from the tax base.

Hollywood Casino Bangor

Hollywood (PENN) grew 6.0% to $52.83m, but strong volume gains were largely given back in hold compression on both slots and tables.

  • Slots $44.68m (+7.0%) on funds-in $393m (+15.4%) across 621 machines (up from ~611); hold on funds-in eased to 11.4% from 12.3%.
  • GCU-reported win on coin-in 8.43% (payback 91.57%); implied coin-in from the 1% gross-slot-income line ~$528m for 9M.
  • Tables $8.15m (+0.7%) on drop $37.2m (+12.2%); hold fell to 21.9% from 24.4%.
  • Win per unit per day ~$264 for slots and ~$2,300 per table (13 tables).

Oxford Casino

Oxford (Churchill Downs) was flat at $76.57m (-0.03%) as 3.0% slot growth was offset by an 18.3% table decline.

  • Slots $67.76m (+3.0%) on funds-in $706m (+8.4%) across ~946 machines (down from 959); win on coin-in 7.50% (payback 92.50%).
  • Tables $8.81m (-18.3%) on drop $42.3m (-8.1%); hold 20.8% vs 23.4%.
  • Slot win per unit per day ~$262, identical to Bangor despite a looser floor; table win ~$1,470 per table per day across 22 tables.
  • New 'ETG win/loss' line from June 2026 ($0.20m through September; $0.25m through 20 Oct) signals response to table weakness with electronic table games.

Market structure

Oxford's share of casino GGR slipped to 59.2% from 60.6%; supply caps are not binding, so catchment and New England competition set the ceiling.

  • Both properties far below the statutory 1,500-machine per-facility cap and 3,000 statewide cap [caps per Title 8 §1020; not re-verified today].
  • Ceiling set by southern-Maine/New Hampshire border catchment and competition from Massachusetts' Encore and MGM Springfield, New Hampshire charitable casinos and historical horse racing venues.
  • Full-year 2025 GGR $166.36m (Hollywood $66.10m, Oxford $100.27m), reconciling with AGA's $141.9m EGD + $24.5m tables.

Operator relevance

The land-based segment is closed to operators; commercial relevance is only as a supplier into a ~1,570-machine, 35-table estate.

  • Two licences, both vertically owned by listed US groups, with no licensing window.
  • 100-mile siting rule [not re-verified today] effectively requires legislation for a third property; tribal casino bills have surfaced repeatedly.
  • Supplier opportunity in cabinets, ETGs and systems; state takes ~42% of GGR, capping operator appetite for aggressive capex and promotions.

4Deep-dive: Sports wagering (tribal mobile + retail)

Handle +10% to $450.2m in 9M; September AGR +36.5% on hold normalising to 10.4%; DraftKings ~83% share; retail immaterial.

Handle and AGR

9M 2026 handle rose 10% to $450.2m; September AGR jumped 36.5% YoY to $6.34m as hold normalised.

  • Market live since 3 Nov 2023 under Title 8 ch. 35; four GCU files: three nations (DraftKings), Passamaquoddy (Caesars), Oxford Sportsbook retail, First Tracks Investments (Oddfellahs) retail.
  • 9M handle $450.2m (+10% YoY, implying ~$409m in 9M 2025); September handle $61.05m (-3.4% YoY, +23.8% MoM with the NFL).
  • September AGR $6.34m (+36.5% YoY, +62.1% MoM); September tax $634,222.
  • Primary GCU PDFs could not be parsed, so sports figures are tagged secondary; Covers tracker shows $444.5m handle / $48.6m AGR / 11.0% hold; GCU-sourced $450.2m used.

Hold and share

Hold is the story: September hold of 10.4% versus ~7.4% a year earlier, with DraftKings holding ~83% of handle.

  • DraftKings' hold rose to 10.7% from 7.2%, lifting AGR 42% to $5.4m on a 3.4% smaller handle of $50.8m.
  • Caesars holds roughly 16% share; two retail books well under 1% combined; AGA says mobile accounted for >99% of 2025 sports revenue ($68.2m, +20.1%).
  • Reported 9M tax of 'nearly $4.9m' at 10% implies 9M AGR ~$48.6m and 9M hold ~10.8% (estimate).

Licensee economics

Sports licensees pay 10% of AGR plus 0.25% federal excise versus ~42% of GGR for casinos, driving lobbying on both sides.

  • No promotional deduction [deduction treatment not re-verified today].
  • Asymmetry plus tribal mobile exclusivity explains casinos lobbying for their own mobile access.
  • Tribes are now pressing to extend exclusivity into iGaming.

Retail

Retail is structurally marginal with handle under 1% of the state total; there is no commercial case for standalone retail entry.

  • Oxford opened its book in 2025; Hollywood Bangor has no reporting sportsbook (PENN's ESPN Bet has no Maine route).
  • Portland Oddfellas permanent licence denied by the Gambling Control Board in January 2025, briefly pausing retail betting; First Tracks continues to file 2026 revenue — licensing basis [unverified].
  • Facility licences remain available to commercial tracks and OTBs.

Outlook and entry

FY2026 AGR could land around $70-75m (estimate, +3-10%); mobile entry is only as a tribe's platform partner and no uncontracted skin exists.

  • Assumes Q4 handle grows mid-single digits on the 2025 base and hold stays near 10% through the NFL/NBA run-in.
  • Two of four nations' skins contracted to DraftKings, one to Caesars; Mi'kmaq/Maliseet rights bundled into the DraftKings arrangement.

5Deep-dive: Licensing, tax & revenue distribution

Closed casino duopoly taxed at ~42% of GGR; tribal-exclusive mobile taxed at 10% of AGR; statutory earmarks route proceeds to education, racing and agriculture.

Regulatory framework

GCU licenses and audits all gambling; casino supply is statutorily capped so a third venue requires legislation.

  • Gambling Control Unit (Department of Public Safety) covers casinos, sports wagering, fantasy, harness racing/OTB and charitable gaming; five-member Gambling Control Board adjudicates licences.
  • Casinos under Title 8 MRSA ch. 31 by referendum (Bangor 2003; Oxford 2010); table games added 2012.
  • Caps of 3,000 slots statewide and 1,500 per facility plus 100-mile siting restriction [not re-verified today]; Wabanaki casino bills filed repeatedly without passage.

Casino tax structure

Casino taxation on GGR is among the heaviest in the US, with Hollywood paying 1% of coin-in plus 39% of net slot income and Oxford 46%.

  • Hollywood: 1% of gross slot income ($5.28m in 9M 2026, implying ~$528m coin-in) to General Fund, plus 39% of net slot income across twelve earmarks (10% Healthy Maine, 10% harness purses, 4% racing, 3% GCU, 3% fairs, 3% Sire Stakes, 2% UMaine, 1% each MCCS, OTB, Bangor, plus Maine Maritime).
  • Oxford: 46% of net slot income (25% K-12, 4% UMaine, 4% Penobscot and Passamaquoddy Tribes, 3% MCCS, 3% GCU, 2% Oxford, 1% each fairs, purses, Sire Stakes, county, 0.5% each dairy and milk pool).
  • Both pay 16% of net table income (Bangor: 9% General Fund, 3% GCU, 2% Veterans, 2% Bangor; Oxford: 10% K-12, 3% GCU, 2% town, 1% county).

State take

9M 2026 state take was $54.53m (+3.6%), roughly 42% of GGR at both properties.

  • Hollywood $21.95m (41.6% of its GGR); Oxford $32.58m (42.5%).
  • FY2025 take $69.34m on $166.36m GGR (41.7%).
  • Hollywood's 1% line on coin-in means effective burden rises when hold falls — a small but real volatility an operator should model.

Sports wagering tax

Sports wagering is taxed at 10% of adjusted gross receipts, with mobile licences reserved to the four Wabanaki Nations.

  • Distribution: 1% GCU, 1% Gambling Addiction Prevention and Treatment Fund, 0.55% Harness Racing Commission, 0.55% Sire Stakes, 0.4% Agricultural Fair Promotion, 6.5% General Fund.
  • Mobile licences one per nation at $200,000 per four-year term [Title 8 §1205; not re-verified today]; facility licences open to casinos, tracks and OTBs; suppliers need own licences.
  • A 16% rate was floated in 2025 budget deliberations but GCU page still states 10% [legislative outcome unverified].

Commercial reading

Fiscal design favours tribes (10% mobile sports) and earmarked beneficiaries (42% casinos); any iGaming statute will be negotiated inside this frame.

  • Expect a tribal-exclusive model with a rate pitched between the two; 2025 LD 1164 debate centred on a mid-teens rate [unverified].
  • Expect a parallel push from the casinos for compensation or their own skins.

6Deep-dive: Online casino (iGaming) & political direction of travel

No legal iGaming; tribal-exclusive LD 1164 is the only live pathway and its fate turns on the 3 Nov 2026 gubernatorial election.

Current status

Maine has no legal iGaming; mobile sports is the only digital channel and iGaming legalisation is the only material step-change available.

  • No online casino, no online lottery sales reported here (n/a), no commercial online poker.
  • Mobile sports is >99% of 2025 sports AGR (AGA) and ~27% of 9M 2026 total GGR (estimate).
  • Casino GGR has grown 2-3% a year.

LD 1164

LD 1164 would extend tribal mobile sports exclusivity into online casino; it passed both chambers in 2025 but was carried over, not enacted.

  • Passed on narrow margins amid gubernatorial opposition; 2026 session disposition could not be verified [unverified].
  • PENN and Churchill Downs oppose a tribal-exclusive model, citing cannibalisation of a 42%-taxed land-based base.
  • March 2026 trade coverage framed the two properties as facing 'competition from new online' products, read as a signal the debate remained live.

Political calendar

The 3 November 2026 gubernatorial election is decisive: a sponsor-friendly administration could enable a 2027 statute and 2028 launch.

  • Governor Mills, who signalled a veto, is term-limited; 133rd Legislature convenes January 2027.
  • A sceptical administration leaves Maine a two-casino, sports-only state through the forecast horizon.
  • Expect tax design to borrow from the 10% sports template under Appropriations Committee pressure toward the casino rate.

B2Tech strategy

No direct licence exists; optionality must come via Wabanaki relationships and a supplier licence, with a modest $150-250m GGR market at maturity.

  • Houlton Band of Maliseet Indians and Mi'kmaq Nation, bundled into the DraftKings sports deal, are natural candidates for a differentiated iGaming partner.
  • A content/platform supplier licence would be required under any statute.
  • Market size estimate benchmarked to New England per-capita comparators; not a top-tier US state.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

Conventional US rails; 21+ across casino and sports; strict GCU advertising code and a ring-fenced addiction fund — compliance cost is modest but conduct expectations are high.

Payments

Payments are mainstream US rails with credit cards restricted for sports accounts and no offshore payment-blocking regime beyond UIGEA.

  • Casino floors run on cash and ticket-in/ticket-out; 9M funds-in $393m at Hollywood and $706m at Oxford.
  • DraftKings and Caesars apps accept ACH/online banking, debit cards and wallets through national stacks.
  • Credit card funding restricted [not re-verified today]; no mobile-money dimension.

Responsible gambling

RG is statutorily funded via 1% of sports AGR (~$0.49m in 9M 2026) and casino slot earmarks, with 21+ age limits.

  • 3% GCU administration/gambling addiction earmark on slots: $1.68m Hollywood and $2.18m Oxford in 9M 2026.
  • GCU runs a statewide voluntary self-exclusion programme across casinos and sportsbooks.
  • Minimum age 21 for casino and sports (18 for lottery and pari-mutuel) [not re-verified today]; 211 Maine / 1-800-GAMBLER signposting mandatory in advertising.

Advertising rules

GCU's sports advertising code is among the tighter US regimes, and the Board has shown it will exercise discretion on suitability.

  • No advertising on or directed at college campuses or college-aged audiences; no depiction of under-21s.
  • Restrictions on 'free' or 'risk-free' language, mandatory RG messaging, operator liability for affiliates [rule text not re-verified today].
  • Little enforcement controversy to date, but January 2025 denial of Portland Oddfellas permanent licence demonstrates Board discretion.

Entrant implications

Compliance burden is light in money terms but reputationally sensitive; iGaming rules will carry stricter advertising and affordability expectations.

  • Legislature is attentive to tribal equity and problem-gambling optics.
  • Deposit-limit, time-limit and cooling-off tooling should be expected as table stakes in any future online casino rule set.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Sports hold normalisation is lifting AGR far faster than handle: September AGR +36.5% on handle -3.4%, hold 10.4% vs ~7.4%; 9M handle +10% to $450.2m. A 10% AGR tax with no promo deduction leaves ~90% of AGR with the licensee and its platform partner — the best fiscal terms in the market. mixed
  • Casino volumes are healthy even if GGR is not: slot funds-in +15.4% at Hollywood and +8.4% at Oxford, with Q3 GGR +4.8% and September +7.5%. The floor is ~1,570 machines, well below the 3,000 statewide cap, so supplier upside exists without any licensing event. structural
  • Oxford has started reporting ETG win (from June 2026) in response to an 18% table decline — a live procurement window for electronic table game and cabinet suppliers at the state's larger property. early
  • Tribal iGaming (LD 1164) is the only legal online casino pathway in New England's least-penetrated state; two of the four Wabanaki Nations' rights are bundled into a sports-only DraftKings deal, creating a natural opening for a differentiated iGaming platform partner if the statute passes. watch
  • Fiscal dependence creates allies: 9M 2026 casino take of $54.5m funds K-12 education ($19.1m from Oxford alone), harness racing, agricultural fairs and tribal payments — a broad beneficiary coalition that tends to support revenue-raising expansion in Augusta. structural
  • Regulatory transparency is high: weekly-updated GCU revenue files by property and product, with distributions itemised to the cent, make diligence and partner monitoring cheap relative to most African jurisdictions B2Tech covers. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • No direct licence route: casinos are a closed duopoly (PENN, Churchill Downs) with statutory supply caps and a 100-mile siting rule; mobile sports is tribal-exclusive with all four nations already contracted (DraftKings ~83%, Caesars). Entry is B2B or via a tribe only. structural
  • Casino tax burden of ~42% of GGR (46% on Oxford slots; 1% of coin-in plus 39% at Bangor) compresses operator margins and promotional capacity; any iGaming regime negotiated against this benchmark risks a rate well above the 10% sports template. structural
  • Table games are in structural decline: statewide -10.1% in 9M and -10.8% in Q3, with hold down ~250-350bp at both properties and Oxford drop -8.1%; the growth in the land-based market is slots-only. structural
  • Scale is small and growth is low: casino GGR is on track for ~$170m in FY2026 (+2-3%) and total market ~$240-245m; even a successful iGaming launch would likely make Maine a sub-$250m online state at maturity (estimate). structural
  • Political uncertainty is acute: the LD 1164 outcome could not be verified, the incumbent governor opposed tribal iGaming, and the 3 Nov 2026 election plus a new Legislature in Jan 2027 reset the agenda; sports tax-rate increases (a 16% figure circulated in 2025) remain a live fiscal temptation. governance
  • Licensing discretion has teeth: the Gambling Control Board denied a permanent licence to the Portland Oddfellas sportsbook operator in Jan 2025, pausing retail betting; the basis on which First Tracks Investments (Oddfellahs) continues to report in 2026 is unverified — a reminder that suitability findings can abruptly end a retail footprint. one-off
  • Data gap: sports wagering figures here rely on three corroborating trade reads of the GCU September files rather than the parsed primary PDFs; the Covers tracker shows a ~$6m lower 9M handle. Treat sports AGR and hold as estimates until reconciled. watch

Independent view. Our independent view: land-based GGR grows 2-3% a year through 2027 with mix shifting further to slots (tables already 13% of GGR, trending to ~11%), and sports AGR grows faster than handle as hold settles at 9.5-10.5% versus 2025's ~7-8% months; state take rises in line, ~$78-80m in FY2026E. On rules: the sports tax rate is more likely to rise than fall over two years (fiscal pressure plus the 42% casino benchmark make 10% look anomalous to Appropriations), while iGaming is a coin-flip dependent on the November 2026 governor — if a supportive administration takes office, expect a tribal-exclusive statute in the 2027 session with launch in 2028 at a mid-teens-to-20% GGR rate and some compensation mechanism for the two casinos; if not, Maine stays sports-only. Leverage/market structure direction: more concentrated, not less — DraftKings' ~83% share is unlikely to be challenged without a third skin or iGaming cross-sell. For B2Tech the actionable path is supplier licensing and a tribal relationship now, so that optionality exists when the statute moves.
