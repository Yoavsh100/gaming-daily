# Germany () — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: EUR (tables in € millions unless stated; ~USD at an ASSUMED EUR/USD 1.17 — see confidence note) · Results released 26 Oct 2026 (Q3-26 Destatis/BMF cash tax data; GGL Marktmonitor Q2-26 and Tätigkeitsbericht 2025 as market base)**

> Germany's Q3-26 gambling tax take rose 11% YoY to €622.1m (~$728m), but the shape — July +26%, August +12.5%, September implied ~−3% — says this is a World Cup timing effect on a 5.3%-of-stakes tax, not a step-change in underlying demand. The regulated market is large (€14.4bn GGR in 2025, flat) yet only ~24% online, with online growth (stakes +9–10% in H1-26) bought under a turnover-tax and €1-stake regime that compresses margins and leaks 23–80% of casino play offshore depending on whose study you believe. The GlüStV 2021 evaluation due 31 Dec 2026 and the 2027 licence-renewal cycle are the swing factors for anyone deciding to enter.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Headline: gambling tax +11% in Q3-26.** Cash receipts of the Rennwett- und Lotteriesteuer family (lottery tax, sports betting tax, virtual slot tax, online poker tax, horse-race betting tax — federal law, revenue accrues to the Länder) reached €622.1m (~$728m) in Jul–Sep 2026 vs ~€560.5m (~$656m) a year earlier, +€61.6m (~$72m). Corroborated across Gaming Intelligence and Mundovideo, both citing Destatis/BMF cash data.
- **The monthly shape is a World Cup artefact, not a trend break.** BMF monthly data: July €209m (~$245m, +26%), August €219m (~$256m, +12.5%); September is therefore implied at ~€194m (~$227m), roughly −3% YoY (derived from rounded figures). The 5.3% stake taxes are filed monthly on the prior month's stakes, so July cash largely captures June World Cup 2026 betting (tournament 11 Jun–19 Jul). Underlying ex-event growth is low-to-mid single digit.
- **9M-26 tax €1,901m (~$2.22bn), ~+4% YoY implied.** Q1 €632.3m (~$740m, slightly down YoY per trade press), Q2 €647.0m (~$757m), Q3 €622.1m. BMF reported Jan–Aug at €1,708m (~$2.00bn, +5.2%). FY-25 was €2.46bn (~$2.88bn, −1%) after €2.48bn in FY-24; the official BMF 2026 estimate of €2,510m (~$2.94bn, +1.9%) now looks conservative — Q4 need only reach ~€609m (−4% YoY) to hit it.
- **Tax base is TURNOVER, not GGR — the defining economic feature.** RennwLottG (as amended 2021): sports betting 5.3% of stakes; virtual slots 5.3% of stakes; online poker 5.3% of stakes; horse-race betting 5.3% of stakes; lotteries 20% of ticket price net of tax (≈16.7% of gross ticket). At the 2025 virtual-slot hold of 11.8% this equates to ~45% of GGR; at the reported 22.8% sports-betting hold it is ~23% of GGR. Online casino table games (Land-level), Spielbanken (Land casino levy) and gaming halls (municipal amusement tax + VAT) sit outside this line.
- **Lottery dominates the tax line.** Applying 5.3% to GGL-reported stakes gives ~€204m (Q1-26) and ~€192m (Q2-26) from sports/slots/poker/horse; the residual ~€429m (~$502m) and ~€455m (~$532m) — ~68–70% of receipts — is lottery tax (derived estimate; Destatis product split not available in sources read).
- **Market size: €14.4bn (~$16.8bn) GGR in 2025, flat YoY** (GGL Tätigkeitsbericht 2025, relayed). Land-based €10.9bn (~$12.8bn, ~75%); online €3.5bn (~$4.1bn, +2%) — online penetration ~24%, low for a large Western European market. All gambling taxes and levies (incl. Spielbankabgabe, lottery concession levies) totalled €6.9bn (~$8.1bn, −1%), of which licensed operators' RennwLottG contribution was €2.46bn (−1%).
- **Online product mix (2025 GGR).** Sports betting €1.89bn (~$2.21bn, −4%) on €8.3bn (~$9.7bn) stakes, football 77% of activity, live betting 53% of wagers; virtual slots €543m (~$635m, +11%) on €4.6bn (~$5.4bn) stakes; online poker rake €69m (~$81m, −0.7%); online horse betting €19m (~$22m, +5%); social lotteries €807m (~$944m), online portion €444m (~$519m, +11%).
- **2026 stakes momentum is stronger than 2025 GGR suggests.** GGL Marktmonitor: Q1-26 stakes €3,842m (~$4.50bn, +9.4% vs €3,513m), online €3,220m (84%, +10.0%); Q2-26 €3,624m (~$4.24bn), online €3,111m (86%). H1-26 ≈ €7,466m (~$8.7bn). Sports betting Q1 €2,431m (+11.6%), slots €1,201m (+9.3%), poker €186m (−11.4%), online horse €23m.
- **Retail sports betting is a shrinking niche.** Retail stakes fell from €622m (~$728m) in Q1-26 to €512m (~$599m) in Q2-26, i.e. 25.6% → 23.0% of sports stakes; online sports stakes €1,809m → €1,711m. Retail shops are Land-licensed with distance rules and carry the same 5.3% stake tax.
- **Licensing regime: GlüStV 2021 + GGL.** The Interstate Treaty (in force 1 Jul 2021) centralised online licensing in the Gemeinsame Glücksspielbehörde der Länder (Halle/Saale; full competence since 1 Jan 2023). Licence types: sports betting (online/retail), virtual slots, online poker, online lottery brokerage, social/class lotteries; online table games are reserved to each Land (Schleswig-Holstein live; NRW process [unverified]). Security deposit €5m (~$5.9m), scalable to €50m (~$58.5m) [statutory, from analyst reference]. In 2025 LUGAS processed data from >60 licensed operators and ~5m registered player accounts.
- **Product and player-protection constraints are the tightest in Western Europe.** €1 (~$1.17) maximum stake per spin, ≥5-second spin, no autoplay, no jackpots, no live casino under the federal regime; €1,000 (~$1,170)/month cross-operator deposit cap enforced through LUGAS limit and activity files (no parallel play); OASIS self-exclusion; panic button; 06:00–21:00 broadcast/online ad ban for slots and poker; no sports-betting ads with active athletes or during live broadcasts [GlüStV §§5–8, 22a from analyst reference].
- **Channelisation is contested and vertical-specific.** A study reported 16 Mar 2026 put legal share at 77.03% / unlicensed 22.97% of online play; industry associations count 382 German-language illegal sites against 34 licensed platforms and cite 70–80% offshore leakage in casino/slots. GGL enforcement 2025: 2,662 illegal gambling/advertising sites reviewed, 1,843 (69%) taken down. The turnover tax and €1 stake cap are the structural cause.
- **Operator landscape — no official share data.** GGL does not publish operator shares. Principal licensed brands (analyst reference, shares n/a): sports — Tipico (market leader), bet365, bwin (Entain), Betano, Interwetten, Admiral (Novomatic), Merkur Bets (Gauselmann), Betway, Winamax; slots — Merkur, Löwen Play, Tipico Games, bwin, LeoVegas, Wildz; lottery brokerage — ZEAL (Lotto24/Tipp24; 9M-26 revenue near €200m per trade press), Lottoland; state lotteries via the 16-member Deutscher Lotto- und Totoblock (monopoly).
- **Payments: bank-rail market, no crypto.** Licensed sites run on SEPA transfer, Sofort/Klarna, PayPal, paysafecard and cards; crypto and credit extension are not permitted; the GGL can order payment-service providers to block flows to unlicensed operators (§9 GlüStV). Deposit caps and KYC at registration make payment friction a conversion cost versus offshore sites [regime description, not quantified].
- **Political direction: evaluation, then slow consensus.** §32 GlüStV requires the Länder to evaluate the treaty by 31 Dec 2026; the treaty runs to 31 Dec 2028 with automatic extension unless terminated [analyst reference]. Any amendment needs all 16 Landtage. Live agenda: stake/deposit limits, live-betting scope, nationwide table-game licences, tax base (industry lobbies for GGR), enforcement against offshore, and player-reimbursement litigation for the pre-2021 period (BGH/CJEU track; status [unverified]). Christian Hochgrebe chairs the GGL Administrative Board since 1 Jul 2026.
- **Fiscal dependence anchors conservatism.** The state took €6.9bn in gambling taxes and levies in 2025 — mostly lottery and Spielbank levies — which protects the Lotto monopoly and makes the Länder reluctant to trade a reliable turnover tax for a GGR tax that would favour operators.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Germany is a large, stable, lottery-heavy market where the addressable online pool for a private operator (sports + slots + poker) is ~€2.5bn GGR and growing low-to-mid single digits in GGR, high single digits in stakes. Economics are gated by the 5.3% stake tax and marketing restrictions, so scale, pricing discipline and low bonus intensity matter more than acquisition spend; a B2B supply model (GGL-compliant content/platform to licensed operators) carries less regulatory beta than B2C entry. Time any B2C move to the 2027 licence-renewal window and the post-evaluation rule-set.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Sports betting (online & retail)

*Largest private vertical (€1.89bn GGR 2025, −4%) with stakes re-accelerating in 2026 (+11.6% Q1) and a World Cup bump in Q3 tax; 5.3% stake tax equals ~23% of GGR at the reported hold.*

### 2025 market size

**Largest private vertical with €1.89bn GGR in 2025 (−4%) on €8.3bn stakes; implied 22.8% hold is likely an upper bound.**

- GGL 2025 stakes €8.3bn (~$9.7bn), GGR €1.89bn (~$2.21bn), down 4% in a year without a major summer tournament.
- Football accounted for 77% of activity; live (in-play) betting for 53% of wagers.
- Implied hold of 22.8% is well above the 15–20% typical of mature European books.
- Bruttospielertrag definition (stakes minus winnings, before bonus cost, 5.3% tax deducted from stakes/winnings) likely inflates the figure versus operator NGR [interpretation, unverified].

### 2026 demand re-acceleration

**Q1-26 stakes rose 11.6% to €2,431m; retail share fell from 25.6% to 23.0% in a quarter as online migration continues.**

- Q1-26 stakes €2,431m (~$2.84bn), +11.6% on €2,179m: €1,809m online, €622m retail.
- Q2-26 stakes €2,223m (~$2.60bn): €1,711m online, €512m retail.
- Retail migration reinforced by Land-level shop licensing with minimum-distance rules.
- Q3-26 stakes not yet published; GGL Marktmonitor typically lags six to eight weeks.

### World Cup Q3 bump

**July tax receipts of €209m (+26%) point to a strong double-digit but unquantified World Cup uplift in sports stakes.**

- July receipts €209m (+26%) across all RennwLottG taxes imply a sharp June/early-July surge, consistent with FIFA World Cup 2026 (11 Jun–19 Jul).
- If the full +€61.6m Q3 tax delta came from sports betting alone it would imply ~+€1.2bn incremental stakes (+~50%).
- True figure is lower because slots (+9–10%) and lottery jackpots also contributed.

### Tax economics

**The 5.3% stake tax is ~23% of GGR at 23% hold and ~29% at 18% hold, payable even in losing months.**

- Sportwettensteuer is 5.3% of stakes regardless of licence status or margin — a losing month still pays.
- Derived receipts ~€129m (~$151m) Q1-26 and ~€118m (~$138m) Q2-26; ~€450–500m annualised (estimate; Destatis product split not retrieved).
- Ban on advertising with active athletes and in-play advertising during live broadcasts, plus live-betting catalogue restrictions (final result and next-event markets) [analyst reference].
- Regime rewards incumbents with brand recall — Tipico, bet365, bwin, Betano, Interwetten — and penalises bonus-led acquisition.

### Channelisation and entry

**Sports betting channelises materially better than casino and is the credible front door, but retail at 23% and falling is not where to deploy capital.**

- Tax applies to every bet taken from Germany; licensed brands dominate search and sponsorship; product gap to offshore (odds, live markets) is narrower than the slot gap.
- Entry requires differentiated pricing or live content, a lean cost base, and a retail strategy limited to selective Länder.

### Outlook

**Analyst estimates FY-26 stakes of €9.0–9.3bn (+8–12%) on World Cup and online migration; the GGL does not guide.**

- GGR growth expected below stake growth if hold normalises after an operator-friendly tournament.
- Q4 Bundesliga/Champions League schedule and no major tournament in 2027 point to mid-single-digit stake growth next year (estimate).

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Online casino: virtual slots, poker and table games

*Slots are the growth engine (GGR +11% to €543m in 2025; stakes +9% in 2026) but a 5.3% turnover tax at 11.8% hold is ~45% of GGR, poker is shrinking (stakes −11%), and table games remain Land-fragmented.*

### Virtual slots

**Slots are the only large online vertical growing double-digit: 2025 GGR €543m (+11%), with 2026 stakes up 9%.**

- GGL 2025 (relayed) GGR €543m (~$635m), +€53m or +11% YoY, on stakes €4.6bn (~$5.4bn); implied hold 11.8%.
- €1 per-spin cap and ≥5-second spin force low-volatility, ~88–90% RTP games.
- Q1-26 stakes €1,201m (~$1.41bn, +9.3% on €1,099m); Q2-26 €1,214m (~$1.42bn), the only product growing sequentially into the weaker second quarter.
- Annualised Q2 stakes ~€4.9bn point to FY-26 slots GGR of ~€570–590m (estimate) if hold is stable.

### Online poker

**Licensed poker is in structural decline: 2025 rake €69m (−0.7%) and Q1-26 stakes −11.4% to €186m.**

- 2025 rake €69m (~$81m, −0.7%); Q1-26 stakes €186m (~$218m, −11.4% on €210m); Q2-26 €158m (~$185m).
- 5.3% tax on buy-ins and prohibition on cross-border liquidity make licensed poker uncompetitive with offshore pools.
- A retention product for sportsbook/slots operators, not a standalone opportunity.

### Tax burden and offshore leakage

**The slots levy equals ~45% of GGR, higher than any GGR-based online casino tax in Western Europe, driving a large offshore market.**

- Derived receipts ~€64m (~$75m) per quarter for slots and ~€8–10m for poker; roughly €290m annualised (estimate).
- Tax payable irrespective of bonus cost, so effective tax on net revenue after promotions is higher still.
- Regulated offer: €1 stake, no jackpots, no autoplay, no live dealer, €1,000/month cross-operator deposit cap via LUGAS, no parallel play.
- Study reported 16 Mar 2026 put unlicensed share at 22.97% of all online play; industry associations argue 70–80% of casino/slot activity sits offshore, 382 German-language illegal sites versus 34 licensed platforms.
- Both can be true — channelisation is high in sports betting and low in casino.

### Table games

**Table games are not federally licensed; the opportunity is a Land-by-Land tender game with small prize pools.**

- Under GlüStV §22c each Land may license online table games, capped at its number of land-based Spielbank concessions.
- Schleswig-Holstein operates its own regime; North Rhine-Westphalia has moved to license a limited number of operators [status unverified in this session].
- Nationwide, the only legal online 'casino' product for a B2C entrant is virtual slots plus poker.

### Enforcement and positioning

**GGL removed access to 1,843 of 2,662 illegal sites reviewed in 2025 (69%); enforcement is improving but limited.**

- Tools are payment blocking (§9 GlüStV), app-store and search delisting, and prohibition orders; Saxony-Anhalt court rulings have constrained ISP-level IP blocking [analyst reference, unverified].
- A licensed slots operator competes on trust, speed of payout and cross-sell from sports rather than on RTP or bonus value.
- A B2B studio needs a GGL-compliant catalogue (€1 stake, 5-second cycle, no jackpot/autoplay) and LUGAS-ready integration to sell into the >60 licensed operators.
- A defensible niche precisely because global content is not plug-and-play here.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Lottery, social lotteries and land-based (Spielbanken, gaming halls)

*Three-quarters of the €14.4bn market is land-based and the state lottery monopoly supplies ~70% of the RennwLottG tax line; private access is limited to online brokerage and social-lottery distribution.*

### Land-based market

**Land-based generated €10.9bn of the €14.4bn regulated GGR in 2025 (~75%) and is flat-to-declining while online took all growth.**

- €10.9bn (~$12.8bn) of €14.4bn (~$16.8bn) spans gaming halls (Gauselmann/Merkur and Novomatic/Löwen devices), Land-concessioned Spielbanken, and 16 state lottery retail networks.
- Land Spielhallen laws impose minimum distances and device caps; online took the entire 2% of market growth.
- State's wider take was €6.9bn (~$8.1bn) in 2025 including Spielbankabgabe and lottery concession levies, explaining Länder protectiveness toward the lottery monopoly.

### Lottery tax weight

**Lottery supplies 68–70% of the RennwLottG tax line, ~€1.7bn annualised, on an implied ~€10bn gross sales base.**

- Residual lottery tax ~€429m (~$502m) in Q1-26 and ~€455m (~$532m) in Q2-26 after deducting 5.3% stake taxes (derived estimate).
- Lottery tax is 20% of ticket price net of tax (≈16.7% of gross ticket), implying ~€10bn annual sales across Lotto 6aus49, Eurojackpot, class and social lotteries (estimate).
- Jackpot cycles, not operator behaviour, drive quarterly swings.

### Private lottery slices

**Social lotteries produced €807m GGR in 2025 with online up 11% to €444m; brokerage is licensed but low-margin.**

- Q1-26 stakes: class lotteries €54m (~$63m), social lotteries €328m (~$384m), combined €382m; online brokerage €258m (~$302m).
- Social lotteries (Deutsche Fernsehlotterie, Aktion Mensch and peers) GGR €807m (~$944m), online portion €444m (~$519m), +11%.
- ZEAL (Lotto24/Tipp24) reported 9M-26 revenue approaching €200m per trade press.
- Secondary 'bet-on-lottery' products are not permitted, closing Germany to the Lottoland model.

### Strategic relevance

**Lottery and land-based matter only as the fiscal reason for a conservative regime and as an already-consolidated brokerage adjacency.**

- Gaming-hall economics (municipal amusement tax plus VAT on top of device limits) and Spielbank concessions (Land tenders, levies exceeding half of GGR) are closed or capital-intensive.
- These segments sit outside B2Tech's online competence.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, tax and enforcement regime

*A centralised online licensing regime (GGL) layered on a federal turnover tax and 16 Land-level land-based regimes; the §32 evaluation due 31 Dec 2026 and the 2027 renewal cycle set the next rule-set.*

### Legal architecture

**Three tiers: GlüStV 2021 and the GGL for online, federal RennwLottG for tax, and Land law for land-based; §32 evaluation due 31 Dec 2026.**

- Treaty in force 1 July 2021; GGL in Halle (Saale) has held full online competence since 1 January 2023, funded by the Länder.
- Federal RennwLottG sets taxes with revenue accruing to the Länder; Land law governs Spielbanken, gaming halls, retail betting shops and §22c online table games.
- Treaty runs to 31 December 2028 with automatic extension unless terminated [treaty provisions from analyst reference].
- Christian Hochgrebe chaired the GGL Administrative Board from 1 July 2026; executive board is Ronald Benter and Benjamin Schwanke.

### Licence mechanics

**Per-product GGL licences require a €5m deposit scalable to €50m, LUGAS/OASIS connection and audited reporting; a 2027 renewal wave is implied.**

- Security deposit €5m (~$5.9m), raisable to €50m (~$58.5m) with stakes; EU-based servers and regulator data access; safety concept [analyst reference, not re-verified].
- German-language offer with segregated player accounts; administrative fee on projected annual stakes (§9a); annual audited reporting.
- Licence duration and fee tariff could not be verified; first slot and poker licences issued 2022 implies renewal from 2027 [unverified].
- In 2025 LUGAS processed data from more than 60 licensed operators and roughly five million registered accounts.

### Tax regime

**5.3% turnover tax unchanged since 1 July 2021 delivered €2.46bn in 2025; BMF estimates €2,510m for FY-26 (+1.9%).**

- Sports betting, virtual slots, online poker and horse-race betting taxed at 5.3% of stakes, indifferent to margin, bonus cost or mix; lotteries pay 20% of ticket price net of tax.
- Receipts €2.46bn (~$2.88bn) in 2025 (−1%), €1,901m (~$2.22bn) in 9M-26 (~+4% implied), €622.1m (~$728m) in Q3-26 (+11%).
- Länder have no fiscal incentive to move to a GGR base; industry lobbying for a GGR tax has gone nowhere in five years.

### Enforcement and litigation

**2025 enforcement reviewed 2,662 illegal sites and removed 1,843; retrospective player-loss claims are a live liability for legacy grey-market operators.**

- Tools: prohibition orders, payment blocking (§9), app-store and search delisting, co-operation with Land media authorities; ISP blocking constrained by Saxony-Anhalt courts [analyst reference].
- German courts have allowed players to reclaim losses from unlicensed pre-July 2021 operators; Federal Court of Justice referred EU-law compatibility to the CJEU [status unverified].
- An entrant with a legacy grey-market book should price this.

### 2027–28 direction

**Analyst expects the evaluation to recommend incremental change only, with 2028 treaty expiry the realistic implementation date.**

- Probable: modest relaxation of €1 stake and €1,000 deposit mechanics for verified high-affordability players, extended live-betting markets, stronger payment-blocking and influencer/affiliate rules.
- Encouragement — not a mandate — for more Länder to license table games.
- Improbable: switch to GGR taxation or a nationwide live-casino licence.
- Any amendment requires all 16 Landtage.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling and advertising

*Bank-rail payments, a €1,000/month cross-operator deposit cap enforced centrally through LUGAS, and a 06:00–21:00 ad ban for slots/poker make Germany the most friction-heavy large market in Europe for customer acquisition.*

### Payments

**Bank-rail payments with a €1,000/month cross-operator deposit cap checked in LUGAS create high KYC friction and a hard ceiling on VIP economics.**

- Licensed sites accept SEPA, Sofort/Klarna, PayPal, paysafecard and cards; no crypto; no credit; every deposit checked against the LUGAS limit file.
- Default cap €1,000 (~$1,170) per month, raisable on proof of means to €10,000 and, for a small share, €30,000 [thresholds from analyst reference].
- GGL can direct payment providers to stop processing for unlicensed operators under §9 GlüStV — now its principal enforcement lever with delisting.
- Offshore sites' absence of these frictions is the main driver of leakage in casino.

### Responsible gambling

**Germany already has the hardest limits in Western Europe, lowering the probability of further tightening in the 2026 evaluation.**

- LUGAS (on Dataport infrastructure) runs the limit file and an activity file preventing parallel play; OASIS is the nationwide exclusion register with a minimum exclusion period.
- One-click 'panic button' triggers 24-hour lockout; slots carry €1 (~$1.17) stake cap, ≥5-second spin, no autoplay, no jackpots; early-detection and reality checks required [analyst reference].
- In 2025 the system covered >60 operators and ~5m registered accounts.
- Neutralises the UK-style affordability debate and marginally raises the probability of selective loosening.

### Advertising

**A 06:00–21:00 ad ban for slots/poker/casino and athlete and in-play restrictions leave sports sponsorship as the dominant channel, favouring incumbents.**

- Advertising may not target minors or vulnerable groups; sports-betting ads may not feature active athletes or officials or run before/during live broadcasts of that event.
- GGL guidance restricts influencer/streamer promotion and bonus messaging [GlüStV §5 and GGL guidance, analyst reference].
- Incumbents benefit from existing Bundesliga and club deals.
- B2Tech's toolkit: search/affiliate within GGL rules, post-21:00 TV and streaming, sports sponsorship — all priced for scale players.

### Commercial reading

**Deposit cap, no parallel play, stake cap and daytime ad bans compress ARPU and acquisition efficiency; unit economics need low CAC and low cost base.**

- Low CAC comes from organic brand or cross-sell from sports.
- Differentiation is constrained to UX, payout speed, odds and content curation within the €1/5-second envelope.
- A technology-led entrant can compete here, but not with a bonus-driven playbook.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale with stability: €14.4bn (~$16.8bn) regulated GGR, €2.46bn (~$2.88bn) of RennwLottG tax from licensed operators, a treaty fixed to end-2028 and a single national online regulator — the rules will not swing on a single election. _(structural)_
- Online stakes are compounding faster than the flat GGR headline: Q1-26 total stakes +9.4% (online +10.0%), virtual slots +9.3% and sequentially up in Q2; slots GGR +11% in 2025 to €543m (~$635m). _(structural)_
- Event elasticity is proven: Q3-26 tax +11% with July +26% on World Cup 2026 betting — a licensed sportsbook with live content captures tournament demand at scale. _(one-off)_
- The §32 evaluation (due 31 Dec 2026) is the first formal window since 2021 to loosen the €1 stake, €1,000 deposit mechanics and live-betting catalogue — the regime is already at the tight end, so the skew of change is toward selective relaxation. _(early)_
- Retail-to-online migration continues (retail sports share 25.6% → 23.0% in one quarter), expanding the digital pool without new regulation. _(structural)_
- Sports-betting channelisation is high and reported hold is rich (22.8% on GGL definitions): a licensed sportsbook is the credible front door and cross-sell base for slots and poker. _(mixed)_
- B2B opportunity: content and platform must be GGL-specific (€1 stake, 5-second cycle, LUGAS/OASIS integration) — global catalogues are not plug-and-play, creating a defensible supply niche into >60 licensed operators. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Turnover tax: 5.3% of stakes on sports, slots, poker and horse betting is ~45% of GGR for slots at an 11.8% hold and ~23–30% for sports — payable on losing months and before bonus cost. No change proposed; the Länder's fiscal interest runs the other way. _(structural)_
- Product handicap versus offshore: €1 stake, 5-second spin, no jackpots/autoplay/live casino, €1,000 cross-operator deposit cap, no parallel play — the stated reason unlicensed share is 23% of online play (Mar-26 study) and, by industry estimates, 70–80% of casino/slot activity. _(structural)_
- Online table games and live casino are not federally licensable; access is Land-by-Land with small, tender-based licence counts (SH live; NRW [unverified]). _(structural)_
- Governance: any treaty amendment needs all 16 Landtage, and €6.9bn (~$8.1bn) of state levies anchored in the lottery monopoly make the Länder conservative — expect incremental change in 2028, not a liberalised regime in 2027. _(governance)_
- Q3 headline growth is flattered: September implied ~€194m (~$227m), ~−3% YoY; underlying ex-World Cup growth is low-to-mid single digit and online poker stakes are down 11–15%. _(one-off)_
- Legacy liability: player-reimbursement claims against operators that took German business pre-2021 without a German licence remain live in the courts (BGH/CJEU track, status unverified) — relevant to any partner or acquisition target. _(watch)_
- Data opacity: no official operator shares, the GGL Q3-26 Marktmonitor is not yet out, and the Destatis product split of the €622.1m was not retrievable — several figures in this brief are derived and flagged as such. _(watch)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The official bridge is the BMF's 2026 estimate for the Rennwett- und Lotteriesteuer family — €2,510m (~$2.94bn), +1.9% on FY-25's €2,460m (~$2.88bn) — against a 9M-26 actual of €1,901m (~$2.22bn), ~+4% implied. Q3's +€61.6m (~$72m) was driven by sports betting (World Cup timing) and virtual slots (+9–10% stakes), partly offset by online poker (stakes −11% to −15%) and a flat lottery base. Q4 needs only ~€609m (−4% YoY) to meet the BMF number; on a flat Q4 (~€636m implied for Q4-25) FY-26 lands at ~€2.54bn (~$2.97bn), and with modest slots growth ~€2.54–2.57bn (+3–4%) — our estimate, not an official figure.

- **FY-25 actual (RennwLottG receipts):** €2,460m (~$2.88bn) (−1% YoY)
- **9M-26 vs 9M-25 (Q3 +€62m, H1 +€16m):** +€78m (~$91m) (~+4.3% implied)
- **Q4-26E (flat to +3% YoY on Q4-25 ~€636m):** €636–655m (~$744–766m) (estimate)
- **FY-26E vs BMF official €2,510m:** €2,540–2,560m (~$2.97–3.00bn) (+3% to +4% (est.))

**Market:** we expect regulated online GGR to grow 3–5% a year through 2027 (estimate) — slots high single digit, sports betting mid single digit ex-tournaments, poker negative — with stakes growing faster than GGR as the €1/5-second format keeps hold low; land-based flat to −2%. Online penetration drifts from ~24% toward ~26–27% by 2027 (estimate). **Tax:** the 5.3% turnover base stays; no Land has a reason to give up a tax that also captures unlicensed operators. Receipts track stakes, so FY-27 receipts ~+2–4% absent a tournament (estimate). **Rules:** the §32 evaluation (due 31 Dec 2026) will frame a GlüStV amendment package for 2027 negotiation and 2028 implementation: likely selective relaxation of deposit-raise procedures and live-betting markets, tighter affiliate/influencer and payment-blocking provisions, and a nudge toward more Land table-game licences; unlikely a GGR tax or federal live casino. **Enforcement** intensifies on payments and app stores rather than ISP blocking. **Leverage/entry:** for B2Tech, the risk-adjusted path is B2B supply first (GGL-compliant content, LUGAS-ready platform) and, if B2C, a sportsbook-led entry timed to the 2027 renewal window with slots as cross-sell; retail and table games are not where to deploy capital.
