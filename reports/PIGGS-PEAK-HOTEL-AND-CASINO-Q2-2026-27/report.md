> A real but event-assisted swing back to profit: Q2 FY26/27 (Jul–Sep 2026) PBT of E1.261m (~$77k) on total income of E10.093m (~$614k), 28% ahead of a conservative internal budget and reversing Q1's E978k (~$60k) loss. ## 1. Executive Summary

- **Back to profit, well ahead of budget.** Q2 FY26/27 (Jul–Sep 2026) profit before tax of E1.261m (~$77k) versus an internal budget of E537k (~$33k), a 135% beat, reversing the E978k (~$60k) loss booked in Q1 (Apr–Jun). There is no sell-side consensus; the Ministry's own budget is the only benchmark.
- **Top line +28% vs budget and +46% QoQ.** Total income of E10.093m (~$614k) against a budgeted E7.873m (~$479k), a E2.220m (~$135k) beat. Implied Q1 income (H1 less Q2) was E6.917m (~$421k), so the sequential step-up was E3.176m (~$193k).
- **H1 is only just above break-even.** Cumulative H1 income E17.010m (~$1.03m) and H1 PBT E282k (~$17k) — a 1.7% margin. The Q1 loss absorbed ~78% of the Q2 profit, which tells you the ex-event, off-peak run-rate of this property remains loss-making.
- **Operating leverage is the real signal.** The implied cost base (income less PBT) rose only 11.9% QoQ to E8.832m (~$537k) while income rose 45.9%, giving a ~70% incremental PBT margin and a 2,660bps QoQ margin expansion to 12.5%. For a fixed-cost hotel-casino that is the expected shape; the question is sustainability of the volume.
- **But costs overran budget by 20%.** Budgeted Q2 costs were E7.336m (~$446k); actual implied costs were E8.832m (~$537k), +E1.496m (~$91k). Of the E2.220m income beat, two-thirds was absorbed by cost, leaving a E724k (~$44k) PBT beat. The Ministry gave no explanation of the overrun (variable F&B/event cost, trade-show travel, refurbishment expensed, gaming levy — all plausible, none confirmed).
- **No formal guidance exists, and none changed.** This is a Government of Eswatini-owned single property reporting via the Ministry of Tourism & Environmental Affairs' (MTEA) quarterly performance report to the House of Assembly Portfolio Committee, debated 4 Nov 2026. The full-year budget was not disclosed. Being a single asset, there is no divisional attribution — the bridge is simply budget-to-actual.
- **Event-led quarter, quantified.** The Eswatini Dual Bike Adventure (27–30 Aug) brought 115 riders for three nights — up to ~345 room-nights by our arithmetic (assumes single occupancy). At the HOTAES Spotlight trade event in Gauteng the hotel engaged 104 travel-trade representatives and reported several follow-up enquiries. No prior-event comparatives were given.
- **Zero product split.** The Ministry provided no breakdown between gaming, rooms, F&B and other hospitality. Casino GGR, drop, hold, slot/table counts, occupancy, ADR and RevPAR are all undisclosed. A directory listing cites 47 slots and 8 tables [unverified, undated]; a South African tour-operator listing from September 2026 describes the property as "once a casino, now a comfortable heritage hotel" — the operational status and materiality of the gaming floor needs confirming, and our working assumption is that the Q2 beat was lodging/events-led.
- **Product refresh underway, amounts undisclosed.** Phase 1 of a bathroom refurbishment was completed after tour operators flagged bathroom condition as a booking deterrent; a billboard was installed at the entrance; traditional homestead structures were completed for a cultural-experience product still under development.
- **People and governance initiatives.** An e-learning centre and a Staff Health & Safety Committee were established in the quarter. The chairman is Malcos Sengwayo (per an April 2025 conference report), and 2025 coverage referenced "new management" — the current turnaround narrative dates from that change.
- **Capital position: not disclosed.** No balance sheet, cash, debt, leverage, capex or cash-flow figures were provided. For context, the parent ministry's entire FY26/27 budget is E194.4m (~$11.8m), of which only E11.4m (~$0.69m) is capital — further refurbishment phases will depend on internal cash generation or specific government appropriations. No dividends or buybacks (state-owned).
- **No cost-transformation programme.** Management's "aggressive turnaround strategy" as described by the Minister is revenue-led (experience-led tourism, SA trade marketing, product refresh), not cost-led. Given the 20% cost overrun, we would want to see a cost framework in the Q3 report.
- **Regulatory read-through is cautious.** At the same 4 Nov 2026 Portfolio Committee session, MPs raised concerns about gambling addiction and government oversight of gambling operators — a tightening tone for Eswatini gaming that bears on the value of the Piggs Peak licence.
- **Currency.** Reporting is in Eswatini lilangeni (SZL), pegged 1:1 to the rand. USD equivalents use ~E16.44/$ (implied from the 9 Nov 2026 trade-press conversions); the Rio Times used ~E16.2/$ on 30 Oct 2026. ZAR volatility flows straight through to USD-equivalents and to the cost of South African-sourced demand.
- **Budget credibility cuts both ways.** A 28% income beat and 135% PBT beat argue the budget was set conservatively after the Q1 loss rather than evidencing a step-change; equally, management did deliver a quarter at a 12.5% PBT margin, which is better than most sub-scale African provincial casinos manage.
- **Seasonality favours H2.** Q3 (Oct–Dec) captures the December festive peak for the Gauteng drive market and Q4 (Jan–Mar) includes Easter 2027 (28 March). Our scenario range for FY26/27 income is E34–36.5m (~$2.1–2.2m) with PBT E0.5–3.0m (~$29–181k) [estimate — see Forecast].
- **Thesis read-through for sector investors.** This is a ~$2m-revenue, state-owned, unlisted asset with no equity access. Its relevance is as a datapoint on SADC drive-market leisure demand and the viability of a provincial casino licence in a market facing regulatory scrutiny — not as an investable security. No share-price reaction exists.
- **Data quality is low.** The underlying MTEA Q2 report is not retrievable online; every figure rests on two independent secondary reports (Focus Gaming News Africa and Eswatini Positive News) that agree with each other on every number. Q1 income and the cost-base lines are our derivations. YoY comparatives are unavailable.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Piggs Peak Hotel & Casino (single property — lodging, F&B and gaming)

*Event-assisted 46% QoQ income jump drove a swing to a 12.5% PBT margin; costs ran 20% over budget and nothing is disclosed below the top line.*

### Headline results

**Event-assisted 46% QoQ income jump drove a swing to a 12.5% PBT margin; costs ran 20% over budget with nothing disclosed below the top line.**

- Total income of E10.093m (~$614k) for July–September 2026 against a budget of E7.873m (~$479k), a 28.2% beat.
- Profit before tax of E1.261m (~$77k) against a budgeted E537k (~$33k); the Minister characterised this as more than double the profit target.
- Versus Q1 loss of E978k (~$60k) on implied E6.917m (~$421k) income: a E3.176m (~$193k) income step-up and E2.239m (~$136k) PBT swing.
- PBT margin moved from −14.1% to 12.5%, a 2,660bps move; incremental margin on extra income was roughly 70%, as expected for a fixed-cost provincial hotel-casino.
- Half-year stands at E17.010m (~$1.03m) income and E282k (~$17k) PBT, a 1.7% margin, the more honest description of the run-rate.

### Cost overrun

**Implied Q2 cost base of E8.832m (~$537k) overran the E7.336m (~$446k) budget by E1.496m (~$91k) or 20.4%, with no attribution offered.**

- Costs rose 11.9% QoQ from E7.895m (~$480k) in Q1.
- Cost ratio improved from 114.1% of income in Q1 and 93.2% in budget to 87.5%; but two-thirds of the income beat was absorbed by cost.
- Plausible drivers: variable F&B and event servicing, Gauteng trade show travel and exhibition costs, refurbishment items expensed, and gaming levy if casino win participated.
- No EBITDA, D&A or finance-cost line to separate operating performance from accounting; cost side deserves more scrutiny than it received in Parliament.

### Gaming disclosure

**No product split was given; working assumption is gaming is a minor contributor and the Q2 beat was lodging- and events-led.**

- Gaming GGR, drop, hold, slot and table counts, casino actives, occupancy, ADR and RevPAR all absent; Ministry gave no separate gaming income breakdown.
- A casino directory cites 47 slots and 8 tables [unverified, undated]; a SA tour-operator listing updated September 2026 describes the property as once a casino, now a heritage hotel.
- If the casino floor is closed or sub-scale, the "Casino" in the name is a licence option rather than an earnings stream.
- The 4 November Portfolio Committee gambling-oversight debate becomes more relevant to that option value than to current earnings.

### Demand drivers

**The emphasised demand drivers are quantifiable and modest, and the South African source market remains the whole story.**

- Eswatini Dual Bike Adventure, 27–30 August, brought 115 riders staying three nights — up to ~345 room-nights on single occupancy, a meaningful shoulder-month block.
- HOTAES Spotlight trade event in Gauteng yielded engagement with 104 travel-trade representatives and several follow-up enquiries; none of that is revenue yet.
- Marketing spend not disclosed, so marketing as a percentage of income cannot be computed; no prior-event or prior-year comparables to judge uplift.
- Property sits on the Kruger-to-Eswatini corridor; the lilangeni's 1:1 rand peg makes SA consumer health and the rand the macro variables that matter.

### Product and capex

**Phase one of a bathroom refurbishment was completed after tour operators said bathroom condition was costing bookings; no capex figures were given.**

- Also installed an entrance billboard and finished traditional homestead structures for a cultural-experience product still under development.
- Internally set up an e-learning centre and a Staff Health & Safety Committee.
- No capex phasing or funding sources disclosed; parent ministry capital budget is just E11.4m (~$0.69m) for FY26/27, so later phases likely depend on own cash or specific appropriations.
- We expect phase two to displace some room inventory when it starts.

### Guidance

**No conventional guidance; the Q3 (Oct–Dec) portfolio report is the next checkpoint and should be the strongest quarter if Q2 momentum is real.**

- Q3 captures the December festive peak.
- The test is whether the Q2 momentum is real rather than event-specific.

## 4. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- **Return to profit with genuine operating leverage.** Q2 PBT E1.261m (~$77k) versus a E978k (~$60k) Q1 loss; income +45.9% QoQ against costs +11.9% gives a ~70% incremental margin and a 12.5% PBT margin — a credible demonstration of what the fixed-cost base can do with volume. _(structural)_
- **Income 28% above budget.** E10.093m (~$614k) vs E7.873m (~$479k); the E2.220m (~$135k) beat is the largest single positive in the half and both independent reports agree on the figures. _(mixed)_
- **Demand generation is now measurable.** 104 travel-trade representatives engaged at HOTAES Spotlight in Gauteng with follow-up enquiries, and 115 riders for three nights from the Dual Bike Adventure (≤345 room-nights, our estimate) — the SA trade channel is being worked rather than waited for. _(early)_
- **Product deficiencies are being addressed.** Phase 1 bathroom refurbishment completed in direct response to tour-operator feedback that bathrooms were costing bookings; entrance billboard and cultural-homestead product add to the experience-led positioning. _(structural)_
- **H1 cumulative PBT positive.** E282k (~$17k) on E17.010m (~$1.03m) income — thin, but a positive half for a property that has historically required state support keeps the fiscal conversation benign. _(mixed)_
- **Cost ratio trending the right way.** 87.5% of income in Q2 vs 114.1% in Q1 and 93.2% budgeted; if Q3's festive volume lands on a similar cost base, H2 margins should hold in positive territory. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- **Costs overran budget by 20%.** Implied Q2 costs E8.832m (~$537k) vs E7.336m (~$446k) budgeted — E1.496m (~$91k) over, consuming two-thirds of the income beat. No explanation was offered; without one we cannot distinguish variable event cost from structural creep. _(watch)_
- **The ex-event run-rate is loss-making.** Q1 lost E978k (~$60k) on E6.917m (~$421k) income (−14.1% margin) and H1 margin is only 1.7%; one good quarter does not change the fact that the property needs peak-season or event volume to cover its fixed costs. _(structural)_
- **Event-dependency and no comparables.** The quarter's narrative leans on a single 115-rider event and a trade show; there is no prior-year Q2, no prior-event benchmark and no monthly phasing, so the underlying organic trend is unknowable from this disclosure. _(one-off)_
- **No product split, no operating KPIs.** Zero disclosure of gaming GGR, occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, actives or marketing spend. A September 2026 SA tour-operator listing describes the property as 'once a casino' — the status of the gaming floor, and hence the relevance of the 'Casino' licence, is unconfirmed. _(governance)_
- **No balance sheet, cash flow or capex.** The Ministry's portfolio report is a budget-variance note, not a set of accounts. Funding for refurbishment phase 2 and beyond is undisclosed, and the parent ministry's capital budget is only E11.4m (~$0.69m) for the whole of FY26/27. _(governance)_
- **Budget looks conservative, which flatters the 'beat'.** A 28% income and 135% PBT beat against an internal target set after a loss-making Q1 says as much about the budget as about the quarter; the only external benchmark is the one management writes itself. _(governance)_
- **Regulatory tone is tightening.** MPs used the same 4 Nov 2026 Portfolio Committee session to press the government on gambling addiction and operator oversight — a headwind for any plan to lean harder on gaming to lift margins. _(watch)_

## 5. Forecast & Outlook

There is no formal guidance to bridge: Piggs Peak Hotel & Casino is a state-owned single property whose only published benchmark is the Ministry's internal quarterly budget, and the full-year budget was not disclosed. The Q2 bridge is therefore budget-to-actual, and it is entirely attributable to the one property. Income beat budget by E2.220m (~$135k, +28.2%), costs overran by E1.496m (~$91k, +20.4%), and PBT beat by E0.724m (~$44k, +134.8%). Combined with Q1's E978k loss, H1 PBT is E282k (~$17k). Nothing in the Minister's remarks changed any forward target because none exists; the implicit message was that the turnaround strategy is 'working', which the Q2 numbers support and the H1 numbers qualify.

- **Q2 income: budget → actual:** E7.873m → E10.093m (~$479k → ~$614k) (+E2.220m / +28.2%)
- **Q2 implied cost base: budget → actual:** E7.336m → E8.832m (~$446k → ~$537k) (+E1.496m / +20.4%)
- **Q2 PBT: budget → actual:** E0.537m → E1.261m (~$33k → ~$77k) (+E0.724m / +134.8%)
- **H1 PBT: Q1 + Q2:** (E0.978m) + E1.261m = E0.282m (~$17k) (1.7% H1 margin)

Our independent view is that **Q3 (Oct–Dec 2026) should be the strongest quarter of the year** given the December festive peak for the Gauteng drive market, and **Q4 (Jan–Mar 2027) benefits from Easter falling on 28 March**, inside the fiscal year. We model three scenarios [estimates, not company figures]: Q3 income E9.5m / Q4 E7.5m gives FY26/27 income **E34.0m (~$2.07m)** and PBT **E0.5m (~$29k)**, 1.4% margin; a central case of Q3 E10.5m / Q4 E8.0m gives **E35.5m (~$2.16m)** and PBT **E2.0m (~$121k)**, 5.6% margin; an upside of Q3 E11.0m / Q4 E8.5m gives **E36.5m (~$2.22m)** and PBT **E3.0m (~$181k)**, 8.2% margin. All three assume the Q2 cost base of ~E8.8m persists in Q3 and eases to ~E8.0m in Q4 — if costs keep flexing up with revenue as they did in Q2, the central case PBT roughly halves. **Margin direction: positive in H2 but below Q2's 12.5%** given Q4 seasonality. **Leverage: n/a** — no debt is disclosed and we treat the entity as funded by own cash flow plus government appropriations. The swing factors are cost discipline, whether the 104 HOTAES trade contacts convert to contracted itinerary business before the festive season, room displacement from refurbishment phase 2, and the rand (and therefore lilangeni) against the dollar, which only affects USD-equivalents but also the propensity of South African leisure travellers to cross the border.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Q3 FY26/27 MTEA portfolio report (expected ~Feb 2027): does income hold at or above ~E9.5m (~$578k) with costs at or below ~E9.0m (~$547k)? Any cost-variance explanation would be a disclosure first.
- Confirmation of casino operating status and any gaming-vs-lodging income split; a 47-slot/8-table floor would be immaterial to earnings but material to licence option value.
- Refurbishment phase 2: scope, cost, funding source (own cash vs government capital vote) and room displacement timing versus the festive peak.
- Conversion of the 104 HOTAES Spotlight trade contacts into itinerary inclusions or contracted group blocks; repeat booking of the Dual Bike Adventure for 2027.
- Eswatini gambling regulation following the 4 Nov 2026 Portfolio Committee debate on addiction and operator oversight — any levy, hours or advertising changes.
- FY2025/26 audited accounts and Auditor-General commentary on the entity: balance sheet, government transfers/subventions, and whether H1 FY26/27 cash generation matches the reported PBT.
- ZAR/USD and South African consumer conditions — the single source market and the peg mean these set both demand and the USD value of every number above.
