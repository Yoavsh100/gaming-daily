# Airtel Africa plc (AAF.L) — H1 FY2027 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $ millions unless stated; company reports in USD — no FX conversion applied) · Results released 30 Oct 2026**
**Market reaction:** Shares fell c.5.5% to ~259p on 30 Oct 2026 (Investing.com; a second secondary source cites -5.3%), having opened as high as 282p before reversing to a new 52-week low intraday (range cited 255.6p–436.2p). The move was attributed to the doubling of capex, Nigeria's sequential deceleration, the $55m exceptional charge and a risk-off tape, rather than to the H1 numbers themselves. Closing level not verified against LSE official data.

> A clean, high-quality half: revenue +28.2% reported / +20.8% constant currency to $3,823m, underlying EBITDA +32.8% to $1,921m with a record 50.4% Q2 margin despite a c.2–2.5pp fuel headwind, pre-exceptional EPS +41% to 11.7c and leverage down to 1.6x (0.5x lease-adjusted). The market nonetheless sold the shares c.5.5% to a new 52-week low: capex doubled to $657m, Nigeria decelerated (cc growth 24.4% in Q2 vs 29.8% in Q1, voice revenue down 6% QoQ), $55m of exceptionals hit PAT, and the NGN/ZMW translation tailwind that flattered reported growth expires in Q3. Guidance is unchanged (FY27 capex ~$1.1bn, margins "above 50%", dividend mid-to-high single-digit growth).

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Headline beat on quality, not on narrative.** H1 FY27 (six months to 30 Sep 2026; FYE 31 March) revenue of $3,823m was +28.2% reported and +20.8% constant currency (cc), underlying EBITDA $1,921m (+32.8% / +23.5% cc) at a 50.3% margin (+172bps reported, +109bps cc), PAT $452m (+20.5%), basic EPS 10.2c (+23.3%). No published consensus was available to us; the operating numbers are strong on any reading, but the shares fell c.5.5% on the day (see below).
- **Q2 standalone shows the run-rate.** Q2 FY27 revenue $1,970m (+25.7% reported, +20.6% cc, +6.3% QoQ), underlying EBITDA $993m (+29.4% / +22.7% cc, +7.0% QoQ) and a record 50.4% margin (+144bps YoY, +35bps QoQ) despite management saying fuel prices (doubling in Nigeria since April) cost c.2–2.5pp of group margin, c.90% of which was already absorbed in Q2.
- **Growth mix: data and mobile money carry the group.** In cc, voice +11.1% ($1,296m), data +28.0% ($1,571m), mobile money +25.8% ($840m segment, $699m post-elimination), other +7.6%. Data is now 50.0% of mobile services revenue (46.5% a year ago). Currency translation added c.$187m to reported revenue (slides) via NGN appreciation (avg 1,360 vs 1,553) and the Zambian kwacha.
- **Regional growth is broad but Nigeria decelerated.** H1 cc revenue growth: Nigeria +27.0% ($1,014m, 58.1% EBITDA margin), East Africa +18.2% ($1,777m, 53.6%), Francophone +19.2% ($1,022m, 44.0%). Q2 cc: Nigeria 24.4% (Q1 29.8%) as the Q4 FY25 tariff uplift fully lapped and an airtime-advance partner disruption dragged; East Africa accelerated to 18.6% and Francophone to 20.3%.
- **Guidance unchanged — the bridge is flat.** FY27 capex guidance stays at ~$1.1bn (set at FY26 results in May 2026) after H1 capex of $657m (+106.5%), implying H2 of only ~$443m; EBITDA margin commentary is 'above 50%'; dividend policy of mid-to-high single-digit growth reaffirmed with the interim raised 9.2% to 3.1c; the 1% buyback is 67% complete with the remaining 0.33% to resume post close-period. No revenue guidance is given. Management explicitly flagged that the NGN/ZMW lapping benefit ends in Q3, so reported growth will converge toward cc.
- **Mobile money is the structural engine and now a listed asset.** Segment revenue $840m (+25.8% cc), customers 60.9m (+22.4%), TPV $130.1bn in H1 (+36.9% cc) and annualised Q2 TPV $275bn (+36.5%); TPV per customer $380/month (+11.7%). Segment EBITDA margin fell 287bps to 48.9% purely because of the renegotiated intra-group agreements (neutral at group level). Mobile money overtook voice in East Africa ($615m vs $593m).
- **Airtel Money IPO completed 14 Oct 2026** — 270m existing shares (10%) sold by minority holders at £1.96 (c.£5.3bn / ~$7bn equity value per press reports); Airtel Africa sold nothing and retains 77.85%; 27m-share over-allotment outstanding. The $509m minority put-option liability will be derecognised in H2 with the gain to equity. Airtel Money's own maiden H1 print (per the iBetAfrica piece supplied): revenue $830m (+34% / +25.1% cc), PAT $228m, underlying EBITDA $398m at 48.0% — the $10m gap to the group segment is Nigeria PSB, which sits outside the listed entity.
- **Cost efficiency programme (running >2 years) is doing the heavy lifting against fuel.** Levers cited: off-grid-to-grid conversion, solar/battery, AI-driven network energy optimisation, technology modernisation. CFO: at Q4 FY26 fuel prices margins would have been 1.5–2pp higher. Network opex rose to 19.0% of revenue in Q2 from 17.8% in Q1 (our calc) — that is the fuel line; S&M held at 13.2–13.3% of revenue.
- **Capex front-loaded; cash conversion lower.** Capex/revenue 17.2% in H1 (10.7% a year ago; 21.0% in Q1, 13.6% in Q2). Operating FCF $1,264m (+12.0%), net cash from operations $1,759m (+26.7%), but management's normalised FCF fell to $280m from $368m after a $713m capex cash payout (incl. $37m Nigeria 900MHz renewal and $65m Kenya 3,500MHz spectrum), $317m cash tax and $430m cash interest.
- **Balance sheet keeps improving.** Net debt $5,870m (of which leases $4,497m); leverage 1.6x (2.1x), lease-adjusted net debt $1,373m and 0.5x (0.8x); >95% of OpCo debt in local currency; weighted average cost of debt 10.1% (-231bps). ROCE 25.7% (+543bps). Cash $717m plus $1,489m in mobile money trust (restricted).
- **Exceptionals and FX muddy the P&L comparison.** $37m exceptional finance cost (Q1, in-principle settlement of a commercial dispute at a subsidiary) and $19m operating exceptional (Airtel Money IPO costs, Q2), total $55m; FX/derivative gains fell to $2m from $90m. Strip both out and EPS rose to 11.8c from 6.9c (+71%). Underlying finance cost (ex-FX, ex-exceptional) rose to $442m from $394m on $47m higher lease interest from c.2,200 sites added in the period.
- **Tax remains the structural drag.** Total tax charge $362m (+29.1%); company-stated effective rate 40.2% (reported tax/PBT 44.5% on our calc) versus a c.32% weighted statutory rate, driven by profit mix and withholding taxes on subsidiary dividends. Contingent liabilities rose $56m to $277m, including a $132m retrospective WHT assessment on historical dividends at one subsidiary (no provision).
- **Shareholder returns.** $108.7m returned via buyback (24.5m shares, 0.67% of capital; first tranche completed 28 Sep 2026; average c.$4.44/share); interim DPS 3.1c (+9.2%, record 13 Nov, paid 11 Dec 2026); $155m dividends paid to owners and $45m to NCI in H1. Nigeria is expected to turn retained-earnings positive this calendar year, allowing dividend upstreaming from 2027.
- **Strategy/new businesses.** c.4,200 sites added over 12 months, 4,400 additional 4G sites, 99.2% of sites 4G-enabled, 4,094 5G sites across seven markets (5G launched in Gabon and Seychelles), fibre >83,000km. MyAirtel app MAU 14m (8m), app TPV $7.7bn in H1 (+75%). Enterprise base +~60%. Three data centres (Lagos 38MW under construction, Kenya 44MW broken ground, DRC 9MW land acquired). Starlink partnership covers backhaul, enterprise and early direct-to-device in DRC/Uganda under the Airtel brand.
- **Governance refresh.** Gopal Vittal became non-executive chair on 9 Jul 2026 (Sunil Bharti Mittal retired as chair), Shravin Bharti Mittal deputy chair, Annika Poutiainen retired, V.K. Viswanathan appointed INED (22 Jul 2026). Regulatory items: Nigeria 900MHz renewed ($37m, 10 years); Kenya 100MHz of 3,500MHz ($65m, 25 years); ongoing engagement on Uganda 90% coverage obligations and Nigeria tariff adjustments (no new increase secured).
- **Share price reaction: sell-the-news.** Shares fell c.5.5% to ~259p on 30 Oct 2026 (secondary sources; another cites -5.3%), reversing an early rally to 282p and marking a new 52-week low intraday. The debate is valuation versus capex intensity and Nigeria momentum, not the quality of the H1 numbers.
- **Thesis read-through.** The two-engine model (mobile services 5-year revenue CAGR 18.4%; mobile money 28.8%) is intact and operating leverage is visible (EBITDA growing faster than revenue in cc), but H2 reported growth decelerates mechanically as FX lapping ends, Nigeria's price-led growth normalises toward volume-led growth, and the capex step-up compresses FCF. The listed Airtel Money stake (77.85% of a c.£5.3bn IPO valuation ≈ £4.1bn vs AAF market cap of ~£9.4bn at 259p, our estimate) now provides a hard valuation marker for the sum-of-the-parts debate.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Mobile services (Nigeria, East Africa, Francophone Africa telecoms)

*Data is now half of mobile-services revenue; margin +258bps to 50.5% despite doubled fuel costs, with capex up 115% to $614m.*

### Revenue and mix

**Mobile services revenue rose 25.9% reported and 18.9% cc to $3,141m in H1; data crossed 50.0% of segment revenue.**

- Q2 revenue $1,614m: +23.8% reported, +18.8% cc, +5.6% QoQ.
- Voice grew 11.1% cc to $1,296m almost entirely on 11.6% customer base expansion; voice ARPU -0.4% cc at $1.1.
- Total minutes +5.7%; usage flat at 278 minutes per month.
- Data grew 28.0% cc to $1,571m on 15.0% more data customers (89.9m) and 10.7% higher data ARPU ($3.0, +17.1% reported).
- Data share 50.0% versus 46.5% a year ago, 50.9% in Q2 alone; other revenue $274m grew 11.4% cc but slowed to 5.6% in Q2.

### Volume versus price

**Smartphone usage reached 13.3GB per month from 10.1GB; data ARPU growth (10.7%) lags usage growth (c.32%) under 'more-for-more' pricing.**

- Smartphone penetration rose 4.8pp to 51.6%, over 100m smartphones on network; usage was 12.5GB in Q1.
- Revenue per GB falls as consumption rises; management framed this as deliberate: recruit at the low end, let consumption migrate up.
- c.71m 4G/5G data customers against a 194m base; the conversion runway is the core of the equity story.

### EBITDA and fuel

**Underlying EBITDA $1,586m (+32.7% reported, +24.4% cc) at 50.5% margin, +258bps reported and +222bps cc, despite fuel roughly doubling in Nigeria.**

- Q2 margin 50.8%, +199bps YoY, c.+70bps QoQ; fuel prices rose across the footprint.
- CFO quantified group fuel drag at 2–2.5pp of margin, c.90% already in Q2; margins would be 1.5–2pp higher at Q4 FY26 fuel prices.
- Sequentially East Africa mobile services margin +350bps QoQ to 52.2%; Nigeria -130bps to 57.5% (58.8% in Q1); Francophone -120bps to 39.9%.
- Mitigants are the energy programme (grid conversion, solar/battery, AI network optimisation, equipment modernisation) and operating leverage.

### Regional performance

**Nigeria revenue $1,008m (+44.6% reported, +26.6% cc) but Q2 cc growth slowed to 24.0% from 29.4% as Q4 FY25 tariff adjustments fully lapped.**

- East Africa $1,258m (+20.2% reported, +15.4% cc) with Q2 accelerating to 16.4%; Francophone $867m (+15.8%, +15.3% cc), Q2 16.5% from 14.0%.
- Regional H1 EBITDA margins: Nigeria 58.2% (+181bps), East Africa 50.5% (+225bps), Francophone 40.5% (+93bps).
- Nigeria sequential softness — voice revenue -6.3% QoQ in USD, data customers +0.4m QoQ — is the one blemish.
- Attributed to regulation-forced airtime-advance partner changes and the pricing lap rather than competition, which management described as rational with no price war.

### Capex and cash flow

**Capex more than doubled to $614m (+115.1%), 19.5% of segment revenue versus 11.4%, yet operating free cash flow still grew 6.8% to $972m.**

- Q2 capex $246m well below Q1 $368m — the front-loading the CFO described.
- Roughly 4,200 sites added over twelve months (c.2,200 in the half) plus 4,400 additional 4G sites; 99.2% of sites 4G-enabled.
- 5G live at 4,094 sites across seven markets (3,609 at June), including new launches in Gabon and Seychelles; fibre exceeds 83,000km.
- Q2 OpFCF $575m (+23.8% YoY) as EBITDA growth outran capex; D&A +25.4% to $587m and lease interest +$47m at group level are lagging costs.

### Adjacent businesses

**Home broadband, enterprise, Nxtra data centres and Telesonic remain small but are where incremental capex is being pointed.**

- Home broadband penetration c.3% across the footprint against >30m households with affordability.
- Enterprise customer base +~60%.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Mobile money (Airtel Money, all 14 OpCos incl. Nigeria PSB)

*Revenue +25.8% cc to $840m on 22.4% customer growth and 37% TPV growth; margin -287bps to 48.9% is the intra-group repricing, not deterioration; now separately listed (AAF retains 77.85%).*

### Revenue and customers

**Revenue $840m in H1 (+34.8% reported, +25.8% cc), with cc growth a remarkably stable 25.8% across Q1, Q2 and H1.**

- Q2 revenue $437m: +31.3% reported, +25.8% cc, +8.2% QoQ.
- Post inter-segment elimination (the cross-charge to mobile services), revenue was $699m against $494m.
- Customers +22.4% to 60.9m (+7.8% QoQ, 4.4m net adds in the quarter), lifting penetration of the telco base 2.8pp to 31.4%.
- cc ARPU rose only 2.6% to $2.5 (+10.0% reported) because renegotiated intra-group agreements depress both revenue and ARPU.
- Management points to the 133m Airtel telecom customers not yet on the wallet as the acquisition pool.

### Transaction value and products

**Volume is the story: total processed value reached $130.1bn in H1 (+46.4% reported, +36.9% cc); implied take rate c.0.65% and drifting down.**

- Q2 TPV $68.6bn (+42.1% reported, +36.5% cc, +11.7% QoQ); annualised run-rate $275bn versus 'over $245bn' at Q1 and $196bn for FY26.
- TPV per customer rose 11.7% to $380 per month.
- Wallet services (cash-in/cash-out) +27.6% cc to $409m; payments and transfers +29.8% to $363m (33.2% in Q2, fastest-growing large line).
- Financial services +56.9% to $45m, share of revenue 5.4% from 4.3%; 'others' (retention revenue) -40.7% to $23m on intra-group changes.
- Take rate falls as payments and transfers outgrow cash-out — the same dynamic the market flagged in Airtel Money's post-IPO print.

### Distribution and digital

**Active agents exceeded 2.5m (+25.3%) and active merchants 587,000 (+85.5%); the growth algorithm is engagement-led rather than purely acquisition-led.**

- Exclusive retail touchpoints 48,000; merchants on interoperable QR, tap-and-pay and payment links.
- App-transacting customers 6.6m; app penetration of smartphone base 19.9% (14.6%); smartphone users over 54% of mobile money base.
- App and multi-service customers carry higher ARPU.

### Regional split

**East Africa generated $615m (+20.5% cc), now larger than East Africa voice revenue; Francophone $215m (+40.2% cc) driven by DRC.**

- East Africa customers 44.1m (+13.5%).
- Francophone customers 12.6m (+41.9%, 10.5% QoQ).
- Nigeria (PSB) $10m from 4.2m customers (+110.9%), still immaterial.

### EBITDA and cash

**Underlying EBITDA $411m (+27.4% reported, +17.2% cc) at 48.9% margin, -287bps reported and -359bps cc; this is intra-group repricing, not deterioration.**

- Q2 margin 48.7% (-223bps YoY, c.-40bps QoQ).
- Management attributes the whole decline to the July 2025 intra-group renegotiation, margin-neutral at group level and pre-disclosed; lap completes in Q3.
- Capex is trivial ($25m); operating free cash flow $386m (+29.0%) converts c.94% of EBITDA.
- D&A rose 63% to $22m; segment absorbed $6m of IPO exceptional costs; operating profit $374m (+24.0%).

### London listing

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Nigeria (mobile services + Airtel Money PSB)

*Still the fastest-growing region (+27.0% cc) at a 58.1% margin, but Q2 decelerated to +24.4% cc as the tariff uplift lapped, fuel doubled and the airtime-advance disruption bit; 26.5% of group revenue.*

### Revenue growth

**H1 revenue $1,014m, +45.0% reported and +27.0% cc; Q2 decelerated to 24.4% cc from 29.8% in Q1 as the tariff uplift fully lapped.**

- Reported/cc gap explained by naira appreciation: weighted average NGN/USD 1,360 versus 1,553 a year earlier; 1,367 in Q1.
- Q2 revenue $514m, +40.2% reported, only 2.6% QoQ — first clear deceleration since Q4 FY25 tariff adjustments, whose full effect landed in Q2 FY26.
- Nigeria is 26.5% of group revenue, which management cites as evidence the portfolio is balanced against single-currency shocks.

### Voice and airtime advance

**Voice revenue $370m grew 20.6% cc but fell 6.3% QoQ in USD to $179m on regulator-mandated airtime-advance partner changes.**

- Voice ARPU +7.0%, customers +15.1%.
- New lenders took longer than expected to stabilise and customers temporarily lost access to airtime credit; management says operations are now normalising.

### Data

**Data revenue $544m grew 33.8% cc (52.7% reported) but Q2 cc growth slowed to 30.1% from 38.0% in Q1.**

- Data customers +11.8% to 32.9m; data ARPU +20.5% cc; smartphone penetration 56.8% (+4.0pp, highest in the group); usage 16.7GB per month from 12.7GB.
- Data customers rose only 0.4m QoQ against 1.6m total net adds; mobile services ARPU growth slowed to 8.4% cc in Q2 from 16.5% in Q1.
- Management cites 'more-for-more' pricing — new customers enter at lower consumption, heavy users see revenue per GB fall — plus still-small home broadband and B2B.
- Competition described as rational with no price war; NCC engagement on further tariffs continues with no minimum interval constraint, but no new increase in hand.

### Profitability and fuel

**H1 underlying EBITDA $589m (+50.2% reported, +31.5% cc) at 58.1% margin (+199bps), the group's best, despite fuel prices doubling since the year started.**

- Fuel impact built progressively; Q2 absorbed the full effect.
- Mobile services margin declined c.130bps sequentially to 57.5% from 58.8%; regional margin including PSB 57.6% from 58.6%.
- The cost efficiency programme limited the damage but did not eliminate it.

### Capex and cash flow

**Mobile services capex $177m (+139.9%), heavily Q1-weighted ($128m then $50m); operating free cash flow rose 28.2% to $409m.**

- The 900MHz licence was renewed for $37m (payable in naira) for ten years.
- Nigeria PSB (SmartCash) remains a rounding error at $10m revenue and 4.2m customers but is doubling.

### Dividend upstreaming

**CFO said Nigeria should turn retained-earnings positive this calendar year, enabling dividend upstreaming from 2027 if currencies stay stable.**

- This follows the post-devaluation losses.
- A meaningful change in the cash story for a market that has been a trapped-cash problem since 2023.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Record Q2 underlying EBITDA margin of 50.4% (+144bps YoY, +35bps QoQ) and H1 50.3% (+172bps) delivered while absorbing a 2–2.5pp fuel headwind — the cost efficiency programme (energy, AI network optimisation, grid/solar conversion) is demonstrably real and the H1 margin would have been 1.5–2pp higher at Q4 FY26 fuel prices. _(structural)_
- Mobile money compounding at a steady 25.8% cc with TPV +36.9% cc to $130bn (annualised $275bn), customers +22.4% to 60.9m, merchants +85.5% and financial services revenue +56.9%; now a separately listed asset at c.£5.3bn equity value with AAF retaining 77.85% — a hard SOTP marker. _(structural)_
- Data revenue +28.0% cc to $1,571m on 15% more data customers and 10.7% ARPU growth, now 50% of mobile services revenue; smartphone penetration 51.6% with c.71m 4G/5G users on a 194m base leaves a long conversion runway. _(structural)_
- Leverage down to 1.6x (0.5x lease-adjusted; lease-adjusted net debt $1,373m from $1,633m), cost of debt -231bps to 10.1%, >95% of OpCo debt in local currency, ROCE 25.7% (+543bps) — the balance sheet can fund the capex step-up and returns simultaneously. _(structural)_
- Francophone and East Africa both accelerated in Q2 (cc +20.3% and +18.6%) — group growth is no longer a Nigeria price-increase story; the three regions are each growing 18–27% cc with East Africa's mobile services margin +350bps QoQ. _(mixed)_
- Nigeria upstreaming: management expects retained earnings to turn positive this calendar year with dividends from Nigeria from 2027, and the 900MHz licence is renewed for ten years at $37m payable in naira — removes a long-standing trapped-cash overhang. _(early)_
- Underlying EPS (pre-exceptional, ex FX/derivative gains) rose 71% to 11.8c from 6.9c; interim DPS +9.2% to 3.1c; $108.7m buyback executed with the final 0.33% tranche to resume and the board to review a further programme on completion. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Nigeria momentum faded in Q2: cc revenue growth 24.4% vs 29.8% in Q1, data cc growth 30.1% vs 38.0%, mobile services ARPU growth 8.4% vs 16.5%, voice revenue -6.3% QoQ and data net adds of just 0.4m. Management blames the tariff lap, 'more-for-more' pricing and the airtime-advance partner disruption — plausible, but Nigeria has no new tariff approval and fuel there has doubled. _(watch)_
- Capex more than doubled to $657m (17.2% of revenue vs 10.7%) and normalised FCF fell to $280m from $368m; D&A +26% and lease interest +$47m are the lagging P&L costs. The ~$1.1bn FY27 guide implies H2 of only ~$443m, which looks optimistic against 'we remain open to possibilities' — upside risk to capex, downside to FCF. _(watch)_
- Reported growth has been flattered by NGN (avg 1,360 vs 1,553) and ZMW appreciation (c.$187m of H1 revenue, c.+7.4pp of reported growth); the CFO explicitly said the lapping benefit ends in Q3, so reported revenue/EBITDA growth should step down toward the c.20% cc rate just as the stock derates. _(one-off)_
- $55m of exceptionals ($37m in-principle settlement of a commercial dispute at an unnamed subsidiary; $19m Airtel Money IPO costs) plus FX/derivative gains collapsing to $2m from $90m; contingent liabilities up $56m to $277m including a $132m retrospective WHT assessment on historical dividends with no provision taken. _(governance)_
- Effective tax remains punitive — total tax $362m, company-stated ETR 40.2% (44.5% reported tax/PBT) against a c.32% statutory blend because of profit mix and withholding tax on subsidiary dividends; NCI absorbs 18% of PAT, and the Airtel Money listing institutionalises a c.22% minority leak on the fastest-growing cash stream. _(structural)_
- Mobile money segment margin -287bps to 48.9% (Q2 48.7%) from the intra-group repricing, and the implied take rate is drifting down (c.0.65% vs 0.70%) as payments outgrow cash-out — neutral at group level but it is the variable the market will now price in the listed subsidiary. _(mixed)_
- Francophone remains structurally low-margin (44.0% region, 39.9% mobile services in Q2, -120bps QoQ) and its OpFCF fell 21.6% on doubled capex; Uganda's 90% coverage obligation and Nigeria tariff engagement are unresolved regulatory items. _(watch)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

There is no formal revenue or EBITDA guidance and nothing changed at this print. The only quantitative guide — FY27 capex of approximately $1.1bn, set at the FY26 results on 8 May 2026 — was reiterated despite H1 spend of $657m (60% of the full-year number), so the arithmetic implies a much lighter H2 of ~$443m; the CEO's 'we remain open to possibilities' leaves the door ajar for an upward revision. On margins, management reiterated 'above 50%' with fuel 90% absorbed in Q2 and only marginal incremental drag expected in Q3 if prices stabilise (though they noted fuel rose again in some markets in the three weeks before the call). The dividend framework (mid-to-high single-digit growth) and the 1% buyback (67% done, 0.33% to resume) are unchanged. The division driving the narrative is mobile services in Nigeria — the deceleration there, rather than any guidance change, is what the market reacted to — while the mobile money segment's intra-group margin lap completes in Q3 and the FX translation tailwind (NGN/ZMW) expires in Q3.
