Q1 FY2027 results · analyst report

The Rank Group plc

RNK.L · Released 15 Oct 2026 · Currency GBP (tables in £m; USD equivalents at ~1.33 shown in parentheses) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Broadly flat on the day's open: 74.10p (~$0.99) at 09:31 BST, +0.3% versus the 73.90p prior close (Sharecast/ADVFN); the stock entered the print down c.14% over one month, c.23% over three and c.43% over twelve (Shore Capital, 15 Oct 2026), implying a market cap of c.£347m (~$461m) on 468.4m shares. The 15 October closing level could not be verified [unverified].

The one-line verdict

A clean but revenue-only Q1: LFL NGR +5% to £216.1m (~$287m), digital +6% and Grosvenor +6% (machines +14%) absorbing the first full quarter of 40% Remote Gaming Duty, with FY27 guidance reiterated as "in line with market expectations" — explicitly conditional on no Budget change to Machine Games Duty. The print neither re-rates nor de-rates the equity; the stock remains a binary on the Autumn Budget, where Shore Capital sizes a hypothetical MGD doubling at £35m (~$47m) gross against FY26 underlying operating profit of £78.6m (~$105m). Beneath the headline, growth decelerated through the quarter (first six weeks +8%, full quarter +5%) and Mecca visits fell again.

1Executive summary

  • Headline in line, no profit disclosure. Q1 FY27 (three months to 30 Sep 2026) Group like-for-like NGR +5% to £216.1m (~$287m): digital +6% to £64.9m (~$86m), venues +5% to £151.2m (~$201m). Rank's Q1 statement is revenue-only — no EBITDA, profit, cash or customer-count disclosure — so the quarter is judged on top-line and tone alone. Growth is at the top end of Shore Capital's unchanged 4–5% FY27 LFL NGR assumption.
  • Guidance unchanged, but explicitly conditional. FY27 Group LFL operating profit guided "in line with market expectations" "based on the current industry tax regime in the UK". No division drove a change because there was none; the caveat is the story. Shore Capital holds FY27 adj. PBT at £54.7m (~$73m), modelling a c.£9m (~$12m) YoY operating profit decline (i.e. c.£70m, ~$93m) on a c.£20m (~$27m) net RGD headwind. Company-compiled consensus was not retrieved [unverified].
  • Deceleration inside the quarter is the under-reported signal. At the 13 Aug prelims, the first six weeks of FY27 showed Group NGR +8%, digital +10% and Grosvenor machines +15%. A full quarter at +5%/+6%/+14% implies weeks 7–13 ran at roughly +2–3% for Group and digital on equal-weekly arithmetic (our estimate). Management cited the UK heatwave for Mecca only; nothing was offered for the digital slowdown.
  • Digital held up at 40% RGD with marketing cut — but growth halved. UK digital +6% (Mecca online +7%), international +4%, in the first full quarter at the 40% duty rate (21% to 31 Mar 2026) and with above-the-line marketing "significantly reduced". This compares with +12% in Q4 FY26 (UK +12%) and +13% in Q1 FY26 (UK +15%, Grosvenor online +31%). Grosvenor online growth was not disclosed this quarter — a notable omission for the largest cross-channel brand (£94.6m, ~$126m FY26 NGR).
  • Grosvenor is the operating engine. LFL NGR +6% to £108.1m (~$144m), visits +1%, spend per visit +5%, +10% QoQ on Q4 FY26's Middle-East-affected £98.3m (~$131m). Machines +14% is the fourth consecutive quarter of acceleration (Q3 FY26 +10%, Q4 +12%) following the 850-machine rollout; tables +3% (FY26 flat), electronic +5%. Implied weekly NGR c.£8.3m (~$11.0m) vs £7.6m (~$10.1m) FY26 average and the £9.5m (~$12.6m) medium-term target.
  • Three Grosvenor casinos closed (Swansea, Reading Central, Sunderland) as "no longer economically viable in the medium term", following nine Mecca closures in FY26. Closure costs will land in H1 FY27 separately disclosed items (unquantified) and the LFL base is being re-cut again — estate pruning flatters LFL op profit margin but shrinks the reported revenue base.
  • Mecca flat, and visits keep falling. LFL NGR £32.2m (~$43m), 0% YoY; visits −3%, spend per visit +3%. The visit trend has deteriorated each period (−1% Q1 FY26, −2% FY26, −3% now) and spend-per-visit growth halved from +6%. Weather is the stated cause; the structural attrition of an older customer base is the unstated one. The August target of "double-digit operating profit in 2026/27" (FY26: £8.9m, ~$12m) was not reiterated in the Q1 statement.
  • Enracha the quiet compounder. Spanish venues LFL NGR +7% to £10.9m (~$14m), electronic gaming +11%, consistent with +5% (Q1 FY26), +7% (FY26) and +6% (Q4 FY26); FY26 LFL op profit a record £12.0m (~$16m) at a 26.5% margin — the group's highest-margin unit and, together with Yo digital, a natural hedge against UK fiscal risk.
  • Tax is the whole debate. RGD rose from 21% to 40% on 1 Apr 2026; Rank quantified the gross annual operating profit impact at c.£40m (~$53m) before mitigation (Nov 2025 Budget RNS) and management said in August that "digital profitability will inevitably step down in 2026/27". MGD sits at 20%; Shore Capital estimates a hypothetical doubling to 40% would be a £25m (~$33m) gross hit at Grosvenor and £10m (~$13m) at Mecca — c.45% of FY26 underlying operating profit. Peel Hunt expects the Budget to pass without negative effect.
  • CEO messaging is lobbying, not guiding. Richard Harris: any MGD increase "will increase costs, reduce venue viability, lead to venue closures and job losses, reduce tax receipts and halt investment"; SBC reports he has suggested a hike could close around a third of venues. Rank paid £225.9m (~$300m) in UK/Spanish taxes and duties in FY26 (£198.9m, ~$265m in the UK).
  • Cost mitigation: delivered in H2 FY26, nothing new quantified. The RGD response (ATL marketing cuts, supplier savings, headcount reductions) held FY26 LFL employment costs flat at £270.3m (~$359m) despite c.4% underlying inflation and let FY26 underlying operating profit reach £78.6m (~$105m), +21%, 9.4% margin. FY27 capex is guided to normalise to c.£40m (~$53m) from £50.2m (~$67m), with "further improvement in underlying cash generation". No new programme or target-year savings were disclosed at Q1.
  • Capital position (last reported 30 Jun 2026) is comfortable. Net cash pre-IFRS 16 £56.8m (~$76m); IFRS 16 net debt £147.2m (~$196m) = c.1.1x FY26 underlying EBITDA of £138.3m (~$184m) (our calc), comprising £204.0m (~$271m) leases and £30m (~$40m) drawn on a new four-year £120m (~$160m) RCF against £86.8m (~$115m) cash. FY26 net free cash flow £25.5m (~$34m) after £10.3m (~$14m) SDI cash outflows. Final dividend 2.50p (~$0.033) paid 23 Oct; FY26 total 3.50p (~$0.047), +35%. No buyback; policy is progressive dividend to >35% payout with surplus returns at Board discretion.
  • Legacy one-offs still bleeding cash. FY26 SDIs of £22.9m (~$30m) included the £6.5m (~$8.6m) Spanish payment fraud, a £5.0m (~$6.7m) proposed Gambling Commission settlement for historical Grosvenor venue compliance failings (review period Nov 2024–May 2025), £6.7m (~$8.9m) Mecca closure costs and £3.7m (~$4.9m) restructuring. Management flagged further SDI cash outflows in FY27; the three Q1 casino closures add to the list.
  • Medium-term targets stand (as of August). ≥£100m (~$133m) underlying operating profit; Grosvenor £9.5m (~$12.6m) weekly NGR with +500bps margin; machine NGR ≥£3m (~$4.0m)/week vs £2.2m (~$2.9m) in FY26; ROCE 18.3% in FY26 (+3.2ppt). Shore notes an MGD rise could delay the £100m. Q1 did not restate these.
  • Governance remains in transition. Richard Harris (ex-CFO) became interim CEO on 30 Jan 2026 and permanent CEO on 13 Jul 2026; interim CFO Cliff Baty sits on ExCo but not the Board; Chair John Ott since Nov 2025; Lucinda Charles-Jones left the Board after the 8 Oct AGM, leaving interim Audit (Keith Laslop) and Remuneration (Katie McAlister) chairs and a vacant SID role. AGM resolutions passed comfortably (McAlister re-election 98.2% for). A permanent CFO and SID are overdue.
  • Strategy pivot to 'casino-led and bingo-led gaming'. FY27 initiatives flagged in August — smaller-format, machine-led casino trial on spare licences; Mecca social lounges (Stockton £1.6m, ~$2.1m); Mecca app rollout due complete by end Q1 FY27 and single membership in H1 FY27; YoBingo Portugal; Intelligent Customer Platform — none received a progress update in the Q1 statement.
  • Comparables frame the slowdown. Quarterly Group LFL NGR growth: Q1 FY26 +9%, H1 FY26 +6% (£419.8m, ~$558m), Q4 FY26 +6% (£208.9m, ~$278m), FY26 +6% (£834.1m, ~$1.11bn), Q1 FY27 +5%. Digital: +13%, c.+4% (Q2 implied), +2% (Q3), +12% (Q4), +6%. The Q4 FY27 comp (+12% digital) is the hardest in the year.
  • Market reaction: a non-event into a known binary. Shares were broadly flat early at 74.10p (~$0.99), +0.3% vs the 73.90p prior close, having fallen c.14% over one month, c.23% over three and c.43% over twelve (Shore Capital). At 74p the market cap is c.£347m (~$461m) and EV/FY26 underlying EBITDA c.3.6x on an IFRS 16 basis (our calc) — the Budget is largely priced, which is Shore's point.
  • Thesis read-through. Operationally the equity story is intact: machine-led Grosvenor growth, a UK digital business proven viable at 40% RGD, estate pruning and falling capex driving FCF. But the quarter adds two non-Budget worries — in-quarter deceleration in digital and a third consecutive decline in Mecca visits — and the only catalysts that matter are the Budget (date not confirmed in our sources) and H1 results on 28 Jan 2027.

3Segment deep-dive: Digital (UK: Mecca, Grosvenor, proprietary brands; International: Yo/Enracha Spain, Portugal)

Resilient at 40% RGD with marketing cut — +6% to £64.9m (~$86m) — but growth halved from Q4 and faded through the quarter; profitability step-down is the FY27 certainty.

Q1 revenue

Digital LFL NGR rose 6% to £64.9m (~$86m), resilient under 40% RGD with marketing cut, but growth roughly halved from Q4.

  • First full quarter under 40% Remote Gaming Duty (up from 21% from 1 April 2026), with above-the-line marketing "significantly reduced".
  • UK grew 6%, led by Mecca online at +7%; international (YoBingo/YoCasino/YoSports Spain plus Portuguese YoBingo) +4%.
  • Sequentially +1.6% on Q4 FY26's £63.9m (~$85m); growth rate halved from Q4's +12% and Q1 FY26's +13% (UK +15%, Grosvenor online +31%, Mecca online +9%).
  • Shore Capital highlighted growth in first-time depositors; Digital was 30.0% of Group NGR, broadly unchanged.
“to demonstrate resilient customer metrics despite the higher tax regime”Management

Intra-quarter fade

First six weeks ran +10%; a +6% quarter implies the remaining seven weeks at roughly +2–3% (our estimate), back toward Q3 FY26's +2% run-rate.

  • Q3 FY26's +2% had been blamed on platform stability before the Q4 rebound.
  • Grosvenor online (13% growth in FY26, largest cross-channel brand at £94.6m, ~$126m) was not disclosed; inference is it grew below the UK average of 6%.
  • Other proprietary brands (Spin & Win, Lucky Pants; £21.0m, ~$28m, −5% in FY26) were again unmentioned and likely remained in decline despite brand refreshes.

Marketing and cost drags

Marketing is the swing variable; management signals a re-balance that may require redeploying spend into performance marketing to sustain the top line.

  • ATL cuts protected FY26 profit; Rank does not disclose marketing as % of NGR, monthly actives or ARPU at quarterly points.
  • FY26 UK average revenue per customer +10%, with free bets and incentives maintained; NGR is defined as GGR less customer incentives.
  • Structural drags persist: statutory RPT levy at 1.1% of GGY (£2.7m, ~$3.6m in FY26) and online slots staking limit (£4.0m, ~$5.3m profit impact in FY26).
“Our focus on the customer proposition and overall marketing spend balance remains a priority as the wider digital landscape continues to reset.”Management

Profitability outlook

Profit undisclosed; we estimate FY27 digital operating profit of £22–27m (~$29–36m), a 10–11% margin, versus FY26's £37.9m (~$50m) at 15.2%.

  • FY26 included only one quarter at 40% RGD; management guided in August that "digital profitability will inevitably step down in 2026/27".
  • November 2025 Budget RNS put gross pre-mitigation annual impact at c.£40m (~$53m); Shore Capital models c.£20m (~$27m) net headwind after mitigation.
  • Q4 FY27 comparison (+12%) is the toughest of the year.
  • Any HM Treasury move to apply 40% to gross gaming yield rather than gross gaming revenue — Rank's lobbying ask — would be upside not in anyone's numbers.

International and watch items

International grew 4% versus Spain's +13% in H2 FY26; at c.£30m (~$40m) annual NGR, diversification remains more narrative than number.

  • Slowdown suggests Spain decelerated or Portugal's start-up is diluting the aggregate — no split given; Spain had resolved platform capacity constraints.
  • International is c.12% of digital.
  • Segment guidance implicit in Group "in line with market expectations" line.
  • Watch the Intelligent Customer Platform implementation, Gambling Commission Financial Risk Assessment rollout, and whether marketing intensity rises into H2.

4Segment deep-dive: Grosvenor Venues (UK casinos)

+6% to £108.1m (~$144m) on spend per visit; machines +14% and accelerating for a fourth quarter — the operating thesis, and the MGD exposure, in one segment.

Q1 revenue

LFL NGR grew 6% to £108.1m (~$144m), driven by spend per visit +5% with visits +1%; part of the +10% QoQ is recovery.

  • Q1 FY26 was +8% (visits +5%, spend per visit +3%) when the £15m (~$20m) Victoria, Edgware Road reopening drove footfall.
  • Q4 FY26 (£98.3m, ~$131m) growth had slowed to +3% as the Middle East conflict hit London table play.
  • Implied weekly NGR c.£8.3m (~$11.0m) vs £7.6m (~$10.1m) for FY26 and £9.5m (~$12.6m) medium-term target paired with 500bps margin ambition.
  • No London versus rest-of-UK split this quarter (FY26: £123.6m / £273.7m, both +5%).

Machines and mix

Gaming machines grew 14%, the fourth consecutive quarter of acceleration, supporting margin but concentrating MGD exposure.

  • Cadence: Q3 FY26 +10%, Q4 +12%, first six weeks FY27 +15%; 850 incremental machines installed across 38 casinos in H1 FY26 with minimal capital.
  • Machines take two to three years to mature; weekly machine NGR £2.2m (~$2.9m) in FY26 should reach at least £3m (~$4.0m); Q1 tracking toward c.£2.5m.
  • Table gaming +3% (flat in FY26; 44% of casino NGR); electronic gaming +5% (16% of NGR).
  • FY26 machine NGR of £113.2m (~$151m) sits under Machine Games Duty at 20%.

Estate reshaping

Swansea, Reading Central and Sunderland closed as "no longer economically viable in the medium term"; estate is being rebuilt around machine economics.

  • Follows FY26 impairment charges of £9.4m (~$12.5m) against reversals of £3.9m (~$5.2m); closure costs fall into H1 FY27 separately disclosed items (unquantified).
  • FY27 brings trials of smaller-format machine- and electronic-terminal-led casinos using spare licences, a real-time machine loyalty trial in H1, and sports betting terminals in 24 venues.
  • Refurbishment capex redirected toward smaller, higher-return schemes after analysis showed these outperform large projects.

Profit and MGD risk

We estimate FY27 Grosvenor operating profit of £38–41m (~$51–55m) before any MGD change; a doubling to 40% would cost £25m (~$33m).

  • FY26 underlying LFL operating profit £35.5m (~$47m), +11%, 8.9% margin; employment costs £161.8m (~$215m) up 2% on National Minimum Wage and 0.5% RPT levy.
  • Semi-fixed cost base has historically delivered c.60% drop-through; loss-making venues removed and depreciation moderating.
  • Shore Capital's £25m hypothetical MGD hit is roughly two-thirds of FY26 segment profit, which is why the CEO's statement devotes more words to the Budget than trading.

Regulatory and guidance

The £5.0m (~$6.7m) proposed Gambling Commission settlement relates to historical Grosvenor compliance failings; machines are the one unambiguous positive.

  • Review period 1 Nov 2024–1 May 2025; remedial actions substantially implemented in H1 FY26, facial-recognition entry trials under way.
  • Segment guidance subsumed in the Group line; machine performance is the lever on which the £100m (~$133m) Group target depends.

5Segment deep-dive: Mecca Venues (UK bingo)

Flat at £32.2m (~$43m) with visits −3% — weather is the stated reason, but visit attrition has worsened for three straight periods; the duty-abolition tailwind still points to double-digit profit.

Q1 revenue

LFL NGR flat at £32.2m (~$43m), visits −3% and spend per visit +3%, with hot weather the stated reason.

  • Absolute figure is 9% below Q1 FY26's £35.5m (~$47m) and Q4 FY26's £35.4m (~$47m), but neither is like-for-like.
  • Nine clubs closed during FY26 (FY26 revenue £12.6m, ~$17m, roughly £3m a quarter) removed from the base, leaving 41 clubs.

Visit trend

Visit attrition has worsened for three straight periods (−1%, −2%, −3%) and spend-per-visit growth has halved from +6% to +3%.

  • Shore Capital expects improvement as weather normalises; Q2 FY26 comparison was "slightly softer", so H1 optics may recover.
  • Mainstage bingo +12% in FY26 and in good health; interval bingo flat; gaming machines +6%, 42% of NGR, carry the growth.
  • 60% of visits played on tablets, electronic customers 80% of mainstage spend; growth depends on digitising an ageing customer base in a shrinking estate.

Profit outlook

Bingo duty abolition gives c.£4.8m (~$6.4m) incremental FY27 benefit; we estimate £10–12m (~$13–16m) profit, but MGD doubling would exceed FY26 profit.

  • FY26 underlying LFL operating profit £8.9m (~$12m), +107%, 6.2% margin; duty abolished from start of Q4 FY26 with £1.6m (~$2.1m) in-year, c.£6.4m (~$8.5m) annualised.
  • August guidance of "well on track to deliver double digit operating profit in 2026/27" was not repeated in the Q1 release.
  • Shore's £10m (~$13m) gross MGD hit would exceed Mecca's entire FY26 profit — bingo clubs are the most MGD-sensitive assets in the group.

6Segment deep-dive: Enracha Venues (Spain)

+7% to £10.9m (~$14m) on electronic gaming +11% — the group's most consistent and highest-margin unit, and its only hedge against UK fiscal risk.

Q1 revenue

LFL NGR grew 7% to £10.9m (~$14m), driven by electronic gaming +11%, with remarkably consistent growth cadence.

  • Nine large multi-game bingo-led venues combining bingo, electronic roulette, gaming machines and sports betting; LFL excludes FX, so local-currency growth.
  • 3.5% below Q4 FY26's £11.3m (~$15m), seasonal rather than a trend break: +5% (Q1 FY26), +7% (FY26), +6% (Q4 FY26), +7% now.
  • No visit or spend-per-visit split for the quarter (FY26: visits −1%, spend per visit +8%).

Profit and economics

FY26 operating profit was a record £12.0m (~$16m), +8%, at a 26.5% margin — highest in the group, a third of Group profit from 5% of revenue.

  • Gaming machines 46% of FY26 revenue, machine numbers +16% since FY24 with revenue per machine +6%; bingo product competitors have withdrawn.
  • Well-invested venues: Sabadell machine-area expansion; Seville Bingo Boom social format being copied into Mecca Stockton.
  • December 2025 payment fraud (£6.5m, ~$8.6m, separately disclosed item) was a control failure, not trading, but Spanish units run on a separate platform and control environment.

Strategic hedge and guidance

Enracha plus Yo digital (c.£75m, ~$100m NGR, c.£15–16m, ~$20–21m profit) carry no RGD or MGD exposure, but the hedge is partial.

  • At current scale they offset perhaps a third of the modelled net RGD headwind.
  • Guidance implicit in Group line; we expect FY27 Enracha operating profit of £12–13m (~$16–17m) on mid-single-digit growth (our estimate).
  • Reported sterling figures sensitive to EUR/GBP.
