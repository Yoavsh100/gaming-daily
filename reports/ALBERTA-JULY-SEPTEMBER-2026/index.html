JULY-SEPTEMBER 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Alberta

Released 23 Oct 2026 (AiGC first Market Performance Report; data 13 Jul–30 Sep 2026) · Currency CAD (tables in CA$ millions unless stated; ~USD shown at USD/CAD 1.40) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Alberta's first three months as Canada's second open iGaming market produced CA$122.1m (~US$87m) of unaudited NAGGR on CA$2.89bn (~US$2.06bn) of wagers, with September alone at CA$56.6m (~US$40m) — an annualised run-rate (~CA$680m) already above the provincial government's implied Year-1 base, before October's near-doubling of live sites from 24 operators to 50-plus brands. It is a casino-led (78% of NAGGR), Ontario-template market with an effective ~22% public take on GGR, a CA$150k per-site annual fee and a near-total ban on bonus advertising; the commercial question is not whether demand exists but how much of it 50-65 brands can hold at an acceptable acquisition cost.

1Executive summary

  • Market is live and scaling fast. Alberta opened its competitive iGaming market on 13 Jul 2026 under the iGaming Alberta Act (SA 2025). AiGC's first Market Performance Report (23 Oct 2026) shows NAGGR of CA$22.2m (~US$15.9m) in the 19 days of July, CA$43.3m (~US$30.9m) in August and CA$56.6m (~US$40.4m) in September — CA$122.1m (~US$87.2m) in total on CA$2,885.1m (~US$2,061m) of wagers. Winnings paid were CA$2,762.9m (~US$1,974m).
  • Growth so far is onboarding, not yet organic. Active operators rose 16 → 20 → 24 across the three months and AiGC itself cautions that the data "are not a full representation of the total iGaming market". September NAGGR grew +30.7% MoM on wagers +20.0% — hold expansion (4.1% → 4.4%) did part of the work; July-to-September NAGGR growth of +155% is mostly cohort and calendar effect.
  • Casino-led mix on the Ontario template. RNG casino delivered 78.2% of Q3 NAGGR (CA$95.5m, ~US$68m) and 78.9% of wagers; sports & event betting 16.5% (CA$20.1m, ~US$14m); live casino 5.4% (CA$6.6m, ~US$4.7m). Fantasy and peer-to-peer products show no reported activity. Ontario's September split was 82% casino, so the structure is near-identical.
  • Online-only dataset; retail and lottery stay with AGLC. AiGC reports only the private online market. Land-based casinos, VLTs, slots, bingo and lottery remain AGLC conduct-and-manage: AGLC budgets gaming net revenue of CA$1,565.1m (~US$1,118m) for FY2025-26 (CA$1,506.5m FY2023-24 actual) and a FY2026-27 target of CA$1,619.6m. Play Alberta, the state platform, had net sales of ~CA$270m (~US$193m) in FY2024-25 [secondary-sourced] and now competes as one of 50-plus sites; it appears excluded from AiGC's report.
  • Player base and yield are building. 388,585 active accounts in September (+23.7% MoM; accounts, not unique players — multi-operator customers are double-counted), ARPPA CA$146 (~US$104) vs CA$98 in July. Ontario runs ~CA$303 ARPPA on 1.41m accounts; Alberta account density (~7.8% of a ~5m population) is already close to Ontario's (~8.8%), consistent with an existing grey-market base migrating rather than new demand being created.
  • Sports betting is small and seasonal — for now. September betting NAGGR of CA$11.1m (~US$7.9m) more than doubled (+113.5% MoM) on NFL/CFL with hold at 7.3% (Aug 4.6%, Jul 6.2%); handle CA$152.7m (+36.1% MoM). The first full NHL season with two Alberta franchises starts in October and is the next lever; expect sports share to rise toward 20-25% in Q4/Q1.
  • Fiscal regime is a revenue share on GGR, not a tax. 2% of GGR goes to First Nations and 1% to social-responsibility programs first; the remaining revenue is split 80/20 operator/province, an effective public take of ~22.4% of GGR (Ontario: 20%). No turnover/stake tax and no Canadian tax on player winnings. Whether promotional costs are deductible before the 80/20 split is not public [unverified]; the 'non-adjusted' label on reported GGR mirrors iGO's and implies a separate adjusted base.
  • Entry fees are modest but per-site. CA$50,000 (~US$36k) one-time application plus CA$150,000 (~US$107k) annual registration for each iGaming site named on the registration; suppliers pay CA$15,000 (~US$10.7k) p.a. for platform/critical-systems providers or CA$3,000 (~US$2.1k) for others, with no application fee. Nearly 50 companies had paid the CA$200k entry ticket by launch. Ontario charges CA$100,000 per site.
  • Entry is a dual-track process. AGLC handles registration (due-diligence, SRIG compliance, accredited-lab certification, SOC 2 Type 1 at go-live and ISO 27001/SOC 2 Type 2 within two years, central self-exclusion API integration); AiGC then signs the operating agreement covering economics, AML, complaints and financial reporting. AGLC registration alone does not authorise play, and registered operators must cease all unregulated activity in Alberta.
  • Government projections already look stale. Minister Dale Nally guided ~CA$76m (~US$54m) of provincial revenue in Year 1 (Budget 2026: AiGC CA$75m FY2026-27 rising to CA$109m FY2028-29), which casino.org back-solves to ~CA$390m of GGR. September's run-rate (~CA$680m, ~US$485m NAGGR annualised) implies ~CA$150m of annual public take at 22.4% — before the October site additions. H2 Gambling Capital's CA$850m (~US$607m) Year-1 GGR estimate (CA$1.57bn in Year 2) looks reachable.
  • Operator landscape is already crowded. 22 sites / 15-16 operators at launch; 29 operators / 50 sites by the 13 Oct transition deadline (late entrants included Stake, BET99, Betway/Super Group, PlayOJO/SkillOnNet, Apollo's seven brands); 42 AGLC operator licences and 52 live sites by 24 Oct; AiGC guides to 60-65 sites by end-2026. No operator-level revenue or market-share data is published.
  • Channelization target is explicit. The province estimated ~70% of pre-launch iGaming sat with operators unregulated in Alberta; AiGC targets 70% channelization after Year 1 and 75% after Year 2. AiGC CEO Dan Keene: "the market was here for decades, operating in Alberta."
  • Enforcement has begun, capacity unproven. The 90-day grace period ended 13 Oct 2026; sites not approved must go dark, AGLC monitors and can declare violators permanently unsuitable, and AiGC shares compliance intelligence with AGLC. The minister concedes enforcement is "a bit of whack-a-mole"; no payment- or ISP-blocking regime was identified in the sources read (n/a).
  • Advertising rules are tight and may tighten further. SRIG (updated 18 Jun 2026; AiGC advertising overview 4 Aug 2026) bans bonus/inducement advertising outside registered sites and consented direct messaging, restricts athletes to responsible-gambling messaging, bans youth-appeal creative, influencers and placements near schools, and lets AGLC order take-downs, fine or de-register. Bill 31 (2026) confirmed AGLC can rewrite these rules by standard rather than statute, and the Premier has signalled the ad rules may change again.
  • Responsible gambling is built into the licence. Legal age 18+ (Ontario 19+), mandatory player verification, a centralised provincial self-exclusion system, deposit and time-limit tools, 211 Alberta signposting and the AiGC logo as the trust mark; 1% of GGR funds mental-health, addiction and research programming.
  • Payments data is a gap. AiGC publishes no deposit-method mix (n/a). E-wallet providers are a registrable supplier class; Interac e-Transfer is the dominant Canadian rail [industry consensus, not regulator-verified]; the treatment of credit-card and crypto deposits under SRIG is [unverified]. AiGC's 'Wagers' definition counts all cash received, including rake and tournament fees.
  • Reporting is transparent but coarse. Monthly publication in the third week of each month with product-level wagers, winnings and NAGGR, active accounts and ARPPA — but unaudited and subject to revision, with no operator-level data, no promo-adjusted revenue and no de-duplicated player count.
  • Read-through for an operator. A ~5m-population, high-gambling-spend province with Ontario economics, lower per-brand fees than most US states, an 18+ base and an enforcement-backed regulated channel — set against a 50-65 brand field, a bonus-advertising ban that pushes acquisition cost into affiliates and brand media, and a sub-scale live-casino segment (2.3% hold). A B2C entry needs differentiated casino content or hockey-centric sports product; a B2B supplier entry (CA$15k p.a.) is the lower-risk route into a market with many small brands needing platforms.

3Deep-dive: Online casino (RNG)

78% of Q3 NAGGR at a 4.2% hold — the engine of the market, and the segment where 50-plus brands will fight hardest.

Scale and share

RNG casino carried the launch quarter with CA$2,275.2m of Q3 wagers (78.9%) and CA$95.5m NAGGR (78.2% of market).

  • Monthly wagers: CA$422.4m in July's 19 trading days, CA$849.6m in August, CA$1,003.2m in September; quarter ~US$1,625m.
  • NAGGR rose CA$17.0m to CA$35.7m to CA$42.8m; CA$95.5m (~US$68m) total.
  • Casino share of NAGGR peaked at 82.3% in August during the sports summer trough, easing to 75.6% in September as football returned.
  • Same seasonal shape as Ontario, where casino was 80% of July GGR and 82% of September's.

Hold

Hold edged up monthly from 4.0% to 4.2% to 4.3%, in line with Ontario's mature 4.0% benchmark.

  • Blended across slots, progressive jackpots, RNG table games, poker-against-the-house and keno.
  • Ontario's September online casino GGR of CA$340.8m on CA$8.5bn of wagers is a 4.0% hold.
  • AiGC reports non-adjusted GGR, so bonus and free-play cost is invisible in these figures.
  • The uptick is consistent with drift from promotion-heavy launch play toward normal game mix rather than any change in RTP settings.

Runway versus Ontario

September casino NAGGR of CA$42.8m is 12.6% of Ontario's CA$340.8m against roughly 30% of its population; NAGGR can plausibly double within twelve months.

  • 24 operators versus about 50 in Ontario, plus partial-month cohorts.
  • 26 more sites added in October, overwhelmingly casino brands: Apollo Entertainment's seven, SkillOnNet's four including PlayOJO, Super Group's five including Betway, plus Stake.
  • Ontario ARPPA ~CA$303 against Alberta's CA$146, so doubling needs no growth in the underlying player population.

Competitive differentiation

Casino is where the money is but also where the long tail is deepest, with 50-plus brands competing without bonus advertising.

  • No bonus advertising permitted in public media; inducements confined to registered sites and consented direct channels.
  • Differentiation will come from content exclusivity, jackpot networks, loyalty and CRM rather than sign-up offers.
  • Product certification runs through AGLC-accredited test labs.
  • Content studios can enter as registered suppliers at CA$15,000 p.a. rather than as operators at CA$200,000 in year one.

Structural constraints

AGLC's June 2026 guidance blunts land-based omnichannel advantage, and AiGC publishes no slots/table split or operator-level data.

  • Land-based casinos (River Cree iGaming, Pure Canadian Gaming) cannot cross-promote online inducements, which incidentally protects pure-online brands.
  • Share tracking will depend on operator disclosures and third-party app-download and traffic data (n/a from the regulator).

4Deep-dive: Sports & event betting

16.5% of Q3 NAGGR; September's +113% MoM jump was hold-driven (7.3%) and the NHL season has not yet been reported.

Volumes and NAGGR

Q3 handle was CA$325.8m (11.3% of wagers) and NAGGR CA$20.1m (16.5% of market); September's +113.5% MoM jump was hold-driven.

  • Handle: CA$60.9m in July (19 days, overlapping the final FIFA World Cup week), CA$112.2m in August, CA$152.7m in September; quarter ~US$233m.
  • NAGGR: CA$3.8m, CA$5.2m and CA$11.1m respectively; CA$20.1m (~US$14m) total.
  • September NAGGR grew +113.5% MoM on handle growth of +36.1%: margin, not volume, did most of the work.
  • Hold moved from 6.2% in July to 4.6% in August to 7.3% in September.

Hold and definitions

Blended Q3 hold of 6.2% sits below mature US parlay-heavy holds of 8-10% but matches Ontario at the same calendar stage.

  • AiGC's 'Wagers' includes rake, tournament and other fees; betting covers sports, esports and novelty markets with single, live, pool and parlay bet types.
  • Peer-to-peer exchange activity would be reported under P2P, where nothing has been recorded.
  • Hold will remain volatile at this scale; a single NFL favourites-heavy weekend can swing it by a point or more.

Ontario comparison

The segment mirrors Ontario almost exactly; September handle is about 14% of Ontario's CA$1.07bn against roughly 30% of the population.

  • Betting's share of market NAGGR moved 17.3% to 12.1% to 19.5%; Ontario July sports GGR of CA$77.8m was 19% of GGR and 10% of handle.
  • The gap indicates incomplete onboarding rather than lower propensity.
  • Brands include bet365, FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, theScore Bet, BET99 and Stake, but AiGC publishes no operator shares.

Calendar outlook

We expect betting's NAGGR share to climb toward 20-25% through winter before falling back below 15% in summer, as in Ontario.

  • First full NHL season with two Alberta franchises (Oilers, Flames) began in October, alongside NFL and CFL through to the Grey Cup.
  • The 2026-27 NHL playoffs fall in the second fiscal quarter of the market's life.

Marketing rules

Marketing rules bite hardest on this product, so differentiation rests on pricing, parlays, in-play, hockey content and consented CRM.

  • Active and retired athletes may appear only in responsible-gambling messaging; bonus and odds-boost advertising cannot run in public media.
  • Celebrity or influencer creative likely to appeal to minors is banned.
  • Affiliate channels (Google Ads opened to licensed Alberta sites in May 2026) do the acquisition work sign-up offers cannot.

5Deep-dive: Live casino, fantasy & peer-to-peer

Live dealer is 10% of wagers but only 5% of NAGGR at a 2.3% hold; fantasy and P2P have yet to register any reported activity.

Live dealer volumes

Live dealer was 9.8% of Q3 wagers (CA$284.1m) but only 5.4% of NAGGR (CA$6.6m) at a flat 2.3-2.4% hold.

  • Wagers: CA$59.5m in July, CA$103.1m in August, CA$121.5m in September; ~US$203m for the quarter.
  • NAGGR: CA$1.4m, CA$2.4m and CA$2.8m; CA$6.6m (~US$4.7m) total.
  • Hold reflects low-edge blackjack, baccarat and roulette with heavy bet recycling.
  • Live's NAGGR share fell each month, 6.3% to 5.6% to 4.9%, as RNG and sports grew faster.

Benchmark and mix

No like-for-like benchmark exists because iGaming Ontario does not break live dealer out of its casino line (n/a for Ontario).

  • Alberta's monthly live-casino disclosure is, if anything, a richer dataset than the mature market's.
  • AiGC's live category covers dealer-hosted slots and keno, traditional tables and game-show formats.
  • Game shows are the higher-margin sub-product; their absence from the mix would explain a sub-2.5% hold.

Fantasy and P2P

Fantasy and peer-to-peer products are eligible iGames but recorded no reported revenue through 30 September.

  • Fantasy covers daily, seasonal and tournament; P2P covers poker and exchange betting between players located in Alberta.
  • The September workbook reports only products with monthly activity and lists none for either.
  • P2P liquidity is ring-fenced to a ~5m-population pool with no interprovincial liquidity-sharing mechanism, versus Ontario's ~16m pool.
  • This structural handicap is a reason the category may stay marginal.

Entrant implication

Live casino is under-penetrated rather than saturated, but the thin hold means the P&L case rests on volume and RNG cross-sell.

  • Studio capacity with Canadian-facing dealers, hockey-themed game shows and localisation could carry upside.
  • Live is not a standalone acquisition product; cross-sell from RNG lobbies is the driver.
  • A live-casino specialist is better placed as a registered platform/critical-systems supplier (CA$15,000 p.a.) than as an operator (CA$200,000 year one plus per-site fees).

6Deep-dive: Licensing, revenue share & fiscal regime

Ontario's conduct-and-manage model with a 3% carve-out on top: ~22.4% effective take on GGR, CA$200k year-one entry cost per brand, and a government revenue line that the run-rate has already overtaken.

Legal framework

Bill 48, the iGaming Alberta Act (SA 2025), created AiGC to conduct and manage online lottery schemes through private operators, mirroring Ontario.

  • AGLC remains regulator under the Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act (RSA 2000) and issues the SRIG (issued 14 Jan 2026, updated 18 Jun 2026).
  • Registration opened 13 Jan 2026; market launched 13 Jul 2026; 90-day transition window for previously unregulated operators closed 13 Oct 2026.

Entry process

Entry is dual-track: AGLC registration followed by an AiGC operating agreement, since registration alone does not authorise gaming operations.

  • AGLC step: eligibility and class determination, suitability and AML checks, Go-Live Compliance Guide and Notification Matrix, accredited-lab certification.
  • Minimum SOC 2 Type 1 attestation at go-live for every site; ISO 27001 or SOC 2 Type 2 within two years; centralised self-exclusion API integration.
  • Elevated-risk applicants (new to regulated iGaming, no licences elsewhere, or compliance history) can be required to file a Compliance Assessment.
  • AiGC operating agreement fixes economics, AML programme, public-complaints handling and financial reporting.

Fees

Fees are low by US-state standards but charged per site: CA$50,000 application plus CA$150,000 annual registration per distinct site.

  • Application fee ~US$36k one-time; annual registration ~US$107k per site; Ontario charges CA$100,000 per site with no application fee.
  • Suppliers pay CA$15,000 (~US$10.7k) p.a. for platform or critical-gaming-systems, CA$3,000 (~US$2.1k) otherwise, no application fee.
  • Per-site structure is punitive for multi-brand strategies: Apollo Entertainment's seven brands imply ~CA$1.05m (~US$750k) a year in registration fees.

Revenue share

The public take is a revenue share of ~22.4% of GGR, not a tax; Q3 NAGGR implies ~CA$27.4m total public take.

  • From GGR, 2% to First Nations and 1% to social-responsibility programmes; remainder split 80% operator, 20% province.
  • No turnover or stake tax and no Canadian tax on player winnings.
  • Not public: whether promotional costs are deducted before the 80/20 split, and detailed AiGC commercial terms.
  • Applied to Q3 NAGGR of CA$122.1m: ~CA$3.7m First Nations and RG, ~CA$23.7m province; ~CA$27.4m (~US$19.5m) upper-bound estimate pending AiGC disclosure.

Fiscal outlook

We estimate FY2026-27 provincial iGaming revenue of CA$110-140m (~US$79-100m), 1.5-1.9x the conservative CA$75m budget line.

  • Minister Nally guided ~CA$76m (~US$54m) Year-1 provincial revenue, which casino.org back-solves to ~CA$390m of GGR.
  • Budget 2026 carries AiGC at CA$75m for FY2026-27 rising to CA$109m by FY2028-29; AGLC gaming and lottery net income CA$1.577bn in FY2026-27.
  • September's CA$56.6m NAGGR annualises to ~CA$680m, implying ~CA$150m public take at 22.4%, before promotional deductions and partial fiscal year (July to March).
  • Ontario's first year netted ~CA$87m; Ontario's Q2 FY2026-27 take at 20% of CA$1.23bn was ~CA$246m.

7Deep-dive: Operator landscape, channelization & enforcement

From 16 operators to 50-plus sites in three months; counting bases differ by source, market shares are undisclosed, and the post-13 October enforcement test is only beginning.

Operator count

Counts differ by source: AiGC shows 16, 20 and 24 active operators across Q3, while sites reached 50-plus after the transition deadline.

  • Canadian Gaming Business: 22 sites from 15 operators on launch day; 50 sites from 29 companies on 14 October (48 AiGC commercial sites plus Play Alberta).
  • Yogonet, citing AGLC: 42 iGaming operator licences and 52 live sites as of 24 October.
  • AGLC listed 46 registered operators on 19 June and about 50 including Play Alberta on 10 July; most had not signed AiGC agreements in Q3.
  • AiGC CEO Dan Keene guides to another 10-15 sites by year-end, i.e. 60-65.

Participants span Ontario incumbents and grey-market migrants, with multi-brand groups shaping the long tail and no operator-level revenue published.

September's 388,585 active accounts (~7.8% of ~5m population) are already near Ontario's ~8.8%, so per-account yield is the bigger lever.

  • Government estimated ~70% of pre-launch iGaming was with operators not regulated in Alberta; targets are 70% regulated after Year 1, 75% after Year 2.
  • Ontario: 1.41m accounts on ~16m population after four years; figures are accounts, not people.
  • Evidence the regulated channel is absorbing an existing base rather than creating one; yield CA$146 vs CA$303.

Enforcement began 14 October with no outcomes yet published; the credibility of the 70% target rests on this.

  • Sites not transitioned or awaiting approval must go dark; AGLC can declare non-compliant operators permanently unsuitable for registration; AiGC shares intelligence.

8Deep-dive: Advertising, responsible gambling & payments

Ontario-plus advertising restrictions that AGLC can tighten by standard rather than statute, an 18+ RG framework funded by 1% of GGR, and no published payments data.

Advertising rules

Ontario-plus restrictions codified in AGLC's 18 June 2026 SRIG update and AiGC's 4 August 2026 overview ban bonus advertising in public media.

  • No targeting minors, self-excluded or vulnerable people; no cartoons, influencers or celebrities appealing to minors; no billboards near schools or youth venues.
  • Active or retired athletes may appear only to advocate responsible gambling.
  • Bonus or inducement advertising only on approved registered sites and consented direct messages; must not describe an inducement as free unless it is.
  • Ads must be truthful, not suggest gambling as financial security, nor encourage chasing losses; sites must display AiGC logo, signpost 211 Alberta, offer limits.
  • AGLC can order take-downs and impose fines or loss of registration; separate section governs promotions (contests, draws, giveaways).

Direction of travel

Rules are heading toward more restriction, with AGLC able to tighten by standard rather than statute and the Premier flagging possible changes.

  • Bill 31, the Red Tape Reduction Statutes Amendment Act 2026, confirmed AGLC may set advertising rules through standards without legislative amendment.
  • June 2026 guidance bars land-based casinos from cross-promoting online inducements, including through retail loyalty programmes.
  • Acquisition resembles Ontario: brand media without offers, affiliate and search (Google opened ads to licensed Alberta sites in May 2026), consented CRM.
  • Non-trivial risk that volume or placement caps follow.

Responsible gambling

RG is a licence condition: 18+ age, identity verification, centralised self-exclusion API, mandatory limits and 1% of GGR ring-fenced.

  • Legal gambling age 18 (Ontario 19); 1% of GGR funds mental-health, addiction and research programming.
  • Gowling's guidance indicates an RG Check accreditation requirement [detail unverified].
  • Active accounts are not de-duplicated across operators, so the regulator has no published view of multi-account play; affordability interventions would need this gap closed.
“for the first time, operators will be paying for the treatment”Minister

Payments

Payments are the least-documented area: no deposit-method mix published (n/a) and crypto rails appear outside the regulated perimeter.

  • AGLC standards on credit-card and crypto deposits were not read directly [unverified]; e-wallet providers are a registrable supplier class.
  • 'Wagers' counts all cash received from players by any rail, including rake and fees.
  • Interac e-Transfer is the dominant Canadian deposit rail; card and e-wallet options are standard [industry consensus, not regulator-verified].
  • Stake's entry as a fiat-only regulated brand (as in Ontario) signals crypto rails are excluded.

9Deep-dive: Land-based & Play Alberta context

The online market is a ~CA$0.7bn run-rate addition to a ~CA$1.6bn AGLC land-based and lottery base; retail remains a state monopoly outside AiGC's reporting.

Retail estate

Alberta's retail gaming estate is an AGLC state monopoly outside AiGC's dataset, with FY2026-27 gaming net revenue targeted at CA$1,619.6m.

  • Roughly 30 casinos and racing entertainment centres [secondary], VLTs, slots, bingo, pull tickets, raffles and lottery.
  • AGLC 2025-28 Business Plan: gaming net revenue CA$1,506.5m (~US$1,076m) FY2023-24 actual, CA$1,565.1m (~US$1,118m) FY2025-26 budget, CA$1,619.6m (~US$1,157m) FY2026-27 target.
  • Gaming net operating income budgeted at CA$1,539.7m for FY2025-26 within total AGLC net operating income of ~CA$2.3bn; fiscal plan carries ~CA$2.4bn a year through FY2028-29.

Play Alberta

September's private-market NAGGR of CA$56.6m is already ~2.5x Play Alberta's monthly net-sales run-rate, showing how much the state platform missed.

  • Play Alberta (casino, instants, live dealer, lottery, sports) generated ~CA$270-275m (~US$193-196m) net sales in FY2024-25 [secondary sources citing AGLC annual report].
  • That figure is after promotional costs and operator expenses, so not comparable to AiGC's pre-promo NAGGR.

Relevance to entrants

Alberta's historically high per-capita gaming spend is the demand base, but AGLC's retail estate competes for the same wallet with no omnichannel bridge.

  • Historically high VLT and casino spend is what the 4.2% online hold is now monetising.
  • AGLC has explicitly fenced retail off from online cross-promotion, so there is no sanctioned omnichannel loyalty bridge for land-based operators moving online.

Budget 2026

Budget 2026 assumes little retail cannibalisation; if the online run-rate holds, the combined take rises faster than planned, raising 'contribution' ask odds.

  • AGLC gaming and lottery net income essentially flat, CA$1.577bn FY2026-27 to CA$1.635bn FY2028-29, plus AiGC line of CA$75m rising to CA$109m.
  • Faster growth strengthens the political case for the regime but raises odds of asks on advertising or RG funding.

10Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Demand is proven, not speculative: NAGGR reached CA$56.6m (~US$40m) in month three with only 24 of 50-plus eventual sites live, a ~CA$680m annualised run-rate against the government's implied ~CA$390m Year-1 base and H2GC's CA$850m estimate. structural
  • Familiar, low-friction economics: effective ~22.4% public take on GGR with no turnover tax and no player withholding; CA$50k + CA$150k per-site fees are a fraction of most US-state entry costs, and the Ontario playbook (standards, iGO-style reporting, conduct-and-manage) transfers directly. structural
  • Yield runway: ARPPA of CA$146 versus Ontario's ~CA$303 with account density already near Ontario's implies NAGGR can roughly double on the existing player base as cohorts mature and onboarding completes. early
  • Sports calendar tailwind: September betting NAGGR +113% MoM at a 7.3% hold before the first NHL season with two Alberta franchises; betting share should rise toward 20-25% through Q4/Q1. one-off
  • Wider legal base: Alberta's 18+ age (vs 19+ in Ontario) adds an 18-year-old cohort to the addressable market and is embedded in the AiGC framework. structural
  • Enforcement-backed channel: the 13 October grace-period expiry, AGLC's power to declare non-compliant operators permanently unsuitable and AiGC's intelligence-sharing give licensed brands a regulatory moat that did not exist a quarter ago. early
  • Supplier route is cheap: CA$15k p.a. platform/critical-systems registration (no application fee) opens a B2B path to a market where Apollo, SkillOnNet, Super Group and a long tail of small brands need content, platforms and payments. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • Acquisition cost without offers: bonus and inducement advertising is banned in public media, athletes are confined to RG messaging, and AGLC can tighten further by standard (Bill 31) — the Premier has already signalled change. CAC will sit in affiliates, search and brand media, favouring incumbents with Ontario brand equity. structural
  • Fragmentation: 50 sites from 29 groups at the deadline, guided to 60-65 by year-end, for a ~5m population — a longer tail per capita than Ontario, with per-site CA$150k fees and marketing restrictions squeezing sub-scale brands first. structural
  • Opaque commercials: the AiGC operating agreement, promotional-deduction treatment under the 80/20 split, and operator-level shares are all undisclosed; the reported NAGGR is unaudited and subject to revision, and active accounts are not de-duplicated. governance
  • Enforcement capacity unproven: the minister concedes 'whack-a-mole'; no payment- or ISP-blocking regime was identified, and a 70% Year-1 channelization target still leaves ~30% offshore, including crypto casinos outside the perimeter. watch
  • Product-mix exposure: live casino yields only 2.3% hold and 5% of NAGGR; fantasy and P2P show zero reported activity and poker liquidity is ring-fenced to Alberta-located players — the market is effectively RNG slots plus seasonal sports. structural
  • Fiscal upside invites policy asks: with the run-rate implying FY2026-27 provincial revenue of CA$110-140m against a CA$75m budget, the political temptation to add levies, RG contributions or ad caps rises — the 3% carve-out is precedent that the formula can be layered. watch
  • Omnichannel is blocked: AGLC bars land-based casinos from cross-promoting online inducements, removing the retail-to-online loyalty bridge that would otherwise advantage Alberta's own casino operators (and any partner strategy built on them). structural

Guidance bridge. The 'guidance' in this market is fiscal, and it has already been overtaken. Independent view. Our independent estimate is first-twelve-month NAGGR (13 Jul 2026–12 Jul 2027) of CA$800-950m (~US$570-680m), built from a Q4-26 of ~CA$230m as October's site additions and the NHL season annualise, ~CA$270m in Q1-27 and ~CA$290m in Q2-27 as ARPPA climbs toward CA$200; blended hold should settle at 4.2-4.5% with casino at 75-80% of NAGGR and sports peaking near 25% in the hockey playoffs. On the fiscal side we model FY2026-27 provincial iGaming revenue of CA$110-140m (~US$79-100m), 1.5-1.9x the CA$75m budget, with the lower bound reflecting a plausible promotional deduction before the 80/20 split; expect Budget 2027 (Feb 2027) to lift the AiGC line materially. On rules, the direction is tighter, not looser: AGLC can amend advertising standards without legislation and the Premier has flagged change, so we assign a better-than-even chance of additional volume, placement or broadcast-time limits within 12 months, and a lower but real chance of a revised RG levy or affordability-style requirements once de-duplicated player data exists. Enforcement will be the swing factor for channelization: without payment or ISP blocking, we would expect regulated share to reach the 70% Year-1 target but stall around 75-80% thereafter, as in Ontario. Operator count should peak at 60-70 sites in 2027 and then consolidate as per-site fees and offer-free marketing cull the long tail; leadership will concentrate in the Ontario incumbents (bet365, FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, theScore Bet) plus one or two casino-first specialists — all estimates.
