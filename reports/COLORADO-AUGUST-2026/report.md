# Colorado () — AUGUST 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in millions unless stated) · Results released 15 Oct 2026 (Division of Gaming release; August 2026 proceeds PDF dated 30 Sep 2026)**

> August 2026 was Colorado's first sub-$400m month of the year: handle $388.1m (−7.0% YoY, −20.2% MoM), hold compressed to 8.49%, GGR $32.95m and state tax $3.15m (−14.9% YoY) — the regulator's "+20%" headline refers to fiscal-year-to-date tax, not the month. With the free-bet deduction now effectively nil (tax base ≈ GGR less 0.25% federal excise) and new deposit/marketing restrictions live since 12 Aug 2026, Colorado has moved from a growth market to a mature, 99.5%-online, tighter-margin market in which the NFL season and the prediction-market leak will decide whether FY2027 holds the FY2026 record ($6.4bn handle, >$47m tax).

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Primary release.** The Colorado Department of Revenue, Division of Gaming published the August 2026 Sports Betting Proceeds report (PDF dated 30 Sep 2026) and a press release on 15 Oct 2026: statewide wagers $388,148,758, payments to players $355,197,529, GGR $32,951,229, net sports betting proceeds (NSBP) $31,983,713 and state tax $3,154,843. All figures are gross of tax, calendar month, regulator-reported.
- **Handle fell 7.0% YoY and 20.2% MoM** to $388.1m — the first month below $400m in 2026 — against $417.2m in Aug-25 (which itself was +12.0% YoY) and a World Cup-inflated $486.3m in Jul-26. On a two-year view August handle is only +4.2% vs Aug-24 ($372.4m): volume growth has stalled.
- **Hold compressed to 8.49%** from 9.61% in July, so GGR fell 29.5% MoM to $32.95m. Parlays/combinations carried the month: $100.1m of handle (25.8%) at a 13.6% hold produced $13.6m, i.e. 41% of all GGR; straight MLB (25.0% of handle) held only 5.8%, basketball 4.9%, and golf and MMA were net losers for the books.
- **Tax take −14.9% YoY to $3.15m** (−30.9% MoM). The Division's headline "tax revenues up 20%" refers to fiscal-year-to-date (Jul–Aug) tax of $7.72m (+20.2%) on FYTD handle of $874.5m (+13.6%); both are flattered by the July World Cup (Jul-26 handle +38.0% YoY, tax +68.0%). Strip July out and the underlying trend is negative.
- **FY2026 (Jul-25–Jun-26) was a record:** the Division reports >$47m of sports betting tax for the fiscal year and trade sources corroborate a ~$6.4bn handle; FY2026 GGR was not retrieved [n/a]. The $47m is the first full year in which the state kept everything above the old $29m Prop DD cap (Proposition JJ, Nov-2024).
- **Online is the market: 99.5% of handle** ($386.2m online vs $2.0m retail). Retail sportsbooks are legally confined to the casinos of Black Hawk, Central City and Cripple Creek (c. 1 hour from Denver), so a land-based channel is immaterial for brand building or cash deposits.
- **Tax regime — base now effectively GGR.** Tax is 10% of NSBP, where NSBP = GGR less the 0.25% federal excise less a capped free-bet deduction. The GGR→NSBP gap was 0.249% of handle in August and 0.245% in July — exactly the federal excise — implying the free-bet deduction has phased to ~nil from 1 Jul 2026 (the HB24-1436 step-down accelerated by subsequent legislation; bill reference [unverified]). Effective tax: 9.6% of GGR, 0.81% of handle (vs 0.64% in Aug-24).
- **Tax cap removed, upside retained by the state.** Prop DD (2019) capped sports betting tax at $29m/yr; Prop JJ (Nov-2024) lets the state keep all revenue above that for the Colorado Water Plan. Because TABOR requires voter approval for a rate increase, the political path of least resistance is base-broadening (promo deductions) rather than a headline rate hike — which is what has happened.
- **Licensing is casino-tethered.** Sports betting master licences are reserved to holders of Colorado retail (limited gaming) casino licences (cap ~33 [count unverified]); each master licence may contract one internet sports betting operator and one retail sports betting operator; licences run two years. Fee schedule not verified in this session [n/a]. Minimum age 21; statewide mobile with remote registration; individual college-athlete prop bets prohibited; eSports permitted ($4.45m handle in August).
- **Operator landscape is opaque by design.** Colorado publishes no operator-level handle or GGR, so market shares are not knowable from primary data; ~15 online brands are reported live [unverified], with FanDuel and DraftKings presumed to hold a combined >60% on national patterns (estimate, not Colorado-reported). Churn of tethers (e.g. ESPN Bet's national closure in Dec-2025 [unverified]) keeps master-licence capacity available for new entrants.
- **New consumer-protection law in force since 12 Aug 2026.** Governor Polis signed legislation on 2 Jun 2026 imposing new limits on sports betting, reported by Axios/Colorado Newsline/Colorado Politics as deposit and marketing restrictions; the enrolled provisions were not read in this session [unverified]. August is therefore the first month that partially reflects the regime; May-26 handle was already −5.3% YoY per trade reporting.
- **Payments.** Colorado has historically been one of the minority of US states permitting credit-card deposits alongside ACH/online banking, debit, PayPal, Play+/prepaid and cash at the casino cage; whether the 2026 statute curtails credit cards is [unverified] and is the single most important payments question for a new entrant.
- **Responsible gambling and advertising.** The Division runs BetSmart.Colorado.gov and the 1-800-GAMBLER line, a statewide self-exclusion programme, requires RG messaging in advertising and bars targeting under-21s; August 2026 was Responsible Gaming Education Month, with the Division explicitly pairing the record tax year with a safer-gambling campaign — the tone of regulation is tightening, not loosening.
- **Land-based gaming is a separate, healthy pool.** Limited gaming (casino) taxes hit a record >$185m in FY2026; August 2026 casino taxes were reported >100% up YoY, which under the graduated 0.25%–20% AGP tax (brackets reset every 1 July) is largely bracket timing [AGP n/a]. iGaming is not authorised and would require a new statute and, for any tax, a statewide vote.
- **Enforcement and political direction.** Direction of travel is (i) broaden the tax base (done), (ii) tighten deposit/marketing conduct (done, Aug-26), (iii) protect Water Plan revenue (Prop JJ). No rate-increase pressure given TABOR; the open regulatory front is federally-regulated sports event contracts (prediction markets), whose leakage from state-taxed handle Colorado cannot currently measure [stance unverified].
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Colorado is an attractive but mature, digital-only state: low nominal tax (10%) but on an almost-full GGR base, cheap market access via a casino tether, deep product permissions (parlays, eSports, table tennis/tennis volume), and no retail requirement. Growth is now hold- and product-driven (SGP/parlay share) rather than acquisition-driven; a late entrant must model flat-to-down handle ex-World Cup, ~8.5–9.5% hold, and marketing constrained by the new statute.
- **Data gaps to close before any commitment:** Aug-25 GGR/NSBP (only handle and tax were verified), FY2026 GGR, the operator roster and master-licence utilisation, the licence fee schedule, and the text of the June 2026 statute — all flagged [unverified] in this brief.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online sports betting (99.5% of the market)

*Handle −7% YoY to $386.2m online, hold 8.43%; parlays are now 41% of GGR and the only product with structural margin.*

### Online volume and tax

**Online handle was $386,185,784 in August 2026, down 20.4% from July and 7.0% YoY statewide; hold 8.43%.**

- Online wagers were 99.49% of the statewide $388.1m; July online handle was $485.0m.
- Online GGR $32,555,783, online NSBP $31,593,108, online tax $3,144,042 — 99.7% of all sports betting tax.
- The retail channel is a rounding error; any model of Colorado is a model of mobile.

### Month-on-month drivers

**The MoM fall is almost entirely parlays (−$66.3m) and soccer (−$40.7m) as the World Cup unwound; YoY decline cannot be blamed on calendar.**

- Parlays/combinations fell $66.3m (−39.9%) to $100.1m; soccer fell $40.7m (−64.9%) to $22.0m.
- The Division had reported World Cup wagering up almost 250% on the 2022 tournament.
- Pro football started: NFL $25.9m from $1.1m, NCAA football $3.1m; 'Other' rose to $15.2m.
- August 2025 had no World Cup either, and still took $417.2m.

### Hold and parlay mix

**Statewide hold fell to 8.49% from 9.61%; parlays held 13.6% and produced $13.6m, 41% of GGR on 26% of handle.**

- Straight-market products were thin: MLB 5.8% on $97.1m, basketball 4.9% on $47.6m, tennis 7.4%, table tennis 6.8%, soccer 7.8%.
- Golf (−3.0%) and MMA (−0.3%) were negative for the books.
- NFL preseason/early-week held 14.7% and NCAA football 27.6% on tiny bases.
- Colorado GGR is now a parlay/SGP product-mix outcome; without a competitive same-game-parlay engine an operator competes for low-hold straight handle against FanDuel/DraftKings pricing.

### Product calendar

**The product calendar is unusually broad: tennis ($34.8m, 9.0%) and table tennis ($25.7m, 6.6%) together exceed soccer.**

- eSports ($4.45m) is permitted — a 24-hour liquidity profile that suits in-play and micro-market suppliers.
- Individual college-athlete props remain prohibited; the Division's catalogue of approved events governs what can be offered.

### YoY GGR and tax gap

**YoY GGR cannot be computed because August 2025 GGR was not retrieved [unverified]; NSBP fell at least 13.7% YoY on a 7.0% handle decline.**

- Tax at 10% implies August 2025 NSBP of at least $37.1m — consistent with a lower hold this August.
- Aggregate tax (9.86% of NSBP) is below 10% because operators with negative monthly NSBP owe nothing; losses are operator-specific and not pooled.

### Operator shares and churn

**Operator shares are not disclosed by the Division; brand churn means master-licence tethers are available for a new entrant without building a casino.**

- No primary market-share data exists for Colorado; any third-party share figures are estimates.
- Live online brands number ~15 [unverified]; WynnBET, SuperBook, Tipico, SI Sportsbook and Betr left in 2023–24.
- ESPN Bet closed nationally in Dec-2025 [unverified].

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Retail sportsbooks & land-based casinos (limited gaming)

*Retail sports betting is 0.5% of handle and confined to three mountain towns; the casino pool is separately healthy with a record >$185m FY2026 tax.*

### Retail sports betting

**Retail wagering was $1,962,974 in August (0.51% of statewide handle), GGR $395,446 on a 20.15% hold — small-base volatility.**

- July retail handle $1,345,969, GGR $40,201, hold 2.99%; the volatility says nothing about trend.
- Retail NSBP $390,605 and retail tax $10,801.
- Parentheses around retail and online sub-totals in the proceeds PDF are a formatting artefact, not losses; wagers less payouts confirm both positive.

### Retail channel limits

**Retail sports betting is confined to casinos in Black Hawk, Central City and Cripple Creek, so it cannot serve as an acquisition touchpoint.**

- Two tribal casinos operate under compact, outside state reporting.
- No track, bar or lottery-retail channel — unlike Nevada, Ohio or Illinois — so no cash-deposit or acquisition role for an online brand.
- For an entrant, the retail skin that comes with a master licence has option value only.

### Limited gaming casinos

**Limited gaming is the larger land-based pool, with record taxes of more than $185m for FY2026.**

- Games: slots, blackjack, poker, craps, roulette and, since Amendment 77 in 2020, unlimited bet sizes where towns opt in.
- The 13 Oct 2026 release said August 2026 casino taxes collected were up more than 100% year-on-year.
- Tax is graduated 0.25% to 20% of AGP on a fiscal-year cumulative basis resetting 1 July, so July/August effective rates are structurally low.
- The doubling is most plausibly bracket timing and collections phasing rather than a doubling of AGP [August AGP n/a; not retrieved].

### Strategic implications

**The casino licence is the gateway: ~33 casino operators [count unverified] are the counterparties for market access and are not distressed sellers.**

- Master sports betting licences are reserved to limited gaming licensees.
- Casinos' own retail economics (record tax year) mean they are not distressed sellers of tethers.
- iGaming is not authorised; given TABOR, any tax on it would require a statewide vote — do not model an online casino cross-sell.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*10% of NSBP on a base that is now ≈ GGR less 0.25% excise; casino-tethered two-year licences; cap on tax receipts removed by Prop JJ; rate hikes need a statewide vote.*

### Regulatory architecture

**Casino-centric regime: master licences only for licensed retail casinos, capping online brands at the number of casino licences (~33 [unverified]).**

- Authorised by Proposition DD (Nov-2019) and HB19-1327; retail and online launched 1 May 2020.
- Regulator is the Division of Gaming within the Department of Revenue; the Colorado Limited Gaming Control Commission is licensing and rule-making authority.
- Each master licence may contract with one internet sports betting operator and one retail sports betting operator.
- Operators and major/minor vendors (platform, data, geolocation, KYC) are licensed separately; terms are two years.
- Fee schedule not verified, shown as n/a; historically modest versus Pennsylvania/New York entry fees, but do not plan on a figure until confirmed.

### Tax base

**Tax is 10% of NSBP; promotional deductions are practically gone, so Colorado now taxes 10% of GGR less excise, 9.6% effective.**

- NSBP is GGR less the 0.25% federal excise on handle and a free-bet deduction HB24-1436 stepped down from 2.5% (FY2025) to 2.25% (FY2026), later accelerated [bill reference unverified].
- August 2026 GGR less NSBP was $967,517, or 0.249% of handle; July's gap was 0.245% — the federal excise alone.
- Effective tax was 0.81% of handle in August (0.64% in Aug-24, 0.89% in Aug-25).
- Still a low-rate state, but losing tax write-offs for bonuses penalises acquisition-heavy launch strategies.

### Prop JJ and fiscal risk

**Prop JJ (Nov-2024) removed the $29m receipts cap; realistic fiscal risk is base-broadening or fee increases, not a rate jump from 10%.**

- Prop DD capped state receipts at $29m a year with excess refundable under TABOR; FY2026 (>$47m) was the first full uncapped year.
- Excess now funds the Colorado Water Plan grant programme; releases are framed around water funding (e.g. San Miguel Watershed wildfire-readiness grant).
- This embeds a political constituency for stable, growing sports betting tax.
- TABOR requires voter approval for rate increases — materially better than Illinois (graduated to 40%, per-wager surcharge) or Ohio/Maryland-style doubling.

### Fiscal year-to-date

**FYTD (Jul–Aug 2026) handle of $874.5m is +13.6% YoY but tax of $7.72m is +20.2%, lifting tax/handle to 0.88% from 0.83%.**

- Aggregate tax runs slightly below 10% of aggregate NSBP (9.86% in August) because operators with negative monthly NSBP pay nothing.
- Losses are not pooled across licensees — a feature favouring scaled, diversified books over sub-scale ones in volatile months.

### Other licence conditions

**Minimum age 21, remote registration with statewide geolocation, and prohibitions on high-school events and college-athlete props.**

- No wagering on Colorado high-school events; individual college-athlete props prohibited.
- eSports and non-traditional events (table tennis, cricket) permitted subject to the approved-event catalogue.
- Fantasy contests licensed under a separate Fantasy Contests Act.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & the 2026 statute

*New deposit and marketing restrictions in force since 12 Aug 2026; credit-card deposits historically allowed; regulator rhetoric has pivoted from growth to safer-gambling.*

### 2026 statute

**New deposit and marketing limits signed 2 June 2026 took effect 12 August 2026; August, the first month under them, was −7.0% YoY.**

- Specific mechanisms (deposit-limit prompts, bonus-advertising restrictions, credit-card status, push-notification limits) are [unverified]; confirm from the enrolled bill before any product or CRM build.
- Trade reporting had May-26 handle −5.3% YoY 'as new deposit and marketing rules take shape'.
- Some operators appear to have adjusted promotional intensity ahead of the effective date.

### Payments

**Colorado payments have been among the most permissive in the US; whether the June 2026 statute bans credit cards is the key open question [unverified].**

- Credit cards accepted alongside ACH/online banking, debit, PayPal, Play+/prepaid, Venmo/Apple Pay-type wallets and cash at the cage or PayNearMe.
- Several states have banned credit cards since 2024.
- Expect a higher share of debit/ACH, lower deposit conversion on instant wallets, and more friction from statutory limit prompts.

### Responsible gambling and advertising

**RG infrastructure is regulator-led rather than levy-led, and the political framing has shifted toward safer gambling.**

- Division operates BetSmart.Colorado.gov, promotes 1-800-GAMBLER, runs self-exclusion and allocates a slice of tax to problem-gambling services [amount unverified].
- Advertising rules require RG messaging, prohibit targeting under-21s and (per 2023 rule changes [unverified]) 'risk-free' terminology.
- The 29 Aug 2026 release paired the record >$47m tax year with Responsible Gaming Education Month.
- The state wants the water money but will not be seen to chase it.

> “record tax haul renews gambling safety concerns” — Press coverage

### Enforcement and prediction markets

**Enforcement is administrative rather than punitive, but untaxed CFTC-regulated prediction markets are an invisible competitive leak.**

- The Division publishes monthly data, approves event catalogues and vendors, and has not been a headline fining regulator; the 2026 statute hands it new conduct levers.
- Sports event contracts on prediction markets are untaxed in Colorado and invisible in Division data; enforcement stance [unverified].
- The −7% August print is at least partly consistent with leakage.

### Implications for B2Tech

**Design for the post-12-Aug rules from day one across CRM, payments, RG tooling and marketing budget.**

- CRM and bonus programme with no reliance on tax-deductible free bets.
- Payments stack weighted to ACH/debit with contingency for a credit-card ban.
- Auditable RG tooling (deposit limits, time-outs, self-exclusion integration), since the Division's tone is now protective.
- Marketing budget assuming advertising restrictions tighten further before they loosen.

## 7. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Pure-digital market: 99.5% of $388.1m August handle is online; remote registration and statewide geolocation mean a new brand needs no retail footprint and can scale on product alone. _(structural)_
- Low nominal tax with a voter-locked rate: 10% of NSBP (9.6% of GGR; 0.81% of handle in Aug-26) and any increase requires a TABOR statewide vote — a structural brake absent in Illinois, Ohio or Maryland. _(structural)_
- Cheap, available market access: master licences are casino-tethered (one online skin each) and exits since 2023 (incl. ESPN Bet, Dec-25 [unverified]) have freed tethers; no beauty-contest or capped-licence auction. _(mixed)_
- Broad product permissions and liquidity: parlays 25.8% of handle at 13.6% hold (41% of GGR), tennis + table tennis $60.5m (15.6%), eSports $4.45m — a 24-hour calendar that rewards SGP and in-play capability. _(structural)_
- Scale and recency of the record: FY2026 handle ~$6.4bn and tax >$47m; FYTD Jul–Aug 26 handle +13.6% and tax +20.2% (World Cup-assisted) show demand elasticity to major events. _(one-off)_
- Retail casino pool is healthy (record >$185m FY2026 limited gaming tax), so casino partners are stable counterparties rather than distressed tether sellers. _(structural)_

**Lowlights**

- Demand is softening: August handle −7.0% YoY (first month <$400m in 2026), only +4.2% vs Aug-24; trade data had May-26 −5.3% YoY — growth has stalled ahead of the NFL season. _(watch)_
- Free-bet deduction effectively eliminated from FY2027 (GGR→NSBP gap = 0.25% excise only); acquisition spend no longer shelters tax, raising the effective rate on launch-phase books. _(structural)_
- New deposit and marketing restrictions in force since 12 Aug 2026 with provisions not yet verified — CRM, bonusing and payments design carry regulatory execution risk. _(structural)_
- Hold volatility and parlay dependence: statewide hold 8.49% (−112bp MoM); straight MLB/NBA held 5–6%, golf and MMA negative; tax fell 14.9% YoY on a 7.0% handle decline. _(mixed)_
- Opaque competitive data: the Division publishes no operator-level figures, so shares (FanDuel/DraftKings presumed >60%) cannot be verified and sub-scale P&Ls are not pooled for tax (operators with negative NSBP get no offset). _(governance)_
- Retail is immaterial (0.5% of handle) and confined to three mountain towns — no land-based acquisition or cash-deposit channel; iGaming not authorised and would need a statewide vote. _(structural)_
- Untaxed prediction-market sports contracts are invisible in state data and a plausible contributor to the YoY decline; Colorado's enforcement stance is unverified. _(watch)_

## 8. Forecast & Outlook

There is no company guidance to bridge; the relevant bridge is the regulator's month-on-month volume print. Colorado went from a World Cup-inflated $486.3m in July 2026 to $388.1m in August, with the entire decline in parlays (−$66.3m) and soccer (−$40.7m), partly offset by the NFL/NCAA kick-off (+$27.8m) and a drag from baseball, basketball, tennis and table tennis (−$27.9m net of golf/MMA/eSports/other gains of +$8.9m). Year-on-year, the −7.0% handle and −14.9% tax prints are the first post-statute (12 Aug 2026) reading and the first fiscal-year data with no free-bet deduction; the FYTD +13.6%/+20.2% headline is a July artefact.

- **Jul-26 handle:** $486.3m (World Cup month; +38.0% YoY)
- **Parlays & World Cup soccer unwind:** −$107.0m (Parlays −39.9%, soccer −64.9%)
- **Football kick-off +$27.8m; other sports net −$19.0m:** +$8.8m (NFL 22.6x; MLB/NBA/tennis/TT lower)
- **Aug-26 handle:** $388.1m (−20.2% MoM; −7.0% YoY)

**Handle:** FY2027 (Jul-26–Jun-27) will struggle to beat the FY2026 record. July's +38% is already banked, but a repeat of −5% to −7% YoY through the NFL season leaves our estimate at **$6.1–6.5bn (−5% to +2%)** — the NFL/NCAA months (Sep–Jan, typically ~55% of annual handle) are the swing. **Margin:** expect hold of **8.8–9.3%** for the year as parlay/SGP share rises structurally but straight-market variance persists, giving GGR of roughly **$540–600m (estimate)**. **Tax take:** with NSBP ≈ GGR less 0.25% excise, state tax should still rise to **$48–54m (estimate)** even on flat-to-down handle, because the deduction removal adds roughly 0.1pp of handle to the base versus FY2026; the regulator will keep framing this as water funding. **Rules direction:** tighter, not looser — implementation guidance under the 12 Aug statute, further advertising constraints, continued pressure on credit-card deposits, and a possible fee review at the next licence-renewal cycle; a rate increase is improbable given TABOR. **Swing factors:** prediction-market leakage and any Colorado enforcement action; FanDuel/DraftKings pricing of SGPs; Denver/Front Range team playoff runs; and whether the Division adds operator-level disclosure (unlikely near term).

**What to watch next quarter:**

- September and October 2026 proceeds reports (release ~mid-Nov/mid-Dec): first full NFL months under the new statute — a YoY handle decline >5% would confirm a structural, not calendar, slowdown.
- Text and Division implementation rules of the June 2026 statute (effective 12 Aug 2026): deposit-limit mechanics, bonus/inducement advertising limits and credit-card status — all currently [unverified].
- Confirmation of the free-bet deduction schedule for FY2027 onward (statutory reference) and the resulting effective tax rate on GGR for launch-phase operators.
- Colorado's posture toward CFTC-regulated sports event contracts (cease-and-desist, litigation or silence) and any measurable handle leakage.
- Master-licence utilisation and tether availability after 2025–26 brand exits; licence fee schedule at renewal; any 2027 General Assembly bills on advertising, iGaming or fees.
- Limited gaming August–December AGP prints to confirm the >100% August tax jump is bracket timing rather than a step-change in casino revenue.
