# The Star Entertainment Group (SGR.AX) — Q1 FY27 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: AUD (A$m; USD equivalents at AUD/USD 0.70 shown as ~$) · Results released 27 Oct 2026**
**Market reaction:** Shares closed +13.0% at A$0.130 (~US$0.09) on 27 Oct 2026 (prior close A$0.115; intraday high ~A$0.138, +19.6%). Halted 28 Oct for the AUSTRAC judgment; resumed 29 Oct +5.8% to ~A$0.14; trading ~A$0.16–0.17 by 9 Nov 2026.

> The Star's Q1 FY27 (Sep-26 quarter) is the first positive EBITDA print in more than two years — A$12m (~$8m) vs –A$13m pcp — driven entirely by the two owned properties (Sydney + Gold Coast EBITDA A$35m / ~$24.5m, +164% YoY) and a 29% cut in unallocated corporate costs, partly offset by the Brisbane operator-fee reset (–67%). The operating turnaround is real but small: a ~A$48m annualised group EBITDA run-rate sits below our ~A$70m estimate of annual cash interest (currently paid from a restricted reserve), statutory operating cash flow was still –A$15.5m, cash fell A$28m to A$239m, and the day after the AGM the Federal Court imposed a A$260m (~$182m) AUSTRAC penalty that triggers a WhiteHawk review event. The equity remains a levered option on licence restoration and Bally's continued support, not a cash-flow story yet.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **First positive quarter in >2 years.** Group EBITDA (before significant items, share-based expense and discontinued ops) was A$12m (~$8m) vs a –A$13m loss in Q1 FY26 and –A$9m in Q4 FY26 — a A$25m YoY and A$21m QoQ swing. There is no meaningful sell-side consensus and the company gives no numerical guidance; the market read it as a clear beat against trajectory (shares +13% to A$0.130).
- **Revenue A$284m (~$199m), +2% YoY / +8% QoQ — but flat on the originally reported basis.** The pcp was restated down from A$284m (as reported at the Nov-25 AGM) to A$280m with Treasury Brisbane reclassified to discontinued operations. Underlying growth sits in the owned properties (+5% YoY to A$279m), offset by the Brisbane operator fee falling 67% to A$4.5m.
- **The owned properties did all the work.** Sydney + Gold Coast segment EBITDA rose to A$35m (~$24.5m) from A$14m pcp (+164%) and A$16m in Q4 (+125%); combined margin 12.5% vs 5.3% pcp (+720bps) and 6.2% in Q4. Sydney A$15m (vs A$3m), Gold Coast A$20m (vs A$11m). Both properties are now EBITDA/operating-cash positive per the Chairman.
- **Growth is EGM-led and promo-funded.** Management attributes the revenue recovery at both properties to electronic gaming machine growth 'driven by recent revenue and marketing initiatives'; Sydney table games and cash non-gaming were soft, the latter 'due to increased complimentary investment' and the Sep-25 sale of the Event Centre. No volume/actives/win-rate KPIs are disclosed, so the quality of the EGM growth (visitation vs reinvestment) cannot be verified.
- **Cost-out is tangible but has no quantified target or end-date.** Unallocated corporate costs A$23m (~$16m), –A$10m/–29% YoY; group opex A$202m, –12% YoY, falling to 71.1% of revenue from 81.8% (–1,070bps). At FY26 the company said corporate costs fell A$75m in the year and June-quarter annualised corporate costs of A$178m were 38% below FY25. Decentralisation of customer-facing functions to the properties was completed in October; management expects further unallocated-cost reductions, explicitly to 'fund increased marketing investments'.
- **Accounting change reduces comparability.** From FY27 corporate shared-service costs are directly attributed to properties (previously a fixed-percentage allocation); FY26 comparatives have been restated with no impact on group EBITDA. Property margins are therefore not comparable with any pre-FY27 disclosure (e.g. Gold Coast H1 FY26 EBITDA of A$15.2m under the old method vs A$11m restated for Q1 FY26 alone).
- **Brisbane: record operating result, near-zero economics to Star.** The managed Star Brisbane delivered EBITDAM of A$37m (~$26m), nearly 3x pcp (A$13m) and +43% QoQ, on revenue of A$136m (+14%) — a 27.2% margin; the Chairman says EBITDAM now annualises at >A$150m vs ~A$50m when Bally's took control. But under the CMA amended on 1 Apr 2026 (Stage 1 of the JVP transaction), Star's operator fee was A$4.5m vs A$13.8m pcp and segment EBITDA was nil (vs A$6m). Stage 2 of the transaction is expected by 31 Mar 2027.
- **Cash fell A$28m to A$239m (~$167m) in the quarter — and to ~A$200m by the AGM.** Total cash and deposits were A$319m (~$223m) vs A$368m at 30 June, including A$80m of restricted deposits (down from A$101m), of which A$48m is the Interest Reserve Account funding WhiteHawk interest to May 2027. The Chairman told the AGM on 27 Oct the cash balance was 'approximately A$200 million', implying a further ~A$40m outflow in October that the company did not explain (timing of duty/other payments — [unverified]).
- **Statutory operating cash flow was –A$15.5m; the 'positive op FCF ex one-offs' claim rests on an unquantified duty payment.** Appendix 4C shows +A$41.5m of one-off receipts (ATO settlement on GST/withholding tax on junket payments, plus returned rebate duty) and an undisclosed one-off payment of historical underpaid NSW casino duty inside operating costs. For management's statement to hold, the duty payment must have exceeded ~A$57m (our arithmetic: 15.5 + 41.5), or ~A$63m after A$5.7m capex. The amount is not disclosed — [unverified].
- **Interest is not yet running through operating cash flow.** No interest was paid via the 4C in the quarter; WhiteHawk interest is being met from the Interest Reserve Account through May 2027. With A$48m left to cover ~8 months, implied cash interest is ~A$6m/month (~A$70m+ p.a., ~13% on A$559m) — our estimate, not company-disclosed. From June 2027 this becomes a direct cash drag that exceeds the current ~A$48m annualised group EBITDA run-rate.
- **Debt: US$390m (A$559.1m / ~$391m) WhiteHawk senior secured facility, fully drawn, maturing May 2029; no undrawn committed debt.** Loan facilities drawn A$570.7m including the A$11.6m 9% PIK liability to Bally's/Investment Holdings, which was converted on 29 Oct into 105.9m shares at A$0.1123 (Bally's 62.57m, IH 43.35m) following 99.5%+ AGM approval. Unused facilities are only A$6.0m of bank-guarantee headroom. Our net debt estimate: ~A$331m (~$232m) ex restricted deposits, ~A$252m (~$176m) including them; leverage is n/m on LTM EBITDA of ~A$9m.
- **Guidance is qualitative and unchanged in direction.** At FY26 (31 Aug) management said it expected to start building cash in FY27 excluding non-operating items; Q1 reiterates that FY27 'is expected to be the first time The Star will generate net annual operating cash inflows, before one-off items, in more than two years' and that 'momentum has continued in October'. No revenue, EBITDA or capex guidance is provided. Note AUSTRAC instalments are 'non-operating' in this framing.
- **Post-quarter AUSTRAC judgment (28 Oct): A$260m (~$182m) penalty, accepted.** A$195m attributed to Sydney conduct and A$65m to Queensland, payable in four instalments over 19 months (avg ~A$65m each). AUSTRAC had sought A$400m; Star had argued anything above A$100m risked liquidation. The company says the quantum is 'materially in line with' the FY26 provision and will not affect going concern, but that it constitutes a review event under the WhiteHawk facility; it is in discussions with the lender and 'confident' the facility remains available.
- **Licence overhang extended, not resolved.** The NICC advised the Star Sydney licence suspension remains in effect and the Manager's appointment runs to 30 Jun 2027 (unless ended earlier); Queensland confirmed the Gold Coast suspension deferral and Special Manager to 31 Mar 2027 and extended the Brisbane External Adviser to 31 Mar 2027. CEO Mathieson called the outcome 'disappointing'; >85% of remediation-plan milestones are complete. Decentralisation is described as a critical regulatory milestone.
- **Going-concern language retained.** The release repeats that liquidity is 'subject to and dependent on the outcome of a number of material uncertainties' (Note G of the FY26 accounts) and that materiality will be reassessed at the H1 FY27 filing (Feb 2027). The 4C's mechanical '15.9 quarters of funding' figure is meaningless against A$260m of penalties.
- **Gaming-tax take rising with mix.** Gaming taxes and levies rose 10% YoY to A$70m on +2% revenue — 24.6% of revenue vs 22.9% pcp (+170bps) — consistent with the shift toward higher-duty EGM revenue (Sydney 29.6% vs 28.0%). This is a structural drag on the flow-through of EGM-led growth.
- **Ownership and governance.** Bally's holds ~38% and, with Investment Holdings (Mathieson family), ~61% combined; every AGM resolution passed with >96% support. Chairman Soo Kim's remark that this is 'my first and last Annual General Meeting… if the reporting is to be believed' hints at a possible change in listed status — unconfirmed and not addressed further.
- **Management rebuild.** New property CEOs John Koster (Sydney) and Ameet Patel (Gold Coast & Brisbane) with property boards; the Gold Coast Tower 2 is delivered and the Andaz hotel opens in November 2026. Star funded A$5.1m of loans and A$1.0m of PP&E for the Destination Gold Coast Consortium JV in the quarter.
- **FY26 backdrop for scale.** FY26 normalised revenue A$1,101m (FY25 A$1,125m), normalised EBITDA –A$16.1m (FY25 –A$76m), statutory net loss A$307.3m (FY25 –A$428m) after A$144m of significant items; H1 FY26 average monthly free-cash burn was ~A$20m. Q1 FY27's A$12m EBITDA therefore represents a ~A$100m+ annualised improvement vs the FY26 average quarter but is still thin against ~A$560m of gross debt.
- **Thesis read-through.** The bear case (structural revenue decline, uncontrolled cost base) has been answered for now; the remaining case is a balance-sheet and regulatory one. Over the next 19 months Star must fund ~A$260m of penalties plus (from June 2027) ~A$70m p.a. of cash interest from ~A$200m of cash and an EBITDA base that annualises at ~A$48m — the arithmetic only works with continued EBITDA compounding, Brisbane Stage 2, asset/JV monetisation, or further Bally's support.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — The Star Sydney

*EBITDA A$15m (~$10.5m) on flat revenue — a cost and EGM-mix story, with tables and non-gaming still soft and the licence suspension extended to June 2027.*

### Revenue and mix

**Sydney revenue of A$162m (~$113m) rose 1% on the pcp and 8% on the June quarter — first evidence of stabilisation, not growth.**

- Management cites strong year-on-year electronic gaming machine growth partially offset by softness in table games and non-gaming.
- Non-gaming drags: higher complimentary investment reducing reported cash non-gaming revenue, and lost events and catering revenue after the Event Centre sale in September 2025.
- Chairman told the AGM the properties endured roughly three years of double-digit year-on-year monthly declines.
- The +8% QoQ move is more encouraging but Q4 FY26 (A$150m) was a weak base.

### Costs and EBITDA

**Segment EBITDA of A$15m (~$10.5m), margin 9.3% versus 1.9% pcp and –0.7% in Q4; on the numbers, essentially all the improvement is cost.**

- Gaming taxes and levies rose 7% to A$48m (~$34m), 29.6% of revenue versus 28.0% pcp (+160bps), consistent with higher-duty EGM mix.
- Operating expenses fell 13% YoY and 7% QoQ to A$99m (~$69m), 61.1% of revenue versus 70.2% — a 910bps improvement and the real driver.
- EBITDA compares with A$3m in the pcp and a A$1m loss in Q4, a A$12m YoY improvement.

> “stabilisation of revenues and improving cost efficiencies” — Management

### Comparability caveats

**Restated cost allocation and missing operating KPIs mean the result cannot be benchmarked against prior disclosures or decomposed between volume and promotion.**

- From FY27 corporate shared-service costs are directly attributed to properties; FY26 figures restated, so neither A$3m pcp EBITDA nor 9.3% margin compares with earlier disclosures.
- No disclosure of EGM revenue, table drop, win rates, visitation or active members.
- Rising comp spend and the statement that cost savings will 'fund increased marketing investments' suggest promotional reinvestment is a meaningful part of the mix.
- Group cash advertising and marketing was only A$10.4m (3.6% of revenue), but complimentaries are netted in revenue rather than shown as marketing.

### Regulation

**The Sydney casino licence remains suspended and the Manager's appointment has been extended to 30 June 2027 unless terminated earlier.**

- This is a further extension after the September 2025 extension to March 2026.
- CEO noted more than 85% of remediation milestones are complete and the decentralised operating model completed in October is itself a regulatory milestone.
- Post-quarter, A$195m (~$137m) of the A$260m AUSTRAC penalty was attributed to Sydney conduct.
- Quarter included an unquantified one-off payment of historical underpaid casino duty agreed with NSW Treasury.

> “disappointing” — CEO

### Analyst read

**A 9% EBITDA margin for Sydney's only EGM-licensed casino is still a fraction of historical earnings; recovery is cost reset plus EGM promotion.**

- Not a table-games or premium-mass rebuild.
- Path depends on new property CEO John Koster holding the opex line while reinvestment rates normalise, and on the NICC timeline now running to mid-2027.
- With roughly A$160m quarterly revenue, every 100bps of margin is ~A$6.5m of annual EBITDA, so leverage to table/non-gaming stabilisation is significant.
- Exposure to phased NSW gaming-duty increases and any pull-back in promotional spend is equally significant.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — The Star Gold Coast

*The cleanest growth story in the group: revenue +12% YoY to A$117m (~$82m), EBITDA +92% to A$20m (~$14m), margin 17.1% — bought with higher marketing, with Andaz opening in November.*

### Revenue growth

**Gold Coast revenue rose 12% YoY and 9% QoQ to A$117m (~$82m), the second consecutive quarter of sequential growth.**

- Growth driven by strong EGM performance from increased marketing investment and promotional initiatives.
- Q4 FY26 revenue was A$107m; the property is now back above its Q1 FY26 level with room to spare.
- No disclosure of EGM turnover, table drop, hotel occupancy or active members, so growth cannot be split between visitation and reinvestment.

> “increasing substantially on the pcp due to strong growth in electronic gaming machines, driven by increased marketing investment and promotional initiatives” — Management

### Costs and margin

**EBITDA of A$20m (~$14m) rose 92% YoY to a 17.1% margin, but QoQ flow-through was only ~30% because A$6m went to marketing.**

- Gaming taxes rose 17% to A$22m (~$15m), 18.8% of revenue versus 18.1% pcp, consistent with EGM mix.
- Operating expenses A$75m (~$52m), 1% lower YoY but 8% higher QoQ; management says the QoQ step-up is marketing.
- Margin 17.1% versus 10.5% pcp (+660bps) and 15.9% in Q4 (+120bps); EBITDA up A$9m YoY and A$3m (+18%) QoQ.
- YoY A$12m incremental revenue produced A$9m EBITDA (~75%, flattered by restated allocation and cost-out); QoQ A$10m produced only A$3m.

### Capital and Andaz

**Gold Coast is the group's main capital call and holds its asset-value optionality, with Andaz opening in November 2026.**

- Star funded A$5.1m of loans to the Destination Gold Coast Consortium JV and A$1.0m of PP&E on its behalf; received A$0.4m dividends.
- Chairman confirmed Tower 2 delivered; Andaz opens into the seasonally strongest December quarter.
- Stage 1 DBC unwind transferred JV partners' Gold Coast interests to Star in exchange for its share of Brisbane and the Treasury property.

### Regulation and leadership

**Queensland deferred the Gold Coast licence suspension and extended the Special Manager's appointment to 31 March 2027.**

- Previous appointment end date was 30 September 2026.
- A$65m (~$46m) of the AUSTRAC penalty was attributed to Queensland conduct.
- Ameet Patel has taken over as property CEO for both Gold Coast and Brisbane.

### Analyst read

**Growth here is unambiguous and the margin respectable, but marketing opex is scaling with revenue so incremental margin is nearer 30% than 75%.**

- Andaz opening gives a clear near-term catalyst.
- We would want QoQ opex growth to decouple from revenue growth over the next two quarters before extrapolating the 17% margin higher.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — The Star Brisbane (managed) & Treasury Brisbane (discontinued)

*Record EBITDAM of A$37m (~$26m) for the managed resort, but Star's operator fee fell 67% to A$4.5m and the segment contributed nil EBITDA — a capability showcase with no P&L.*

### Operator fee

**Star's operator fee fell 67% to A$4.5m (~$3m) and the segment contributed nil EBITDA versus A$6m in the pcp.**

- Pcp fee was A$13.8m; fee flat on Q4 FY26.
- Reset follows the amended Casino Management Agreement effective 1 April 2026 on Stage 1 completion: Star transferred its Brisbane and Treasury interests, was released from a A$700m guarantee and received Gold Coast interests.
- Directly attributable overheads of A$5m (–37% YoY) absorbed the entire fee.
- The A$6m YoY drag offsets a fifth of the owned-property improvement at group level.

### Managed property performance

**The Star Brisbane posted record revenue of A$136m (~$95m), +14% YoY, and EBITDAM of A$37m (~$26m) at a 27.2% margin.**

- Revenue up 12% QoQ; EBITDAM nearly triple the A$13m pcp and 43% above Q4's A$26m.
- Margin 27.2% versus 10.9% pcp and 21.5% in Q4.
- EBITDAM excludes costs DBC incurs directly as owner; management says read it as an operating-trend indicator, not a consolidated metric.

> “now annualises to more than A$150 million as compared to just A$50 million annualised when we took over” — Chairman Kim

### Valuation implications

**The A$148m annualised EBITDAM run-rate is nearly three times the group's A$48m annualised EBITDA, but almost none accrues to SGR shareholders.**

- Shareholder exposure is the ~A$18m p.a. fee stream, currently roughly break-even after attributable costs.
- Release does not disclose any incentive or performance component in the amended CMA; fee upside treated as [unverified].
- Stage 2 of the JVP transaction remains subject to conditions, expected by 31 March 2027; Brisbane External Adviser extended to 31 March 2027.
- Treasury Brisbane stays in discontinued operations with nil EBITDA.

### Analyst read

**Brisbane proves the Bally's/Mathieson playbook works under Australian regulation, but for the equity it is an advertisement rather than earnings.**

- A 27% EBITDAM margin on a new-build IR with an External Adviser in place strengthens the suitability case with the Queensland regulator.
- Star's economic exposure is now a low-margin management contract plus whatever is embedded in Stage 2.
- Management led with the EBITDAM record; the operator fee line is where actual earnings sit, and it is the only line that went backwards.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Corporate, cash flow & balance sheet

*Unallocated corporate costs –29% to A$23m, but statutory operating cash flow was –A$15.5m, cash fell A$28m to A$239m (and ~A$200m by the AGM), interest is being paid from a reserve that runs out in May 2027, and A$260m of AUSTRAC penalties now sit on top.*

### Corporate costs

**Unallocated corporate costs fell 29% YoY to A$23m (~$16m), 8.1% of group revenue versus 11.8%, with no quantified FY27 target.**

- Down A$10m YoY and 4% QoQ; residual covers remediation, legal and compliance, technology, audit, tax and consolidated reporting.
- Customer-facing functions moved to properties; Chairman said decentralisation of operational control completed in October.
- Management expects further falls and says cost-out is ongoing 'in order to fund increased marketing investments'.

**Properties are self-funding at the EBITDA line but the group is not; the WhiteHawk review-event outcome is the single most important near-term disclosure.**

## 7. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Owned-property EBITDA (Sydney + Gold Coast) rose A$21m YoY to A$35m (~$24.5m) with margin +720bps to 12.5%; both properties are now EBITDA-positive and the group printed its first positive EBITDA (A$12m / ~$8m) in more than two years. _(structural)_
- Cost base reset is visible in the numbers: group opex –12% YoY to A$202m (71.1% of revenue, –1,070bps), unallocated corporate –29% to A$23m, decentralisation completed in October with further reductions flagged. _(structural)_
- First YoY revenue growth at the owned properties (+5% to A$279m) since quarterly reporting began in Q1 FY25, following ~3 years of double-digit monthly declines; management says momentum continued in October and the Gold Coast Andaz opens in November. _(early)_
- Star Brisbane EBITDAM hit a record A$37m (~$26m), nearly 3x pcp, at a 27.2% margin and annualising >A$150m — strong evidence of operating capability and a supportive data point for Queensland suitability. _(structural)_
- AUSTRAC penalty landed at A$260m (~$182m) — well below the A$400m sought, in line with the FY26 provision, and payable over 19 months — removing the largest binary liability from the going-concern equation (post-quarter, 28 Oct). _(one-off)_
- Management reiterated that FY27 should be the first year of net operating cash inflows before one-off items in more than two years, with Q1 operating free cash flow excluding one-offs stated as positive. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- Brisbane operator fee –67% to A$4.5m under the amended CMA; the segment now contributes nil EBITDA (vs A$6m pcp) and group revenue was flat on the originally reported Q1 FY26 base (A$284m). _(structural)_
- Statutory operating cash flow was –A$15.5m and cash fell A$28m to A$239m (~A$200m by the AGM per the Chairman); the 'positive op FCF ex one-offs' claim depends on an unquantified NSW duty payment that our arithmetic says must have exceeded ~A$57m. _(watch)_
- Interest is being paid from a A$48m reserve that runs out in May 2027; implied cash interest of ~A$70m p.a. (our estimate) exceeds the current ~A$48m annualised group EBITDA run-rate. _(structural)_
- Sydney licence suspension continues with the Manager extended to 30 Jun 2027; Gold Coast Special Manager and Brisbane External Adviser extended to 31 Mar 2027 — management itself called the outcome 'disappointing'. _(governance)_
- The A$260m AUSTRAC penalty constitutes a review event under the fully drawn WhiteHawk facility (no undrawn revolver; A$6m unused facilities) and going-concern material-uncertainty language is repeated pending the H1 FY27 assessment. _(watch)_
- Growth quality is unproven: EGM-led and funded by higher complimentaries and marketing (Gold Coast opex +8% QoQ, ~30% incremental flow-through), Sydney tables and non-gaming still soft, and gaming-tax take up 170bps of revenue on mix. _(mixed)_
- Disclosure is thin and comparability is impaired: no P&L below EBITDA, no actives/visitation/win-rate KPIs, FY26 property comparatives restated under a new cost-attribution method, and the one-off cash items are not quantified. _(governance)_

## 8. Forecast & Outlook

Star does not issue numerical guidance; its outlook statements are qualitative. At the FY26 result (31 Aug 2026) management said it expected to start building cash in FY27 excluding non-operating items, with July owned-property revenue +6% YoY. The Q1 FY27 release holds that line — 'FY27 is expected to be the first time The Star will generate net annual operating cash inflows, before one-off items, in more than two years' — and adds that momentum continued into October. The A$25m YoY EBITDA improvement was driven by the owned properties (+A$21m: Sydney +A$12m, Gold Coast +A$9m) and the corporate cost programme (+A$10m), partly offset by the Brisbane operator-fee reset (–A$6m). The A$260m AUSTRAC penalty, confirmed the day after the release, is treated by management as non-operating and is not captured in that framing.

- **Group EBITDA Q1 FY26 → Q1 FY27:** –A$13m → +A$12m (~$8m) (+A$25m)
- **Owned properties (Sydney +A$12m, Gold Coast +A$9m):** A$14m → A$35m (+A$21m)
- **Unallocated corporate costs:** –A$33m → –A$23m (+A$10m)
- **Star Brisbane segment (operator fee reset):** +A$6m → nil (–A$6m)

**Q2 FY27 (Dec-26 quarter, our estimate): group EBITDA A$15–25m (~$10–18m)** on owned-property revenue of ~A$290–305m (+6–10% YoY), helped by holiday-season seasonality, the Andaz opening and continued EGM momentum, with corporate costs ~A$21–22m and the Brisbane fee ~A$4.5m. **FY27 (est.): revenue A$1.10–1.13bn (~$0.77–0.79bn), roughly flat on FY26's A$1,101m** because the ~A$42m lower operator fee absorbs most of the owned-property growth; **group EBITDA A$55–85m (~$39–60m), a 5–7% margin, vs –A$16m in FY26**, predicated on owned-property EBITDA holding at A$35–45m per quarter and unallocated corporate drifting toward A$20m. **Leverage direction: net debt rises.** Gross debt is USD-denominated (A$559m at 0.6975) and stable to maturity in May 2029, but unrestricted cash of ~A$200m must absorb ~A$260m of AUSTRAC instalments over 19 months, DGCC funding, maintenance capex and — from June 2027 — ~A$70m p.a. of cash interest (our estimate). On our FY27 EBITDA range net debt/EBITDA stays >5x and is unlikely to be a meaningful metric before FY28; the equity case remains dependent on EBITDA compounding toward A$100m+, Brisbane Stage 2, and continued Bally's support. **Swing factors:** the WhiteHawk review-event outcome (terms, fees, any covenant reset); the timing and size of the first AUSTRAC instalment; NICC/Queensland licence decisions (Mar/Jun 2027); promotional reinvestment discipline at the Gold Coast; Sydney table-games stabilisation; and the phased NSW gaming-duty step-ups.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- WhiteHawk review-event resolution — any amended pricing, fees, covenants or additional security, and whether the ~A$50m additional liquidity provision referenced at signing remains available.
- First AUSTRAC instalment (amount and date) and the cash balance at 31 Dec 2026 in the Q2 FY27 4C; reconciliation of the ~A$40m Oct-26 cash drop flagged by the Chairman.
- H1 FY27 results (Feb 2027): going-concern assessment, quantification of the one-off NSW duty payment and ATO receipts, P&L below EBITDA including interest expense, and any impairment.
- Regulatory calendar: Gold Coast Special Manager and Brisbane External Adviser to 31 Mar 2027; Sydney Manager to 30 Jun 2027; any NICC commentary on a path to licence reinstatement.
- Brisbane JVP Stage 2 completion by 31 Mar 2027 and disclosure of the amended CMA fee economics (base vs incentive).
- Monthly owned-property revenue trajectory through the Dec-26 quarter (Andaz opening, holiday season) and whether Gold Coast opex growth decouples from revenue growth.
- Interest Reserve Account run-off toward May 2027 and the transition of ~A$70m p.a. (est.) of cash interest into operating cash flow.
