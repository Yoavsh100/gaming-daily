Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

Angler Gaming

Released 12 Nov 2026 · Currency EUR (company reports in euro; tables in € millions unless stated, with ~USD equivalents at EUR/USD 1.105) · Data confidence: HIGH

Market reaction: Shares fell ~26% to SEK 4.29 by 09:21 CET on 12 Nov 2026 (prior close ~SEK 5.80 implied; Inderes/Spotlight data); by 16 Nov the stock was ~SEK 3.55 (market cap ~SEK 266m), roughly −39% since the release. Official 12-Nov closing level not verified.

The one-line verdict

A clean, margin-rich quarter — revenue +10.0% to €8.72m (~$9.63m), EBIT +79.6% to €3.04m (~$3.36m) at a 34.9% margin, net profit more than doubled — that the market ignored because the same release announced a second re-cut of the B2B platform deal from 1 Nov 2026: lower fees, affiliate/royalty costs pushed to partners, and a €5.25m (~$5.8m) one-off "incentive" to be settled by forgiving partner receivables in exchange for five-year contracts. The P&L is strong; the thesis now lives in the cash line (9M operating cash flow €2.2m against €7.5m net profit) and in a €21.6m receivable book that is 82% of total assets. Shares fell ~26% intraday.

1Executive summary

  • Headline beat on every P&L line, but no consensus to beat. Q3-26 revenue rose 9.96% YoY to €8,717,367 (~$9.63m), EBIT +79.6% to €3,044,040 (~$3.36m), EBIT margin 34.92% (Q3-25: 21.38%), net profit +114.8% to €2,628,384 (~$2.90m), EPS €0.0351 (~$0.039) vs €0.0163. Figures cross-check to the IAS 34 condensed statements (ISRE 2410-reviewed), the Cision/Inderes release and Finwire/Privata Affärer coverage. Sequentially revenue was +3.2% vs Q2-26 (€8.45m) and EBIT +6.3% (€2.86m). The only published sell-side numbers (Carlsquare, Aug-26) implied ~€9.9m/€2.3m for Q4, not Q3; there is no Q3 consensus.
  • 9M-26 shows the full effect of the January-2025 B2B cost shift. Revenue +12.0% to €25.48m (~$28.15m), gross margin 56.1% (44.1%), opex −4.1%, EBIT +103.7% to €8.82m (~$9.75m) at 34.6% (19.0%), net profit +147.5% to €7.54m (~$8.33m), EPS €0.1005 (~$0.111) vs €0.0406. FY-25 as a whole delivered €30.56m revenue / €6.48m EBIT, so 9M-26 EBIT already exceeds all of FY-25.
  • The quarter decelerated hard after mid-August — management did not say so, the maths does. The Q2 trading update put average daily NGR through 18 Aug at +21.08% vs the whole of Q3-25; the quarter closed with net game win +12.0% and revenue +10.0%. Back-solving (our estimate), the final ~43 days ran roughly +2% YoY on net game win and about −3% on revenue — i.e. growth stalled from late August. PremierGaming's 'higher customer winnings' explains only ~€0.2m of that.
  • Margin structure: gross margin 55.3% (+835bps YoY), opex 20.4% of revenue (−520bps). Direct costs (payment processing, affiliate, licence/software fees, gaming taxes) fell 7.4% YoY to €3.89m (~$4.30m) on revenue +10%; marketing −24% to €0.25m (2.8% of revenue), personnel −8% to €0.80m, other external −16% to €0.70m. Derived EBITDA (EBIT + D&A, not a company metric) €3.08m (~$3.40m), 35.3% margin.
  • Below the line is odd for a debt-free company. 'Finance expenses' of €414k (~$0.46m; 4.8% of revenue; 9M €1.28m) are described as mainly FX-rate effects, not interest. Tax was €1.4k on €2.63m PBT — Starfish Media N.V. (Curaçao) treats its profits as foreign-sourced and untaxed, and the Maltese entities report no chargeable income. A ~0% effective tax rate is a structural feature and a structural risk.
  • Platform KPIs: volume up, monetisation down. Customer deposits +25.4% YoY to €26.03m (~$28.8m); gross game win +8.3% to €12.29m; net game win +12.0% to €11.87m (~$13.1m); 'hold' (NGW/deposits) 45.6% vs 51.0% (−540bps YoY, −90bps QoQ). Active customers 27,783 (−1.7% YoY, +2.4% QoQ); new registrations +59% to 25,971 but new depositing customers −2.9% to 7,662 — registration-to-deposit conversion collapsed to 29.5% from 48.3%. NGW per active rose to €427 (~$472) from €375; bonus cost (GGW−NGW) fell to 3.4% of GGW from 6.6%.
  • B2C (PremierGaming, Malta/Sweden) shrank to 1.11% of revenue from 3.48% in Q2 — roughly €0.10m (~$0.11m) vs €0.29m, our derivation — on 'higher customer winnings'. Management pivoted it to a niche product strategy: Prontolive.se moved to the latest platform build, a 5-year Swedish gaming-software licence (28 Aug 2026–27 Aug 2031) was obtained, the ProntoChoice slot and the 'Volameter' volatility search tool launched, and marketing was stepped up in Q4. October new unique depositing players were +103% MoM; LTV is unproven.
  • Marlin Media (marvn.ai / marvnBoost) remains a cost centre with no disclosed revenue. marvnBoost moved to three subscription tiers plus a free trial and exhibited at SBC Summit Lisbon; the group is now repositioning it toward custom content pipelines for aggregators/providers under a new brand, and has added headcount. marvn.ai organic search traffic came under pressure as search engines cut referrals to independent publishers. The Q2 report extended Marlin's break-even target to end-2026 — Q4 will tell.
  • The real news: a second B2B model reset from 1 Nov 2026. Angler will confine its B2B offer to the proprietary iGaming platform and discontinue non-software B2B services. Under an agreement in principle (formal contracts not yet executed), partner fees fall ('lower revenues'), while affiliate and royalty costs tied to partners' operations move to the partners, and Angler's 'exposure to certain operational and commercial risks' is reduced. Management expects 'significant changes to the Group's revenue and cost structure' and says profitability 'may' be significantly affected during a transition period.
  • A €5.25m (~$5.8m) one-off 'commercial incentive' will be settled by writing down partner receivables. It is conditional on partners signing new five-year contracts and 'would be effected through a corresponding reduction in existing amounts receivable'. That equals 24% of the €21.6m receivable book, 26% of group equity and 70% of 9M-26 net profit. IFRS treatment is 'being assessed' — an immediate P&L charge (credit-loss/consideration payable to a customer) versus a contract asset amortised against revenue over five years is the swing factor for Q4 and FY-26 reported earnings.
  • No guidance, by design. Angler has never given numeric guidance; it now states the financial impact of the new arrangements 'cannot currently be estimated with sufficient reliability'. The CEO's letter is explicit that revenue and profit will fall from 1 Nov while arguing the streamlined software model builds 'a strong foundation for future growth and improved profitability'. Carlsquare's August FY-26 estimates (revenue €35.4m / EBIT €11.1m) are stale.
  • Cash conversion is the weak link. 9M-26 operating cash flow €2.20m (~$2.43m) versus net profit €7.54m — 29% conversion — because debtors absorbed €7.42m. Trade and other receivables reached €21.60m (~$23.9m), +€3.1m QoQ and +52% since December, equal to 82% of total assets and ~230 days of 9M revenue. Note 18 discloses a €3.5m (~$3.9m) 'business development' facility to a B2B client that stopped accruing interest after Q1-25 and is repayable only out of that client's revenue share; no impairment is recognised.
  • Balance sheet is debt-free but asset-light and receivable-heavy. Cash €3.24m (~$3.58m; Jun-26 €2.59m, Dec-25 €1.24m), no borrowings, equity €20.32m (~$22.5m; 77% equity ratio), trade/other payables €6.07m. 9M capex €0.20m (intangible additions €0.20m); derived 9M FCF €2.00m (~$2.2m). Net cash/EBITDA is not meaningful; the relevant leverage is to partner credit quality.
  • Capital returns are on hold. No FY-25 dividend was recommended and no interim is declared (FY-24 paid €0.0133/share, €997k, in June 2025). A 2023 option scheme covers up to 2.88m shares (3.7%) with extended vesting; share count unchanged at 74,984,995, so basic = diluted EPS.
  • Organisation: technology is being ring-fenced in Aegirsoft Ltd. Software development is being consolidated into Aegirsoft (incorporated 12 Jan 2026), to be completed in 2026, with Nicholas Formosa (ex-head of the B2B platform for seven years) appointed its CEO. This is the entity that will carry the 'software-only' B2B proposition; Starfish Media N.V. (Curaçao) still books 'a significant portion' of group revenue.
  • Share price: the market priced the reset, not the quarter. The stock fell ~26% to SEK 4.29 by 09:21 CET on 12 Nov (prior close ~SEK 5.80 implied, Inderes data) and by 16 Nov was ~SEK 3.55 (market cap ~SEK 266m), roughly −39% since the release; Inderes shows a trailing P/E of ~5.4x. The official 12-Nov close could not be verified.
  • Thesis read-through: earnings quality, not earnings level, is the debate. Bulls get a 35%-margin, debt-free platform business with five-year partner lock-in and lower operational risk at ~5x trailing earnings. Bears note that 2025-26 profits were largely booked as receivables, that a quarter of those receivables is now being forgiven to keep the partners, that the partners evidently hold the negotiating leverage, and that the growth engine stalled in September before the fee cut even began.
  • Disclosure quality is thin for a company making a structural pivot. One reportable segment (IFRS 8), partners unnamed, no B2B/B2C revenue split beyond a percentage, no quantification of the fee reset, KPI definitions quietly changed in Q3 ('sites utilising the Group's proprietary iGaming platform' vs 'sites supported by the Group'), and 'available cashflow from operations' used to describe the cash balance. The next hard data point is the Q4/FY-26 report on 18 Feb 2027.

3Segment deep-dive: B2B iGaming platform (Starfish Media N.V. / Aegirsoft Ltd) — c.99% of group revenue

Record platform quarter on deposits +25% and a 55% gross margin, but monetisation slipped, growth stalled from late August, and the fee structure is being cut from 1 Nov in exchange for five-year contracts and a €5.25m receivable write-down.

Revenue and volumes

B2B and other revenue about €8.62m (~$9.53m) in Q3-26, up roughly 5.8% QoQ, driving the group's +10% YoY print on deposits +25.4%.

  • Angler reports a single IFRS 8 segment; B2B read through group KPIs net of the B2C share; Q2 implied €8.15m.
  • Management attributes growth to 'strong momentum' from prioritising the proprietary, partner-configurable platform over the past two years.
  • Customer deposits rose 25.4% YoY to €26.03m (~$28.8m) and 10.7% QoQ; gross game win +8.3% to €12.29m (~$13.6m); net game win +12.0% to €11.87m (~$13.1m).
  • Hold (net game win over deposits) fell to 45.6% from 51.0% a year ago and 46.5% in Q2; roughly thirteen points of deposit growth leaked out.
  • Revenue captured 73.4% of net game win versus 77.2% in Q2 and 74.8% in Q3-25; it moved the wrong way before the fee cut started.

Customer economics

Monetisation per head improved but the funnel deteriorated, with registration-to-deposit conversion falling to 29.5% from 48.3% a year earlier.

  • Period-unique actives 27,783, down 1.7% YoY though up 2.4% QoQ; new registrations +59% to 25,971 (flat on Q2); new depositing customers −2.9% to 7,662.
  • Conversion drop is consistent with cheaper, lower-intent traffic being pushed into partner sites.
  • Net game win per active €427 (~$472) against €375; deposits per active €937 (~$1,035) against €735.
  • Bonus cost fell to 3.4% of GGW from 6.6%, the cleanest sign that partners, not Angler, now carry promotional spend.
  • Nine months: cumulative actives 56,106 (−20%), new depositors 20,882 (−22%), offset by deposits +18% and NGW +10%; the active-customer definition was narrowed in Q3, which may flatter QoQ comparison.

Cost structure

Gross margin reached 55.3% (+835bps YoY, +25bps QoQ) and EBIT margin 34.9% on a fixed cost base of ~€1.8m a quarter.

  • Direct costs €3.89m (~$4.30m), 44.7% of revenue versus 53.0% in Q3-25; nine-month gross margin 56.1%.
  • Marketing €0.25m (~$0.27m), 2.8% of revenue (Q3-25: 4.1%); personnel €0.80m (−8%); other external €0.70m (−16%).
  • Total opex €1.78m (~$1.97m), 20.4% of revenue, down 520bps; incremental revenue has dropped through at close to gross margin for three quarters.
  • The same operating leverage works in reverse when the fee base is cut.

Intra-quarter trajectory

Growth effectively stopped from late August: after +21.08% through 18 August, the remaining 43 days ran at roughly +2% YoY on net game win.

  • The 20 August trading update stated average daily net gaming revenue through 18 August was 21.08% above the Q3-25 full-quarter average.
  • Quarter closed with net game win +12.0% and revenue +10.0%; our estimate puts the remaining 43 days at about −3% YoY on revenue.
  • PremierGaming's adverse customer-winnings swing accounts for only ~€0.2m of the shortfall, so the platform partners themselves slowed.
  • Carlsquare's August note had taken the update as confirming the revenue inflection; the exit rate says otherwise.
“the revenue inflection ... is now firmly established”Carlsquare

B2B fee re-cut

From 1 November 2026 Angler provides software only under a lower fee structure, trading a one-off €5.25m (~$5.8m) receivable reduction for five-year contracts.

  • Agreement in principle only; formal five-year contracts not yet executed, expected 'in the near term'.
  • Angler discontinues 'non-software-related B2B services'; affiliate and royalty costs transfer to partners and exposure to certain operational and commercial risks falls.
  • The €5.25m 'commercial incentive' is conditional on five-year signatures and effected through a reduction in existing amounts receivable from B2B partners.
  • A reasonable trade for a vendor whose counterparties hold the leverage, but an admission that 2025-26 accrued revenue was partly uncollectable at face value.
  • IFRS treatment unresolved: an immediate charge versus a contract asset amortised over five years decides whether Q4-26 prints a loss.
“revised fee structure, resulting in lower revenues”Management

Receivables and concentration

Trade and other receivables of €21.60m (~$23.9m) equal 82% of total assets and ~230 days of nine-month revenue; cash conversion only 29%.

  • Receivables up €3.1m QoQ and €7.4m since December; nine-month operating cash flow €2.20m against €7.54m net profit.
  • Note 18: €3.5m (~$3.9m) 'business development support' facility to a B2B client; interest ceased after Q1-25, repayment linked to the client's revenue share; no impairment indicator seen.
  • Note 7: 'a significant portion' of revenue comes from the Curaçao subsidiary and performance is 'materially dependent' on it.
  • Concentration, credit and counterparty leverage are the same risk wearing three hats.

Guidance

Guidance is qualitative only; we assume platform revenue runs 25-45% below the Q3 run-rate and the EBIT base 20-35% lower (estimate, wide range).

  • The board states the overall impact 'cannot currently be estimated with sufficient reliability'; no partner names, fee rates or cost-transfer amounts given.
  • Direct costs expected to fall proportionately more, leaving EBIT margin broadly intact, before any one-off charge.
  • Technology consolidation into Aegirsoft Ltd under Nicholas Formosa, due to complete in 2026, is the organisational counterpart of the 'software-only' pitch; it does not change economics.

4Segment deep-dive: B2C — PremierGaming Ltd (Malta/Sweden licences, Northern Europe)

Down to 1.11% of group revenue on adverse customer winnings; the niche re-launch (Swedish software licence, ProntoChoice, Volameter) doubled October depositors but lifetime value is unproven and the unit is immaterial to group earnings.

Revenue contribution

PremierGaming fell to 1.11% of Q3-26 revenue, about €0.10m (~$0.11m), from 3.48% (≈€0.29m) in Q2-26 on higher customer winnings.

  • Swing of roughly €0.2m QoQ, or ~2% of group revenue; the only luck-driven element in the quarter the company identifies.
  • No separate disclosure of B2C actives, deposits, ARPU or marketing, so the unit cannot be benchmarked against Swedish peers.
“higher customer winnings in Q3”Management

Re-launch initiatives

The quarter was spent re-tooling for differentiation among recreational players under own Swedish brands rather than scale.

  • Prontolive.se migrated to the latest version of the group's B2B platform software.
  • Swedish Gambling Authority granted a licence to supply gaming software for online gaming, valid 28 August 2026 to 27 August 2031.
  • First in-house slot ProntoChoice launched under that licence, development outsourced 'for efficiency'.
  • The Volameter, a tool filtering slots by volatility, went live at the very end of September.

October depositors

New unique depositing players in October rose 103% versus September after increased marketing, encouraging but not yet material.

  • Management says the next step is to 'follow up the lifetime value and plan accordingly'.
  • Q2 report committed to repeating campaigns only where lifetime value by margin exceeds campaign cost.
  • What matters is whether Q4 B2C revenue recovers toward the 3-7% of group it represented in 2025 without eroding the 2.8%-of-revenue marketing ratio.

Strategic role

No segment guidance; PremierGaming's value is as in-house reference customer and the group's only fully regulated, taxed market exposure.

  • It is the counterweight to a revenue base otherwise concentrated in a Curaçao-registered subsidiary.
  • The unit is immaterial to group earnings.

  • Second B2B fee reset from 1 Nov 2026: management explicitly expects lower revenue and profit, cannot estimate the impact, and the formal five-year agreements were still unsigned at publication — the entire earnings base is being repriced with no numbers. structural
  • €5.25m (~$5.8m) one-off 'incentive' settled by forgiving partner receivables = 24% of the €21.6m book, 26% of equity and 70% of 9M net profit; IFRS treatment undecided, so Q4 could print a loss of ~€3m if expensed (our estimate). one-off

7Forecast & outlook

Guidance bridge. Angler does not issue numeric guidance, so the 'bridge' is from implicit expectations to an explicit warning. Before Q3 the only published framework was Carlsquare's August model (FY-26 revenue €35.4m / EBIT €11.1m, implying ~€9.9m / €2.3m for Q4) built on the +21% trading update. The Q3 release replaces that with a qualitative statement from the B2B division — effectively the whole company — that revenue and profit will fall from 1 November 2026 under a revised fee structure, partly offset by affiliate/royalty costs transferring to partners, with 'significant' transition-period effects on profitability and a €5.25m (~$5.8m) incentive whose accounting is unresolved. B2C and Marlin are immaterial to the bridge.

Independent view. Q4-26 (our estimate, wide range): October runs on the old model at roughly the Q3 pace (~€2.9m revenue, ~€1.0m EBIT); November–December run on the new fee structure. Assuming platform revenue 25–45% below the Q3 run-rate and direct costs falling from 45% of revenue toward ~30–35% as affiliate and royalty costs transfer, we model Q4 revenue of €6.1–7.3m (~$6.7–8.1m) and EBIT of €1.9–2.6m (~$2.1–2.9m), i.e. an EBIT margin still in the low-to-mid 30s but an absolute EBIT 15–35% below Q3 — before any one-off. FY-26E therefore lands at revenue ~€31.6–32.8m (~$35–36m) versus Carlsquare's €35.4m, and EBIT ~€10.7–11.4m (~$11.8–12.6m) pre-charge, close to the old street number only because Q3 over-delivered. If the €5.25m is taken through the P&L in Q4 (credit-loss or consideration-payable-to-customer treatment), FY-26 EBIT drops to roughly €5.5–6.2m and Q4 prints a loss; if it is capitalised as a contract asset, the drag is ~€1.05m a year against revenue from 2027. We lean toward an immediate charge being the more conservative reading of 'reduction in existing amounts receivable', but the company has not decided. FY-27 is where the reset bites fully. A full year at the new run-rate implies revenue of roughly €21–26m (~$23–29m) against ~€32m in FY-26E, and EBIT of €7–9m (~$8–10m) if the margin holds in the 33–36% band — a 20–35% earnings reset that the ~39% share-price fall has broadly discounted at ~5x trailing earnings. The margin assumption is the key risk: management's own language ('may have a significant impact on profitability during a transition period') suggests costs do not fall in lock-step with fees in the first quarters. Leverage direction: none to speak of, but cash is the tell. With no debt, the balance-sheet question is whether the €16.3m of receivables remaining after the incentive actually convert to cash under the new contracts. If partners pay down even half of that over 2027, cash would multiply from €3.2m and a dividend could return; if receivables keep growing faster than revenue, the 'incentive' was the first write-down, not the last. We would also expect FX-driven finance expenses (~€0.4m a quarter) and the ~0% tax rate to persist, so net profit should track EBIT less ~€1.5m a year. Swing factors: (1) the fee rate and cost-transfer mechanics in the final contracts; (2) IFRS treatment and timing of the €5.25m; (3) hold normalising back toward 50% (worth ~€1m of quarterly NGW at current deposit levels); (4) whether the September stall was seasonal or partner-specific; (5) PremierGaming cohort LTV versus the stepped-up Q4 marketing.

What to watch next quarter

  • Execution of the definitive five-year B2B agreements and disclosure of the fee structure, plus the stated IFRS treatment of the €5.25m incentive (P&L charge vs contract asset) — likely in the Q4/FY-26 report on 18 Feb 2027.
  • Q4-26 revenue and direct-cost ratio for November–December (first two months under the new model) and any January–February 2027 trading update; we model a 25–45% revenue step-down vs the Q3 run-rate.
  • Receivables: does the €21.6m book (net €16.3m after the incentive) shrink and convert to cash; recoverability of the €3.5m B2B client facility; any further expected-credit-loss provisions.
  • Platform KPIs: hold recovery from 45.6% toward the ~50% of 2025, new-depositing-customer trend after −2.9% YoY, and whether registration-to-deposit conversion recovers from 29.5%.
  • PremierGaming: Q4 share of revenue versus 1.11% in Q3, lifetime value of the October cohort, and marketing as % of revenue versus the 2.8% group ratio.
  • Marlin Media: evidence of recurring marvnBoost revenue against the end-2026 break-even target, and impairment testing of the €1.5m software intangible at year-end.
  • Capital allocation: dividend resumption for FY-26 (Carlsquare had pencilled in 2027) versus the need to fund transition-period cash flow; option-scheme vesting.
  • Regulatory/tax: continued ~0% effective tax under the Curaçao territorial stance and the 'material dependence' on the Curaçao subsidiary; any change in partner licensing jurisdictions.
