# Las Vegas Sands Corp. (LVS) — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released 21 Oct 2026 (after US close)**
**Market reaction:** Shares closed at $37.51 on 21 Oct (-1.75% pre-print), fell ~2.2% to $36.70 after hours, then hit a new 52-week low of $35.03 intraday on 22 Oct (-6.6% vs prior close; 52-week high $70.45) before recovering to ~$37.09 by mid-session. Official 22 Oct closing level [unverified]. Market cap ~$24bn.

> A clean miss driven by the high end not showing up in either Singapore or Macao: net revenue -3.5% YoY to $3.22bn and adj. property EBITDA -16.7% to $1.12bn, ~13-14% below consensus on a hold-adjusted basis, with group margin -555bps to 34.8% as Macao opex was held flat sequentially into weaker premium spend and MBS mass hold collapsed to 18.7%. Management is doubling down rather than retrenching — $950m of buybacks (19% of the share count retired in 12 quarters), a 20% dividend hike for 2027, and no cuts to the Macao opex base or the $8bn MBS expansion — so the equity case is now a bet on the premium customer returning before leverage and payout optics become a constraint.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Headline miss on both lines.** Q3-26 net revenue $3,216m (-3.5% YoY, +2.0% QoQ) vs Street ~$3.33-3.35bn; adj. EPS $0.54 vs consensus ~$0.68-0.71 (Zacks $0.71, MarketBeat $0.68); GAAP diluted EPS $0.48 vs $0.61. Consolidated adj. property EBITDA $1,119m (-16.7% YoY, flat QoQ) vs prior-year $1,344m.
- **The miss is almost entirely the high end, in both markets.** Management's own framing: premium/VIP patrons visited less frequently in July and September in Singapore and across the premium mass tier in Macao; the quarter was labelled 'aberrational'. No single cause was offered — the World Cup final (19 Jul), general macro uncertainty and (unanswered) questions about Chinese offshore-wealth/tax enforcement were all cited by analysts.
- **Which division drove it: MBS.** Marina Bay Sands EBITDA fell $153m YoY (-20.6%) to $590m on revenue -9.9% to $1,294m; Macao EBITDA fell $72m (-12.0%) to $529m despite revenue +1.8% to $1,940m. JPM put MBS hold-adjusted EBITDA ($594m) 13-14% below the bank/consensus range of $680-685m — that is the bulk of the group shortfall.
- **Hold was a genuine headwind but not the whole story.** Company-reported rolling-chip hold adjustment was only -$7m to group EBITDA (Macao -$11m, MBS +$4m), so hold-adjusted EBITDA was $1,112m vs $1,299m in Q3-25 (-14.4%). The bigger swing is mass hold, which the company does NOT adjust: MBS non-rolling win 18.7% (vs 24.5% Q3-25, 22.9% Q2-26) and Londoner 18.6% (vs 23.4%). At a 22-23% normalised MBS mass hold, our estimate is ~$95-120m of additional gross win.
- **Volumes in Macao were strong — this is a spend-per-head and cost problem, not a traffic problem.** Rolling volume +31% YoY to $5.74bn (our aggregation of property data), non-rolling drop +11% to $7.18bn, slot/ETG handle +26% to $6.60bn; patron count up double digits, property visitation up high single digits; slot/ETG revenue +18% vs a +9% market; retail tenant sales +10%. Mass GGR share 26.1%, the best since Q4-23 — but Macao mass table gross win still fell ~2% YoY on hold.
- **Opex is the structural issue in Macao.** Group resort-operations expense +5.3% YoY to $2,103m (65.4% of revenue vs 60.0%) while revenue fell. Macao has added table hours, gaming/sales/hospitality headcount over the past year; management says fixed costs are now flat sequentially and headcount will not rise materially, but explicitly refused to cut — the hires are framed as pre-investment for the renovated product. Macao margin 27.3% (-426bps YoY, +325bps QoQ off a hold-hit Q2).
- **MBS non-gaming remains the quality part of the print.** Rooms +5.2%, F&B +9.5%, ADR +5.7% to $1,038, RevPAR +6.1% to $994 at 95.8% occupancy; non-gaming revenue +5.3% YoY to $378m. Casino revenue -14.9% to $916m did the damage. Management reiterated an unchanged $2.7-2.8bn annual EBITDA run-rate for MBS (c.$675-700m/qtr) — Q3 was $590m.
- **Macao target reiterated: $700m quarterly EBITDA 'and more over time'.** Q3 was $529m; the 9M-26 run-rate is $531m/qtr. The bridge relies on Venetian renovation completion (CNY 2028; ~400 rooms out in Q3, ~500 through Q4-27; ~600 renovated rooms already back), Conrad-to-Londoner rebrand, and market GGR recovery after what management called several months of contracting market GGR.
- **Capital returns stepped up hard into a falling share price.** $950m repurchased (~21m shares at $45.31) vs $787m in Q2 and $5.06bn still authorised under the $6bn July programme; cumulative 145m shares (19.0%) for $6.98bn at $48.03 since Q4-23. Dividend held at $0.30 for Q4-26, raised 20% to $0.36/qtr ($1.44/yr) from Q1-27. A further 62m SCL shares bought for HKD793m (~$101m), lifting ownership to 75.56%.
- **Balance sheet absorbing the payout.** Unrestricted cash $2.86bn (from $3.38bn at Q2) and gross debt $15.30bn (from $15.11bn); net debt ~$12.44bn, up ~$0.7bn QoQ. On annualised 9M-26 adj. property EBITDA (~$4.88bn, our estimate) gross leverage is ~3.1x and net ~2.5x, at the top of the stated 2-3x gross target (which excludes development debt). Liquidity: $4.27bn of revolvers plus a $4.57bn delayed-draw term loan ring-fenced for the MBS expansion.
- **Cash flow (our estimate, 10-Q not reviewed):** Q3 EBITDA $1,119m less interest ~$186m, cash taxes ~$100m, corporate ~$88m and capex $281m implies ~$460m of pre-minority free cash flow versus ~$1.24bn returned (buybacks $950m, dividend ~$192m, SCL purchases ~$101m) — consistent with the $520m cash decline and $190m debt increase. Buybacks are running at roughly 2x FCF.
- **Capex and the MBS expansion.** Q3 capex $281m (Macao $154m, MBS $121m, corporate $6m). The MBS expansion is quantified at $8bn including ~$2bn of land premium, with a ~15,000-seat arena, opening targeted early 2031 subject to approvals; a 'good portion' of construction cost is locked, with fit-out still exposed to inflation. Macao maintenance capex is guided at ~$300m/yr against $750-800m of D&A; 2027 Macao development capex covers the Venetian, the Conrad rebrand and an accelerated botanic-garden concession commitment.
- **Tax and below-the-line drag.** Effective tax rate 22.5% (vs 15.6%), driven by the 17% Singapore statutory rate on a larger MBS share of profit and lower Macao contribution; interest income fell to $23m from $39m on lower cash; interest expense flat at $186m with a 4.7% average cost. Net income $352m (-28.3%), attributable $304m (-27.4%). Diluted shares -6.6% YoY to 640m cushioned EPS.
- **Sands China (SCL):** GAAP net revenue +1.4% to $1.93bn, net income -29.8% to $191m. SCL minorities absorbed $48m of group net income (vs $72m).
- **9M-26 is still ahead on revenue but behind on profit.** Revenue $9,955m (+6.3%), operating income $2,140m (+1.4%), adj. property EBITDA $3,659m (-4.2%), GAAP EPS $1.86 (+5.1%, share-count driven), adj. EPS $2.04 (-5.1%). Macao 9M revenue +8.0% with EBITDA -6.5%; MBS 9M revenue +4.4% with EBITDA -2.3%.
- **No formal guidance; sell-side reset.** JPM cut Q4-26 adj. property EBITDA 4% to $1.26bn and 2027/2028 by ~2% to $5.06bn/$5.15bn; BofA (Neutral) cut its PT to $45 from $50, Mizuho (Outperform) to $54 from $61, Barclays (Overweight) to $50, JPM to $54. October was flagged on the call as potentially a fifth consecutive month of YoY Macao market GGR decline (analyst assertion, not company data).
- **Strategy and M&A.** Management ruled out M&A ('interested in buying more of our company'), dismissed take-private speculation, and said reinvestment (promo) in Macao is being optimised lower sequentially while gaining share; competitive intensity is unchanged with no escalation seen. NBA Games Week year two in October was described as more successful than 2025, financials to come in January.
- **Thesis read-through.** Volume share gains, rising SCL ownership and shrinking share count are real. But the quarter exposes the gearing of the model to a thin cohort of super-premium players in two buildings, into which the company is layering a higher fixed-cost base and an $8bn project. Until the premium customer visibly returns (Q4 seasonality plus Golden Week/NBA week is the first test), the equity is a leverage-tolerant buyback story rather than an earnings-growth story.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Macao Operations (Sands China Ltd, 75.56% owned)

*Volumes and share up strongly, but hold at the Londoner and a fixed-cost base built for a bigger market pulled EBITDA -12% YoY to $529m; the $700m quarterly target is now a 2028 story.*

### Headline results

**Net revenue grew 1.8% YoY to $1,940m but adjusted property EBITDA fell 12.0% to $529m; the $700m quarterly target is now a 2028 story.**

- Net revenue +8.4% QoQ off a hold-hit Q2; margin compressed 426bps to 27.3% (+325bps sequentially).
- Rolling-chip hold slightly above the 3.3% expectation; hold-adjusted EBITDA would have been $11m lower at $518m versus $599m hold-adjusted in Q3-25.
- Sands China GAAP basis: net revenue +1.4% to $1.93bn, net income -29.8% to $191m.
- Casino revenue across five properties $1,454m, +1.7% YoY and +10.4% QoQ; non-gaming revenue (ex ferry) broadly flat at ~$448m.

### Volumes and share

**Volumes and share rose strongly, but mass-table gross win still fell ~2% YoY to ~$1.47bn because Londoner hold dropped to 18.6% from 23.4%.**

- Rolling volume +31% YoY to $5.74bn (our sum; -24% QoQ as Plaza/Four Seasons VIP normalised from $2.8bn Q2); non-rolling drop +11% to $7.18bn; slot/ETG handle +26% to $6.60bn.
- Mass GGR share 26.1%, strongest since Q4-23; patron count up double digits, visitation high single digits; slot/ETG revenue +18% versus market +9%.
- Retail tenant sales +10% YoY despite a weak China consumption backdrop.
- Management attributed Londoner hold to playing unlucky against the super-high end of premium mass, captured this quarter for the first time in a while.
- Share gains are being bought at the top of the mass pyramid, where hold volatility is highest and the customer base thinned in the quarter.

### Property performance

**Londoner EBITDA fell 18.7% to $178m on low hold despite strong volumes; Venetian down mid-renovation; Plaza, Parisian and Sands Macao grew revenue.**

- Londoner: revenue flat at $685m, margin 26.0% (-590bps); rolling volume +39% to $3.22bn, non-rolling drop +24% to $2.80bn; normal ~21-22% hold would have added ~$70-95m gross win (our estimate).
- Venetian: ~400 rooms out (rising to ~500 through Q4-27); revenue -6.2% to $649m, EBITDA -15.3% to $205m (31.6% margin); tables 629 from 657, slots 1,410 from 1,660; full reintroduction targeted Chinese New Year 2028.
- Plaza/Four Seasons: revenue +15.5% to $238m, EBITDA +14.9% to $85m (35.7% margin), RevPAR +6% to $485.
- Parisian: revenue +6% to $231m on slot handle +39%, EBITDA -13% to $46m (19.9% margin, -440bps); Sands Macao revenue +37.5% to $99m, EBITDA $14m, slot handle more than doubled to $1.57bn.
- Hotel metrics firm: occupancy 96-99%, Londoner RevPAR $257 (+$4), Venetian $197 flat.

### Cost base

**The fixed-cost base built for a bigger market is the bear point and management will not cut it; headcount will not rise meaningfully next year.**

- Over the past year added table operating hours, gaming, sales/marketing and hospitality headcount and raised service levels.
- Grant Chum: step-up now embedded in Q2 and Q3, fixed costs essentially flat sequentially, headcount growth limited to cost-of-living inflation next year.
- Patrick Dumont: focus is on investing for growth, not cutting newly hired staff being trained ahead of renovated product.
- Market reinvestment intensity unchanged with no escalation; Sands has optimised its programme lower since Q1 while gaining share; competitors reportedly fine-tuning as industry margins are lower.

### Outlook

**The $700m quarterly EBITDA goal was reiterated but depends on 2028 projects; we model Q4-26 EBITDA at $560-600m, still ~$100m+ below target.**

- Target contingent on Venetian refresh (CNY 2028), Conrad-to-Londoner rebrand and accelerated botanic-garden concession project; ~$300m/yr maintenance capex against $750-800m D&A.
- Q4 seasonally stronger on October Golden Week, extended by NBA Games Week year two, described as more successful than 2025 across resort, fan zone and MICE.
- Company conceded several months of contracting market GGR and would not forecast seasonality against that; World Cup drag sharper in June/July than August/September.
- Track the 26.1% share print for evidence the opex investment is paying back.

> “and more over time” — Management

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Marina Bay Sands (Singapore)

*Revenue -9.9% and EBITDA -20.6% to $590m as super-premium visitation fell in July/September and mass hold dropped to 18.7%; non-gaming and volumes held up, and the $2.7-2.8bn run-rate was reiterated.*

### Headline results

**Net revenue fell 9.9% YoY to $1,294m and adjusted property EBITDA 20.6% to $590m as mass hold dropped to 18.7%; the $2.7-2.8bn run-rate was reiterated.**

- Revenue -6.2% QoQ; EBITDA -14.4% QoQ; margin 45.6%, down 615bps YoY and 433bps sequentially.
- Rolling hold 4.53% marginally below the newly raised 4.6% theoretical; hold-adjusted EBITDA $594m versus $700m in Q3-25 (4.84% win against 4.2% theoretical) and $726m in Q2-26.
- Theoretical rolling hold lifted to 4.6% from 4.2%, flattering the comparison; on the old 4.2% basis Q3-26 rolling play would have been 'lucky', not unlucky.
- Casino revenue -14.9% to $916m; every non-gaming line grew.

### Volumes and hold

**Volumes were not the problem; mass hold of 18.7% versus 24.5% in Q3-25 cut mass-table gross win 14% to ~$537m on 13% more drop.**

- Rolling volume $8.43bn, -7.1% YoY and -9.1% QoQ, still third-highest quarter in recent history; non-rolling drop +12.6% to $2.87bn; slot handle +2.1% to $6.54bn.
- Mass hold of 22-23% would have added ~$95-120m gross win (estimate); at ~70-75% flow-through EBITDA would have been close to the $675-700m quarterly run-rate.
- Rolling gross win -13% to ~$382m on lower volume; slot win ~$288m, +2%.

### High-end visitation

**Management said highest-value patrons visited less in July and September and called the quarter aberrational, maintaining the $2.7-2.8bn annual EBITDA view.**

- World Cup final, broad macro uncertainty and unspecified sentiment factors cited; side-bet product adds hold volatility in both directions.
- Catchment is Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Korea, Japan), so overlap with Macao premium customers is limited, yet sentiment weakened in both simultaneously, unexplained by management.
- A fuller Q4 will tell whether this was timing or a cohort issue in a casino concentrated in a small number of very high-value players.

> “the door swings both ways” — Management

### Non-gaming

**Non-gaming revenue grew 5.3% YoY to $378m with RevPAR up 6.1% to $994, showing the suite programme continues to price up.**

- Rooms +5.2% to $162m, F&B +9.5% to $104m, mall +1.4% to $70m, convention/other +2.4% to $42m; non-gaming +6.2% QoQ.
- ADR +5.7% to $1,038 at 95.8% occupancy; 2023-25 suite programme expanded from 132 to ~770 suites.
- Shoppes at MBS: $70m gross revenue, 88.6% operating margin, 99.5% occupancy, $3,318 tenant sales per sq ft.
- MBS capex $121m in the quarter versus $215m in Q2.

### Expansion and outlook

**The $8bn expansion targets early 2031 opening, but this quarter shows returns depend on visit frequency of a few hundred customers; we model Q4-26 EBITDA at $640-680m.**

- Budget $8bn including ~$2bn land premium; ~15,000-seat arena, new tower with wellness/residential feel for younger super-premium cohort; subject to approvals.
- Good portion of construction cost locked, but fit-out exposed to inflation; management cautious.
- Funded via dedicated $4.57bn delayed-draw term loan outside the 2-3x gross leverage target.
- Management argues peak-period capacity already constrains its top tier.
- Q4-26 estimate assumes mass hold normalises toward 22-23% and partial return of high-end visitation.

## 5. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Macao volume and share momentum: rolling volume +31% YoY, non-rolling drop +11%, slot/ETG handle +26%, mass GGR share 26.1% (best since Q4-23), slot/ETG revenue +18% vs +9% market, retail tenant sales +10% — all while management says promotional reinvestment is being optimised lower sequentially. _(structural)_
- MBS non-gaming pricing power intact: ADR +5.7% to $1,038, RevPAR +6.1% to $994, rooms +5.2%, F&B +9.5%; mall at 99.5% occupancy and $3,318 psf. The suite programme is still monetising. _(structural)_
- Capital return stepped up into weakness: $950m buyback at $45.31 (vs Q2 $787m), $5.06bn authorisation remaining, 19% of shares retired since Q4-23, dividend +20% to $0.36/qtr from Q1-27, SCL stake lifted to 75.56%. Diluted share count -6.6% YoY. _(structural)_
- Hold at the Londoner (18.6% mass) and MBS (18.7% mass, 4.53% rolling vs 4.6% theo) was adverse; at normalised mass hold we estimate ~$165-215m of additional gross win across the two properties — the single largest reconciling item vs consensus and reversible. _(one-off)_
- Macao opex step-up is now in the base: management says fixed costs are flat sequentially and headcount will not rise materially, so operating leverage should return if/when revenue recovers; Macao margin already +325bps QoQ. _(mixed)_
- Development expense fell to $37m from $72m and loss on disposal to $4m from $68m; D&A -7.6% — below-the-line GAAP comparisons are getting easier. _(one-off)_
- Plaza/Four Seasons rebounded from negative Q2 VIP hold to $85m EBITDA (35.7% margin) with RevPAR +6%; Sands Macao EBITDA +75% on relocated slot capacity — the portfolio re-orientation to premium and base mass is showing in the tails. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- The super-premium customer stopped showing up in both markets at once, and management cannot say why. MBS rolling volume -7% YoY, Macao spend per visitor down, July and September singled out as weak. Until Q4 proves otherwise, the 'aberrational' label is an assertion, not evidence. _(watch)_
- Group margin -555bps to 34.8% and resort opex up 5.3% YoY (65.4% of revenue vs 60.0%) on falling revenue. Management explicitly refused to revisit Macao staffing; the cost base is sized for a $700m/qtr Macao that is currently printing $529m. _(structural)_
- MBS theoretical rolling hold quietly raised to 4.6% from 4.2%, converting what would have been a 'lucky' quarter on the old basis into a 'hold-adjusted' tailwind of +$4m. Hold-adjusted comparisons across periods are no longer like-for-like. _(governance)_
- Buybacks (~$950m) plus dividends (~$192m) and SCL purchases (~$101m) ran at ~2.7x our estimated pre-minority FCF (~$460m): cash -$520m to $2.86bn, gross debt +$190m to $15.30bn, net debt ~$12.4bn. Gross leverage ~3.1x on annualised 9M EBITDA (est.) is already at the top of the 2-3x target before any MBS development debt. _(structural)_
- Macao market GGR has been contracting for several months per management, with October potentially a fifth consecutive down month (analyst assertion on the call); the Venetian carries ~500 rooms out of service through Q4-27, so the $700m target is effectively a 2028 event. _(watch)_
- Effective tax rate 22.5% (vs 15.6%) and interest income -$16m YoY as cash is spent — the mix shift toward Singapore (17% statutory) and lower cash balances structurally compress EPS conversion from EBITDA. _(structural)_
- $8bn MBS expansion (opening early 2031) is being committed on the thesis that peak-period super-premium capacity is the constraint; this quarter shows demand from that cohort can disappear for two months with no warning, and fit-out costs are not yet locked. _(watch)_

## 6. Forecast & Outlook

LVS gives no formal guidance; the 'bridge' is management's reiterated targets versus consensus resets. Nothing was cut: the Macao $700m quarterly EBITDA goal, the MBS $2.7-2.8bn annual run-rate, the $8bn MBS expansion (early-2031 opening) and the Venetian reintroduction by CNY 2028 were all reaffirmed, and the capital-return framework was raised (2027 dividend +20%, $5.06bn buyback capacity). The division that moved the numbers is MBS — JPM put its hold-adjusted EBITDA 13-14% below the $680-685m consensus — with Macao a secondary drag from opex. The sell-side responded by trimming rather than rebasing: JPM cut Q4-26 group adj. property EBITDA 4% to $1.26bn and 2027/28 by ~2% to $5.06bn/$5.15bn; BofA, Mizuho, Barclays and JPM cut price targets to $45-54.

- **Macao quarterly EBITDA target:** $700m+ (unchanged) (Q3 actual $529m; 9M run-rate $531m/qtr; path = Venetian refresh (CNY 2028) + market recovery)
- **MBS annual EBITDA run-rate:** $2.7-2.8bn (unchanged) (Q3 actual $590m (annualised $2.36bn); hold-adjusted $594m vs consensus $680-685m)
- **Capital return:** Dividend $0.36/qtr from Q1-27 (+20%); $5.06bn buyback remaining (Q3 buyback $950m (Q2 $787m); gross leverage target 2-3x ex development debt)
- **Street Q4-26 adj. property EBITDA (JPM):** $1.26bn (-4% post-print; 2027 $5.06bn / 2028 $5.15bn (-2% each))

Our Q4-26 estimate is net revenue of **$3.30-3.40bn** and adj. property EBITDA of **$1.20-1.28bn** (margin ~36-38%), built from Macao **$560-600m** (Golden Week plus NBA Games Week seasonality and a flat fixed-cost base, offset by a contracting market and ~500 Venetian rooms out) and MBS **$640-680m** (mass hold reverting toward 22-23% is worth ~$70-90m of EBITDA on our arithmetic; only partial return of super-premium visitation assumed). That implies FY26 adj. property EBITDA of roughly **$4.85-4.95bn** versus $3.82bn for 9M-25 plus an unverified Q4-25, and Q4-26 adjusted EPS of about **$0.65-0.72** (estimate: EBITDA less ~$345m D&A, ~$85m corporate, ~$165m net interest, ~21% tax, ~$60m minorities, on ~628m diluted shares). The risk to these numbers is skewed down if October's Macao market GGR is indeed a fifth consecutive decline and if MBS high-end visitation did not recover in the Golden Week window. **Margin** should recover sequentially on hold alone but will not regain Q3-25's 40%+ until revenue grows into the Macao cost base. **Leverage direction is up**: with buybacks running ~2x FCF, net debt should climb a further ~$0.5-0.7bn per quarter at the current pace, taking gross leverage above 3x on an LTM basis during 2027 before the $4.57bn MBS delayed-draw term loan is drawn — expect management to lean on the 'ex development debt' definition and the investment-grade rating, and expect the buyback pace to moderate from $950m if EBITDA does not rebound. The structural positives (share gains, non-gaming pricing, shrinking share count, 75.6% SCL ownership) are intact; the structural negative is that earnings are increasingly geared to a few hundred customers' travel decisions, into which the company is adding fixed cost and $8bn of capacity.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Q4-26 Macao: whether Golden Week/NBA Games Week and the 26.1% mass share convert into EBITDA >$560m with opex flat QoQ — and whether DICJ market GGR turns positive YoY after several down months.
- MBS mass hold and rolling volume in Q4: mass hold back above 22% and rolling volume >$9bn would validate 'aberrational'; a second quarter of <20% mass hold or rolling <$8.5bn would point to a cohort/behavioural shift.
- Capital return vs leverage: buyback pace vs $950m, cash below $2.5bn, gross debt/LTM EBITDA relative to the 3x ceiling, and any rating-agency commentary ahead of MBS DDTL drawdowns.
- Venetian renovation cadence: rooms out of service (~500 guided through Q4-27), ADR/RevPAR on renovated inventory, and progress on the Conrad-to-Londoner rebrand and botanic-garden commitment in the 2027 capex plan.
- MBS expansion cost discipline: proportion of the $8bn (ex $2bn land premium) locked, fit-out inflation, and timing of government approvals for the early-2031 opening.
- Chinese policy/tax developments affecting offshore wealth and premium outbound travel — the question analysts asked three times and management declined to address.
