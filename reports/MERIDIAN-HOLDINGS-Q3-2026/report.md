# Meridian Holdings () — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (tables in $ millions unless stated; company reports in USD, so no FX conversion required) · Results released 10 Nov 2026**
**Market reaction:** Shares closed -23.9% at $6.24 on 10 Nov 2026 (prior close ~$8.20), having been indicated higher pre-market on the deleveraging headline; they were down ~37% over the following week to $5.77 (13 Nov).

> A messy, low-quality quarter: Q3-26 revenue fell 3% to $46.0m (11% below the $51.6m consensus) and adj. EBITDA collapsed 93% to $0.4m as the online sports margin dropped to 7.4%, the UK/Australian prize-competition arm shrank 19%, and World Cup marketing plus GMAG bad debt pushed SG&A up 5% on falling revenue; $11.5m of non-cash impairments (Brazil licence write-off, goodwill) turned that into a $13.3m attributable loss and FY26 revenue guidance was cut to $195-198m. The balance sheet (net debt $8.1m, 0.48x) and the online casino engine (+17%) are the only clean positives; the shares fell 24% on the day.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Clear miss on every line.** Q3-26 revenue $45.96m vs Investing.com consensus $51.59m (-11%); GAAP diluted EPS $(1.05) vs consensus +$0.18. Adj. EBITDA (non-GAAP, new definition excluding unrealised FX) was $0.4m vs $4.8m recast ($5.7m as originally reported) in Q3-25 and $5.9m in Q2-26; margin 0.8% vs 10.2% / 11.8%.
- **Three named culprits, all at once.** Management attributes the quarter to (1) customer-favourable sports results (online sports margin 7.4% vs 8.2% Q3-25 and 10.7% Q2-26), (2) Brazil's Provisional Measure 1,394/2026 banning fixed-odds betting and online casino (platforms offline early Oct), and (3) higher operating costs (SG&A +5.3% YoY to $28.2m, 61% of revenue, while revenue fell 3%).
- **Adj. EBITDA bridge, -$4.5m YoY:** -$1.6m gross profit (online sports betting and RKings/Classics, partly offset by online casino), -$0.9m bad debt (GMAG B2B content resale, Articulate/Elray receivables written off), -$0.4m World Cup marketing, -$0.4m rent/utilities on new betting shops, -$0.3m salaries, ~-$0.9m other opex. Partly offset within SG&A by ~$1.6m lower D&A, professional fees and stock comp (which do not help adj. EBITDA).
- **$11.5m of non-cash impairments drove the GAAP loss.** $5.5m full write-off of the Brazilian licence ($4.1m) and domain/player database ($1.4m); $5.9m goodwill impairment in the reporting unit holding RKings, the 80%-owned Classics for a Cause and GMAG after continued losses. Group goodwill now only $3.0m ($2.0m left in that unit). Net loss $14.7m; attributable $13.3m after $1.5m absorbed by non-controlling interests (NCI balance fell from $1.8m to $24k).
- **Guidance cut and reframed.** The 29 Jul outlook of H2-26 constant-currency revenue growth of ~8-10% (≈$205-207m FY, our derivation from a ~$96.9m H2-25 base) is withdrawn; new FY26 revenue guide is $195-198m assuming no Brazil restart. That implies Q4-26 of $48.7-51.7m, i.e. -2% to +4% YoY on a ~$49.6m Q4-25 base (derived) — a ~$9m (~4.6%) cut at the midpoint.
- **Which division drove the cut:** RKings & Classics for a Cause (-$2.2m, -19% YoY to $9.1m) explain more than the entire group revenue decline (-$1.4m) and are guided to stay soft in Q4; Brazil was <1% of revenue; and the CFO admitted being 'a little bit more conservative on the Meridian side from the bottom end'. Meridianbet Group itself grew 2% to $33.1m (72% of revenue).
- **World Cup economics, as disclosed:** no group handle/actives figures were given. Management says World Cup marketing was elevated in Q2 and Q3 (Q3 marketing ~$7.3m, 15.8% of revenue vs 14.6% in Q3-25 and 11.7% in Q2-26 — derived from 10-Q quarterly disclosures) and that the tournament is 'rarely good' for hold; July was hit by the World Cup and, unusually, both August and September (start of European football season) were also poor. Q2 brought record registrations (+37% YoY); Q3 registrations +25% to 458,680.
- **Online casino is the structural engine.** Revenue +17% YoY / +18% QoQ to $17.1m (GGR $18.0m, +17%); 9M +21% to $46.9m; now ~52% of Meridianbet revenue; 2,500+ games after new providers added. Management's stated margin-recovery plan rests on this mix shift plus 'more targeted marketing and promotional costs' and retail footprint optimisation — there is NO quantified cost-transformation programme (no $ target, no target year).
- **Customer economics are mixed under the hood.** Deposits +9% to $101.1m and average deposit per depositor +18% to $457 imply the number of depositors fell ~8% YoY to ~221k (our derivation) despite +25% registrations — a conversion/retention question management did not address. No monthly actives, ARPU or market-share data are disclosed.
- **Sports volume vs luck:** management says online betting volume was 'broadly stable' QoQ and higher YoY; online sports revenue fell $4.4m QoQ (-34%) and $1.6m YoY (-15%) to $8.7m purely on margin. Our cross-check (revenue/margin) implies ~$118m online handle in Q3-26 vs ~$122m Q2-26 and ~$126m Q3-25 — which would contradict the 'higher volume' claim unless revenue is stated net of bonuses while margin is on a GGR basis; 9M implied handle +14% is consistent with growth. No structural-margin or promo-%-of-handle disclosure.
- **Cash generation held up at 9M level, weakened in Q3.** 9M operating cash flow $17.7m (+3%), capex $4.6m (vs $12.7m), lease repayments $2.2m → FCF (non-GAAP) $10.9m vs $2.5m. Derived Q3-26 OCF ~$4.8m (vs $7.8m Q2-26, ~$7.0m Q3-25) and Q3 FCF ~$2.5m; cash fell $1.3m QoQ to $16.0m after $2.4m of Q3 debt repayment.
- **Balance sheet is the bull case.** Bank/other loans $7.8m (-55% since Dec-25); total debt incl. acquisition consideration $24.1m (-47% YoY, -10% QoQ); net debt $8.1m (-65% YoY); leverage 0.48x annualised 9M adj. EBITDA (0.39x at Q2). 9M interest expense -74% to $0.9m. No equity raised in 2026, no buybacks; shares 12.68m (+0.32% YTD). Excluding the related-party consideration, the group is net cash (~$8.2m).
- **Related-party consideration pushed out to 2031.** The remaining $15.6m owed to Meridianbet's former owners (related parties; Aleksandar Milovanović holds voting control) was extended on 28 Sep to 1 Nov 2031, interest-free, settleable earlier at the company's discretion. This moved $16.2m out of current liabilities (current liabilities -$18m YTD) and is the main reason the liquidity picture looks comfortable; it is also a governance point.
- **Brazil: binary, low revenue, high optionality lost.** PM 1,394/2026 (25 Sep) terminated federal authorisations 30 days after publication with no refund of fees; operators had to go offline and return balances in early October. Brazil was <1% of revenue; the company keeps a minimal office, is litigating (four operators won lower-court relief that was then challenged) and notes Congress must act by end-January or the measure lapses. No restart assumed in 2026.
- **Australia/UK regulatory overhang on the prize-competition unit.** Amendments to Australia's Interactive Gambling Act covering trade promotions take effect 1 Jan 2027; Classics for a Cause must change its business model. A decision on RKings' path is expected 'in two, three weeks'; the 10-Q also flags potential UK VAT liabilities on prize competitions. Combined, this ~20%-of-revenue segment has had its goodwill written down and faces a structural reset in 2027.
- **B2B/other: real growth but still small.** Expanse Studios third-party revenue +114%, players +52%, 101 proprietary titles live on 2,000+ operator sites (1,881 at Q2), Sweden certified; New Jersey and Ontario approvals pending (Ontario 'weeks away', may slip into 2027). GMAG revenue +5% to $3.7m with gross margin +270bps to 35.8% but a $(0.6)m operating loss after $0.8m bad debt; MexPlay +55% to $0.9m with FTDs +35%. Management intends to break out Expanse and MexPlay separately from Q4.
- **Management and reporting changes.** Zoran Milosevic (founder-CEO of Meridianbet) is now group CEO; William Scott, interim CEO at Q2, is President, CFO, Treasurer AND Chairman — an unusual concentration. Zhe 'Scott' Yan became Chief Accounting Officer/Principal Financial Officer on 2 Oct 2026. The results 8-K was filed only late on 10 Nov after the call because of 'internal delays', prompting a second explanatory 8-K. Adj. EBITDA was also redefined in Q1-26 to exclude unrealised FX, which lowers the Q3-25 comparative from $5.7m to $4.8m.
- **Market reaction was severe.** Shares closed -23.9% at $6.24 on 10 Nov (prior close ~$8.20) despite an initially firmer pre-market; they were down ~37% over the following week to $5.77.
- **Thesis read-through.** The Meridianbet casino/retail engine is intact and self-funding, and leverage is no longer an issue. But earnings quality is thin: H1-26's ~12% adj. EBITDA margin now looks like a peak rather than a run-rate, ~28% of revenue (RKings/Classics/GMAG) is in decline or regulatory flux, and sports hold has been 'customer-favourable' for several consecutive quarters. Q4 seasonality should restore positive adj. EBITDA; the real test is whether FY27 can be guided with margins back above 10% without Brazil, with a re-modelled Classics, and with marketing held near 12-13% of revenue.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Meridianbet Group (B2C online & retail sportsbook and casino; Serbia-based, 18 jurisdictions across Europe, Africa, Central & South America)

*Casino +17% carried a segment whose operating income fell 85% to $0.7m on a 7.4% online sports margin, World Cup marketing and new-shop costs.*

### Segment revenue and profit

**Revenue grew 2% to $33.1m (72% of group) but operating income collapsed 85% to $0.7m, a 2.1% margin.**

- Revenue fell 7.5% sequentially from $35.8m in Q2-26.
- Operating income was $4.6m (c.14% margin) a year ago and $6.1m (17%) in Q2.
- Management framed this as a luck-and-cost quarter rather than a demand problem.

### Online sports betting

**Online sports revenue fell 15% year-on-year and 34% quarter-on-quarter to $8.7m as margin dropped to 7.4%.**

- Margin was 8.2% in Q3-25 and 10.7% in Q2-26; betting volume described as 'broadly stable' sequentially and higher year-on-year.
- World Cup hit July; August and September, when pricing statistics are thinnest, were also poor.
- Nine-month online sports revenue +12% to $33.5m on an 8.9% margin (9.1% a year ago), helped by wider live-streaming coverage.
- No expected margin, parlay mix or promotional cost as a percentage of handle is disclosed, so price cannot be separated from luck with confidence.
- Our cross-check implies online handle of roughly $118m in Q3-26 versus ~$122m in Q2-26 and ~$126m in Q3-25, sitting awkwardly with the 'higher volume' claim.

> “is rarely good for betting companies” — CEO

### Online casino

**Casino is doing the heavy lifting: revenue rose 17% to $17.1m and is now roughly 52% of segment revenue.**

- Revenue was $14.6m in Q3-25 and $14.5m in Q2-26; online casino GGR rose 17% to $18.0m; nine-month casino revenue +21% to $46.9m.
- Casino share was about 45% a year ago; library exceeds 2,500 games after new providers added.
- Mix shift is core of management's margin-recovery argument, since casino hold does not swing with football results.
- Group economics increasingly those of a casino operator in Serbia, Montenegro, Bosnia and Africa; CEO identified licensing as the 'number one' issue post-pandemic.

### Retail

**Retail revenue was $6.5m against $6.7m, with $0.4m of higher rent and utilities from new and relocated shops.**

- Nine-month retail casino up 24% to $12.5m on 100-plus new slot machines.
- Two new betting shops opened in Serbia and a third relocated to larger premises.
- Management's line that new shops take time to contribute is reasonable but should be checked against Q4 retail revenue.

> “take time to show their full contribution” — Management

### Customer metrics and marketing

**Registrations +25% to 458,680 and deposits +9% to $101.1m, but figures imply depositors fell roughly 8% to about 221k.**

- Average deposit per depositor rose 18% to $457; growth is from fewer, larger-depositing customers.
- Q2 World Cup registration surge (+37%) has not obviously converted; no monthly actives, ARPU, cohort retention or market shares disclosed.
- UFC Belgrade sponsorship delivered younger, casino-oriented registrations and will be repeated in 2027.
- Marketing ran at roughly $7.3m or 15.8% of group revenue versus 14.6% a year ago and 11.7% in Q2; CFO guided World Cup-level marketing and pre-ban Brazil costs will not recur.

> “by far the biggest marketing event we ever sponsored” — CEO

### Regions, Brazil and Expanse

**Nine-month group revenue growth of 10% included Africa +33% and Central & South America +27%; Brazil is now closed.**

- FY25 mix: Europe ex-UK ~56%, UK ~19%, Africa ~8.5%, APAC ~7%, CSA ~5%, Australia ~5%.
- PM 1,394/2026 banned fixed-odds betting and online casino on 25 Sep; authorisations cancelled 30 days later with no refund; company wrote off $4.1m licence and $1.4m domain/player database.
- Brazil was under 1% of revenue; write-off and 30%-minority loss absorption explain most of the $1.5m loss attributed to non-controlling interests; Congress deadline end-January.
- Expanse Studios third-party revenue +114%, players +52%, 14 games released (101 proprietary titles), live on 2,000-plus operator sites (1,881 at Q2), Swedish certification gained.
- Ontario approval 'weeks away' and may slip into 2027; Expanse and MexPlay to be disclosed separately from Q4.

### Guidance

**We read Q4 revenue of roughly $36-37m and a segment operating margin in the low-to-mid teens, with sports hold the entire swing factor.**

- Q4 is historically the strongest quarter; management expects higher betting volume and continued online casino growth.
- Our estimate assumes online sports margin normalises to 9-9.5% and casino holds +15-18%; margin below the 17% of Q2.
- Three consecutive quarters of 'customer-favourable' results, including two $1.2m-combined casino wins in Q2, look less like noise and more like a risk-management or promotional-intensity question.

> “returning to the profitability we demonstrated in the first half of 2026” — Management

## 4. Segment deep-dive — RKings & Classics for a Cause (UK pay-to-enter prize competitions; Australian trade promotions, 80%-owned)

*Revenue -19% to $9.1m, goodwill impaired, Australian law change on 1 Jan 2027 and a UK VAT overhang: a ~20%-of-group segment in structural reset.*

### Revenue decline

**Combined revenue fell 19% year-on-year and 16% quarter-on-quarter to $9.1m, roughly 20% of the group.**

- Reverses the +4% growth a quarter earlier (Q2-26: $10.8m, RKings $7.0m, +5%, Classics about 10,100 active VIP subscriptions).
- Management blamed lower volumes and average spend from competition, changed purchasing behaviour and weaker digital advertising; paid social is converting less well.
- Nine-month revenue is $32.0m, down 3%, so deterioration is concentrated in Q3 and guided to continue in Q4.

> “reduced effectiveness of certain digital advertising channels” — Management

### Profit and impairment

**Operating income of $0.1m versus $0.6m a year ago, while a $5.9m goodwill impairment is the more honest signal of expectations.**

- Q2 was a small loss; management presented Q3 as an improvement, which we find thin.
- Impairment leaves $2.0m of goodwill in the reporting unit and $3.0m group-wide.
- Management's plan is a 'strong offer for customers and tight control of acquisition and promotional costs'; CFO offered no path back to growth for 2027.

> “we don't like businesses which are soft... we're not giving 2027 at the moment” — CFO

### Regulatory risk

**Australian Interactive Gambling Act amendments take effect 1 January 2027 and Classics must change its business model; UK VAT overhang persists.**

- 10-Q says the Australian impact is still being assessed.
- For RKings the CEO said the situation is 'still unclear' with a decision expected 'in two, three weeks'.
- 10-Q risk factors flag potential UK VAT liabilities on prize competitions.
- A fifth of group revenue enters 2027 with a model change, undecided UK strategy and a tax question; guidance cut leans on this segment rather than Brazil.

### Outlook and strategic question

**We see Q4 revenue of roughly $8.5-9.5m and an immaterial adj. EBITDA contribution; the question is whether the group keeps these businesses.**

- Management guides 'continued lower revenue'.
- Disposal or wind-down would simplify the equity story at the cost of ~$35-40m of annual revenue.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — GMAG incl. MexPlay (B2B gaming-content aggregation, mainly Asia-Pacific; regulated online casino in Mexico)

*Revenue +5% to $3.7m and gross margin +270bps, but $0.8m of bad debt turned the segment to a $(0.6)m operating loss; MexPlay +55% is the bright spot.*

### GMAG revenue and bad debt

**Revenue rose 5% to $3.7m with gross margin up to 35.8%, but $0.8m of bad debt pushed the segment to a $(0.6)m operating loss.**

- Revenue +3% sequentially, 8% of group; gross margin was 33.1% a year ago; 2,311 new games deployed (+25%) and 10 content providers added.
- Nine-month bad debt $1.16m versus $0.23m a year ago, including write-offs of roughly $130k and $127k owed by Articulate and Elray.
- CEO's instinct implies slower growth or tighter credit; receivables of $7.7m group-wide against ~$46m quarterly revenue warrant monitoring.

> “growth there has to come with customers who pay on appropriate payment terms” — CEO

### MexPlay

**MexPlay revenue grew 55% to $0.9m with first-time depositors up 35% and registrations up 19%.**

- Q2 growth was +31%, +53% and +50% respectively; management stressed Mexican licences are scarce and valuable.
- Still immaterial at group level; to be reported separately, probably from Q4, and moved under Meridianbet as 'classic sports betting' economics.
- Separate disclosure should make the GMAG core's decline more visible.

### Capital allocation

**Non-Meridianbet businesses (28% of revenue) generated roughly $(0.5)m combined operating income and absorbed a $5.9m goodwill write-down.**

- Group quarterly adj. EBITDA is $0.4m, making management time on these businesses a capital-allocation debate.
- CEO would consider acquisitions only where they bring a hard-to-build licence, technology or market position.

> “only where they give us a license, technology, or market position that is hard to build ourselves” — CEO

## 6. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Online casino revenue +17% YoY / +18% QoQ to $17.1m (GGR $18.0m) and +21% over 9M to $46.9m; now ~52% of Meridianbet revenue, 2,500+ games — the one line that is compounding regardless of sports results. _(structural)_
- Seventh consecutive quarter of deleveraging: bank loans $7.8m (-55% YTD), total debt $24.1m (-47% YoY), net debt $8.1m (-65% YoY), leverage 0.48x; 9M interest expense -74% to $0.9m; no equity issued, share count +0.32% YTD. _(structural)_
- 9M free cash flow $10.9m vs $2.5m as capex halved to $4.6m from $12.7m; OCF $17.7m (+3%) funded all investment and $9.0m of debt repayment. _(structural)_

## 7. Forecast & Outlook

The 29 July outlook called for H2-26 constant-currency revenue growth of ~8-10% YoY, which on our derived H2-25 base of ~$96.9m implied roughly $205-207m for FY26. That has been withdrawn and replaced with a FY26 revenue range of $195-198m, assuming no Brazil restart — a cut of ~$9m (~4.6%) at the midpoint. Management attributes the revision to the Brazil suspension (<1% of revenue, so a small piece), lower expected revenue at RKings and Classics for a Cause (the main driver: -$2.2m in Q3 alone and guided lower again in Q4), and the Q3 sports results; the CFO added that the Meridianbet assumptions were deliberately set 'a little bit more conservative... from the bottom end'. Arithmetically the new range implies Q4-26 revenue of $48.7-51.7m, i.e. -2% to +4% YoY against a derived ~$49.6m Q4-25, with a 'modest' sequential increase in sports betting revenue on seasonally higher volume and continued casino growth. No EBITDA or EPS guidance is given, and no 2027 guidance.

- **Prior outlook (29 Jul):** H2-26 rev +8-10% cc ≈ FY $205-207m (derived) (withdrawn)
- **RKings/Classics softness + Q3 sports hold:** -$2.2m Q3 YoY; Q4 guided lower (largest driver)
- **Brazil suspension:** <1% of revenue; no Q4 contribution (-)
- **New FY26 outlook:** $195-198m → Q4 $48.7-51.7m (-2% to +4% YoY) (-$9m midpoint (~-4.6%))

**Revenue:** we model Q4-26 at ~$50m (mid-range): Meridianbet ~$36.5m assuming online sports margin normalises to ~9-9.5% on seasonally higher handle and online casino of ~$17.5-18m (+15-18% YoY); RKings/Classics ~$9m; GMAG ~$3.7m. That puts FY26 at ~$196m, inside the range, with upside only if December football hold is kind. **Margin:** with gross margin back near 55% and SG&A ex-impairment held at ~$27m, Q4 adj. EBITDA should recover to roughly **$3-4m (6-8% margin)**, giving FY26 adj. EBITDA of ~**$15.5-16.5m (~8%)** versus $12.6m at 9M — well below the 11.8% printed in Q2, which we now treat as a peak, not a run-rate, until marketing is demonstrably back at 12-13% of revenue post-World Cup. GAAP Q4 should be modestly positive absent further impairments; remaining goodwill is only $3.0m so impairment risk is largely spent, but the Brazilian NCI and Classics minority will keep NCI volatile. **Leverage:** Q4 FCF of ~$3m (est.) and scheduled repayments should take net debt to **~$6-7m by year-end (~0.4x)**; with bank loans of $7.8m against $16.0m cash, the group is net cash excluding the 2031 related-party consideration, so the balance sheet is not a constraint — the question is whether cash is used for Expanse/MexPlay licences, a retail footprint that is lifting fixed costs, or the acquisitions management hinted at. **Swing factors:** (1) sports hold — every 100bps of online margin on ~$120m quarterly handle is ~$1.2m of revenue dropping almost entirely to EBITDA; (2) the Classics for a Cause model change on 1 Jan 2027 and the RKings decision due within weeks, which could remove $35-40m of annual revenue or reset its margin; (3) Brazil — Congressional lapse by end-January would be a free option, but we assign no value; (4) GMAG receivables; (5) Ontario/New Jersey approvals for Expanse. Our base case is that FY27 revenue is flat-to-down low single digits on a reshaped perimeter with margin recovery to ~10%; the bull case needs a disposal or restructuring of the non-Meridianbet businesses so the equity reads as a deleveraged Balkan/African casino-led operator growing casino 15-20% a year.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Brazil: Congressional decision on PM 1,394/2026 by end-January 2027 and court rulings; any reversal would be upside not in guidance
