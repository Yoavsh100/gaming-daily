# South Africa () — FY 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: ZAR (R) — tables in R bn unless stated; ~USD alongside at USD/ZAR 16.5 (ESTIMATE — see confidence note) · Results released 3 Nov 2026 (NGB National Gambling Statistics FY2025/26, period 1 Apr 2025–31 Mar 2026; NGB Annual Report 2025/26 published Oct 2026)**

> South Africa's regulated gambling market set a record R84.46bn (~$5.12bn) GGR in FY2025/26 (+13.4%), but the growth is a single engine — online sports betting (R55.77bn, +25%) licensed province-by-province and taxed at ~6.5% of GGR plus VAT. The rules are now moving against the grain of that growth: the SCA has shut the bookmaker 'casino-game' loophole, Treasury wants a 20% national online GGR tax with the design due in the Feb-2027 Budget, advertising norms are drafted, and a site-blocking tender is live — while online casino stays illegal and an estimated majority of online play sits offshore. Attractive scale, deteriorating rule-set.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Record year, decelerating.** The NGB's audited FY2025/26 statistics (1 Apr 2025–31 Mar 2026) put gross gambling revenue (GGR = stakes less winnings, before tax) at R84.46bn (~$5.12bn), +13.4% on R74.50bn (~$4.52bn). Growth has slowed from +25.7% (FY23/24) and +25.6% (FY24/25). The headline is corroborated by Gaming Intelligence, The Citizen, News24/GroundUp and Daily Maverick (all R84.46–84.5bn).
- **One engine: betting delivered more than 100% of growth.** Betting GGR rose R10.18bn (~$617m) to R62.16bn (~$3.77bn, +19.6%) while casinos (-R142m), bingo (-R186m) and LPMs (+R110m) netted to a decline. Betting is 73.6% of GGR (50% three years ago; 12% in FY11/12); casinos 19.5%, LPMs 5.0%, bingo 1.8%.
- **Online penetration is at least 66% of the whole market.** In the six provinces that disaggregate it (WC, MP, LP, NW, EC, NC) online betting GGR was R55.77bn (~$3.38bn), +25.4%, equal to 66.0% of total GGR and 89.7% of betting GGR; retail betting fell 15.0% to R6.39bn (~$387m). Gauteng, KZN and Free State are not split, so true online share is higher. Daily Maverick reports mobile at 91% of digital wagering.
- **Stakes of R1.76tn (~$106.9bn), +17.5%.** Betting turnover was R1.40tn (~$84.9bn, +24.5%), 79.5% of all money wagered and ~57x the R24.5bn of FY2013/14 (News24). Market hold on betting is 4.4% (RTP 95.6%); sports bookmaking hold 4.1%; casino hold 5.9% on R280.5bn (~$17.0bn) of turnover (-4.2%).
- **Tax take R6.45bn (~$391m), +10.9% — growth is in the lowest-taxed mode.** Provincial taxes/levies on GGR: betting R4.11bn (~$249m, 6.6% effective), casinos R1.66bn (~$100m, 10.0%), LPMs R543m (~$33m, 12.7%), bingo R132m (~$8m, 8.5%). Betting generates 74% of GGR but 64% of tax. These figures exclude VAT (15% applies to betting), corporate tax and the National Lottery.
- **Tax regime is provincial and GGR-based, with VAT on top.** Nine Provincial Licensing Authorities (PLAs) levy tax under provincial Acts: Gauteng bookmakers 6.5% of gross betting revenue + 1% Sports Development Fund levy; casinos 9% (Gauteng) or 8–19% progressive (Western Cape); LPM route operators 15% (Gauteng) / 10–20% (WC); bingo 12% / 10–15%. Treasury characterises bookmaker taxation as 6–7% (elsewhere 6–9%) of GGR; player winnings are untaxed unless habitual income.
- **The 20% national online gambling tax is the dominant swing factor.** Treasury's Nov-2025 discussion paper proposes a separate national 20% tax on online betting and interactive GGR, in addition to provincial taxes (combined 26–29% before VAT), SARS-administered, worth '>R10bn' (~$606m) at current GGR and framed as a 'sin tax'. The Finance Minister's MTBPS (21 Oct 2026) deferred final design to the Feb-2027 Budget; industry models an all-in burden approaching 40% of GGR including VAT; passage requires a money bill through both houses and the DA (a GNU partner) has opposed it.
- **Legal framework: a 2004 statute, concurrent competence, no online casino.** Gambling is a concurrent national/provincial competence (Constitution Sched. 4). National Gambling Act 7 of 2004 s11 prohibits interactive gambling; the 2008 Amendment Act that would have created national online licences was never proclaimed; the National Gambling Amendment Bill is stalled (NGB AR cites 'delayed enactment'); the Remote Gambling Bill (B11-2024, DA private member's bill) is not expected before 2027. Only fixed-odds betting on sport, horse racing and 'other contingencies' via a provincial bookmaker licence is lawful online.
- **The SCA closed the 'casino games as fixed-odds bets' loophole.** Portapa (Pty) Ltd t/a Supabets v Casino Association of SA [2025] ZASCA 158 (21 Oct 2025) held that under the Gauteng Gambling Act bookmakers may not offer fixed-odds bets on casino games including roulette. NGB then advised dtic/NRCS that Remote Gaming Servers are unlawful under s11; NRCS stopped issuing or renewing Letters of Certification for RGS products under SANS 1718. The data shows the hit: Gauteng betting GGR -19.2% to R3.77bn (~$228m), quarterly betting turnover collapsed from R8.46bn (Q1) to R2.34bn (Q3), and Gauteng betting RTP fell from 88% to 61–73% in H2 as high-RTP casino-style content was withdrawn.
- **Licensing geography drives the numbers.** Mpumalanga (GGR R29.23bn, ~$1.77bn, 34.6% share, +31.4%) and Western Cape (R24.66bn, ~$1.49bn, 29.2%) host the major online bookmakers' primary licences and collect 59% of all gambling tax (R1.94bn and R1.86bn). Eastern Cape (betting GGR +676% to R1.46bn) and North West (+205% to R1.29bn) show new licensees locating there. Gauteng's share of GGR fell to 14.6% (-8.4%). Effective betting tax: WC 6.4%, MP 6.6%, GP 7.0%, KZN 10.5%.
- **Operator landscape: an online duopoly, a land-based oligopoly.** 315 operational bookmakers (539 outlets), 340 tote outlets, 36 casinos, 2,594 LPM site operators, 62 bingo halls. Blask demand data had Betway and Hollywoodbets at >75% of online demand (Mar-2025), with Hollywoodbets ~27% by Feb-2026 and Betway gaining 4.8pp; Q1-2026 traffic data puts Betway, YesPlay and Hollywoodbets at ~50% of 380m visits. Sun International's SunBet grew income 35.5% to R1.18bn (~$72m) in H1-2026 'ahead of market growth of ~19%'. Tsogo Sun (15), Sun International (11) and Peermont (8) own 34 of 36 casinos; Sun holds 49.0% land-based casino share.
- **Land-based is ex-growth but stabilising.** Casino GGR R16.50bn (~$1.00bn), -0.9%, but Q3 +0.8% and Q4 +6.7% YoY; gambling positions fell to 24,246 (from 28,362), slots 20,981, tables 865. LPMs are the resilient land-based product: 15,988 machines (+1.6%), GGR R4.26bn (~$258m, +2.6%). Bingo is in structural decline: GGR -10.7%, outlets 73→62, EBTs 8,868→8,413.
- **Lottery sits outside the NGB numbers and is in legal limbo.** Sizekhaya Holdings took over the National Lottery and sports pools on 1 Jun 2026 under the fourth licence (Ithuba had operated since 2015 plus a 12-month temporary term); on 3 Nov 2026 the Pretoria High Court set aside the licence award and declared the licence agreement invalid — operator continuity is now uncertain.
- **Payments are bank-rail, not mobile-money.** Licensed operators run on cards, instant EFT and retail vouchers; offshore sites also accept rands, local cards and digital vouchers (Daily Maverick). Exchange-control rules require Authorised Dealers to refuse FX for offshore gambling and residents may not use cards for it; s16 NGA lets banks withhold illegal winnings for the NGB (R775k ~$47k forfeited in FY25/26; ~R3m ~$182k cumulative). The FIC has been AML/CFT supervisor of gambling since Dec-2022 and has now signed MOUs with the NGB and all PLAs.
- **The illegal online market is large and currently unblockable.** Yield Sec estimates ~62% of online activity on unlicensed sites, >R50bn (~$3.0bn) GGR offshore and ~16m users; CGCSA puts tax leakage at ~R7.7bn (~$467m)/yr. The NGB restricted 27 illegal sites, ran 35 enforcement interventions, and issued an EOI (30 Jun 2026; closed 4 Sep 2026) for a national monitoring/tracking/blocking solution; the Communications Minister is exploring Icasa network-level powers, which gaming lawyers argue are ultra vires.
- **Advertising and RG are tightening, slowly.** s15 NGA and reg. 3 bar ads aimed at minors and require RG messaging; the NGB has issued directives to PLAs and is drafting national advertising norms and standards; dtic promised norms and regulations 'before July 2026' but none had been gazetted by mid-Aug 2026. Civil-society petitions seek a tobacco-style ban on ads and sponsorships. The National Self-Exclusion Register remains inoperative 22 years after the Act; the NRGP helpline logged 1.26m calls and the NRGF treated 3,541 people in FY25/26. NGB flags escalation among NSFAS students and SASSA grant recipients; StatsSA puts gambling at 54.5% of household recreation/culture spend.
- **Regulatory fragmentation is itself a risk.** The NGB reports an open dispute with certain PLAs over s11 (licensed operators 'engaging in prohibited gambling activities'), escalated to the National Gambling Policy Council under ss62–63; it issued only 9 of 45 planned PLA compliance evaluations (20%). Gauteng data in the stats is provisional/unaudited.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** This is Africa's largest regulated market (~$5.1bn GGR; >$3.4bn disclosed online) with a low headline tax, deep card/EFT/voucher rails and lawful online sports betting — but it is a sports-only licence, the casino upsell is judicially closed, the fiscal burden could roughly double from April 2027, and advertising/sponsorship rules are tightening. The practical route is a Western Cape or Mpumalanga bookmaker licence (modest fees; B-BBEE, probity and SANS 1718 testing; 3–12 month timelines), a mobile-first sports book with 'other contingencies' products that survive s11 scrutiny, and an entry case stress-tested at 26–40% all-in tax against an entrenched Betway/Hollywoodbets duopoly.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Sports betting & horse racing (online + retail bookmakers, totalisators)

*R62.16bn (~$3.77bn) GGR, +19.6%; online R55.77bn (+25.4%) is ~90% of betting; growth decelerating, Gauteng shrinking post-SCA, Mpumalanga/WC licence hubs now 80% of betting GGR.*

### Betting GGR and mix

**Betting GGR reached R62.16bn (~$3.77bn) in FY2025/26, +19.6%, still the fastest-growing mode but on a visibly flattening curve.**

- Prior two years grew +44.7% and +51.2%, so growth is decelerating.
- Fixed-odds bookmaking on sport and 'other contingencies' is the business: R56.78bn (~$3.44bn), +18.6%, or 91% of betting GGR.
- Bookmaker horse-racing GGR surprised at R4.81bn (~$292m), +36.9% on turnover of R13.5bn (~$821m), almost all booked in Western Cape (R4.49bn).
- Western Cape Q3/Q4 horse-racing GGR jumped to R1.34–1.37bn per quarter from ~R0.9bn.
- Totalisator (4Racing in WC, Gold Circle in KZN) is marginal at R0.56bn (~$34m), -0.7%; NGB flags tote B-BBEE non-compliance (average level 6.4).

### Betting taxes

**Betting taxes rose 20.2% to R4.11bn (~$249m), an effective 6.6% of GGR nationally.**

- Sports bookmaking effective rate 6.4%, horse racing 9.2%.

### Channel shift

**Online betting GGR in six disclosing provinces was R55.77bn (~$3.38bn), +25.4%, while retail fell 15.0% to R6.39bn (~$387m); the shift is nearly complete.**

- Bookmaker shopfronts fell from 553 to 539 even as tote outlets rose to 340.
- Gauteng, KZN and Free State do not split online from retail, so the 66.0% online share of total GGR is a floor.
- Daily Maverick's 91% mobile share of digital wagering is consistent with the smartphone, not the shop or casino floor, being the point of sale.
- Hold is thin and falling: betting RTP rose to 95.6% (92.0% in FY24/25), giving 4.4% hold on R1.40tn (~$84.9bn) of stakes.
- Hold on the R1.386tn sports book was 4.1%; stakes grew 24.5% but GGR only 19.6%.

### SCA regulatory shock

**Quarterly growth dipped to +13.8% in Q3 (Oct–Dec 2025) when the SCA outlawed fixed-odds casino games under the Gauteng Gambling Act.**

- Betting GGR growth: +23.3% (Q1), +23.0% (Q2), +13.8% (Q3), +19.7% (Q4).
- Portapa t/a Supabets v Casino Association of SA (21 Oct 2025) was followed by the NRCS halting certification of Remote Gaming Servers.
- Gauteng betting turnover collapsed from R8.46bn in Q1 to R2.34bn in Q3 (-72%); Gauteng GGR slid from R1.01bn to R0.84bn by Q4 (full year R3.77bn, -19.2%).
- Gauteng betting RTP fell from 88% to 61% (Q3) and 73% (Q4), as expected when high-RTP casino-style content is pulled.
- Elsewhere growth continued: Mpumalanga betting GGR hit R7.65bn in Q3 and Western Cape R5.83bn in Q4.
- Judgment is confined to Gauteng's Act, but the NGB has adopted its logic nationally and is in a s11 dispute before the National Gambling Policy Council; status of lucky-numbers, virtuals and 'betgames'-type products outside Gauteng is the live question.

### Provincial licence migration

**Mpumalanga (R28.35bn, 45.6%, +33.0%) and Western Cape (R21.18bn, 34.1%, +8.5%) show licences, not punters, drive provincial numbers.**

- Limpopo R4.16bn (+5.1%); Gauteng R3.77bn (-19.2%); KZN R1.40bn (-8.1%); Eastern Cape R1.46bn (+676%); North West R1.29bn (+205%); Northern Cape R0.48bn (+66%).
- A bookmaker licensed in any province may take bets nationwide, so operators domicile where processing, rates and supervision suit them.
- Effective betting tax rates: WC 6.4%, MP 6.6%, Gauteng 7.0%, Limpopo 6.5%, EC 6.6%, NW 5.0%, KZN 10.5%.
- The spread explains why KZN, home of Hollywoodbets' retail roots, has only 2.3% of betting GGR.

### Competitive landscape

**The online book is a duopoly of Betway and Hollywoodbets with a long tail; casino groups' digital arms are the growth challengers.**

- Blask data had Betway and Hollywoodbets above three-quarters of online demand in March 2025; Hollywoodbets ~27% by February 2026, Betway up 4.8pp.
- Q1-2026 traffic put Betway, YesPlay and Hollywoodbets at about half of 380m site visits; LottoStar, World Sports Betting, Supabets and JackpotCity mid-tier.
- SunBet lifted income 35.5% to R1.18bn (~$72m) in six months to June 2026, with online-gaming GGR +43.2% versus sports betting +10.9%, showing bookmaker growth from non-sport products under s11 scrutiny.
- Entrant economics: ~4% hold sports book, 6.5% provincial tax + 1% levy + 15% VAT (~18–20% of GGR all-in), high marketing intensity, narrowing product perimeter.

> “ahead of market growth of approximately 19%” — Sun International

### Demand and vulnerability

**Demand is seasonal around European football, and NGB flags gambling 'as a substitute for income' among NSFAS and SASSA recipients.**

- Mid-year trough and November–December peak on EPL/UCL fixture density plus year-end spending; Friday-evening and Saturday peaks (Blask).
- Horse racing is a Western Cape and Gauteng phenomenon.
- Advertising and affordability debate will bite first among NSFAS student-allowance and SASSA grant recipients.
- An operator's RG design will need to anticipate payday-cycle controls and marketing limits before they are legislated.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Land-based: casinos, LPMs & bingo

*Casino GGR R16.50bn (~$1.00bn), -0.9% but back to growth in H2; three groups own 34 of 36 casinos; LPMs the only land-based product growing (+2.6%); bingo in structural decline (-10.7%).*

### Casino GGR trend

**Casino GGR was R16.50bn (~$1.00bn) on R280.5bn (~$17.0bn) turnover, down 0.9%, but the trend turned to growth in H2.**

- Hold 5.9%, RTP 94.1%; quarterly GGR -3.6% and -6.8% YoY in Q1/Q2, then +0.8% in Q3 and +6.7% in Q4 (R4.27bn).
- Sun International interim to June 2026 corroborates: land-based casino income +1.5% to R3.4bn (~$206m) with group land-based share up 2.3pp to 49.0%.
- Casino share of national GGR has fallen from 81% in FY2011/12 to 19.5%.
- Statutory cap of 40 licences (36 operating; Morula, Carousel, Naledi, Mayfair closed) means no capacity growth; positions fell to 24,246 from 28,362, slots 20,981, tables 865.
- Casino taxes R1.66bn (~$100m), -3.5%, effective 10.0% (Gauteng 9% flat; Western Cape 8–19% progressive, KZN comparatively heavy).

> “heavy bonusing and incentive spending” — Sun International

### Casino concentration

**Three groups own 34 of 36 casinos and Gauteng produces 43.8% of casino GGR; entry is only via supply/platform relationships or LPM route operation.**

- Gauteng R7.22bn (~$438m, -3.5%): Montecasino, Gold Reef City, Silverstar, Emerald (Tsogo Sun); Time Square, Carnival City (Sun International); Emperors Palace (Peermont).
- KZN R3.87bn (~$235m, +9.0%) with a R1.28bn Q4 spike; Western Cape R2.56bn (~$155m, -5.0%); Eastern Cape R1.04bn (-5.4%).
- NGB lists Tsogo Sun 15 casinos, Sun International 11, Peermont 8, two independents (Desert Palace, Queens).
- Casinos are the most transformed segment (average B-BBEE level 1.6).

### LPMs

**LPMs are the only growing land-based product: GGR R4.26bn (~$258m, +2.6%) across 15,988 active machines (+1.6%) and 2,594 site operators.**

- RTP 92.3%; Western Cape (R909m), KZN (R896m) and Gauteng (R878m) each over a fifth of the total.
- NCEMS captures every machine event (99.87% availability in FY25/26); Type B site applications streamlined.
- LPM taxes R543m (~$33m), highest effective rate of any mode at 12.7% (Gauteng 15%; WC 10–20%).
- NGB reported it 'facilitated' R4.2bn of LPM revenue against a R2.5bn target; growth is politically welcome as monitored, local and job-creating.

### Bingo decline

**Bingo GGR fell 10.7% to R1.54bn (~$94m) after -8.6% the prior year, a structural decline against LPMs and mobile bookmaking.**

- Outlets fell from 73 to 62 and EBTs from 8,868 to 8,413.
- Play confined to Gauteng (R477m), KZN (R348m), Eastern Cape (R345m), North West, Mpumalanga and Limpopo.
- Taxes R132m (~$8m), 8.5% effective.
- EBTs compete with LPMs and mobile bookmaking for the same lower-stake customer and are losing on convenience.

### Commercial implication

**Land-based GGR of R22.3bn (~$1.35bn) is a mature, consolidated, capex-heavy pool with no licence headroom, with owners redeploying capital into digital.**

- Owners are investing in SunBet and Tsogo's online arm and in bonusing to defend share.
- A national online tax exempting land-based play would widen the fiscal gap; Treasury's paper argues casinos deserve lower rates for employment and infrastructure.
- Casino lobby (Casino Association of SA, successful SCA litigant) has shown it will use the courts to defend the perimeter.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Licensing, legal framework & tax

*Nine provincial licensing authorities under a 2004 national Act; online casino prohibited (s11); SCA and NRCS have narrowed bookmaker product scope; Treasury's 20% national online GGR tax lands in the Feb-2027 Budget.*

### Regulatory structure

**Nine provincial licensing authorities hold exclusive licensing jurisdiction under the National Gambling Act 7 of 2004; the NGB licenses no operators.**

- Gambling is a concurrent national-provincial competence (Schedule 4); lotteries and sports pools are national under the Lotteries Act 1997.
- PLAs: Eastern Cape, Free State, Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, Mpumalanga, Northern Cape, North West and Western Cape boards/authorities.
- Licences cover casino, bookmaker, totalisator, route operator and LPM site, bingo, manufacturer/supplier/maintenance, key employee.
- NGB–PLA disputes go to the National Gambling Policy Council (ss62–63), where the current s11 'interactive gambling' dispute sits.
- 2008 Amendment Act giving NGB interactive licence jurisdiction was never proclaimed; later Amendment Bill is stalled.

### Online product scope

**Online scope is defined by the bookmaker licence; interactive casino games are prohibited by s11 and the SCA/NRCS have narrowed bookmaker products.**

- SCA in Portapa t/a Supabets v CASA ([2025] ZASCA 158, 21 Oct 2025) held 'contingencies' under the Gauteng Gambling Act do not include roulette.
- NGB advised that Remote Gaming Server architectures facilitate unlawful interactive gambling; NRCS stopped SANS 1718 certification of RGS products, existing Letters lapsing on expiry.
- Provincial technical approval of new products takes three to twelve months (Daily Maverick).
- DA's Remote Gambling Bill (B11-2024) introduced April 2024, lapsed with the sixth Parliament, unlikely to be enacted before 2027.

### Licensing barriers

**Licensing cost is not the barrier; probity, transformation and time are.**

- Trade press cites Western Cape bookmaker fees of R15,000 (~$910) application, R3,000 (~$180) annual, R12,000 (~$730) probity; other provinces R10,000–20,000 — unverified against gazetted tariffs.
- No statutory local-ownership cap, but PLAs score on B-BBEE codes (casinos 1.6, LPM 2.4, bingo 3.1, totes 6.4).
- Mpumalanga and Western Cape collected R1.94bn (~$118m) and R1.86bn (~$113m) of tax (30% and 29% of national); EC, NW, NC betting GGR grew 676%, 205%, 66%.

> “don't come to South Africa just looking for a licence” — Mpumalanga regulator

### Provincial tax regime

**Provinces tax GGR at varying rates; FY25/26 effective rates were betting 6.6%, casinos 10.0%, LPMs 12.7%, bingo 8.5%, blended 7.6%.**

- Gauteng (GN 570 of 1997): bookmakers 6.5% + 1% Sports Development Fund levy, horse-racing 6% + 6%, totalisators 9% + 8.5%, casinos 9%, bingo 12%, route operators 15%.
- Western Cape progressive: casinos 8% (<R14.2m) to 19% (>R71m), LPMs 10–20%, bingo 10–15%.
- On top: 15% VAT (~12% of GGR VAT-inclusive per industry) and 27% corporate income tax; winnings untaxed unless habitual income.
- 2011 Budget's 15% withholding tax on wins above R25,000 and 2012's 1% national levy were abandoned.
- Provinces rely on national transfers for ~97% of revenue, aligning PLAs with licensee growth (Daily Maverick).

### National online tax

**Treasury's proposed 20% national online GGR tax, worth 'over R10 billion' (~$606m), will be finalised in the February 2027 Budget.**

- November 2025 paper proposes 20% on online GGR including interactive gambling, additional to provincial taxes, SARS-administered, applying to unlicensed revenue; combined 26–29% of GGR.
- Stated objective is harm reduction; comments closed January, extended to February 2026; Treasury met provincial regulators and ~200 industry participants in mid-November 2026.
- Industry (SA Bookmakers' Association, Whitesmans, Lurie Inc, Free Market Foundation) argues all-in burden near 40%, offshore leakage, provincial erosion and smaller-licensee bankruptcy; DA reportedly opposed.
- Money bill process makes an effective date before 1 April 2027 implausible; slippage into FY2027/28 or rate dilution is realistic.
- Treasury concedes the Kenya precedent of operators relocating offshore when rates rose.

> “generally recognised the need to address the growth of online gambling” — Finance Minister

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

*Bank-rail market (cards, instant EFT, vouchers; 91% mobile) with exchange-control and s16 forfeiture levers against offshore play; RG architecture incomplete (no self-exclusion register); advertising norms promised for July 2026 still unpublished amid ban petitions.*

### Payments and enforcement levers

**South Africa is a bank-rail market (cards, instant EFT, vouchers) where the state's levers against offshore play are financial rather than technical.**

- Unlicensed platforms accept rands, local cards and digital vouchers, marketing via influencers and sponsorships (Daily Maverick).
- Exchange-control rules oblige Authorised Dealers to decline foreign-currency purchases for offshore gambling and bar debit/credit card use.
- s16 forfeitures: approximately R775,000 (~$47k) in FY25/26 and about R3m (~$182k) cumulatively, including R2.3m early FY26/27.
- NGB pursuing MOUs with SABRIC and SARB; FIC (AML/CFT supervisor since December 2022) has MOUs with NGB and all nine PLAs.
- Expect bank-side transaction monitoring for gambling MCCs to tighten as blocking matures.

### Responsible gambling

**RG infrastructure is thinner than the market warrants: the National Self-Exclusion Register is still 'inoperative' and there are no statutory deposit or affordability limits.**

- NRGF logged 1.26m incoming calls in FY25/26, 5,443 treatment-related, 3,541 treated — measuring response capacity rather than prevalence.
- NGB cites 'marked escalation' among NSFAS and SASSA recipients; StatsSA puts gambling at 54.5% of household recreation, sport and culture spend.
- Exclusion remains operator-by-operator; mandatory RG messaging already required under reg. 3.
- Plan for a national exclusion feed and possibly stake/deposit controls once the Amendment Bill or advertising regulations move.

### Advertising

**Advertising norms promised before July 2026 remain unpublished amid ban petitions; watershed and sponsorship restrictions are likelier than an outright ban.**

- s15 NGA and regulation 3 prohibit ads aimed at minors, misleading claims and ads without RG warnings; NGB noted a 'notable increase' in advertising in FY25/26.
- By mid-August 2026 civil-society groups petitioned that nothing had appeared; amandla.mobi's tobacco-style ban petition remains active.
- Communications Minister Malatsi is testing an Icasa route lawyers argue cannot create powers Parliament has not granted.
- Football title sponsorships, the duopoly's main brand channel, are the obvious target.

> “as well as gambling advertising regulations before July 2026” — dtic

### Implications for B2Tech

**Payments reality is positive, but RG and advertising direction raises acquisition cost and narrows channels just as tax compresses margin.**

- Instant settlement, low fraud, card and voucher ubiquity, no telco revenue share.
- Building RG above current law is a licence-defensibility investment, not a cost line, given the Mpumalanga regulator's 'earn the trust' posture.
- Design for interoperable self-exclusion, NSFAS/SASSA pay-cycle affordability signals and PLA-reviewable ad compliance.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Enforcement, illegal market & political direction

*NGB has blocked 27 sites and run 35 raids, but admits it has no blocking architecture; a monitoring/blocking tender closed 4 Sep 2026; estimates put ~62% of online activity offshore. Politics point to more tax, less advertising, and no online-casino legalisation before 2027.*

### NGB enforcement

**NGB ran 35 interventions and restricted 27 illegal sites in FY25/26 but admits it has no blocking architecture; a tender closed 4 September 2026.**

- 32 interventions the prior year including 22 raids and 10 warning notices; targeted investigations closed within five days against a 25-day standard.
- Unlawful-winnings remittances from banks fell; effective action requires NGB–ISP–bank collaboration.
- EOI (ref. 01/2026_ED) issued 30 June 2026 for a monitoring, tracking, blocking and reporting solution, amended 17 July.
- Vendor appointment and the legal basis for ISP-level blocking are the next milestones.

> “no operational, state-level IP or website-blocking architecture” — NGB

### Illegal market scale

**Estimates put ~62% of online activity on unlicensed sites, with more than R50bn (~$3.0bn) GGR diverted offshore; figures are directional, not regulator data.**

- Yield Sec (industry-commissioned) estimates about 16 million users; CGCSA estimates ~R7.7bn (~$467m) direct tax loss and 30,000+ displaced jobs.
- Mechanics: offshore servers, Curaçao licences, rand pricing, local cards and vouchers, influencer marketing.
- Structural driver is the s11 prohibition: the only supplier of RNG and live-dealer product to South Africans is unlicensed.
- SCA ruling and NRCS directive arguably increase offshore appeal unless blocking and payment interdiction arrive quickly.

### Political direction

**Government direction is consistent but uncoordinated: more tax, less advertising, no online-casino legalisation before 2027.**

- Treasury (Christopher Axelson) frames the 20% online GGR tax as a sin tax for the February 2027 Budget.
- dtic (Minister Parks Tau) drafts advertising norms; NGB prosecutes the s11 line and procures blocking; Communications Minister tests an Icasa route.
- DA sponsors the Remote Gambling Bill yet opposes the 20% tax; provinces collecting R6.45bn (~$391m) counterweight both levy and ad curbs.
- Courts are active: casino lobby won at the SCA; Sizekhaya lottery licence set aside by Pretoria High Court on 3 November 2026.

### Operator outlook

**Expect a tighter perimeter, fiscal load rising from ~18–20% to a plausible 30–40% of GGR from FY2027/28, and narrower marketing.**

- Tax and ad rules are 2027 events with high probability; blocking and online-casino legalisation are 2027–2028 events with lower probability.
- Better-protected licensed demand only if blocking and payment interdiction work.
- Build the market-entry case on sports-betting economics under the higher tax scenario, valuing the online-casino option at zero until a national remote licence exists.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale and momentum: Africa's largest regulated market at R84.46bn (~$5.12bn) GGR, with disclosed online betting GGR of R55.77bn (~$3.38bn) growing 25.4% and betting stakes of R1.40tn (~$84.9bn) up 24.5% — a legal, bankable online sports-betting pool larger than most European mid-tier markets. _(structural)_
- Low headline fiscal load today: provincial bookmaker tax of ~6.5% of GGR (+1% levy) plus 15% VAT — an effective 6.6% provincial take in FY25/26 and roughly 18–20% all-in — with no player-winnings withholding, versus 20–40%+ in many benchmark jurisdictions. _(structural)_
- Payments and distribution are ready-made: 91% of digital wagering is mobile, settlement runs on cards, instant EFT and retail vouchers with no telco revenue share, and 315 licensed bookmakers show the licence is obtainable — Western Cape and Mpumalanga process quickly at immaterial fees (R15,000/~$910 application, trade-press figure). _(structural)_
- Provincial licensing arbitrage is real and legal: a licence from any province serves punters nationwide; Eastern Cape (+676%) and North West (+205%) betting GGR show new domiciles opening up, and effective betting tax ranges from 5.0% (NW) to 10.5% (KZN). _(structural)_
- Enforcement is tilting toward the licensed channel: SCA/NRCS have levelled the product perimeter across licensed books, 27 illegal sites have been restricted, a national monitoring/blocking tender closed 4 Sep 2026, and FIC/SABRIC/SARB payment cooperation is being formalised — if delivered, licensed demand recaptures part of the estimated R50bn (~$3.0bn) offshore pool. _(early)_
- B2B demand from incumbents: casino groups are redeploying into digital (SunBet income +35.5% to R1.18bn/~$72m in H1-2026 vs market ~19%; Sun land-based share 49%), and a fragmented long tail of ~310 smaller bookmakers facing a possible tax doubling will need platform, trading and compliance outsourcing or exits. _(early)_
- Horse racing is an unexpected growth niche: bookmaker horse-racing GGR +36.9% to R4.81bn (~$292m), concentrated in the Western Cape, at a 9.2% effective tax — a differentiated vertical where the duopoly is less dominant. _(mixed)_

**Lowlights**

- Tax cliff: Treasury's 20% national online GGR tax (additional to provincial tax and VAT) would lift the all-in burden from ~18–20% to ~38–40% of GGR, equivalent to >R10bn (~$606m) p.a.; final design in the Feb-2027 Budget. Even a diluted 10–15% version would compress bookmaker margins by 10–15pp on a ~4% hold product. _(structural)_
- No online casino, and the workaround is gone: s11 prohibits interactive gambling, the SCA (21 Oct 2025) barred fixed-odds casino games for bookmakers, and NRCS stopped certifying Remote Gaming Servers. Gauteng betting GGR fell 19.2% and its RTP dropped from 88% to 61% — a preview for any province where 'online gaming' products are challenged. No remote-gambling legislation before 2027. _(structural)_
- Entrenched duopoly: Betway and Hollywoodbets held >75% of online demand (Blask, Mar-2025), with Betway still gaining share; the top three brands take ~50% of 380m quarterly site visits; both leaders own football title sponsorships, the marketing channel most exposed to the coming advertising rules. _(structural)_
- Illegal market the size of the legal one: industry estimates put ~62% of online activity offshore, >R50bn (~$3.0bn) GGR and ~16m users, with the NGB admitting it has no blocking architecture; a higher tax on the licensed channel widens the price gap with untaxed offshore sites. _(structural)_
- Advertising and RG tightening with unclear scope: dtic's national advertising norms/regulations (promised before July 2026) are overdue, a ban-the-ads/sponsorships campaign is active, the National Self-Exclusion Register is still inoperative and the NGB is publicly flagging NSFAS and SASSA recipients — affordability-style controls could follow. _(watch)_
- Regulatory fragmentation and governance: nine PLAs with divergent rules, an open NGB–PLA s11 dispute before the Policy Council, the NGB completing only 9 of 45 planned PLA compliance evaluations, Gauteng statistics provisional/unaudited, and online/retail splits for six provinces only — material data and legal-certainty gaps for underwriting. _(governance)_
- Growth is decelerating: betting GGR growth has stepped down from +51.2% to +44.7% to +19.6%, Q3 FY25/26 was +13.8%, and hold is drifting lower (RTP 95.6%); entrants are arriving as the market matures and a Treasury-designed 'sin tax' is explicitly intended to reduce volumes. _(mixed)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The primary release (NGB audited statistics, 3 Nov 2026) shows a regulated base still compounding at +13% while the rule-set is rewritten around it. The bridge from FY2025/26 to our FY2026/27 outlook is driven by one division — online sports betting in the Mpumalanga and Western Cape licence hubs — and by three regulatory moving parts: the Feb-2027 Budget decision on the 20% national online GGR tax (effective no earlier than FY2027/28 given money-bill procedure), the overdue dtic advertising norms and regulations, and the NGB's blocking-vendor appointment. Nothing in the pipeline legalises online casino before 2027, and the SCA/NRCS actions remove the grey-product tailwind that flattered FY24/25.

- **FY2025/26 GGR (NGB audited):** R84.46bn (~$5.12bn) (+13.4% YoY)
- **Online/retail betting FY26/27E (+15–19%; estimate):** +R9–12bn (~$0.55–0.73bn) (Sports-led; no casino-style content; Q3 base effect)
- **Land-based (casino, LPM, bingo) FY26/27E (estimate):** +R0–0.4bn (~$0–24m) (Casino flat to +2%; LPM +2–3%; bingo -8–10%)
- **FY2026/27E GGR (estimate):** R93–96bn (~$5.6–5.8bn) (+10–14%; tax not yet in base)

Our independent view: regulated GGR of **R93–96bn (~$5.6–5.8bn) in FY2026/27 (estimate)**, with betting above 76% of the total and disclosed online above 70%, then a step-down in FY2027/28 if the national tax is enacted at or near 20% (we would model licensed betting GGR growth falling to low single digits and 20–30% of the 315 bookmakers exiting or consolidating). On the tax itself, our base case is that **a national online levy is enacted but below the 20% headline or phased** — the money-bill route, DA opposition inside the GNU, provincial revenue dependence and Treasury's own Kenya caveat all argue for dilution; we put the probability of 20% as proposed, effective 1 April 2027, at roughly one in three [judgement]. All-in operator burden moves from ~18–20% of GGR to **30–40%** in the adverse case. Advertising: **norms and standards gazetted by H1-2027 with watershed, sponsorship-disclosure and bonus-advertising limits rather than a ban**. Online casino: **no lawful product before 2028**; the Remote Gambling Bill will not pass in 2027 and the NGB's s11 posture, now backed by the SCA, will be extended to lucky-numbers and virtual products in other provinces via the Policy Council. Enforcement: a blocking vendor appointed in 2027 with ISP-level blocking contested in court; payment interdiction via FIC/SABRIC is the more effective lever. Leverage direction for the market is **toward consolidation**: fee-light licensing keeps entry cheap, but a ~4% hold product facing a possible 20pp tax increase and tighter marketing favours scale players (Betway, Hollywoodbets, SunBet) and B2B suppliers over new B2C brands.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Feb-2027 Budget: rate, base (online betting only vs all online incl. lucky numbers/virtuals), effective date, SARS administration and interaction with provincial taxes; then passage through the NA and NCOP finance committees as a money bill.
- dtic national advertising norms & standards and gambling advertising regulations (promised before Jul-2026, still pending): watershed, sports-sponsorship, influencer and bonus-ad rules; any Icasa/ECA-based measures and legal challenges.
- NGB monitoring/blocking vendor appointment following the EOI that closed 4 Sep 2026; MOUs with SABRIC/SARB; bank card-blocking of offshore gambling MCCs.
- National Gambling Policy Council determination on the NGB–PLA s11 dispute and any follow-on litigation extending the SCA logic to lucky numbers, virtuals and 'betgames' outside Gauteng; NRCS treatment of existing RGS certifications on expiry.
- Lottery: appeal or re-tender after the 3 Nov 2026 High Court ruling setting aside Sizekhaya's licence; interim operator arrangements and any read-across to NLC/dtic licensing credibility.
- Quarterly NGB data: audited Gauteng restatement, extension of the online/retail split to Gauteng, KZN and Free State, and FY26/27 H1 betting growth versus the +23% comparatives (deceleration below +15% would confirm maturity).
- Operator moves: Betway/Hollywoodbets share, SunBet and Tsogo digital growth, mid-tier M&A (Supabets, WSB, LottoStar, YesPlay), and any licence migration into Eastern Cape/North West; Remote Gambling Bill and National Gambling Amendment Bill scheduling; activation of the National Self-Exclusion Register.
