Washington DC

Released 20 Nov 2026 (OLG October 2026 unaudited sports wagering financials); trade coverage 25 Nov 2026 · Currency USD (market reports natively in US dollars; tables in $ millions unless stated) · Data confidence: HIGH

The one-line verdict

October 2026 was the District's largest month on record by handle ($95.76m) but growth has stalled at +0.9% YoY, hold is flat at 8.8%, and tax take fell 1.7% as volume shifted toward the 20%-taxed venue books. The bigger story is regulatory: B26-0656 (online casino at 25% GGR plus 4% of assessments, sweepstakes folded into licensing, gambling age to 21) cleared committee 3-1 on 18 Nov and must survive two full-Council readings before the Council period closes in mid-December. For a would-be entrant, DC is a ~$90m-GGR, seven-brand, FanDuel-dominated market where the 30% Class C tax and $2m licence make a mobile-only sportsbook entry marginal; the credible angles are B2B supply or an iGaming licence if the bill lands.

1Executive summary

  • Record handle, no growth: OLG's unaudited October 2026 report shows District-wide sports wagering handle of $95.76m across 2.95m wagers, the highest month since launch but only +0.9% YoY (Oct-25: $94.89m) and +19.3% MoM on the NFL/NBA calendar. GGR was $8.46m (+1.1% YoY, +51.8% MoM) at an 8.83% hold (Oct-25: 8.81%; Sep-26: 6.94%). Tax collected was $1.94m, −1.7% YoY, because growth came from Class A venue books taxed at 20% rather than Class C mobile-only books taxed at 30%.
  • CY2026 on course for a record on handle, not on GGR: January–October handle is $724.05m (+3.8% YoY) but GGR is $70.16m at a 9.69% hold versus 11.1% for CY2025 (handle $855.6m, GGR $94.4m, tax $22.06m). The District needs $131.6m in Nov–Dec to beat 2025's handle (Nov–Dec 2025 delivered $158.3m); on our estimate CY2026 ends at ~$885–890m handle but only ~$86–88m GGR and ~$21m tax, both below 2025 (estimate).
  • FY2026 (Oct-25 to Sep-26) closed strongly: summing OLG's per-operator fiscal-year totals gives handle $881.45m (+9.9% vs FY2025's $801.9m), GGR $91.50m (10.4% hold) and tax $21.97m (+13.3% vs $19.39m), a blended 24.0% effective rate on GGR. October is month one of FY2027.
  • A mobile market with a venue-licensing skin: OLG's FY2025 report puts online handle at $773.2m (96.4%) versus $28.7m retail (3.6%); online generated 96% of sports-wagering tax. Monthly OLG reports do not split Class A operators' retail and mobile volumes, so the retail share is a fiscal-year figure only.
  • Regulator and law: the Office of Lottery and Gaming (OLG), an agency under the DC Office of the Chief Financial Officer, is both lottery operator and sports-wagering regulator. The framework is the Sports Wagering Lottery Amendment Act of 2018 (DC Code § 36-621.01 et seq.) and 30 DCMR Chapter 21, overhauled by the Sports Wagering Amendment Act of 2024 (effective 15 Jul 2024), which replaced the District-operated GambetDC monopoly with private District-wide mobile licences.
  • Three licence classes, all five-year terms (DC Code § 36-621.06; OLG Licensing page): Class A for designated venues (Capital One Arena, Audi Field, Nationals Park, St. Elizabeths East arena) at $1.0m initial / $0.5m renewal with District-wide mobile rights; Class B for retail-only venues outside a two-block radius of a Class A facility at $100k / $50k; Class C for team-linked mobile-only operators at $2.0m / $1.0m. CBE-majority joint ventures pay $250k (A) or $25k (B). Licences are non-transferable; provisional licences run six months.
  • Tax is on GGR, graded by class, no loss carry-forward observed: Class A 20%, Class B 10%, Class C 30% of gross sports wagering revenue, filed monthly on form FR-426G by the 20th. Fanatics booked negative GGR (−$48k) in September and paid tax of exactly 30.0% of its positive October GGR, i.e. no offset of prior-month losses. The blended rate was 22.9% in October and 24.0% CY-to-date.
  • Seven live brands, two of them are the market: FanDuel (Audi Field, Class A) took 44.8% of October handle ($42.95m) and 59.5% of GGR ($5.03m) on an 11.72% hold; DraftKings (Class C) 27.7% / 19.7% on 6.26%; Caesars (Capital One Arena) 10.3% / 4.7% on 4.04%; Fanatics 9.3% / 7.2%; BetMGM (Nationals Park) 6.2% / 6.8%; bet365 1.7% / 2.2% in its first full month; Sports & Social (U Street, Class B) $21.5k handle. Top-two share: 72.6% of handle, 79.2% of GGR. FanDuel alone paid 52% of October tax.
  • Churn at the margin: PENN's theScore Bet (ex-ESPN Bet, launched Jan 2025) ceased DC operations on 23 Feb 2026 after 13 months; bet365 (Hillside LLC) launched 8 Sep 2026 with a Monumental Sports & Entertainment partnership (Capitals, Wizards, Mystics) and reached $1.59m handle at 11.56% hold in October. Four Class B venues listed in September (Grand Central, Cloakbook, Grand Central H Street, Ugly Mug) reported zero activity and were dropped from the October report.
  • Sharp-money tell in the venue books: Caesars' October bet count fell 75.7% YoY while handle rose 21% to $9.82m, pushing average stake to $103 and hold to 4.0% (11.1% a year ago; GGR −56%). DraftKings' bet count fell 55% while average stake doubled to $37.45 and hold compressed to 6.3%. FanDuel's 11.7% hold on a $23.77 average stake is the recreational benchmark the others are not matching.
  • Lottery is the other half of OLG's book and it is shrinking: FY2025 lottery operating revenue was $210.7m, net revenue $83.4m, and the General Fund transfer $32.5m — down 17.4% from $39.35m in FY2024 per the DC OIG-commissioned audit. Adding $19.3m of sports-wagering tax, total regulated-gaming transfers were $51.8m, the highest since FY2017. Cumulative lottery contributions since 1982: $2.43bn.
  • iGaming is one vote series from the finish line: B26-0656, the Internet Gaming and Consumer Protection Act of 2026 (CM Wendell Felder, introduced 9 Apr 2026, hearing 4 May), passed the Committee on Human Services 3-1 on 18 Nov 2026 (CM Henderson against). Committee print: 25% tax on adjusted gross internet gaming revenue, 12.5% on live-dealer, plus 2% community-impact and 2% regulatory assessments; $2m application fee and $1m five-year renewal; maximum two brands per licensee; in-District live-dealer studio with a labor-peace agreement; supplier licence $50k / $25k. It needs two full-Council readings before the Council period ends in mid-December, then Mayoral signature and the 30-day Congressional review.
  • The committee rewrote two things that matter: sweepstakes/dual-currency casinos would be licensed and taxed as iGaming rather than banned outright, and the minimum age for all non-lottery gambling (including sports betting) would rise from 18 to 21. The CFO's fiscal note scores the bill at +$62.6m net revenue over FY2027–30 ($47.3m gaming tax), net of a $3.4m lottery-earnings loss and $1.8m of lost sports-wagering tax from the age change.
  • Prediction markets are an untaxed overhang: as of 25 Nov 2026 all Kalshi and Polymarket markets, including sports event contracts, are live in DC — the CFTC's home jurisdiction — with no District enforcement action identified, in contrast to Washington State's court-ordered halt. DFS also operates without a DC-specific geofence. Neither pays the 20–30% GGR tax.
  • Payments are card/ACH-based and permissive: DC imposes no credit-card funding ban (CASPR scorecard), no deposit-to-bet waiting period and no in-play restrictions; operators must verify identity and location, with new customers allowed to deposit and wager for 30 days before completing verification (or before first withdrawal). Statutory minimum age is 18; BetMGM and Caesars self-impose 21.
  • Responsible gambling and advertising are rule-based but light: 30 DCMR §§ 2128–2131 require an OLG-approved responsible-gaming plan, helpline information on all advertising, balanced win/lose messaging and an OLG-licensed statement; Class B operators may not place physical advertising within two blocks of a Class A venue; a single District-wide self-exclusion list covers lottery, iLottery, retail and mobile sports betting with terms from 18 months to lifetime (secondary source). CASPR grades DC 'F' on consumer-protection breadth.
  • Political direction is expansion with a higher consumer-protection floor, not tax hikes: the Council moved from a protectionist lottery monopoly (2019–24) to an open, high-tax private model (2024), and is now pushing iGaming plus sweepstakes regulation plus age 21. No pending change to sports-wagering tax rates or fees was found. OLG's Executive Director (Thomas 'Randy' Burnside) has backed iGaming despite acknowledged iLottery cannibalisation.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: at October run-rates a 5% handle share equals ~$57m annual handle, ~$5.5m GGR at the market's 9.7% YTD hold and ~$3.9m after 30% Class C tax — before marketing, the $2m licence and a mandatory team tie-up (estimate). A direct mobile sportsbook entry does not clear a sensible hurdle rate. The actionable options are (i) B2B platform/managed-services to a Class A/B venue or a Class C team partner, (ii) an iGaming operator or $50k supplier licence if B26-0656 passes (two brands per licence, preference language for existing sports licensees in the bill as introduced), and (iii) using DC as a low-cost regulatory reference licence rather than a P&L market.

3Deep-dive: Sports betting — market structure, operators and the October 2026 result

A record $95.8m month that masks flat growth, a 9.7% YTD hold and a two-operator market in which FanDuel's venue licence delivers 60% of GGR at a 20% tax rate.

October market result

Record $95.76m October handle masks flat growth: +0.9% handle, +1.1% GGR and −1.7% tax YoY as the market plateaus.

  • OLG unaudited October 2026 filing (posted 20 Nov 2026): 2,952,180 wagers, handle $95,757,609, payouts $87,300,236, GGR $8,457,374, tax $1,936,511 across seven licensees.
  • October 2025 comparables: handle $94.89m, GGR $8.36m, tax $1.97m (Sports Handle and Covers series differ by under $1k).
  • Two-year growth versus October 2024 handle of $65.8m is +45.5%; the 2024–25 step-change followed District-wide mobile opening to private operators.
  • Month-on-month jump is seasonal: September handle $80.24m at 6.94% hold; October added NBA and NHL tip-off to a full NFL slate, hold normalised to 8.83%.

FanDuel

FanDuel, on the Audi Field Class A licence, is the market: 44.8% of handle and 59.5% of GGR in October at a 20% tax rate.

  • October handle fell 4.9% YoY to $42.95m but hold of 11.72% (Oct-25: 8.8%) lifted GGR 26% to $5.03m; $1.01m tax was 52% of the District's take.
  • Year-to-date: 41.8% of handle, 55.5% of GGR on a 12.84% hold, 12.85m wagers at average stake $23.58, the lowest stake and highest hold of any operator.
  • A recreational, parlay-heavy book whose position is structural, not cyclical.
  • Inherited the OLG customer base when it replaced GambetDC as lottery subcontractor in April 2024, bringing back more than 22,000 active users from Maryland and Virginia, before converting to Class A under the 2024 Act.

Class C mobile tier

The three Class C books held 38.7% of October handle but only 29.0% of GGR, and 30% tax on sub-7% holds drove PENN's exit.

  • DraftKings: $26.55m handle (27.7% share, +2.9% YoY), wagers down 55.1% to 708,912, average stake up from $16.34 to $37.45, hold down from 8.5% to 6.26%, GGR down 24.5% to $1.66m, tax $0.50m.
  • Fanatics: $8.93m (9.3%) at 6.79% hold for $0.61m GGR, recovering from negative September GGR of −$48k.
  • bet365 (live 8 Sep 2026 under Hillside LLC): $1.59m handle, $183k GGR at 11.56% hold in first full month, 1.7% of handle but 2.2% of GGR.
  • Class C paid 38.0% of October tax; year-to-date the tier is 40.8% of handle, 33.7% of GGR and 44.2% of tax.
  • PENN withdrew theScore Bet on 23 Feb 2026 after $2.2m of CY2026 handle; the tier's economics are the key input to any entry decision.

Caesars and BetMGM

The other two venue books show the Class A model's limits when the venue partner is not the market leader.

  • Caesars at Capital One Arena: $9.82m handle (+21% YoY) from only 94,890 bets (−75.7%), average stake $103.44, 4.04% hold, GGR down 56% to $396k.
  • Caesars CY2026 hold is 4.25% on $64.7m handle, which looks like a small number of large, well-informed accounts rather than a mass-market book.
  • BetMGM at Nationals Park: $5.91m at 9.74% hold for $576k GGR; CY2026 hold 8.03% on $60.2m.
  • Both pay 20% but lack retail footfall or brand pull to convert the venue licence into District-wide share.
  • Caesars' arena landlord, Monumental Sports & Entertainment, has made bet365 its teams' official betting partner.

Concentration and dynamics

Top two operators hold 72.6% of handle and 79.2% of GGR; share is available from soft-holding incumbents but must be won on pricing and product.

  • Caesars, Fanatics and BetMGM together sum to 25.8% of handle and 18.7% of GGR.
  • Per-resident intensity is high: CY2025 handle of $855.6m against roughly 0.7m residents is ~$1,200 per head (analyst estimate; daytime and visitor population inflates this).
  • Average stake rose from $26.70 (FY2026) to $32.44 (October), consistent with fewer, larger wagers rather than broader participation.
  • An entrant competes at 30% tax against a leader whose 12–13% hold funds promotional reinvestment; Caesars and DraftKings are the soft-holding incumbents.

Seasonality and fiscal calendar

CY2026 GGR is estimated at $86–88m, below 2025 despite record handle, with tax of ~$21m versus $22.06m in CY2025.

  • OLG fiscal year runs October to September; FY2026 handle of $881.45m produced $21.97m of tax at a 24.0% blended rate and 10.38% hold.
  • November 2025 set the GGR record: $11.7m at 13.16% hold on $89.0m handle.
  • Estimate assumes hold reverts to 10–11% in Nov–Dec 2026 and tax at ~24% of GGR.

4Deep-dive: Licensing & tax regime

A three-class, five-year licence system with GGR taxed at 20/10/30% and a $2m ticket for mobile-only entry — expensive by US standards and tied to venues or teams.

Legal framework

The 2024 Act opened District-wide mobile to Class A at 20%, created Class C at 30%, and FY2026 tax of $21.97m is up 13% on FY2025.

  • Authorised by the Sports Wagering Lottery Amendment Act of 2018 (DC Code § 36-621.01 et seq.), rules in 30 DCMR Chapter 21; OLG sits within the Office of the Chief Financial Officer.
  • Original 2019–24 design gave OLG's GambetDC app (built by Intralot) a District-wide monopoly; private Class A confined to two-block zones and paid 10% of GGR.
  • Sports Wagering Amendment Act of 2024 (FY2025 Budget Support Act, effective 15 Jul 2024) raised Class A tax to 20%, created 30% Class C, doubled Class A application fee from $500k to $1m.
  • OLG testimony on Bill 25-0753 argued 20%/30% was below the ~40% retained from GambetDC and revenue would fall unless handle grew; in the event FY2026 tax is comfortably above the FY2024 base.

Fee schedule

Five-year licences cost $1m (Class A), $100k (Class B) or $2m (Class C), are non-transferable and tied to venues or teams.

  • Class A: designated facilities only (Capital One Arena, Audi Field, Nationals Park, St. Elizabeths East arena), $1,000,000 non-refundable fee, $500,000 renewal, one facility per licence, District-wide mobile.
  • Class B: retail outside two-block radius of any Class A facility, $100,000 fee, $50,000 renewal, retail only.
  • Class C: designated professional sports teams or their assigned MSP/operator, District-wide mobile, $2,000,000 fee, renewal reported at $1,000,000; first three Class C fees generated $6m for the General Fund.
  • Certified Business Enterprise majority joint ventures pay $250k (Class A) and $25k (Class B); OLG may add a processing fee equal to projected background investigation cost.
  • Provisional licences for applicants licensed elsewhere are valid six months while the four-to-six-month standard application runs; prerequisites include DC Basic Business License, Certificate of Clean Hands and surety bond (e.g. $1.5m on $10m five-year GGR at 30%).

Tax mechanics

GGR is taxed at 20/10/30% by class, with no visible promo deduction or loss carry-forward and a blended rate of 24.0% in FY2026.

  • Gross Sports Wagering Revenue is cash received less cash paid to players and annuity purchases; monthly return FR-426G filed with OTR by the 20th.
  • Promotional credit deductibility is not stated in OLG materials [unverified]; Class A tax equals exactly 20.0% of reported GGR for every operator, implying no material deduction.
  • Fanatics' negative September GGR produced zero tax and October tax was exactly 30.0% of October GGR, indicating no loss carry-forward.
  • Blended effective rate was 22.9% in October and 24.0% for CY2026 YTD and FY2026; it drifts down as FanDuel's share grows and up if Class C gains share.

Commercial comparison

DC is one of the most expensive small US markets for mobile-only entry; Class A is cheaper but capacity is physically limited.

  • The $2m Class C fee compares with Massachusetts' $5m per five years for a market with roughly a tenth of the population.
  • The 30% rate is matched only by Pennsylvania (36%), New York (51%), New Hampshire (51%), Rhode Island (51%) and Illinois' top bracket.
  • Class A at 20% is competitive but limited to four venues, three taken; St. Elizabeths East has no licensee and unproven value.
  • Class C team linkage carries a sponsorship cost — bet365's MSE deal is the template.
  • No statutory cap on Class B retail licences, provisional licences are processed quickly, and the supplier/MSP route is open and cheap relative to operator entry.

5Deep-dive: Retail venues, Class B and the DC Lottery

Retail sports betting is a rounding error (3.6% of FY25 handle) and the lottery's General Fund transfer fell 17% in FY2025 — context for why OLG supports iGaming despite cannibalisation.

Retail share

Retail sports betting is a rounding error: $28.7m (3.6%) of FY2025 handle against $773.2m (96.4%) online.

  • Three retail forms: Class A venue sportsbooks (FanDuel, Caesars, BetMGM), Class B bar/restaurant books, and legacy OLG retail terminals that survived GambetDC's mobile app.
  • Retail contributed 4% of sports-wagering tax ($773k of $19.39m) per OLG's FY2025 Progress Report.
  • Monthly OLG reports do not separate Class A retail from mobile, so there is no October retail figure; Class B is the only pure-retail datapoint.

Class B

Class B has not worked as a business model; the sole active licensee took $21,521 in October.

  • Sports & Social on U Street: 1,176 bets, 7.6% hold, $164 of tax in October and $547k year-to-date.
  • Grand Central (Adams Morgan), Cloakbook (K Street), Grand Central H Street and Ugly Mug (Barracks Row) showed no September activity and were absent from October, read as lapsed, surrendered or suspended [unverified].
  • With District-wide mobile from every major brand, a retail-only licence two blocks from the arenas has no catchment.
  • The cheap $100k entry route is closed off as anything other than a hospitality amenity.

DC Lottery

The lottery's General Fund transfer fell 17.4% to $32.5m in FY2025, while sports wagering now supplies roughly 37% of OLG's fiscal contribution.

  • FY2025 operating revenue $210.7m, net revenue $83.4m; transfer down from $39.35m in FY2024, with the OIG-commissioned audit citing lower draw-game sales.
  • Traditional-game GGR had turned positive in FY2024 for the first time in several years, so FY2025 is a reversal.
  • Adding $19.3m of private sports-wagering tax, total regulated-gaming transfers reached $51.8m, the highest since FY2017, up from zero sports contribution in FY2019.
  • Lifetime the lottery has contributed $2.43bn to the General Fund since 1982 and paid $513m in retailer commissions.

iGaming cannibalisation

OLG will absorb a $3.4m iLottery hit over FY2027–30 for $47.3m of new gaming tax, removing the usual lottery-lobby obstacle.

  • DCiLottery e-instant games are the product line most exposed to regulated online casino.
  • Executive Director Burnside told the Council in May 2026 that iGaming would cut iLottery sales but casino tax would more than offset the loss.
  • The CFO's fiscal note on B26-0656 is the source of the $3.4m and $47.3m figures.
  • A reasonable trade for the District and a positive for prospective iGaming licensees.

6Deep-dive: Regulatory direction — iGaming bill B26-0656, sweepstakes and prediction markets

Online casino at an effective 29% of GGR, sweepstakes licensed rather than banned, and a gambling age of 21 are one Council vote series away; prediction markets remain live and untaxed.

Bill as introduced

B26-0656 proposed 25% tax on adjusted gross internet gaming revenue, a $2m operator fee, age 21 and a sweepstakes ban.

  • Introduced by Ward 7 Councilmember Wendell Felder on 9 Apr 2026; heard 4 May by Committee on Human Services in a near four-hour session.
  • Caesars, DraftKings and BetMGM supported; ARB Interactive/Publishers Clearing House opposed sweepstakes provisions; Stop Predatory Gambling opposed the whole measure.
  • After six months, on 18 Nov 2026 the committee chaired by Matthew Frumin reported it out 3-1, Christina Henderson the lone no.
  • Terms: $500k five-year renewal, $50k supplier fee ($25k renewal), no licence cap, preference for existing DC sports-wagering licensees, first $500k of tax to Department of Behavioral Health.

Committee print changes

The committee print lifts the effective rate on RNG casino to about 29%, licenses sweepstakes rather than banning them, and raises all gambling to age 21.

  • Tax stays 25% but live-dealer revenue taxed at 12.5%; two further 2% assessments (community-impact and regulatory) are layered on; renewal doubles to $1m per five years.
  • Maximum two brands per licensee; live-dealer games must stream from a District studio staffed by licensed dealers under a labor-peace agreement, favouring operators willing to build locally.
  • Sweepstakes and dual-currency products need the same iGaming licence, tax and player-protection rules; OLG and Attorney General get cease-and-desist and enforcement tools.
  • Minimum age for all non-lottery gambling rises to 21, moving sports betting from 18 to 21.
  • CFO scores $246k cost in FY2027 and $62.6m net new revenue over FY2027–30, including $47.3m gaming tax after $3.4m lottery decline and $1.8m lost sports-wagering tax.

Process and timing

Process risk is about the calendar: without final reading by mid-December 2026 the bill lapses, and realistic launch is late 2027 to H1 2028.

  • Two readings before the full Council are required; trade reports cite a 15 Dec cut-off; if not passed it must be reintroduced in 2027.
  • If passed it goes to the Mayor, then 30-day Congressional review, so earliest enactment is Q1 2027, followed by OLG rulemaking and licensing (launch window is analyst estimate).
  • Policy direction is unmistakable: from protectionist lottery monopoly to open high-tax sportsbook to full online casino with sweepstakes inside the tent in two years.
  • Consumer-protection floor tightens: age 21, mandatory deposit/spend/loss/time limits, 72-hour cooling-off, self-exclusion.

Prediction markets and DFS

Kalshi and Polymarket operate fully in the District untaxed and unchallenged, a federal-law question the Council cannot resolve.

  • As of 25 Nov 2026 every Kalshi and Polymarket market, including sports event contracts, is available in the District, the CFTC's home jurisdiction.
  • No District cease-and-desist, lawsuit or OLG guidance against federally registered prediction markets was identified, unlike Washington State where a court halted Kalshi's sports and election markets in August 2026.
  • Daily fantasy sports operate in DC without a District-specific geofence.
  • Both are untaxed substitutes for a 20–30%-taxed product; the sweepstakes rewrite converts the one reachable grey market into licence fees and 29% tax.

7Deep-dive: Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

Card- and ACH-funded, 18+, no credit-card or in-play restrictions and only generic advertising rules — a permissive consumer regime that B26-0656 would tighten on age and limits.

Payments and verification

Standard US card- and ACH-funded payments with no credit-card ban, no deposit waiting period and geolocation to the District boundary.

  • Methods: debit card, ACH/online banking, PayPal-type wallets and operator-branded prepaid cards; no mobile-money dimension.
  • CASPR scorecard records the credit-card ban as not in place; no deposit-to-bet waiting period.
  • Secondary guidance indicates new customers may deposit and wager up to 30 days before completing verification but must verify before first withdrawal.
  • Boundary geolocation matters because much of DC's weekday population lives in Maryland and Virginia; moving the lottery app to FanDuel in 2024 repatriated more than 22,000 active users.

Age and limits

Minimum age is 18 with no statutory stake, deposit or loss limits today; B26-0656 would raise the age to 21 and mandate limits.

  • BetMGM and Caesars apply 21 by house policy despite the statutory 18.
  • CFO estimates the age change costs $1.8m of sports-wagering tax over four years, implying the 18–20 cohort is roughly 2% of GGR (analyst inference).
  • In-play betting and micro-markets are permitted; operators offer deposit, wager and time limits as account tools (FanDuel's DC house rules describe these).
  • The iGaming bill would make deposit, spend, loss and time limits plus a 72-hour cooling-off mandatory.

Responsible gambling

Operators need an OLG-approved responsible-gaming plan and are covered by a single District-wide Self-Exclusion Program.

  • Obligations under 30 DCMR §§ 2128–2129: display problem-gambling and self-exclusion information with the 1-800-522-4700 helpline and state underage-play consequences.
  • Self-exclusion covers lottery, DCiLottery, Class A/B retail sportsbooks (since 1 Mar 2022) and all mobile apps; one application bars every licensed channel plus advertising, promotions and complimentary goods.
  • Terms run from 18 months to lifetime (secondary source; OLG's page does not list durations).
  • Problem-gambling services are funded through the Department of Behavioral Health, which would receive the first tranche of casino tax.

Advertising

Advertising is lightly constrained with no time-of-day, campus or stadium restrictions, earning an 'F' on the CASPR scorecard but a low-friction environment for entrants.

  • 30 DCMR § 2131 requires problem-gambling and helpline information, social responsibility, a balanced winning/losing message and a statement of OLG licensing.
  • Class B operators may not place physical advertising within two blocks of a Class A venue; no marketing to self-excluded persons; no tobacco-style warnings.
  • Team partnerships with in-broadcast odds integration (bet365/Monumental Game Center) are explicitly allowed.
  • Operators should underwrite on the assumption the age floor rises to 21 and mandatory limit tools arrive within the licence term.

8Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Volume is at a record and still edging up: October handle of $95.76m is the highest month since the 2020 launch, FY2026 handle grew 9.9% to $881.45m and CY2026 YTD is +3.8%; a ~$1,200-per-resident handle intensity (estimate) reflects a dense, affluent, commuter-swollen population that over-indexes on mobile betting. structural
  • Hold is cyclically depressed, so GGR has mechanical upside: CY2026 hold of 9.69% compares with 11.1% in CY2025 and 10.4% in FY2026; a reversion to 10.5–11% on the same handle adds $6–9m of annual market GGR without any volume growth (estimate). one-off
  • iGaming is closer than in any other US jurisdiction without a land-based casino: B26-0656 passed committee 3-1 on 18 Nov 2026 with a 25% headline rate (12.5% live dealer), two brands per licence, no licence cap and the lottery regulator openly supporting it; a $50k supplier licence is a cheap B2B entry point if it passes. early
  • New entrants can ramp quickly despite incumbency: bet365 went from launch (8 Sep 2026) to $1.59m monthly handle at an 11.56% hold in its first full month on a team partnership (Capitals, Wizards, Mystics) with in-broadcast odds integration — a template the advertising rules permit. early
  • Share is available from soft-holding incumbents: DraftKings (6.26% hold, bet count −55% YoY), Caesars (4.04%, average stake $103) and Fanatics (5.48% CY hold) are ceding GGR share to FanDuel; a better-priced, parlay-led product could take share without needing the market to grow. mixed
  • The regulator has a track record of outsourcing: OLG replaced its Intralot-built GambetDC app with FanDuel as subcontractor in 2024 before converting it to a licence, and still runs retail wagering and DCiLottery — a platform/managed-services supplier to OLG or a Class A/B/C licensee faces a $10k–$50k licence rather than a $2m one. structural
  • Tax and licensing rules are stable: no pending change to the 20/10/30% sports-wagering rates or fees was identified, and the FY2026 tax outturn ($21.97m, +13%) gives the CFO no revenue reason to reopen them. structural

▼ Lowlights

  • Class C unit economics are thin: a 30% GGR tax plus a $2m five-year fee, a mandatory team tie-up and the market's 6.6% Class C hold mean a 5% handle share (~$57m/yr) yields only ~$3.9m after tax before marketing (estimate) — PENN exited after 13 months and $2.2m of CY2026 handle. structural
  • The market is small and now ex-growth on handle: +0.9% YoY in October, a ~$90m GGR pool, and two operators holding 79% of GGR; FanDuel's venue licence (20% tax, 12.8% CY hold) gives it a structural cost advantage of roughly 10 points of GGR over any Class C challenger. structural
  • Tax take is falling even as handle rises: October tax −1.7% YoY and CY2026E tax of ~$21m versus $22.06m in 2025 (estimate), because mix is shifting to 20%-taxed Class A books and hold is down; a CFO looking for revenue could revisit the Class A rate or promo deductibility. watch
  • Age 21 and mandatory limits are in the committee print of B26-0656: sports betting would lose the 18–20 cohort (CFO: −$1.8m tax over FY2027–30) and all operators would need deposit/spend/loss/time-limit tooling and a 72-hour cooling-off — modest in revenue terms but a compliance build. watch
  • Prediction markets are an untaxed, unlicensed substitute operating openly in the CFTC's home city: all Kalshi and Polymarket sports markets are live in DC with no District enforcement, and DFS runs without a geofence; both erode the addressable pool for a 30%-taxed licensee. structural
  • The sweepstakes rewrite legalises a competitor class: by licensing dual-currency operators under iGaming rather than banning them, the committee print could let incumbent social-casino brands with existing DC databases convert into licensed rivals on day one. governance
  • Retail and Class B do not work: retail is 3.6% of handle, the sole active Class B book took $21.5k in October, and four Class B venues went dormant and were delisted; the lottery's own transfer fell 17.4% in FY2025, so OLG's fiscal pressure (and appetite for cannibalising products) is rising. structural
