ATG

Released 23 Oct 2026 · Currency SEK (tables in SEK millions; USD at USD/SEK 9.94, Riksbanken fix 23 Oct 2026) · Data confidence: HIGH

Market reaction: n/a — AB Trav och Galopp is unlisted (wholly owned by Svensk Travsport and Svensk Galopp); no public equity or traded bonds identified, hence no market reaction to report.

The one-line verdict

A weak quarter on the only line that matters to ATG's owners: Swedish net gaming revenue fell 7.1% and parent EBIT 11.4%, dressed up by a 45% surge in Denmark that contributes almost no profit. The V85 experiment is being unwound (V75 returns 28 Nov) with NPS at -16, opex rose while revenue fell, and liquidity headroom is thin (SEK 15m / ~$1.5m undrawn) heading into a Q4 of restructuring charges. Cash generation (Q3 OCF SEK 499m / ~$50.2m) and 0.4x net bank debt/EBITDA keep this a sound credit; the open question is whether the incoming CEO can halt a four-year erosion in profit before owner transactions.

1Executive summary

  • Third straight quarter of revenue decline, second straight double-digit EBIT drop. Q3 group net gaming revenue (NGR) SEK 1,247m (~$125.5m), -3.8% YoY and -4.2% QoQ; total revenue SEK 1,421m (~$143.0m), -3.1%; IFRS operating profit SEK 392m (~$39.4m), -9.7%; EBIT margin 27.6% (-200bps YoY, but +350bps QoQ on seasonality); net profit SEK 386m (~$38.8m), -9.8%; EPS SEK 970 (~$98) on 400k shares. ATG is unlisted and gives no numerical guidance, so there is no consensus to miss — the benchmark is the prior year and the Q2 run-rate, and both were missed. Figures tie across the interim report, the press release and Finwire/Börsvärlden.
  • Sweden is the whole story; Denmark is the make-up. Swedish (parent) NGR fell SEK 86m (-7.1%) to SEK 1,128m (~$113.5m) while Danish 25syv added SEK 37m (+45%) to SEK 119m (~$12.0m), limiting the group decline to SEK 49m. The -3.8% headline therefore understates the core-market deterioration by almost half. Parent EBIT -11.4% to SEK 387m (~$38.9m), margin 30.2% (-180bps).
  • Horse betting — volume loss, not price or luck. Group horse NGR SEK 902m (~$90.7m), -5.8%; Swedish horse NGR -6.7%. Swedish gross horse turnover SEK 2,818m (~$283.5m), -7.5%, while NGR/turnover (takeout proxy) was stable at 30.8% (30.6%). The calendar was neutral (13 V85/V75 Saturdays both years) and there were two MORE jackpots than Q3-25, so the decline came despite favourable jackpot support. International commingled turnover stalled to +0.6% (SEK 864m / ~$86.9m) after +13% in Q2.
  • V85 misstep acknowledged — V75 back on 28 November. NPS collapsed to -16 from +1 a year ago (five consecutive quarterly declines: +1, -3, -7, -9, -16). Management explicitly ties this to V85, which customers find harder, pricier and less attractive for small stakes, and to a perception that ATG did not listen. The Board decided in September to reinstate V75 as the Saturday flagship from 28 Nov with a SEK 0.60 row price and a V7-only option in non-jackpot rounds. Only ~5 V75 Saturdays fall in Q4, so the P&L test is Q1-27.
  • Sport betting: share loss admitted. Group sport NGR SEK 156m (~$15.7m), -8.8%; Sweden -10.9% to SEK 139m (~$14.0m) and -20% QoQ. Management concedes it lost Swedish sports market share in H1-26 after a weak spring-winter; Q3 market data are not yet available. The quarter contained the World Cup knockout rounds (to 19 July) and the report does not mention them — the absence of any uplift is itself telling.
  • Casino growth is 100% Danish. Group casino NGR +13.2% to SEK 189m (~$19.0m), but Swedish casino fell 5.5% to SEK 120m (~$12.1m) — its first decline after +9% in H1 — while Danish casino rose 72.5% to SEK 69m (~$6.9m). All three Swedish products were down YoY for the first time.
  • Denmark (25syv/Bet25) is now 9.5% of group NGR (6.3%) but profit contribution is opaque. Danish NGR +45% in SEK (FX a headwind; local-currency growth not disclosed) and +14% QoQ; 9M +28% to SEK 310m (~$31.2m). Segment profit is not disclosed; group EBIT less parent EBIT (which also carries Kanal 75 and Finnish start-up costs) was just SEK 5m (~$0.5m) in Q3 and SEK 13m (~$1.3m) for 9M — growth is being bought at thin margin.
  • Costs rose while revenue fell. Q3 operating expenses (net of SEK 30m capitalised work) SEK 720m (~$72.4m), +1.6% vs revenue -3.1%; opex/revenue 50.7% (+230bps). Personnel -3.4% on average headcount 579 (-5.5% YoY) but other expenses +2.8%. Gambling tax SEK 309m (~$31.1m), 24.8% of NGR; the 18%→22% hike from 1 Jul 2024 remains the structural drag management keeps lobbying against.
  • Cost programme: SEK 300m (~$30.2m) over three years, two reorganisations in 2026. The programme was disclosed in the Q2 CEO letter; the spring reorganisation (c.25 roles per Travronden) and the autumn round (c.30 roles per Travronden/duochjobbet — secondary sources) feed into a new organisation effective 1 Oct 2026. Autumn restructuring charges will land in Q4 and are unquantified. 9M opex SEK 2,312m (~$232.6m), -0.7%, already absorbed CEO severance and spring redundancy costs against a trademark-dispute one-off in the base.
  • 9M flattered by Q1. 9M NGR SEK 3,763m (~$378.6m), -2.6%; total revenue SEK 4,307m (~$433.3m), -1.8%; EBIT SEK 1,079m (~$108.6m), -2.4% with margin flat at 25.1%; net profit SEK 1,055m (~$106.1m), -2.1%. The trajectory is the point: NGR +0.5% in Q1, -4.1% in Q2, -3.8% in Q3; EBIT +22%, -11%, -10%.
  • Profit before owner transactions keeps eroding. Parent profit before transactions with owners (non-IFRS; EBIT plus SEK 118m paid to the owners for racing information, rights and sponsorship) was SEK 505m (~$50.8m), -9.5%; 9M SEK 1,503m (~$151.2m) vs SEK 1,712m in 9M-22 — a 12% decline over four years, the cleanest measure of franchise economics.
  • Cash flow intact, but balance-sheet headroom is thin. Q3 OCF SEK 499m (~$50.2m), -2.3%; capex SEK 47m; FCF (our calc) SEK 452m (~$45.5m). 9M OCF SEK 1,146m (~$115.3m), -5.4%; FCF SEK 985m (~$99.1m). Cash SEK 272m (~$27.4m, -34% YoY); bank debt SEK 998m (~$100.4m) vs SEK 649m; net bank debt SEK 726m (~$73.0m) = 0.4x LTM EBITDA (0.5x incl. SEK 98m leases). Undrawn facilities were only SEK 15m (~$1.5m) vs SEK 65m a year ago and SEK 165m at Q2 — a facility renewal/upsizing is a near-term necessity.
  • The debt funds the owners, not the business. Receivables from Group companies (advances to Svensk Travsport/Svensk Galopp) stood at SEK 1,330m (~$133.8m) vs SEK 95m at year-end; 9M financing outflows for loans to owners, group contribution and dividend were SEK 1,442m (~$145.1m) vs SEK 1,388m. These are settled via the year-end group contribution (FY25: SEK 1,615m / ~$162.5m, plus SEK 165m dividend paid in 2026). ATG financed the horse industry with c.SEK 2.3bn (~$231m) in 2025; with parent PBT down 3% YTD the FY26 contribution should be lower.
  • Management churn: three CEOs in 2026. Hans Lord Skarplöth left 12 Feb; chair Peter Norman stood in; Jörgen Forsberg (CEO of Svensk Travsport) has been acting CEO since 5 Mar; Anna Romboli (ex-head of Svenska Spel Tur) takes office in December. Lotta Nilsson is CFO/deputy CEO. New CPO (Jesper Komstadius) and CIO (Thérése Haeggblom Widmark) joined after quarter-end; Richard Woodbridge joined the Board at the 7 May AGM.
  • Finland: licence secured, launch 1 Jul 2027. 50%-owned Hippos ATG Oy was granted a Finnish gambling licence on 19 Oct 2026 ahead of the market re-regulation. Four FTEs in Finland so far; the 9M non-controlling-interest loss of SEK -7m (~$0.7m) implies roughly SEK 14m of JV start-up losses at 100% (our inference). Management itself warns competition 'across the Baltic' will be hard.
  • Data breach via a subcontractor. Personal data was exposed to unauthorised parties; the incident is under investigation and has been reported to authorities. Regulatory (IMY/Spelinspektionen) and reputational tail risk on top of the NPS problem.
  • Customers: more of them, worth less each. Active customers (12-month) 1.4m vs 1.3m (+~8%), yet NGR per active (our crude proxy on rounded actives) fell ~11% to c.SEK 891 (~$90) per quarter. Management's own framing — reaching more customers 'must be converted into activity' — confirms the acquisition is low-value. Marketing spend is not disclosed.
  • Retail keeps shrinking. Retail NGR SEK 91m (~$9.2m), -16.5% (Sweden -18.1%); now 7.3% of group NGR vs 8.4%. c.1,430 agents. Digital NGR SEK 1,156m (~$116.3m), -2.6%.
  • No guidance, next catalyst 12 Feb 2027 (FY26 report). Our Q4-26E: NGR c.SEK 1,350m (-2.5%), EBIT SEK 385–410m (~$39–41m) vs SEK 439m, after an assumed SEK 20–40m restructuring charge; FY26E EBIT c.SEK 1,465–1,490m (~$147–150m), -3.5% to -5%. Thesis read-through: ATG remains a highly cash-generative quasi-monopoly in Swedish pool betting, but the product self-harm (V85), share loss in sport, a Swedish casino downturn and owner-driven leverage mean the equity-like value is now dependent on a strategy reset under a new CEO, while sector read-across on Swedish online sport/casino volumes is negative.

3Segment deep-dive: Sweden (parent ATG — horse, sport, casino)

Core market down 7.1% with all three products negative; takeout stable so it is volume loss, and the V85 reversal is an admission that the damage was self-inflicted.

Swedish revenue and profit

Swedish NGR fell 7.1% to SEK 1,128m (~$113.5m) with all products negative; this is the number that matters, as Denmark cushions the group -3.8%.

  • NGR was 5.8% lower than Q2; nine months down 4.6% to SEK 3,453m (~$347.4m).
  • Parent total revenue dropped 6.1% to SEK 1,283m (~$129.1m); parent gambling tax fell to SEK 271m (~$27.3m) from SEK 297m.
  • Parent operating profit declined 11.4% to SEK 387m (~$38.9m), a 30.2% margin against 32.0% a year ago.
  • ATG is the de facto sole pari-mutuel horse operator and #1 horse brand, so the volume decline cannot be blamed on competition; no numeric market shares published.

Horse betting and V85

Horse NGR fell 6.7% to SEK 869m (~$87.4m) on a stake decline, with takeout stable; the V85 reversal admits the damage was self-inflicted.

  • Horse is 77% of Swedish NGR (unchanged mix); down 3.9% quarter on quarter; nine months 4.9% lower at SEK 2,616m (~$263.2m).
  • Swedish gross horse turnover SEK 2,818m (~$283.5m), -7.5%; horse NGR to turnover ratio held at 30.8% versus 30.6%.
  • Calendar offered no excuse: 13 V85/V75 Saturdays in both years and two more jackpots than Q3-25.
  • International commingled turnover stalled to +0.6% at SEK 864m (~$86.9m) after +13% in Q2; total horse turnover -5.7% at SEK 3,682m (~$370.4m).
  • Year to date international +8.8% at SEK 2,702m (~$271.8m); total horse turnover -2.8% at SEK 11,130m (~$1.12bn).

V75 return

V75 returns from 28 November at SEK 0.60 row price, but with only about five V75 Saturdays in Q4 the earnings read-out is Q1-27, not Q4-26.

  • Weakness attributed squarely to V85, perceived as harder, more expensive and unattractive for small-stake players.
  • Board decided in September to bring V75 back with a V7-only pool in non-jackpot rounds.
  • Longer-dated structural worry: falling horse ownership, breeding and training numbers, linked to the 2024 gambling-tax increase constraining ATG's industry contribution.
“we will monitor the reception closely”Management

Sport and casino

Sport fell 10.9% to SEK 139m (~$14.0m) and casino 5.5% to SEK 120m (~$12.1m); every Swedish product line was negative.

  • Sport down 20.1% quarter on quarter, 12% of Swedish NGR (13%); nine-month decline 8.7% to SEK 473m (~$47.6m).
  • Management states ATG lost Swedish sports-betting market share in the first half; Q3 market data not yet available.
  • World Cup knockout rounds through 19 July not mentioned; absence of event uplift is a negative signal on competitive position rather than calendar.
  • Casino flat on Q2, first decline after roughly 9% growth in H1; nine months +4.0% (SEK 364m / ~$36.6m).
  • The 13% group casino growth headlined in the press release is entirely Danish.
“results- and pulse-driven”Management

Channels and customers

Digital NGR fell 6.0% to SEK 1,042m (~$104.8m) and retail 18.1% to SEK 86m (~$8.7m); customer adds are low-value.

  • Retail across roughly 1,430 agents is now 7.6% of Swedish NGR; year to date retail -16.8% at SEK 267m (~$26.9m).
  • Group actives rose to about 1.4m from 1.3m, but NGR per active (our proxy) fell roughly 11% to c.SEK 891 (~$90) per quarter.
  • Marketing spend not disclosed; no promotional-intensity metric.
  • NPS fell for a fifth consecutive quarter to -16 (Q2 -9, Q3-25 +1), tied to V85 and customers feeling unheard.
  • GameScanner risk distribution stable: 82.1% lowest-risk category, 1.6% in categories 4–5.
“must be converted into higher activity”Management

Costs and efficiency

Group opex rose 1.6% to SEK 720m (~$72.4m) while revenue fell 3.1%, lifting opex to 50.7% of revenue (+230bps).

  • Opex is net of SEK 30m capitalised development; personnel costs fell 3.4% to SEK 168m (~$16.9m) on headcount 579 (-5.5%).
  • Other expenses rose 2.8% to SEK 519m (~$52.2m); owner payments for racing information, rights and sponsorship SEK 118m (~$11.9m) versus SEK 121m.
  • Parent profit before transactions with owners fell 9.5% to SEK 505m (~$50.8m); nine months SEK 1,503m (~$151.2m) versus SEK 1,712m in 2022, a 12% erosion.
  • SEK 300m (~$30.2m) three-year efficiency programme meant to arrest this; two 2026 reorganisations (c.25 and c.30 roles per Travronden, secondary sources) from 1 October, autumn charges in Q4 unquantified.
  • No segment guidance; ATG does not guide.

4Segment deep-dive: Denmark (25syv / Bet25) & Nordic expansion (Finland — Hippos ATG Oy)

Danish NGR +45% in SEK on a casino surge, now 9.5% of group, but implied profit contribution is near zero and Finland start-up losses are beginning to build.

Danish revenue growth

Danish NGR rose 45% in SEK to SEK 119m (~$12.0m), now 9.5% of group, driven almost entirely by casino.

  • Up 14% on Q2; nine months SEK 310m (~$31.2m), +28%; currency was a headwind so local-currency growth higher, unquantified.
  • Share of group NGR 9.5% from 6.3% a year ago and 8.0% in Q2; absorbed SEK 37m of the SEK 86m Swedish decline.
  • Danish casino NGR +72.5% to SEK 69m (~$6.9m), +21% quarter on quarter, 58% of Danish mix from 49%.
  • Horse +22% to SEK 33m (~$3.3m); sport +13% to SEK 17m (~$1.7m); digital SEK 114m (~$11.5m), retail SEK 5m (~$0.5m).
  • No Danish market-share, actives or marketing data disclosed.

Danish profitability

Implied Danish profit contribution is near zero: 25syv is at best marginally profitable on 45% growth, bought with bonus and marketing spend.

  • Group operating profit less parent operating profit was SEK 5m (~$0.5m) in Q3 versus SEK -3m; SEK 13m (~$1.3m) nine months versus SEK 1m.
  • Residual also carries Kanal 75 media subsidiary and Finnish JV start-up costs; Danish GGR-tax rate is 28%.
  • Diversification is real at the revenue line but does not yet move group earnings.
  • Headlining 'casino +13%' and '25syv +45%' while Swedish casino fell 5.5% is the clearest example of emphasising the periphery over the core.

Finland start-up

Hippos ATG Oy won a Finnish licence on 19 October and opens 1 July 2027; start-up losses are building with no guidance on size.

  • 50%-owned JV consolidated with non-controlling interest; four employees; ATG among the first licensees ahead of re-regulation.
  • Non-controlling-interest share of losses SEK -2m in Q3 and SEK -7m nine months, implying roughly SEK 14m (~$1.4m) at 100% year to date (our inference).
  • Finnish demand already drove 13% international commingled growth in H1; licence allows direct service rather than pool commission.
  • ATG has shown it can grow abroad, not yet profitably; expect Finnish opex to step up through H1-27 ahead of revenue.

5Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Denmark is scaling fast: 25syv NGR +45% YoY in SEK to SEK 119m (~$12.0m), +14% QoQ, casino +72.5%; Denmark now 9.5% of group NGR (6.3%) and absorbed SEK 37m of the SEK 86m Swedish decline. structural
  • V75 relaunch on 28 Nov is a direct, fast response to the NPS collapse — SEK 0.60 row price and a V7-only option; the Board acted within a quarter of NPS hitting -9. If Saturday turnover recovers even to flat YoY from -7.5%, that is a c.SEK 150–200m annual NGR swing on the Swedish horse base (our estimate). early
  • Finland licence secured (19 Oct) for a 1 Jul 2027 launch via 50%-owned Hippos ATG Oy — among the first licensees in a re-regulating market where ATG already sees Finnish demand through commingled pools (international turnover 9M +8.8%). structural
  • Cash conversion intact: Q3 OCF SEK 499m (~$50.2m), FCF c.SEK 452m (~$45.5m); 9M FCF c.SEK 985m (~$99.1m). Net bank debt/LTM EBITDA only 0.4x and the SEK 998m (~$100.4m) bank debt is mirrored by SEK 1,330m (~$133.8m) of advances to owners. structural
  • Margin recovered sequentially: EBIT margin 27.6%, +350bps QoQ; headcount 579 (-5.5% YoY); SEK 300m (~$30.2m) three-year efficiency programme with the new organisation live from 1 Oct. mixed
  • Horse decline is volume, not price or luck: takeout proxy stable at 30.8% (30.6%), same 13 Saturdays, two more jackpots — so the product fix (V75) addresses the actual problem rather than a results swing. mixed
  • Active customers 1.4m vs 1.3m — the funnel is still filling even while NPS falls; the conversion job is monetisation, not acquisition. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Swedish NGR -7.1% to SEK 1,128m (~$113.5m) — nearly twice the group headline; horse -6.7%, sport -10.9%, casino -5.5%: all three Swedish products negative for the first time. Parent EBIT -11.4%, margin -180bps. structural
  • NPS -16 from +1 a year ago, five consecutive declines, explicitly attributed by management to V85 and to customers feeling unheard. A product change damaged the core franchise of a de facto monopoly; the reversal costs a year of turnover. structural
  • Cost base moved the wrong way: opex +1.6% to SEK 720m (~$72.4m) vs revenue -3.1%; opex/revenue 50.7% (+230bps); other expenses +2.8%. EBIT -9.7% follows -11% in Q2; Q4 will carry unquantified autumn restructuring charges. mixed
  • Liquidity headroom is thin: undrawn facilities SEK 15m (~$1.5m) vs SEK 165m at Q2 and SEK 65m a year ago; cash SEK 272m (~$27.4m), -34% YoY; bank debt SEK 998m (~$100.4m), +54% YoY, funding advances to owners. Facility renewal/upsizing needed before year-end settlement. governance
  • Sports share loss admitted for H1-26 with no Q3 market data; a World Cup quarter still produced -9% group sport NGR and the report does not mention the tournament. structural
  • Swedish casino turned negative (-5.5%) after +9% in H1 — the only domestic growth product has rolled over, leaving group casino growth entirely Danish and at near-zero implied margin. watch
  • Governance and execution churn: three CEOs in 2026, two reorganisations, a subcontractor data breach under investigation, and a new CEO (Anna Romboli) only arriving in December — strategy reset risk on top of operational risk. governance
  • International turnover growth stalled to +0.6% (from +13% in Q2) and retail NGR fell 16.5%; the two offsets to Swedish digital horse weakness both weakened in the quarter. watch

6Forecast & outlook

Guidance bridge. ATG issues no numerical guidance, so there is no formal bridge to reconcile: the Q2 report's outlook language (disposable income as the long-run driver; the 2024 gambling-tax hike as a structural drag) is repeated verbatim. What changed qualitatively is (i) a new Q4 cost item — autumn restructuring charges, unquantified — flagged by the parent/Swedish division, which is also the division driving the earnings decline (parent EBIT -11.4% vs group -9.7%); (ii) the V75 relaunch on 28 Nov, which management will 'monitor closely' rather than quantify; and (iii) Finland start-up costs ahead of a 1 Jul 2027 launch. Independent view. Revenue: we model Q4-26 group NGR of c.SEK 1,350m (~$136m), -2.5% YoY, assuming Swedish NGR -5% (c.SEK 1,230m — a modest improvement from -7% as comps ease and the last five V75 Saturdays add some reactivation) and Danish NGR +35% (c.SEK 123m). That puts FY26E NGR at c.SEK 5,110m (~$514m), -2.5%, and total revenue at c.SEK 5,840m (~$588m), -2%. Margin: Q4 is seasonally the strongest revenue quarter (Q4-25 EBIT SEK 439m, 27.7% margin), but we deduct an assumed SEK 20–40m of autumn restructuring charges (not disclosed; our placeholder) and higher Finnish start-up opex, giving Q4-26E EBIT of SEK 385–410m and FY26E EBIT of SEK 1,465–1,490m, roughly -4% YoY at a c.25% margin (25.9% FY25); FY26E EBITDA c.SEK 1,710–1,735m (~$172–175m). Net profit is not meaningful at group level because the year-end group contribution to Svensk Travsport (FY25 SEK 1,615m / ~$162.5m) and its tax effect run through equity; parent PBT of SEK 1,084m at 9M (-3.4%) points to a lower FY26 contribution to the owners than FY25. Leverage: bank debt should stay near SEK 1.0bn (~$100m) into year-end because the owner advances (SEK 1,330m) are settled by the group contribution rather than in cash; with only SEK 15m undrawn, a renewed or larger facility before the Q4 distribution cycle is the key balance-sheet event — net bank debt/EBITDA stays well under 1x either way. 2027 swing factors: the V75 reception (a return to flat Swedish horse turnover from -6% is worth c.SEK 150–200m of NGR, our estimate), whether the Swedish sport/casino declines are cyclical (disposable income) or share loss, Finnish launch costs in H1-27 with revenue only from July, DKK/SEK, the incoming CEO's strategy reset from December, and any political movement on the 22% gambling tax that ATG keeps lobbying against. Our bias is that Q4 prints another mid-single-digit NGR decline and that FY27 is a transition year in which V75 stabilises horse but Finland and the cost programme's execution dominate the EBIT bridge.
