2026 · market & regulatory brief

Latin America

Released 22 Oct 2026 (NEXT.io "iGaming Market in Focus: Latin America"); data to Oct 2026 · Currency Local currency primary (BRL, COP, PEN, MXN, ARS, CLP) with ~USD alongside; tables in millions unless stated · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Latin America in 2026 is no longer one growth story. Brazil — the region's largest regulated market at BRL 37bn (~$7.1bn) GGR in 2025 and BRL 20.07bn (~$3.9bn, +15% YoY) in H1-26 — has been switched off by Provisional Measure 1.394/2026 since 6 October, and Lula's re-election on 25 October removes the quickest political route back. The five remaining markets now diverge on maturity rather than size: Colombia (regulated since 2016, 21 online operators, online VAT struck down in April), Peru (cleanest framework, but ISC-on-stakes risk of 1%→5%), Mexico (scale under a 1947 law with a 50% headline IEPS), Argentina (~50% offshore, acquisition-only entry) and Chile (bill still in Congress, framework 2027-28 at best).

1Executive summary

  • Brazil is dark. President Lula signed Provisional Measure (MP) 1.394/2026 on 25 Sep 2026 prohibiting the operation, offer, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting (sports and online casino). Deposits stopped on publication, licensed sites and apps went offline on 6 Oct, player balances were refunded via banks 7–14 Oct, and the Ministry of Finance was scheduled to formally revoke all authorisations on 25 Oct (Agência Brasil; Vixio).
  • The market that was switched off was the region's largest. The SPA/MF reported ~BRL 37bn (~$7.1bn) GGR for 2025, the first full year of regulation under Law 14.790/2023, from 79 authorised companies and 25.2m bettors; H1-26 GGR reached BRL 20.07bn (~$3.9bn), +15% on H1-25's BRL 17.4bn (~$3.3bn), averaging BRL 3.3bn (~$0.63bn) a month with 30.8m active registered CPFs (SPA data via BNLData/LAI; brmkt.co reads BRL 20.28bn, 84 licensees, 193 brands).
  • Brazil's fiscal stack was material and is now foregone. Receita Federal collected ~BRL 9.95bn (~$1.9bn) from licensed operators in 2025, including BRL 4.5bn (~$0.87bn) of the 12%-of-GGR legal destinations, plus ~BRL 2.5bn (~$0.48bn) of BRL 30m (~$5.8m) five-year authorisation fees and BRL 95.5m (~$18m) of inspection fees; H1-26 federal tributes were BRL 2.49bn (~$0.48bn). The government's own estimate is BRL 5.3bn (~$1.0bn) less revenue in 2027 (sigma.world citing government analysis).
  • Four exit paths, none fast. Vixio maps (1) permanent ban, (2) sports betting restored/iGaming banned — the position of runner-up Flávio Bolsonaro and the enlarged PL caucus, (3) full restoration if Congress rejects or lets the MP lapse (~4 Mar 2027), and (4) an STF injunction (Justice Fux, petitions from ANJL/IBJR, AGU opposing). Lula's 51.8%–48.2% runoff win on 25 Oct strengthens the executive's hand; a Senate public consultation ran 85.6% in favour of the ban (561,930 votes).
  • The region 'changed shape' rather than slowed. NEXT.io's comparative report (22 Oct 2026, with DATA.BET; 20+ interviews) concludes that 'stage now matters more than size': Colombia is the mature acquirer's market, Peru the cleanest framework with tax unresolved, Mexico scale with entrenched incumbents under a 1947 law, Argentina a provincial patchwork, Chile still pre-regulation.
  • Colombia is the structural beneficiary. Coljuegos' 2022–26 accountability report shows online exploitation rights of COP 396.8bn (~$104m) for Jan–Jul 2026 from 21 authorised operators, 10.8m active accounts and ~1.9m monthly players; the online segment contributed COP 473.0bn (~$124m) for Jan–Aug 2026 and total Coljuegos collections rose 39% YoY, driven by an 84.24% jump in sports betting around the 2026 World Cup.
  • Colombia's implied online GGR is approaching the $1bn+ run-rate. Online rights are levied at 15% of GGR (17% if RTP <83%, Law 1955/2019); 2025 online rights of COP 361.9bn (~$95m) imply ~COP 2.4tn (~$0.64bn) GGR [analyst estimate], and the Jan–Jul 2026 figure implies an annualised ~COP 4.5tn (~$1.2bn) [estimate, World Cup-inflated].
  • Colombia's VAT whiplash is the cautionary tale. A 19% VAT on player deposits under emergency Decree 175/2025 (22 Feb–31 Dec 2025) raised ~COP 1.35tn (~$355m) from the then-14 online operators — roughly 3.7x the sector's exploitation rights. A second emergency (Decrees 1390 and 1474/2025) moved the VAT to a GGR base from Jan 2026, but the Constitutional Court struck the emergency down in April 2026, leaving the VAT inapplicable. Petro's 20 Jul 2026 tax bill sought to make it permanent (COP 1.7tn/~$447m expected from online); its fate under President Abelardo de la Espriella (sworn in Aug 2026) is unresolved [unverified].
  • Mexico: scale, but the headline tax is a fiction. The federal IEPS excise on gaming is 50% on paper (NEXT.io; raised from 30% from 1 Jan 2026 [rate change unverified against the DOF]), yet market experts put the effective rate at ~12–15% after recognised cost-structuring. Entry remains via sub-licensing under incumbents' 1947-law permits; a new regulatory path is 'untested'. No official GGR series exists.
  • Argentina: half the market is outside the system. H2 Gambling Capital (via NEXT.io) puts regulated GGR at ~$1.1bn against ~$1.2bn offshore; advisers with sight of the informal market say up to 80%. The black market is domestic — agents reselling credit over WhatsApp and social media — so site-blocking alone will not fix it. Major provincial tenders (CABA/LOTBA, Buenos Aires/IPLyC, Córdoba) are closed, making acquisition of a licence-holder the only realistic entry route.
  • Peru: cleanest framework, tax the swing factor. Law 31557 (regulated by MINCETUR) went live in 2025; SUNAT levies a 12% tax on the operator's net gaming base plus a 1% ISC on the value of every bet (0.3% Jan–Jun 2025, 1% from 1 Jul 2025 under DS 008-2025-EF). The sector generated PEN 465m (~$134m) in taxes in 2025 (SNJA), of which the ISC yields ~PEN 324m (~$93m) a year. Bill 00519-2026 (filed 6 Oct 2026) would lift the ISC to 5%, targeting PEN 1.62bn (~$467m) — a turnover tax that would consume most of a sportsbook's margin.
  • Chile is a cheap option on a 2027–28 framework. The online gambling bill remains in Congress under the Kast administration (inaugurated Mar 2026); NEXT.io expects legislation to take shape in late 2027 or 2028. Offshore brands already have a foothold; positioning is cheap but its value is wholly contingent on the final tax and licence terms.
  • 2026 was the year of the ballot box. Three of the five NEXT.io markets changed governments (Kast in Chile, Mar; Fujimori in Peru, Jul; de la Espriella in Colombia, Aug), and Brazil re-elected Lula on 25 Oct. Legal sources link the election calendar to delayed M&A; dealmaking is expected to return in 2027 with pricing driven by licence transferability, tax exposure and regulatory stability, structured with earn-outs, indemnities and escrow.
  • Predictability is worth more than rate. The NEXT.io taxation chapter's conclusion — 'a predictable 20% is more investable than an unpredictable 12%' — is borne out by Brazil (12%→15% GGR tax, then prohibition), Colombia (19% VAT on, off, on, off) and Peru (ISC 0.3%→1%→5% proposed).
  • Enforcement is maturing even where policy is volatile. Brazil's SPA blocked 25,000+ illegal sites with Anatel in 2025, opened 132 cases against 133 operators, closed 550 bank accounts via 54 financial/payment institutions' reports and removed 324 influencer profiles; ~5m people are blocked (3m Bolsa Família/BPC beneficiaries by STF order; 217k self-exclusion requests in the platform's first 40 days). Colombia's Coljuegos reports stepped-up actions against illegal operators alongside 110,642 authorised slot machines.
  • Supply-side gaps are consistent across the region. NEXT.io flags four: an underdeveloped affiliate sector, fragmented payments, product mixes that miss local demand, and an overlooked retail channel; prediction markets are the wild card. Colombia's retail route is 'genuinely open'.
  • Responsible gambling is now the political lever. Brazil's ban was justified on a Comsefaz estimate of a BRL 62.5bn (~$12bn) household-budget shortfall in 2025 and a Ministry of Health social-cost figure of BRL 38.8bn (~$7.5bn)/yr; Peru's ISC bill is framed as problem-gambling prevention; Colombia already withholds 20% on prizes. Expect advertising, deposit limits and welfare-recipient exclusions to spread.
  • Read-through for B2Tech. Treat Brazil as a 2027 call option, not a 2026 budget line; keep the entity and bet.br assets alive pending the STF/Congress outcome. Colombia is the only market where scale, legal certainty and an open licensing window coincide — enter via concession or acquisition now while VAT is off. Peru is a build case contingent on the ISC vote; Mexico and Argentina are buy-not-build; Chile is a watching brief. Price every market on effective tax and channelisation, not headline rate or population.

3Deep-dive: Brazil — from BRL 37bn regulated market to prohibition

The region's anchor market has been switched off by decree; restoration depends on the STF or a Congress deadline in March 2027, and Lula's re-election makes the executive's position harder to dislodge.

Market scale pre-ban

The regulated market produced ~BRL 37bn (~$7.1bn) GGR in 2025 from 79 operators and 25.2m bettors, accelerating into H1-26 at +15% YoY.

  • Launched 1 Jan 2025 under Law 14.790/2023; SPA one-year balance (Jan 2026): ~BRL 37bn (~$7.1bn) GGR, 79 authorised companies, 25.2m individual bettors.
  • Bettors were 68.3% men; 28.6% aged 31–40, 22.7% each for 18–24 and 25–30.
  • H1-25 GGR BRL 17.4bn (~$3.3bn) (TCU audit; SPA Panorama Semestral), so the second half accelerated.
  • H1-26 GGR BRL 20.07bn (~$3.9bn), +15% YoY, BRL 3.3bn (~$0.63bn) a month; 30.8m active registrations; semester spend BRL 649.60 (~$125) per bettor.
  • Monthly spend BRL 108 (~$21), up 5% on H2-25; brmkt.co's Sigap read is BRL 20.28bn, 84 licensees, 193 brands.

Fiscal stack

Total federal collection was ~BRL 9.95bn (~$1.9bn) in 2025 on a 12% GGR levy rising to 15%, plus corporate taxes and fees; illegal play up to 50%.

  • 12% of GGR to legal destinations (BRL 4.5bn/~$0.87bn in 2025), rising gradually to 15% under the late-2025 law change, plus IRPJ, CSLL, PIS/Cofins and payroll contributions.
  • BRL 2.5bn (~$0.48bn) from BRL 30m (~$5.8m) five-year authorisation grants; BRL 95.5m (~$18m) inspection fees; 15% income tax on player winnings.
  • H1-26 federal tributes BRL 2.49bn (~$0.48bn); sport (BRL 870m/~$167m) and tourism (BRL 678m/~$130m) largest beneficiaries.
  • Illegal-market estimates ran up to 50% of total play (iGB).

Enforcement maturity

Enforcement had matured, with 25,000+ offshore sites blocked, 132 administrative cases and ~5m people blocked from betting.

  • 25,000+ offshore sites blocked with Anatel; 132 administrative cases against 133 operators (80 pending penalties).
  • 54 banks and payment institutions filed 1,255 reports on 1,687 individuals paying unlicensed operators, leading to 550 account closures.
  • 412 influencer cases closed with 324 profiles and 229 posts removed.
  • Centralised self-exclusion platform took 217,000 requests in its first 40 days (37% citing loss of control).
  • ~5m people blocked in total, including ~3m Bolsa Família/BPC beneficiaries under the STF's 2024 order.

The ban

On 25 Sep 2026 Lula signed MP 1.394/2026 banning fixed-odds betting nationwide, with sites offline on 6 Oct and authorisations revoked 25 Oct.

  • Signed on the eve of a tight presidential runoff; bans operation, offer, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting.
  • Deposits ceased on publication; withdrawals until 5 Oct; sites and apps offline 6 Oct; balances handed to banks 7–8 Oct for refunds 9–14 Oct, Caixa as fallback.
  • Justification cites Comsefaz's BRL 62.5bn (~$12bn) household-budget shortfall in 2025, Ministry of Health's BRL 38.8bn (~$7.5bn) annual social cost, 20.4% of secondary-school students betting online.
  • Lower-court injunctions (e.g. Stake) were dissolved within 24 hours.
“cancer”Lula

Path to restoration

Relief depends on the STF in late October–November or the MP lapsing around 4 Mar 2027; Lula's 51.8% win makes partial restoration more likely.

  • ANJL and IBJR petitioned the STF on 28 Sep; AGU asked Justice Fux to keep the ban and await the PGR; Fux deferred until after the 25 Oct runoff.
  • MP has 26 amendments, enters urgency status 9 Nov 2026, could be voted before December recess or slip to Feb 2027; lapses around 4 Mar 2027 if not converted.
  • Lapse would restore Law 14.790 and require a follow-up decree on validity of acts, including revoked licences, during the blackout.
  • Vixio's four scenarios: permanent ban (least likely given fiscal hole and club sponsorships worth 6–7% of team budgets per Lula), sports restored with iGaming banned (Flávio Bolsonaro's position, backed by larger PL bloc after 4 Oct vote), full restoration, or judicial resuscitation.
  • Lula's 51.8% runoff win removes the lame-duck scenario and raises the probability any restoration is partial and conditioned on new restrictions.

Commercial implications

Even full reversal will not return the pre-ban market; budget Brazil as a 2027 option with a materially smaller iGaming TAM than 2025.

  • Payment rails, sponsorship contracts and affiliate relationships have been severed; the government estimates a BRL 5.3bn (~$1.0bn) revenue loss in 2027.
  • The black market now has an open field.
  • The asset worth preserving is the authorised entity and its compliance record, so reinstatement — ideally without a second BRL 30m (~$5.8m) fee — can be executed within weeks.

4Deep-dive: Colombia — the mature benchmark and the region's default beneficiary

Twenty-one licensed online operators, ~COP 4.5tn (~$1.2bn) annualised implied online GGR, a World Cup-driven +39% in regulator collections, and a VAT that the Constitutional Court has (for now) removed.

Market scale

Online is the growth engine: 21 operators, 10.8m active accounts, sports betting +84.24% YoY on the World Cup and total Coljuegos collections +39%.

  • Licensed online since 2016 under Law 643/2001 and Coljuegos' concession model; 2022–26 accountability report published Aug 2026.
  • Online exploitation rights COP 396.8bn (~$104m) Jan–Jul 2026 from 21 operators; 10.8m active accounts; average 1.9m monthly players.
  • Jan–Aug online contributed COP 473.0bn (~$124m); land-based rights rose 12.25% to COP 280.9bn (~$74m) across 110,642 authorised slot machines.
  • Health transfers 2022–26 totalled COP 7.21tn (~$1.9bn); annual series COP 990.6bn (~$261m) 2024, COP 972.2bn (~$256m) 2025, projected COP 1.356tn (~$357m) 2026.

Tax and implied GGR

At 15% exploitation rights, implied online GGR is ~COP 2.4tn (~$0.64bn) for 2025 and ~COP 4.5tn (~$1.2bn) annualised for 2026.

  • Exploitation rights 15% of GGR, or 17% where return-to-player is below 83% (Law 1955/2019); players face 20% withholding on prizes.
  • 2025 online rights of COP 361.9bn (~$95m) imply ~COP 2.4tn (~$0.64bn) GGR [analyst estimate].
  • Jan–Jul 2026 implies ~COP 2.65tn (~$0.70bn) in seven months, annualised ~COP 4.5tn (~$1.2bn) [estimate, flattered by the World Cup].
  • Fecoljuegos puts the online sector at 0.18% of GDP.

VAT episode

A 19% VAT on deposits raised ~COP 1.35tn (~$355m) in 2025, roughly 3.7x annual exploitation rights, before the Constitutional Court struck the emergency decree.

  • Emergency Decree 175/2025 applied 19% VAT to every deposit from 22 Feb to 31 Dec 2025; the then-14 operators remitted more than COP 123.1bn (~$32m) a month, peaking in Q4, earmarked for Catatumbo.
  • Decree 1390/2025 with Decree 1474/2025 re-based VAT on GGR from January 2026; in April 2026 the Constitutional Court declared the emergency decree unconstitutional, rendering Decree 1474 ineffective pending final ruling.
  • Petro government's 20 Jul 2026 tax bill proposed permanent 19% VAT on online gambling, expecting COP 1.7tn (~$447m), 7.8% of a COP 21.8tn (~$5.7bn) package.
  • President Abelardo de la Espriella took office in August; the bill's fate is unresolved [unverified].
“the real mathematics of the business”Fecoljuegos

Licensing window

The licensing window is open, with operator count rising from 14 at end-2025 to 21 by July 2026.

  • Coljuegos authorised MrYoker (Global Vitxo S.A.S.) under concession C2261 of 2026 for sports betting, live casino and virtual slots to 2031.
  • NEXT.io characterises Colombia as the market that 'rewards acquisition' and notes the retail route is 'genuinely open'.
  • Relevant given Coljuegos' large localizado estate and Colombia's cash-heavy payments culture.

B2Tech view

Colombia is the only Latin American market where legal certainty, scale and an available licence coincide in 2026; risks are fiscal, not regulatory.

  • A right-leaning administration may be less inclined to re-impose the VAT, but the Constitutional Court's final word is pending.
  • A 19%-on-GGR VAT stacked on 15% rights would take the effective GGR tax to ~34% before prize withholding.

5Deep-dive: Mexico — scale under a 1947 law, with a 50% excise that nobody pays in full

The largest of the five NEXT.io markets by scale, but entry runs through incumbents' permits and the tax case depends on structuring the regulator tolerates.

Framework and size

Mexico is the largest of the five NEXT.io markets but has no published official GGR series; treat any market-size figure as [unverified].

  • Governed by the Ley Federal de Juegos y Sorteos of 1947, administered by SEGOB's Dirección General de Juegos y Sorteos via a 2004 regulation (tightened in 2023 to curb new slot permits [unverified detail]).
  • NEXT.io describes Mexico as 'now the largest market in this report' with 'entrenched incumbents'; H2-type estimates were not available to verify.

Tax distortion

The IEPS excise is 50% on paper but experts put the effective rate at roughly 12–15% after cost-structuring, which is both thesis and risk.

  • 50% rate up from the long-standing 30% as part of the 2026 federal revenue package [rate change not verified against the Diario Oficial].
  • Base is receipts net of prizes paid, i.e. GGR-like [base per LIEPS art. 18, unverified]; states layer their own levies [unverified].
  • Profitability depends on structures that a future administration or SAT audit could reject.

Licensing closed

New federal permits have not been issued for years; the route to market is a sub-licence under an existing permit-holder.

  • Caliente, Codere, Logrand, Big Bola and others are the commonly cited incumbents [unverified list].
  • NEXT.io notes a new regulatory path that 'remains untested but could replace sub-licensing as the only route to market'.
  • Until tested, assume incumbents control access, pricing and timing.

Operations and B2Tech view

Mexico is a buy-or-partner market: a sub-licence with tax-structure warranties or a minority stake, not a greenfield build.

  • Combines the region's deepest retail casino estate with fast-growing mobile sportsbook; co-hosted the 2026 World Cup.
  • Payments are card- and OXXO/cash-voucher-heavy with SPEI transfers; the report flags payments fragmentation and an immature affiliate market as supplier gaps.

6Deep-dive: Argentina — provincial patchwork and the channelisation test

About $1.1bn of regulated GGR against ~$1.2bn offshore; tenders are closed and the black market is domestic and social-media-based, so entry means buying a licence-holder.

Market and channelisation

Regulated GGR is about $1.1bn against roughly $1.2bn offshore, a ~48% channelisation rate, with no national regulator or consolidated statistics.

  • Regulated province by province: City of Buenos Aires (LOTBA), Buenos Aires Province (IPLyC), Córdoba, Mendoza, Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and others each run their own licensing, tax and technical regimes.
  • Figures are H2 Gambling Capital via the NEXT.io report; advisers with sight of the informal market estimate the unlicensed share at up to 80%.

Informal market

The black market is domestic and social-media-based, so DNS and payment blocking are blunt instruments against it.

  • Run by agents reselling gambling credit through social media and messaging apps, on a cash-and-Mercado-Pago network.
  • NEXT.io frames channelisation as 'the test regulation has not yet passed'.
“the bookmaker model of 40 years ago, moved onto WhatsApp”an executive

Taxation

Provincial GGR-based levies plus a 2021 national indirect tax on stakes make the effective burden hard to model and materially different between CABA and PBA.

  • National indirect tax on online bets introduced in 2021 at a base rate on stakes, higher for foreign or non-cooperating-jurisdiction operators [rates unverified].
  • Inflation, FX controls and periodic restrictions on card use for gambling compound the burden.

Entry route

Tenders are closed, so acquisition of a licence-holder is the only realistic route; for B2Tech it is a platform-and-content play through existing licensees.

  • Deal activity slowed by the political calendar but expected to return in 2027.
  • Buyers price licence transferability (requires provincial consent) and tax exposure, protecting themselves with earn-outs, escrow and indemnities.
  • Active licensees include Codere, bplay/Boldt, Betsson, bet365 partners and local lottery operators [list unverified].

7Deep-dive: Peru — the cleanest framework, with a turnover tax as the swing factor

Law 31557 gave Peru a workable MINCETUR licensing regime and a 12% GGR-type tax; the 1% ISC on every bet is the unresolved dispute, and Bill 00519-2026 would raise it to 5%.

Regime

Law 31557 (2022) under MINCETUR gives Peru a regime NEXT.io rates 'among the most workable anywhere', with platforms live since 2025.

  • MINCETUR said in January 2025 more than 60 companies had been authorised; a secondary reading of the register on 1 Oct 2026 lists 46 remote-gaming authorisations [secondary].
  • Operators praise the clarity of authorisation, the technical standards and the absence of local-ownership requirements.

Taxes

Platforms pay a 12% tax on the taxable base plus a 1% ISC on every bet; the activity generated PEN 465m (~$134m) in fiscal revenue in 2025.

  • Impuesto a los Juegos a Distancia y Apuestas Deportivas a Distancia at 12% (Law 31557 art. 42).
  • ISC introduced by Legislative Decree 1644 (Sep 2024) on 'the value of each bet' including bonuses: 0.3% Jan to 30 Jun 2025, 1% from 1 Jul 2025 under DS 008-2025-EF.
  • ISC yields about PEN 324m (~$93m) a year at 1%, implying annual stakes of roughly PEN 32bn (~$9.3bn) and a 12%-tax base of ~PEN 1.2bn (~$0.34bn) [analyst estimate; assumes 465m covers only these two taxes].

ISC dispute

Bill 00519-2026 would raise the ISC from 1% to 5% of each bet, which would push effective GGR-equivalent taxation above 60% for sports and likely collapse channelisation.

  • A bill correcting ISC application to domestic versus foreign platforms (and criminalising match-fixing) was observed by the Executive in July 2025, archived June 2026, and revived September 2026 as proposal 00389/2026-2031-CD.
  • On 6 Oct 2026 deputy Luis Masco Cáceres (Partido Cívico Obras) filed Bill 00519-2026, projecting collections of ~PEN 1.62bn (~$467m), framed as problem-gambling prevention.
  • A 5% turnover tax equals roughly half of a typical sportsbook's gross margin [analyst calc].

Politics and B2Tech view

Peru is the one greenfield build case among the five, but the go/no-go hinges on the ISC vote: investable at 1%, not at 5%.

  • Keiko Fujimori took office in July 2026 after interim president José María Balcázar.
  • The new bicameral Congress has shown both pro-enforcement and pro-tax instincts, with the Executive pursuing clandestine operators while the ISC debate runs.

8Deep-dive: Chile — the pre-regulation option

The last major Latin American market without online regulation; a bill sits in Congress under the Kast government and NEXT.io expects a framework only in late 2027 or 2028.

Regulatory status

Chile is the last major Latin American market without online regulation; NEXT.io expects legislation only in late 2027 or 2028.

  • SCJ regulates land-based casinos; Polla Chilena and Lotería de Concepción hold state lottery and sports-pool rights.
  • Online betting is served by offshore brands, several sponsoring Chilean football and targeted by SCJ complaints and blocking litigation [details unverified].
  • The bill passed the Chamber of Deputies and has sat in the Senate for an extended period under President José Antonio Kast (inaugurated March 2026).

9Deep-dive: Cross-market: tax, licensing, payments, responsible gambling and advertising

The headline rate is the least reliable input; what matters is the base (turnover vs GGR), the stack (VAT, prize withholding, excise), and the likelihood the rule survives the next election.

Tax base

The headline rate is the least reliable input; the region splits into GGR-based, turnover-adjacent and headline-vs-effective regimes.

  • GGR-based: Brazil (12% rising to 15%, plus corporate taxes and 15% on winnings), Colombia (15%/17% rights plus 20% on prizes), Peru's core tax (12%).
  • Turnover-adjacent add-ons: Peru's ISC on every bet (1%, 5% proposed), Colombia's 2025 VAT on deposits (19%), Argentina's national indirect tax on stakes.
  • Headline-vs-effective: Mexico's 50% IEPS versus a ~12–15% effective rate.
  • NEXT.io concludes the announced headline rate is the least reliable part of any Latin American investment case.
“a predictable 20% is more investable than an unpredictable 12%”NEXT.io

Licensing availability

Licence availability is the differentiator: open in Colombia and Peru, revoked in Brazil, closed in Mexico and Argentina, non-existent in Chile.

  • Colombia issues concessions continuously (14 → 21 online operators in seven months; terms to 2031); Peru's MINCETUR authorisation is open and praised for clarity.
  • Brazil's BRL 30m (~$5.8m) five-year authorisation was open but is now revoked.
  • Mexico's federal permits are closed, leaving sub-licensing; Argentina's provincial tenders are closed, leaving acquisition; Chile has no licence to apply for.
  • Local ownership not a barrier in Colombia or Peru; Brazil required a Brazilian-incorporated entity with a local partner holding at least 20% [recall; unverified].

Payments

Payments remain fragmented per NEXT.io's supplier-gap analysis, and Brazil's blackout showed how tightly rails and regulation are coupled.

  • Brazil was Pix-dominant (instant, free, bank-linked); the blackout required banks to refund balances by CPF.
  • Colombia relies on PSE bank transfers, Nequi/Daviplata wallets and cash via Efecty/Baloto, with the 2025 VAT charged at the deposit.
  • Mexico on cards, SPEI and OXXO cash vouchers; Argentina on Mercado Pago and cards under FX controls, unlicensed market on agent cash.
  • Peru on Yape/Plin wallets and cards; Chile on cards and bank transfers [analyst characterisations, not regulator statistics].

Responsible gambling and advertising

Rules are tightening everywhere and now justify fiscal measures; expect deposit limits, ad windows and sponsorship curbs in Colombia and Peru within 12–24 months.

  • Brazil had the most developed toolkit: central self-exclusion (217k requests in 40 days), welfare-recipient blocking (~3m), influencer takedowns (324 profiles), payment-institution reporting (1,255 reports, 550 closures) — then banned advertising and sponsorship outright.
  • Peru's ISC bill is explicitly framed as problem-gambling prevention.
  • Colombia's regulator pairs growth with responsible-gambling campaigns and prize withholding.

2027 planning hierarchy

Practical hierarchy: Colombia first, Peru conditional on the ISC vote, Brazil optionality, Mexico and Argentina via partners, Chile monitored.

  • (1) Colombia — enter or scale now.
  • (2) Peru — build, conditional on the ISC vote.
  • (3) Brazil — preserve optionality for a smaller, sports-weighted market.
  • (4) Mexico and Argentina — partner or acquire with tax and licence-transfer protections.
  • (5) Chile — monitor.

10Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Colombia's licensing window is open and growing: 14 → 21 online operators between end-2025 and July 2026, concessions running to 2031, 10.8m active accounts, and regulator collections +39% YoY (sports betting +84% on the World Cup). Implied annualised online GGR ~COP 4.5tn (~$1.2bn) [estimate]. structural
  • Colombia's 19% online VAT is currently void after the Constitutional Court struck down the emergency decree in April 2026, leaving the effective GGR tax at 15% (17% for low-RTP products) — the lowest certain rate among the regulated markets. mixed
  • Peru's MINCETUR regime is rated among the most workable anywhere: open authorisation, clear technical standards, 12% GGR-type tax, no local-ownership barrier — a genuine greenfield build case if the ISC stays at 1%. structural
  • Brazil's authorised-entity status is a valuable option: Vixio's scenarios include STF injunctive relief or MP lapse by ~4 Mar 2027 with a follow-up decree reinstating compliant operators without a second BRL 30m (~$5.8m) fee. A sports-only restoration is the modal outcome. one-off
  • M&A is delayed, not cancelled: NEXT.io's sources expect deal flow to return in 2027 with valuations driven by regulatory certainty, favouring buyers who can underwrite licence transferability and tax exposure with earn-outs and escrow — a window for a disciplined acquirer in Argentina and Mexico. structural
  • Supply-side gaps recur across all five markets — underdeveloped affiliates, fragmented payments, mismatched product mixes and an overlooked retail channel — giving a B2B/B2C hybrid like B2Tech room to differentiate on localisation rather than price. structural
  • Chile offers cheap optionality: offshore brands already have a foothold, and early relationships with local counsel, media and football properties can be secured ahead of a 2027–28 framework. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Brazil demonstrates the ultimate regulatory tail risk: a BRL 37bn (~$7.1bn) regulated market, 84 licensees and BRL 2.5bn (~$0.48bn) of paid licence fees were eliminated by a single provisional measure, and Lula's 25 Oct re-election removes the lame-duck path to reversal. structural
  • Even a Brazilian reversal returns a smaller market: payment rails, sponsorships and affiliates are severed, the government books a BRL 5.3bn (~$1.0bn) 2027 revenue loss, and the politically viable compromise bans iGaming — the higher-margin vertical. structural
  • Peru's ISC on stakes could go from 1% to 5% (Bill 00519-2026, filed 6 Oct 2026, targeting PEN 1.62bn/~$467m): a 5% turnover tax would exceed half of typical sportsbook gross margin and make the regulated market uncompetitive against offshore. watch
  • Colombia's VAT could return: the Constitutional Court's final ruling on Decree 1474/2025 is pending and the July 2026 bill to make the 19% VAT permanent (COP 1.7tn/~$447m expected) is in the new Congress; a GGR-based VAT stacked on 15% rights would lift effective GGR tax to ~34%. watch
  • Mexico's profitability rests on cost-structuring that converts a 50% headline IEPS into a ~12–15% effective rate; a tax-authority challenge or political shift would re-price every incumbent and sub-licensee at once, and the 1947 law offers no modern licensing route. governance
  • Argentina's black market is domestic and agent-based (WhatsApp credit resale), with regulated GGR ~$1.1bn vs ~$1.2bn offshore and adviser estimates of up to 80% unlicensed; licences are closed, so entry means paying for a provincial licence-holder whose transferability needs regulator consent. structural
  • Data quality is uneven: only Brazil (pre-ban) and Colombia publish regulator-grade GGR or proxy series; Mexico, Argentina, Peru and Chile require reliance on H2/trade estimates or back-solving from tax receipts, which widens valuation error bars. mixed

11Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The regional bridge from 2025 to 2027 is dominated by Brazil: the ~BRL 40bn (~$7.7bn) annualised regulated GGR run-rate of H1-26 goes to zero from Q4-26 and, in our base case, returns in H2-27 as a sports-only market worth perhaps 40–55% of its prior size [analyst estimate]. Against that, Colombia adds growth (implied online GGR ~COP 4.5tn/~$1.2bn annualised, World Cup-inflated, with VAT void), Peru's trajectory bifurcates on the ISC vote, Mexico and Argentina move only through M&A, and Chile contributes nothing before 2028. Three new presidents (Chile, Peru, Colombia) and a re-elected Lula set the policy tone; NEXT.io's sources expect much of what stalled in 2026 to resolve within 12 months.

Independent view. Brazil: we assign roughly 55% probability to a sports-betting-only restoration via amended MP or follow-on bill by mid-2027, 25% to full restoration (STF injunction or MP lapse with reinstatement decree), 20% to prohibition persisting through 2027 [analyst judgement]. Any restored regime will carry tighter advertising, deposit limits and likely the 15% GGR rate from day one; licence fees are unlikely to be re-charged for compliant operators. Colombia: online GGR growth of 20–30% in 2027 [est.] off a World Cup-inflated 2026 base, with the main swing being the VAT — we think a right-leaning government and the Constitutional Court make a permanent 19% VAT less likely than not, but a GGR-based version at a lower rate is a plausible compromise. Peru: the 5% ISC is unlikely to pass unamended, but a 2–3% rate is a realistic risk; the ISC-correction bill for foreign platforms is likelier to pass. Mexico: no structural change before the 2027 mid-terms; effective tax stays well below the 50% headline unless SAT acts. Argentina: provincial regulators will prioritise agent-network enforcement and payment controls over new licensing; expect two or three licence-holder transactions in 2027. Chile: Senate passage in 2027 is possible; licences no earlier than 2028. Leverage direction: regional regulated GGR falls in 2027 versus 2026 because of Brazil, even as the other five grow; operators with Brazil concentration face covenant pressure, which will surface distressed assets.
