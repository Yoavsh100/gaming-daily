9M 2026 · market & regulatory brief

Macau

Released 1 Oct 2026 (DICJ Sept/9M GGR); 17 Oct 2026 (DICJ Q3 by game); 26 Oct 2026 (DSEC 9M arrivals); 12 Oct 2026 (DSF 9M gaming tax) · Currency MOP (Macau pataca) — tables in MOP billions unless stated, with ~USD alongside at USD/MOP 8.07 · Data confidence: MEDIUM

The one-line verdict

Macau's 9M-2026 casino GGR of MOP187.12bn (~$23.19bn, +3.2% YoY) flatters a market that has rolled over: four straight monthly year-on-year declines through September (five through October), a Q3 of MOP60.22bn (~$7.46bn, -3.8%) driven by a 7.9% fall in mass/premium-mass baccarat, and GGR per visitor down ~5% even as arrivals rose 8.5%. The regime is stable but closed — six concessions locked to 31 Dec 2032, a 40% effective levy on GGR, zero licensed online — so for B2Tech the only realistic exposure is B2B supply into the one product line still growing, slots (+~19% YoY).

1Executive summary

  • World's largest casino market, but growth has stalled: DICJ-reported casino GGR for Jan–Sep 2026 was MOP187.12bn (~$23.19bn), +3.2% YoY; through October it was MOP208.79bn (~$25.87bn), +1.6%. FY2025 was MOP247.40bn (~$30.66bn), +9.1%, still only 84.6% of 2019's MOP292.46bn (~$36.24bn). All figures are gross gaming revenue (win before tax); Macau does not report NGR.
  • The trajectory inside the 9M number is negative: cumulative growth decayed from +24.0% (Jan) to +12.1% (4M), +6.8% (H1), +3.2% (9M) and +1.6% (10M). Quarterly: Q1 +14.5% (MOP66.04bn, ~$8.18bn), Q2 flat (MOP61.2bn, ~$7.58bn), Q3 -3.8% (MOP60.22bn, ~$7.46bn). Monthly YoY: Jun -12.1%, Jul -8.4%, Aug -1.2%, Sep -1.2%, Oct -10.0% — five consecutive declines, October falling despite Golden Week.
  • Product mix — it is a premium-mass problem, not a VIP problem: Q3 mass baccarat (DICJ's line includes premium mass) fell 7.9% to MOP33.61bn (~$4.16bn), a MOP2.88bn (~$357m) decline larger than the whole market's MOP2.35bn (~$291m) fall; VIP baccarat was flat at MOP16.91bn (~$2.10bn, +0.1%); slots +18.8% to MOP4.09bn (~$0.51bn); live multi-game ETGs -8% to ~MOP1.14bn (~$0.14bn). Summing the three DICJ quarterlies: 9M mass baccarat MOP105.4bn (~$13.06bn, ~-1%), VIP MOP52.4bn (~$6.49bn, ~+10%), slots MOP12.2bn (~$1.51bn, ~+19%).
  • Baccarat is the market: baccarat (mass + VIP) was 85.1% of GGR across 16 approved games of fortune in 2025 (DICJ); in Q3-26 mass baccarat was 55.8% of GGR and VIP baccarat 28.0%, so ~84% combined. Slots have only reached 6.8% of GGR (from 5.5% a year earlier) — the single structural growth line.
  • Channel: 100% land-based, 0% licensed online. Law 16/2001 (as revised by Law 7/2022) licenses only casino games of fortune in physical casinos; there is no interactive/online licence class and no proposal to create one. 'Online penetration' is therefore nil by law; any digital exposure is illegal cross-border activity targeted by Macau's 2022 illegal-gambling law and the PRC's criminalisation of cross-border gambling.
  • Licensing regime — closed until 2032: six concessions (SJM Resorts, Galaxy Casino, Venetian Macau/Sands China, Melco Resorts (Macau), Wynn Resorts (Macau), MGM Grand Paradise) awarded 16 Dec 2022 by Executive Orders 218–223/2022 after a 29 Jul–14 Sep 2022 tender with seven bidders; 10-year contracts effective 1 Jan 2023. The revised law caps concessions at six and abolished sub-concessions. No new B2C entry is possible before a re-tender ahead of 31 Dec 2032.
  • Consolidation is complete: 20 casinos now operate (SJM 5, Sands 5, Galaxy 3, Melco 3, Wynn 2, MGM 2) versus a 2021 peak of 42, after the three-year transition for third-party 'satellite' casinos ended 31 Dec 2025 with none surviving; their ~480 tables and ~270 machines (June 2025, DICJ) were reallocated to the concessionaires. The floor stock is ~6,000 tables and ~12,000 machines (DICJ, end-2025 and end-H1 2026).
  • Tax regime — base is GGR, headline 35%, effective 40%: Article 27 of the gaming law levies a 35% special gaming tax on gross gaming revenue, payable by the 10th of the following month, plus mandated contributions of up to 5% of GGR (public foundation, urban development/tourism/social security), giving a 40% effective rate — among the highest GGR-based rates of any major casino jurisdiction. No change was enacted or proposed in 2026. Fixed/variable annual premiums per table and machine and minimum-GGR-per-table top-ups also apply [parameters not re-verified this session].
  • Tax take: DSF recorded MOP74.56bn (~$9.24bn) of gaming revenue in Jan–Sep 2026, +5.9% YoY (Sept alone MOP8.39bn, ~$1.04bn), equal to 80.6% of the FY2026 gaming-tax budget of MOP92.53bn (~$11.47bn) and 84.1% of government current revenue (MOP88.71bn, ~$10.99bn). FY2025 gaming taxes were MOP94.9bn (~$11.76bn), +7.6%, 76.6% of total government revenue of MOP124.1bn (~$15.38bn).
  • Government target will be met, but says little: the 2026 budget assumes GGR of MOP236bn (~$29.24bn), already below 2025's actual MOP247.4bn; 79% was achieved by September, and Nov–Dec need only MOP27.2bn (~$3.37bn) versus MOP41.98bn (~$5.20bn) in Nov–Dec 2025. CE Sam Hou Fai has refused to revise the figure. Our estimate for FY2026 is MOP245–250bn (~$30.4–31.0bn), i.e. -1% to +1% YoY [estimate].
  • Visitation is rising while spend per head falls: DSEC counted 32,181,272 arrivals in 9M-26 (+8.5%); September's 3,216,535 (+15.9%) was driven by same-day visitors (+23.8% to 1.93m) rather than overnight (+5.7% to 1.28m). International arrivals were only 1,949,417 (+3.4%), ~6% of the total. Implied GGR per visitor was ~MOP5,815 (~$721) in 9M-26, ~-4.9% YoY, and ~MOP5,615 (~$696) in September, ~-15% YoY.
  • Operator landscape: six concessionaires; DICJ does not publish operator GGR shares, so market-share data is n/a from primary sources (company filings would be required). Galaxy's Q3 release corroborates the market: HKD181.7bn 9M market GGR (+3%) and HKD58.5bn in Q3 (-4%).
  • VIP and junkets — smaller, concessionaire-controlled, and volatile: DICJ defines VIP by table classification, not by junket play. VIP baccarat jumped +35.4% in Q1 (MOP19.56bn, ~$2.42bn; 29.7% share), fell 18.8% QoQ in Q2 on low hold and the World Cup, and recovered +6.3% QoQ in Q3. Licensed gaming promoters numbered 29 at end-June 2026 (31 at end-March). Seaport estimates October VIP GGR fell >35% YoY, citing mainland capital controls — the segment remains hostage to PRC policy.
  • Payments reality: play is cash/chip-based (HKD-denominated chips dominate), with mainland UnionPay and cross-border cash subject to PRC capital controls; there is no licensed digital-wallet or online funding channel. Capital-control enforcement is a direct GGR driver (Seaport's October VIP read) rather than a compliance footnote.
  • Responsible gambling and advertising are restrictive and stable: minimum entry/employment age is 21 (Law 10/2012), a DICJ-administered self-exclusion scheme exists, main-floor smoking is prohibited with approved lounges, and direct advertising of games of fortune is prohibited in Macau — concessionaires market the resort, not the game [regime details from statute, not re-read this session].
  • Enforcement and political direction of travel: tighter, not looser. The 2022 package (Law 7/2022 gaming law, Law 16/2022 on gaming promoters, the illegal-gambling law) ended junket revenue-share and junket-run VIP rooms, closed satellites, and tied concessions to non-gaming investment pledges; Beijing's criminalisation of cross-border gambling continues. Policy priority is fiscal stability and 'adequate diversification', not market expansion.
  • Labour: 53,272 persons engaged in gaming at end-2025 (10.7% of a 496,600 labour force, DSEC); average full-time gaming earnings MOP28,660 (~$3,551)/month in June 2026 (+4.6%), dealers (41.9% of the workforce) MOP22,130 (~$2,742, +1.5%). Wage inflation against falling GGR compresses operator margins.
  • Non-casino betting is immaterial: Macau Slot's exclusive football and basketball concession generated MOP90m (~$11.2m, -10%) and MOP73m (~$9.0m, +23.7%) respectively in Q1-26 — ~0.25% of casino GGR; the concession was extended to 5 Jun 2026 pending a review of the sports-betting model (outcome after that date: n/a). Horse racing ended 1 Apr 2024; greyhound racing ended 20 Jul 2018.
  • Read-through for B2Tech: there is no B2C route (no licence class, no online, no sub-concessions, next tender 2032). The commercially live exposure is B2B — slot/ETG supply and content into a floor that is shifting premium-mass players onto machines (slots +19%, 12,000 machines, consolidated into six buyers) — or services to concessionaires/promoters, each requiring DICJ approval. Entry economics must assume a 40% GGR levy on the operator side, a demand base that is ~95% Greater China, and policy risk set in Beijing rather than Macau.
  • Data caveats: DICJ's quarterly by-game series sums to MOP187.46bn for 9M-26 versus MOP187.12bn in the monthly series (~0.2% definitional/timing gap); one outlet (GGRAsia) printed September GGR as MOP17.84bn against MOP18.06bn in all other reports — we use MOP18.06bn, consistent with October's reported +20.0% MoM.

3Deep-dive: Market trajectory & demand (monthly GGR, visitation)

A +3.2% nine-month print built entirely on Q1; the run-rate since June is negative and October's -10% came in a Golden Week month.

Monthly GGR series

A +3.2% nine-month print was built entirely on Q1 (+14.5%); the run-rate since June is negative.

  • January MOP22.63bn (~$2.80bn, +24.0%), March MOP22.61bn (~$2.80bn, +15.0%), May MOP22.61bn (+6.7%) — three of the strongest post-pandemic months.
  • June MOP18.52bn (~$2.29bn, -12.1%), July MOP20.26bn (~$2.51bn, -8.4%), August MOP21.89bn (~$2.71bn, -1.2%).
  • September MOP18.06bn (~$2.24bn, -1.2%), the lowest month since September 2024.
  • October MOP21.68bn (~$2.69bn, -10.0%) against MOP24.09bn (~$2.99bn) a year earlier, in a Golden Week month.
  • 9M total MOP187.12bn (~$23.19bn), +3.2% only because Q1 was +14.5%.

World Cup and base effects

The World Cup explains June and July, but August, September and October declined without a tournament; spend per head is weakening, not just calendar noise.

  • World Cup (expanded 48-team, 104-match format, June–July) is the consensus explanation; CreditSights argues those months should be excluded when assessing structural health.
  • October contained National Day Golden Week and the NBA China Games at the Venetian, yet fell by the second-largest margin of the year.
  • The honest reading: 2025's strong second-half base is now compounded by weaker spend per head.

Visitation and mix

Visitation is not the constraint; the composition is, with GGR per visitor ~4.9% below 9M-25 and ~15% below in September.

  • DSEC recorded 32,181,272 arrivals in 9M-26 (+8.5%) and 3,216,535 in September (+15.9%); Q3 ~11.2m (+8% YoY, +15% QoQ).
  • September growth came from same-day visitors (+23.8% to 1,933,257) rather than overnight guests (+5.7% to 1,283,278).
  • International arrivals 1,949,417 in 9M (+3.4%), only ~6% of total despite being the concession contracts' stated diversification target.
  • GGR per visitor ~MOP5,815 (~$721) in 9M-26 versus ~MOP6,113 (~$757) in 9M-25; ~MOP5,615 (~$696) in September, ~15% below September 2025.

FY2026 outlook

We model FY2026 at MOP245–250bn (~$30.4–31.0bn), -1% to +1% YoY [estimate], the first flat-to-down year since reopening.

  • 10M GGR MOP208.79bn (~$25.87bn) leaves only MOP27.2bn (~$3.37bn) needed in November–December to hit the government's MOP236bn (~$29.24bn) budget.
  • The same two months of 2025 delivered MOP41.98bn (~$5.20bn).
  • Seaport's November forecast is -6.1% YoY (~MOP19.8bn, ~$2.45bn), with VIP down more than 35% in October.
  • The government target was set below the prior-year actual, masking the flat-to-down outcome.

Operator implication

Macau's volume engine (visitor count) is intact but its value engine (overnight, premium-mass mainland spend) is not, and the gap widens monthly.

  • Track the same-day/overnight mix and GGR-per-visitor rather than headline GGR.
  • These are the earliest indicators of whether Q4-26 stabilises.

4Deep-dive: Mass market: premium mass, grind mass, slots & ETGs

Mass baccarat — 56% of GGR and the post-junket growth engine — fell 7.9% in Q3; slots are the only line compounding.

Mass baccarat

Mass baccarat, 56% of GGR and the post-junket growth engine, fell 7.9% YoY in Q3, exceeding the whole market's decline.

  • Q1 MOP36.56bn (~$4.53bn, +6.5%), Q2 MOP35.2bn (~$4.36bn, -1.2%), Q3 MOP33.61bn (~$4.16bn, -7.9% YoY, -4.4% QoQ).
  • Share fell from 59.4% a year earlier to 55.8%.
  • Q3 decline of MOP2.88bn (~$357m) exceeded the market's MOP2.35bn (~$291m) fall — every other product combined was positive.
  • 9M mass baccarat ~MOP105.4bn (~$13.06bn), roughly -1% YoY, against +6.5% at end-Q1.

Broader mass segment

Mass including slots and ETGs fell 5.2% YoY in Q3 to MOP43.31bn, the segment that determines EBITDA margins.

  • Q2 MOP45.13bn (~$5.59bn, +0.8%); Q3 MOP43.31bn (~$5.37bn, -5.2%, -4.0% QoQ), 71.9% of GGR.
  • Premium mass is where concessionaires reinvested after junket VIP was dismantled in 2022–23; most exposed to mainland wealth effects, property sentiment and capital controls.
  • Trade commentary on April cited 'low VIP hold', but no hold explanation exists for three consecutive quarters of mass-baccarat weakening.

Slots and ETGs

Slots are the only line compounding, up 18.8% in Q3, but a 6.8% share cannot offset premium-mass losses.

  • Slots MOP3.98bn (~$0.49bn, +21.6%) Q1, MOP4.11bn (~$0.51bn, +17.0%) Q2, MOP4.09bn (~$0.51bn, +18.8%) Q3; share 6.8% from 5.5%.
  • Live multi-game ETGs MOP1.26bn (~$0.16bn, +3.5%), MOP1.30bn (~$0.16bn, +3.1%) and ~MOP1.14bn (~$0.14bn, -8%).
  • Drivers are structural: tables capped (~6,000), satellite tables and machines reallocated to concessionaires' floors at end-2025, lower-value play steered to machines.
  • Q3 slot gain ~MOP0.65bn (~$80m) YoY versus MOP2.88bn (~$357m) mass-baccarat loss.

Floor productivity

Supply is static at ~6,000 tables and ~12,000 machines over 20 casinos, yielding ~MOP2.4bn mass GGR per 1,000 tables per quarter.

  • DICJ counts at end-2025 and end-H1 2026.
  • Quarterly mass segment ~MOP43–45bn implies ~MOP2.4bn (~$0.30bn) per 1,000 tables per quarter.
  • Productivity can only be lifted through premium-mass yield — exactly the variable deteriorating.

B2B implications

Machine product spend is growing double-digit while everything else is flat or down; model revenue-share deals on mass GGR falling mid-single-digit in H2-26.

  • Consolidation into six buyers simplifies distribution but raises buyer negotiating power.
  • Performance-linked structures should not be modelled on the 9M headline.

5Deep-dive: VIP baccarat & gaming promoters (junkets)

VIP is now a concessionaire-run, ~28%-of-GGR product: +35% in Q1 on hold and base effects, -19% QoQ in Q2, flat YoY in Q3, and reportedly -35% in October.

VIP baccarat performance

VIP baccarat is ~28% of GGR: +35% in Q1 on hold and base effects, -19% QoQ in Q2, flat YoY in Q3.

  • DICJ 'VIP baccarat' is a table classification, not junket-intermediated play; concessionaires' own VIP definitions differ materially.
  • Q1-26 MOP19.56bn (~$2.42bn, +35.4% YoY, 29.7% share); Q2 MOP15.90bn (~$1.97bn, -2.6% YoY, -18.8% QoQ, 26.0% share).
  • Q3 MOP16.91bn (~$2.10bn, +0.1% YoY, +6.3% QoQ, 28.0% share); ~MOP52.4bn (~$6.49bn) over nine months, roughly +10% YoY on our summation.
  • Q1 surge coincided with entertainment-led premium promotion and a weak Q1-25 base; Q2 drop attributed to low hold and the World Cup.

Structural ceiling

VIP oscillates in a 26–30% band rather than recovering toward the junket-era 50–60%; Law 16/2022 sets the regulatory ceiling.

  • Share was 30.7% in Q4-25 and ~25% in Q1-25.
  • Law 16/2022 (effective 1 Jan 2023) limits promoters to one concessionaire, bans revenue-sharing, prohibits junket VIP rooms and deposit-taking, makes concessionaires liable [statute parameters not re-read this session].
  • 29 licensed gaming promoters at end-June 2026, down from 31 at end-March, versus well over a hundred before 2022.

Forward indicators

Seaport estimates October VIP GGR fell more than 35% YoY on mainland capital controls, with November market GGR forecast at -6.1%.

  • Mass fell low-single-digit in October.
  • VIP is now mostly premium-direct credit from concessionaires, so volatility flows to operator receivables and provisions rather than junket balance sheets.

B2Tech read-through

The VIP/junket channel is not an entry point; VIP is a ±MOP3–4bn (~$0.4–0.5bn) quarterly swing factor making forecasting unusually noisy.

  • Promoter licences are annual, DICJ-vetted, commission-capped and tied to a single concessionaire.
  • Economics without revenue share do not support an independent operator model.
  • Any revenue-linked B2B contract inherits this hold- and policy-driven noise.

6Deep-dive: Licensing & concession regime

Six 10-year concessions to 31 Dec 2032, no sub-concessions, no online class and no satellites: the most closed major casino jurisdiction in the world.

Legal framework

Six 10-year concessions, no sub-concessions, no online class and no satellites make Macau the most closed major casino jurisdiction.

  • Law 16/2001 revised by Law 7/2022 (passed 21 June 2022, gazetted 22 June 2022); Administrative Regulation 28/2022 governed the tender.
  • DICJ regulates and inspects; DSF assesses and collects gaming taxes and may audit jointly with DICJ.
  • Maximum six concessions, sub-concessions abolished (previously three plus three), terms capped at ten years, extendable up to three years in exceptional circumstances [extension clause not re-read this session].

2022 tender

All six incumbents were re-awarded concessions running 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2032; the next entry window is the re-tender ahead of 2033.

  • Tender ran 29 July–14 September 2022 with seven bidders; provisional results 26 November 2022 (Executive Order 205/2022), definitive awards 16 December 2022 (Executive Orders 218–223/2022).
  • Winners: MGM Grand Paradise, Galaxy Casino, Venetian Macau, Melco Resorts (Macau), Wynn Resorts (Macau), SJM Resorts.
  • Genting was unsuccessful; no public signal of licence expansion.

Concession conditions

Local incorporation, MOP5bn share capital, resident managing director with 15%, non-gaming pledges and the end of satellites define this cycle.

  • Minimum share capital MOP5bn (~$620m); Macau-permanent-resident managing director holding at least 15% [thresholds not re-read this session].
  • Non-gaming investment pledged across ten categories (MICE, entertainment, sport, culture, health, themed attractions).
  • Satellite casino transition expired 31 December 2025; none continued; 11 venues' ~480 tables and ~270 machines reallocated to sponsoring concessionaires.
  • Casino count fell from 42 (2021) to 20.
“follow up closely on the implementation of the investment projects”DICJ

Economic terms

Beyond the 35% tax, premiums and minimum GGR per table make marginal tables a cost, pushing grind-mass play to machines.

  • Annual premium: fixed MOP30m (~$3.7m) plus MOP300,000 (~$37k) per VIP table, MOP150,000 (~$19k) per mass table, MOP1,000 (~$124) per machine, subject to a minimum.
  • Minimum annual GGR per table and machine below which the shortfall is taxed as if earned [all unverified this session].
  • Table allocations are a managed resource.

Entry routes

No B2C path exists until the early 2030s; realistic routes are equipment supply, services to a concessionaire or a promoter licence.

  • The 2022 process showed incumbency, local shareholding, non-gaming pledges and political acceptability outweigh gaming expertise.
  • Equipment must be certified against DICJ technical standards and supplied through approved manufacturers/distributors.
  • Each route is DICJ-approved and dependent on a concessionaire counterparty.

7Deep-dive: Tax & fiscal dependence

A 40% effective levy on GGR delivered MOP74.56bn (~$9.24bn) in nine months — 84% of government current revenue — which is precisely why the regime will not loosen.

Tax structure

A 40% effective levy on GGR — 35% special tax plus up to 5% contributions — with no change enacted or proposed in 2026.

  • Tax base is gross gaming revenue, not turnover or net of promotional allowances; Article 27 sets 35%, payable monthly by the 10th.
  • Contributions up to 2% to a public foundation and up to 3% to urban development, tourism and social security [not re-read this session].
  • No player-level withholding; concessionaires historically exempted from complementary tax on gaming profits [unverified this session].

2026 receipts

9M gaming receipts of MOP74.56bn (~$9.24bn, +5.9%) are 80.6% of budget; we estimate FY2026 at MOP96–99bn (~$11.9–12.3bn) [estimate].

  • Receipts: MOP34.87bn (~$4.32bn, +16.9%) at 4M, MOP51.19bn (~$6.34bn, +13%) at H1, MOP66.17bn (~$8.20bn, +6.9%) at 8M.
  • Growth decay lags GGR by roughly a month; September receipts MOP8.39bn (~$1.04bn) relate largely to August GGR of MOP21.89bn.
  • Receipts equal 39.8% of 9M GGR, consistent with the 40% effective rate.
  • FY2026 gaming-tax budget MOP92.53bn (~$11.47bn) will be exceeded even on a weak Q4.

Fiscal dependence

Gaming taxes were 84.1% of government current revenue in 9M-26; dependence is extreme and rising.

  • Current revenue MOP88.71bn (~$10.99bn) in 9M-26; gaming share 86.4% at 8M, 86.5% at 4M.
  • FY2025 gaming concession revenue was 76.6% of total revenue of MOP124.1bn (~$15.38bn); gaming taxes MOP94.9bn (~$11.76bn, +7.6%) beat the MOP93.1bn (~$11.54bn) budget.
  • FY2025 expenditure MOP102.9bn (~$12.75bn), so the surplus depends on the 40% levy.
“remains unchanged”Legislative Assembly budget committee

Policy consequence

The government has no room to raise the rate, but its leverage over operators is total; assume 40% plus premium plus mandated reinvestment with no relief.

  • Every signal (target maintenance, refusal to revise forecasts, event subsidies) is about protecting GGR.
  • Dependence drove minimum-GGR-per-table rules, non-gaming pledges and the power to terminate concessions on national-security grounds.

8Deep-dive: Payments, AML, responsible gambling & advertising

Cash, chips and UnionPay under PRC capital controls; no legal digital funding rail; a 21+ floor with no gaming advertising — restrictions are stable, but Beijing's enforcement is the swing variable.

Payments

No legal digital funding rail exists; play is funded by cash, UnionPay under PRC capital controls, and concessionaire credit.

  • No licensed online channel, so no e-wallet, card-not-present or open-banking funding.
  • Floors transact in HKD-denominated chips (MOP pegged to HKD at 1.03, HKD to USD).
  • UnionPay cards subject to PRC annual overseas cash-withdrawal caps and monitoring [limits and operator credit not re-verified this session].
  • Seaport's attribution of October's >35% VIP fall to 'China capital controls' shows Beijing-set payment friction is a first-order GGR driver.

AML and criminal risk

Any model touching mainland customer acquisition, including digital marketing, carries criminal rather than merely regulatory risk.

  • AML under Law 2/2006 and DICJ instructions; Financial Intelligence Office (GIF) is the FIU [thresholds not re-read this session].
  • 2022 amendments made concessionaires jointly responsible for promoter conduct; 2022 illegal-gambling law criminalised unauthorised gaming, side-betting and illegal online gambling.
  • 2021 PRC Criminal Law Article 303 amendment criminalised organising cross-border gambling for PRC citizens.

Responsible gambling and advertising

A codified 21+ floor, exclusion scheme, smoking ban and a prohibition on gaming advertising keep all marketing non-gaming in form.

  • Law 10/2012 sets entry and employment age at 21 and bars off-duty staff from floors; DICJ runs self-/third-party exclusion.
  • Main-floor smoking ban with approved lounges since 2019 [not re-read this session].
  • Direct advertising of games of fortune is prohibited; marketing is concerts, NBA China Games and branded entertainment, routed through entertainment and MICE.

Entrant implication

Customer acquisition in Macau is a venue-and-events business, not performance marketing; standard online levers are prohibited or legally hazardous.

  • No 2026 data located on problem-gambling prevalence, exclusion enrolments or AML filings (n/a).
  • Bonusing, affiliate marketing and app distribution are prohibited or hazardous when aimed at the ~95% Greater-China customer base.

9Deep-dive: Non-casino betting & online

Sports betting is a sub-0.3%-of-GGR monopoly in regulatory limbo; racing is gone; online does not exist as a licensed product.

Sports betting

Macau Slot's exclusive football and basketball concession is about 0.25% of Q1 casino GGR and sits in regulatory limbo after 5 June 2026.

  • Q1-26 football GGR MOP90m (~$11.2m, -10% YoY), basketball MOP73m (~$9.0m, +23.7%); MOP163m (~$20.2m) combined.
  • Concession in place since 1998, extended one year to 5 June 2026 for a review of the sports-betting model alongside lottery and non-gaming entertainment.
  • Outcome and post-5 June 2026 status could not be established (n/a); Q2 and Q3 figures not located (n/a), though Q3 trade coverage referenced 'higher revenue'.

Racing and lotteries

Racing is gone and lotteries are immaterial; the DICJ-regulated universe is ~99.7% casino games of fortune.

  • Canidrome ended greyhound racing on 20 July 2018; Macau Horse Racing Company ended horse racing on 1 April 2024 (DICJ).
  • Chinese lotteries (pacapio and similar) remain licensed but immaterial; no 2026 figures located (n/a).

Online gaming

Online gaming is absent from the legal framework and illegal for both Macau operators and mainland customers, with no consultation launched.

  • Law 16/2001 and its 2022 revision license 'casino games of fortune' in approved physical premises only; no interactive or remote class.
  • 2022 illegal-gambling law and mainland cross-border gambling offences compound the prohibition.
  • Hengqin/Guangdong-Macao cooperation-zone initiatives include no gaming liberalisation.

B2Tech implication

Macau offers no addressable online market and no credible timeline; treat it as land-based B2B supply and a Greater-China demand barometer.

  • The sports-betting monopoly review is the only live licensing process, it is tiny, and nothing suggests opening to foreign online operators.
  • Not a market to enter.

10Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Slots/ETGs are the only compounding product: 9M slot GGR ~MOP12.18bn (~$1.51bn, ~+19% YoY), share up to 6.8% of GGR from 5.5%, driven by capped table counts, satellite-floor reallocation and the deliberate migration of grind-mass play to machines — a structural B2B opening into a ~12,000-machine installed base now bought by just six counterparties. structural
  • Scale and fiscal alignment: Macau remains the world's largest casino market (FY25 GGR MOP247.4bn, ~$30.66bn, more than double the Las Vegas Strip) and the government's 84% revenue dependence means policy is oriented to protecting GGR — targets maintained, events subsidised, no tax rise contemplated. structural
  • Regulatory certainty to 2032: six concessions with fixed terms, a 35%+5% levy unchanged since 2002, consolidation finished (20 casinos, no satellites) — contract and counterparty risk for suppliers is low by emerging-market standards. structural
  • Top-of-funnel demand is still growing: 9M arrivals 32.18m (+8.5%), September +15.9%, Q3 11.2m; entertainment-led promotion (concerts, NBA China Games) is demonstrably pulling volume even if conversion to spend is weak. mixed
  • VIP within concessionaire-run rooms is up ~10% over 9M (MOP52.4bn, ~$6.49bn) and recovered +6.3% QoQ in Q3, showing premium-direct capacity survived the junket purge — though Q1's +35% was hold- and base-assisted and October's reported -35% shows the volatility. mixed
  • Non-gaming investment pledges and the 'international visitor' mandate create demand for entertainment, MICE and technology partners, with international arrivals (+3.4%) the KPI DICJ monitors — an adjacent services opening for non-gaming B2B. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Demand has rolled over: five consecutive monthly YoY declines (Jun -12.1%, Jul -8.4%, Aug -1.2%, Sep -1.2%, Oct -10.0%), Q3 -3.8%, and an October fall in a Golden Week month — the World Cup excuse no longer covers it. watch
  • Premium-mass erosion is the core risk: mass baccarat -7.9% in Q3 (MOP33.61bn, ~$4.16bn), a MOP2.88bn (~$357m) decline exceeding the whole market's drop; GGR per visitor ~-15% in September as same-day visitors (+23.8%) displace overnight guests (+5.7%). structural
  • Closed market: no B2C entry before a re-tender ahead of 31 Dec 2032, no sub-concessions, no online licence class, a 2022 tender won entirely by incumbents on non-gaming pledges and local shareholding — an outside operator's probability of a licence is effectively nil this decade. structural
  • Levy economics: 40% effective tax on GGR plus annual premiums per table/machine and minimum-GGR-per-table top-ups [parameters unverified this session] leave operators with thin margins when GGR falls and wages rise (full-time gaming earnings +4.6% to MOP28,660, ~$3,551/month). structural
  • Policy risk is set in Beijing, not Macau: capital-control enforcement (Seaport's October VIP >-35%), cross-border gambling criminalisation and visa policy move GGR by double digits with no local recourse; ~94% of visitors are Greater China. governance
  • The government's 84% fiscal dependence makes it an aggressive principal: minimum-GGR rules, investment-pledge monitoring, national-security termination rights and the satellite ban show the state will restructure the industry when revenue is threatened. governance
  • Transparency gaps: DICJ does not publish operator shares; VIP is defined by table class not player type; quarterly and monthly series differ (~0.2%); one outlet misreported September GGR — any contract indexed to 'market GGR' needs a precise definition. watch

11Regulatory & market outlook

Guidance bridge. The only official 'guidance' is the 2026 budget's GGR assumption of MOP236bn (~$29.24bn), set in late 2025 below the MOP247.4bn (~$30.66bn) actually earned in 2025 and left unchanged by CE Sam Hou Fai despite four (now five) months of YoY declines. With MOP208.79bn (~$25.87bn) banked through October, the target needs only MOP27.2bn (~$3.37bn) from November–December versus MOP41.98bn (~$5.20bn) a year earlier, so it will be met; the real question is the exit rate. Seaport models November at -6.1% YoY; carrying a similar decline into December yields FY2026 GGR of roughly MOP245–250bn (~$30.4–31.0bn), -1% to +1% YoY [estimate], and FY2026 gaming-tax receipts of MOP96–99bn (~$11.9–12.3bn) against a MOP92.53bn (~$11.47bn) budget [estimate]. The deterioration is concentrated in mass/premium-mass baccarat (Q3 -7.9%), partially offset by slots (+19%) and a flat-to-recovering VIP line; the 2027 budget assumption, due in the Legislative Assembly's November–December debate, is the next formal data point.

Independent view. Market: we expect FY2026 to be Macau's first flat-to-down year since reopening and 2027 to deliver at best low-single-digit growth, with premium-mass GGR per visitor the binding constraint; slots should keep growing mid-teens and reach ~7–8% of GGR by end-2027 [estimate], while VIP oscillates within a 25–30% share band on hold and capital-control swings. Rules: no change to the 35%+5% levy, the six-concession cap or the online prohibition through 2032; direction of travel is incremental tightening (AML thresholds, promoter oversight, minimum-GGR enforcement, investment-pledge audits) plus pressure on concessionaires to deliver international visitation. The only live licensing decision is the Macau Slot sports-betting model, which is immaterial in revenue terms. Fiscal: receipts will beat budget again, keeping the government in surplus and removing any incentive to liberalise. Swing factors: PRC capital-control and visa policy (the dominant variable), mainland property/wealth effects on premium mass, a hold-driven VIP rebound, and whether Golden Week-style event programming can convert same-day volume into overnight spend. For B2Tech: treat Macau as a B2B machine/systems opportunity sized to a ~MOP16bn (~$2.0bn) annual slot win pool [estimate] and as a Greater-China demand barometer, not as an operating market.

What to watch next quarter

  • November and December DICJ monthly GGR (released 1 Dec / 1 Jan): whether the YoY decline narrows from October's -10% and whether FY2026 lands above or below MOP247.4bn.
  • The 2027 budget's GGR assumption and gaming-tax budget in the Legislative Assembly (Nov–Dec 2026) — a figure below ~MOP240bn would signal official acceptance of a down-cycle.
  • Q4-26 DICJ by-game data (mid-January): mass-baccarat YoY (Q3 -7.9%) and whether slot growth holds >15%; VIP share versus the 30.7% of Q4-25.
  • DSEC visitor mix — overnight vs same-day arrivals, average length of stay and international share (6.1% in 9M) — the leading indicators for GGR per visitor.
  • Licensed gaming-promoter count (29 at end-June) and any DICJ action on promoter or concessionaire AML/CDD thresholds.
  • Outcome of the Macau Slot sports-betting concession review (extended to 5 Jun 2026) — whether the model is re-tendered, extended again or absorbed.
  • PRC policy: UnionPay/cash-withdrawal enforcement, cross-border gambling prosecutions, mainland visa and multi-entry schemes (Hengqin/Zhuhai), and any mainland macro stimulus affecting premium-mass wealth.
  • DICJ follow-up on concessionaire non-gaming investment delivery and any adjustments to table/machine allocations after the satellite reallocation.
  • Wage inflation vs GGR: gaming employment (53,272) and average earnings (+4.6%) against falling revenue — a margin signal for concessionaire capex and procurement appetite.
