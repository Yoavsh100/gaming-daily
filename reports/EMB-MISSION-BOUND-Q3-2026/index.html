Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

EMB Mission Bound

Released 20 Nov 2026 · Currency SEK (tables in SEK m; ~USD m in brackets at USD/SEK 10.23 as of 20 Nov 2026) · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Shares closed around SEK 0.59 (~$0.058) on 20 Nov, c.+2% vs the SEK 0.578 prior close per Yahoo Finance quote data [day-move partially unverified — no official daily close series retrieved]; the stock had been SEK 0.522 on 17 Nov. By the morning of 24 Nov it was trading 3.4% lower at SEK 0.57 (Gaming Intelligence). Market cap ~SEK 175m (~$17m); ~40% below the 52-week high of SEK 0.96 and -37% over 12 months.

The one-line verdict

A genuinely better quarter on mix — service fees +69% and technology access fees +132% more than replaced a royalty book that is now 74% smaller — but the SEK 23.6m (~$2.30m) reported EBITDA and "+1,360%" headline are flattered by a SEK 4.9m (~$0.48m) provision reversal this year and SEK 8.6m (~$0.84m) of redundancy costs in the base; like-for-like EBITDA was SEK 18.6m (~$1.82m), +83%, at a 36.1% margin. The two things a PM should actually price are the SEK 39.3m (~$3.84m) receivables build (DSO ~70 days vs 8 a year ago, Q3 cash conversion 32%) and the share-settled MegaBet Plus deal that will add roughly a fifth to the share count at today's price.

1Executive summary

  • Revenue beat its own trajectory, not a consensus (there is none): Q3-26 revenue SEK 51.7m (~$5.05m), +28% YoY and +11% QoQ, following SEK 38.6m in Q1 and SEK 46.4m in Q2; 9M revenue SEK 136.6m (~$13.4m), +9% after a flat H1. EMB has no sell-side coverage and issues no numerical guidance, so the reference points are the company's own run-rate and the Q4-25 base of SEK 40.5m (~$3.96m).
  • The headline '+1,360% EBITDA' is a base-effect artefact — strip both one-offs: reported EBITDA SEK 23.6m (~$2.30m, 45.6% margin) vs SEK 1.6m (4.0%) in Q3-25. Q3-26 includes a SEK 4.9m (~$0.48m) reversal of a Taiwan office restoration provision; Q3-25 carried SEK 8.6m (~$0.84m) of redundancy costs. Like-for-like EBITDA was SEK 18.6m (~$1.82m) vs SEK 10.2m (~$1.00m), +83%, with margin +1,080bp to 36.1% (and +480bp QoQ vs 31.3%).
  • Net profit SEK 15.1m (~$1.48m), EPS SEK 0.05 (~$0.005) vs a SEK 5.3m loss / SEK (0.02) a year ago and SEK 7.3m / SEK 0.02 in Q2; 9M profit SEK 27.5m (~$2.69m) vs a SEK 3.7m loss. Operating income SEK 16.8m (~$1.65m, 32.6% margin) vs a SEK 5.3m loss. Tax charge only SEK 0.2m — retained losses still SEK 118.6m, so cash taxes stay negligible.
  • Which 'division' drove it — the two platform streams, at the expense of royalties: service fees +69% YoY to SEK 32.6m (~$3.18m, 63% of revenue) and technology access fees +132% to SEK 15.3m (~$1.50m, 30%) together grew SEK 22.0m, more than offsetting a SEK 10.6m (-74%) collapse in game-licensing royalties to SEK 3.8m (~$0.37m, 7%). On a 9M basis royalties are down SEK 48.0m (-76%); the Lady Luck Games content business that was 50% of revenue a year ago is now 11%.
  • Revenue-quality caveat management itself flags: technology access fees are 'fixed income received in advance' recognised at a point in time when access is granted. Q3's SEK 15.3m came from one new customer (SEK 14.9m of it booked in Asia), so ~30% of the quarter is a single, non-recurring recognition event. Contract liabilities (prepaid access fees) were only SEK 3.5m (~$0.35m) at 30 Sep vs SEK 1.7m at 30 Jun — a modest forward indicator for Q4.
  • Geography — Asia carried the quarter, 'Others' carried the year: Asia revenue SEK 34.5m (~$3.37m), +8% YoY, +33% QoQ, 67% of group; 9M Asia -6.5% as royalties fell SEK 34.6m. Rest-of-world ('Others') SEK 17.2m (~$1.68m), +107% YoY, -16% QoQ, with service fees up ~4x to SEK 14.7m; 9M Others +38% to SEK 60.6m (~$5.92m), 44% of group. EMB discloses no end-market licensing detail for its Asian customers [regulatory status unverified].
  • Customer concentration is improving but remains a single-point-of-failure risk: the top two customers were 48% of 9M revenue (27% / 21%) vs 71% (33% / 38%) in 9M-25 and 54% (31% / 23%) in H1-26 — i.e. Q3 added revenue outside the top two.
  • Cost base — 2025 restructuring is paying off, but underlying costs are now rising: total opex SEK 34.8m (~$3.40m), -24% YoY. Personnel SEK 23.1m (~$2.26m), -20% reported but +SEK 2.6m (+13%) ex-redundancy on a 25% YoY headcount increase; other opex SEK 4.9m, -44% reported but +SEK 1.0m (+11%) ex-reversal. The 2025 restructuring program booked SEK 12.4m (~$1.21m) of redundancy costs in 9M-25 (SEK 8.6m in Q3-25); no quantified savings target or end-year was ever disclosed [FY25 total unverified]. Personnel is 44.8% of revenue vs 51.0% underlying a year ago.
  • Cash conversion was the weak spot: Q3 operating cash flow SEK 7.5m (~$0.73m), -9% YoY and -64% QoQ, only 32% of EBITDA, after a SEK 12.5m working-capital outflow into receivables. Trade receivables stand at SEK 39.3m (~$3.84m) — 76% of quarterly revenue, DSO ~70 days vs ~53 in Q2 and ~8 a year ago, and up from SEK 21.1m at Dec-25. 9M CFO SEK 35.9m (~$3.51m), +38%; 9M free cash flow after capitalised development, capex and lease payments SEK 19.1m (~$1.87m) vs SEK 5.8m.
  • Balance sheet is clean but IFRS16-heavy: cash SEK 24.0m (~$2.35m), +77% YoY and up from SEK 4.6m at Dec-25; no bank borrowings. Lease liabilities SEK 33.4m (~$3.26m), more than doubled from SEK 15.8m at Dec-25 after new Thailand and two Taiwan offices (SEK 12.2m ROU asset added in Q3). Net debt including leases SEK 9.4m (~$0.92m), ~0.14x LTM EBITDA. Equity SEK 73.7m (~$7.20m), +97% YoY. No dividend, no buyback.
  • MegaBet Plus — the post-period strategic event (21 Oct): agreement to buy 51% of Cleverpath Holdings Ltd (Cyprus; Class A retail licence across 200+ locations and Class B online licence from the National Betting Authority) for a preliminary EUR 4.1m (~SEK 45.6m / ~$4.46m): EUR 3.0m for the equity and EUR 1.1m for the Class A/B NBA bonds. Settlement is 100% in new EMB shares via promissory-note set-off at the 30-day VWAP before closing; completion expected Q1-27 subject to Cyprus NBA approval. The target reported FY24 EBITDA ~EUR 1.77m (~SEK 19.7m / ~$1.92m) and net profit ~EUR 0.33m (~SEK 3.7m); FY25 accounts are still under audit.
  • Deal arithmetic — cheap on paper, dilutive in practice: EUR 3.0m for 51% implies ~EUR 5.9m for 100% of the equity, ~3.3x FY24 EBITDA. But at SEK 0.59 the SEK 45.6m consideration equals ~77m new shares, ~21% of the enlarged 374m share count — and because the price is fixed in EUR, dilution rises if the stock falls into closing. The seller's put over a further 39% at EUR 2.67m (~SEK 29.7m / ~$2.90m), exercisable 12–15 months post-close in cash and/or shares, could add ~50m more shares at today's price. EMB has also lent the target EUR 300k (~SEK 3.3m), due 21 Apr 2027.
  • Strategy read-through — from slots supplier to platform partner to operator-owner: the 2025 pivot (EMB RGS platform, Confetti Group asset purchase settled with 10.4m shares in Apr-25, AI-enabled tooling) has now delivered three consecutive quarters of sequential growth. MegaBet is a step-change in risk profile: asset-light B2B economics give way to retail sportsbook operations in a small, competitive regulated market with a 49% minority partner.
  • Management is emphasising the growth rates and burying the quality questions: the release leads with +28% / +1,360% / +385% and, to its credit, shows ex-one-off EBITDA. Less prominent: the receivables build (one sentence in the CEO letter), the single-customer nature of the Q3 access fee, the 48% concentration (note 2 only), the 25% headcount growth, and the fact that the MegaBet share count and dilution 'cannot be determined at this time'.
  • Governance and disclosure hygiene: interim report not reviewed by the auditors; no financial guidance; CEO Chi Ho Li, Executive Chairman Calvin Lim (re-elected June 2026); Certified Adviser Corpura Fondkommission; 297.0m shares outstanding, unchanged YoY. A SEK 10.7m (~$1.04m) equity-accounted associate sits on the balance sheet with a SEK 4.7m 9M-25 loss now reduced to SEK 21k [associate identity not disclosed in the interim].
  • Valuation arithmetic (not a recommendation): market cap ~SEK 175m (~$17m) at SEK 0.59; LTM revenue SEK 177m (~$17.3m), LTM reported EBITDA SEK 66.3m (~$6.48m, incl. the 4.9m one-off), LTM net profit SEK 36.4m (~$3.56m) — roughly 2.8x EV/LTM EBITDA (leases included) and ~4.8x LTM P/E. The discount prices in micro-cap illiquidity (avg ~130k shares/day), concentration, point-in-time revenue, Asian end-market opacity and pending dilution.
  • Share price: ~+2% to SEK 0.59 (~$0.058) on results day per Yahoo quote data [day-move partially unverified], after SEK 0.522 on 17 Nov; by the morning of 24 Nov the stock was 3.4% lower at SEK 0.57 (Gaming Intelligence). Still ~40% below the 52-week high of SEK 0.96 and -37% over 12 months.
  • Bottom line for the thesis: the quarter validates that the service business can compound sequentially (SEK 28.8m → 29.6m → 32.6m across Q1–Q3) and that the cost base is sized for 30%+ underlying margins. What it does not yet prove is cash durability — Q4 must show the receivables unwinding — or that tech access fees are repeatable rather than a series of one-offs. MegaBet will decide whether 2027 is a story about an operator or a platform.

3Segment deep-dive: Revenue streams: Technology access fees / Service fees / Royalties

Platform streams are now 93% of revenue and growing sequentially; the royalty book is in terminal run-off and Q3's access fee is a single-customer event.

Disclosure and service fees

Service fees rose 69% YoY to SEK 32.6m (~$3.18m), 63% of revenue, compounding sequentially; this is the recurring-ish core of the equity story.

  • EMB reports no operating segments; disaggregation is only by revenue type and customer location, with no GGR, handle, actives, ARPU or market-share data.
  • Service fees cover technology development, integration, maintenance, consulting and game-integration start-up fees, recognised over time.
  • Sequential build: SEK 28.8m in Q1, SEK 29.6m in Q2, SEK 32.6m in Q3, attributed to new customers and expanded scope with existing ones.
  • 9M service fees of SEK 90.9m (~$8.89m), +84%, are the cleanest evidence the 2025 pivot is commercially real.

Technology access fees

Access fees were SEK 15.3m (~$1.50m), +132% YoY and +29% QoQ, 30% of the quarter, but essentially a single-customer licence event.

  • Fixed income received in advance for access to proprietary technology, recognised at a point in time; SEK 14.9m of SEK 15.3m landed in Asia.
  • Management was candid that one new customer was granted access to selected components of the proprietary technology and system architecture.
  • We read the stream as licence events (SEK 3.7m Q1, SEK 11.9m Q2 from two customers, SEK 15.3m Q3 from one) rather than a run-rate.
  • Forward indicator is the contract-liability balance, which doubled QoQ to SEK 3.5m (~$0.35m) but is small relative to recent quarterly recognitions.
“their timing can influence quarterly performance”CEO

Royalties in run-off

Royalties fell 74% YoY to SEK 3.8m (~$0.37m), 7% of revenue, and are down 76% to SEK 14.8m (~$1.45m) for 9M.

  • Variable, a percentage of operators' revenue, recognised as gaming transactions occur; trend SEK 6.1m, 4.9m, 3.8m across Q1–Q3.
  • Management framed this as the 'strategic shift towards platform-led engagements and direct technology partnerships'; in practice legacy Lady Luck Games slot content is de-emphasised.
  • Irony: royalties were the one stream with true variable, recurring, usage-based economics, swapped for faster-growing but inherently lumpier project fees and point-in-time licences.

Costs and margin

Underlying EBITDA of SEK 18.6m (~$1.82m) at 36.1% reflects operating leverage, with revenue up 28% on a cost base that grew ~12%.

  • No cost-of-sales line; gross margin nominally 100% and profit pool by stream undisclosed. Personnel SEK 23.1m is 44.8% of revenue.
  • Ex the SEK 8.6m prior-year redundancy charge, personnel was up SEK 2.6m (+13%) YoY on a 25% headcount increase.
  • Underlying other opex (ex the SEK 4.9m provision reversal) was SEK 9.9m, +11% YoY.
  • If Q4 access fees fall back toward the Q1 level, the margin would drop into the high-20s on our arithmetic.
“investing in the capacity to serve customers”Management

Concentration and Q4 set-up

Two customers were 48% of 9M revenue, down from 71% a year ago; Q4 depends almost entirely on whether another access-fee event lands.

  • Customer A 27%, Customer B 21% for 9M-26, versus 33%/38% in 9M-25 and 31%/23% in H1-26; Q3 growth came largely from outside the top two.
  • No stream-level guidance; priorities are commercialising the proprietary platform, expanding technology access agreements, onboarding new platform clients and converting 'initial engagements into lasting relationships'.
  • Our Q4 reading: service fees hold or grow modestly (SEK 33–35m), royalties drift toward SEK 3m.

4Segment deep-dive: Geography: Asia vs Rest of World ('Others')

Asia (67% of Q3) was lifted by one SEK 14.9m access-fee event while its royalty base has collapsed; 'Others' has quietly become a SEK 60m-a-year service business.

Asia

Asia generated SEK 34.5m (~$3.37m), +8% YoY and +33% QoQ, 67% of group, lifted by a single SEK 14.9m access-fee event.

  • Share was 56% in Q2 and 79% a year ago. Asian access fees jumped to SEK 14.9m (~$1.46m) from SEK 5.9m in Q2 and SEK 6.3m a year ago.
  • Asian royalties fell 83% YoY to SEK 1.7m (~$0.17m); Asian service fees flat QoQ at SEK 17.9m (~$1.75m), +15% YoY.
  • 9M Asia revenue down 6.5% to SEK 76.0m (~$7.43m): royalties shrank SEK 34.6m (SEK 41.8m to SEK 7.3m), partly offset by SEK 15.4m more access fees and SEK 13.8m more service fees.
  • Asia is where legacy royalty exposure sat and where the lumpiest revenue now originates.

Rest of World

'Others' was SEK 17.2m (~$1.68m), +107% YoY but -16% QoQ, 33% of group, and has become a SEK 60m-a-year service business.

  • Service fees quadrupled YoY to SEK 14.7m (~$1.44m), +27% QoQ, the most consistent stream (SEK 17.6m Q1, SEK 11.6m Q2, SEK 14.7m Q3).
  • Others' access fees were negligible at SEK 0.4m after SEK 5.9m in Q2, explaining the QoQ decline; Others' royalties fell 51% to SEK 2.1m.
  • 9M Others revenue rose 38% to SEK 60.6m (~$5.92m), now 44% of group versus 35% a year ago.
  • Diversification is real but almost entirely service-fee driven and, like Asia, concentrated in a handful of relationships; the company gives no further split.

Operating footprint

The footprint is Asian regardless of customer location; lease liabilities have doubled since December to SEK 33.4m (~$3.26m).

  • Offices in Taiwan (two existing plus a new one signed in August 2026 with a SEK 12.2m right-of-use asset), Malaysia, Singapore and, from April 2026, Thailand.
  • SEK 36.2m of undiscounted rental commitments through 2029+; right-of-use depreciation of SEK 3.7m in Q3 is now a structural cost line.
  • Headcount up 25% YoY. Registered head office is Östermalmstorg, Stockholm, with no evidence of operational substance in Sweden beyond the listing.

Regulation and market position

EMB discloses no customer licences, GGR, market-share or deployment jurisdictions, so Asian grey-market risk must be priced without company help.

  • Much of Asian online gaming is unregulated or grey [unverified — no disclosure].
  • The pending MegaBet Plus acquisition is pitched as strengthening 'operational presence in a regulated European market', which reads as acknowledging the profile lacks a regulated anchor.
  • Until closing, Cyprus contributes nothing to revenue; the 'Others' line is the only non-Asian exposure.

5Segment deep-dive: MegaBet Plus (Cyprus) — pending 51% acquisition, pro forma

Cheap on the target's 2024 numbers (~3.3x EBITDA for 100%) but paid entirely in EMB paper at a depressed price, with a put that can take EMB to 90% and the share count higher still.

Deal terms

On 21 October 2026 EMB signed to acquire 51% of MegaBet Plus for a preliminary EUR 4.1m (~SEK 45.6m / ~$4.46m), closing expected Q1-27.

  • Target is Cleverpath Holdings Limited, trading as MegaBet Plus, bought from majority shareholder Dennis Ang, broadly on the January 2026 non-binding LOI terms.
  • Founded 2007, more than 200 retail locations across Cyprus, holds Class A (retail) and Class B (online) licences from the Cyprus National Betting Authority.
  • Price split: EUR 3.0m for the 51% equity stake and EUR 1.1m for all rights to the Class A and Class B NBA bonds, subject to customary adjustments.
  • Closing needs NBA approval; consolidated from completion.

Valuation

EUR 3.0m for 51% implies ~EUR 5.9m for 100%, ~3.3x FY24 EBITDA and ~18x FY24 net profit, on year-old numbers.

Structure and dilution

Consideration is paid in EMB shares at the 30-day VWAP before closing, ~77m shares (~21%) at SEK 0.59, with a put taking EMB to 90%.

  • Price fixed in euros, so share count floats inversely with the stock: ~91m shares (~23%) at SEK 0.50, on an enlarged ~374m count.
  • Seller's put requires EMB to buy a further 39% for EUR 2,668,422 (~SEK 29.7m / ~$2.90m), exercisable 12–15 months after closing, in cash, shares or both.
  • Equity-settled at today's price that is another ~50m shares; EMB's SEK 24m cash could not fund it in cash without a raise.
  • EMB has advanced EUR 300k (~SEK 3.3m / ~$0.33m) in two tranches under a loan due 21 April 2027 (extendable), visible in Q4 cash flow. A shareholders' agreement governs the 51/49 relationship.

Strategic assessment

The deal converts an asset-light B2B platform supplier into part-owner of a retail-heavy sportsbook; we withhold credit for accretion pending FY25 numbers.

  • Founder-partner retains 49% (potentially 10%); management's framing of a regulated European anchor plus shared group support functions is sensible.
  • A 200+ shop estate carries labour, rent and compliance costs EMB has never managed, and Cyprus retail betting competes against large international online brands.
  • Acquisition accounting introduces goodwill, intangibles and a non-controlling interest line.
  • We want FY25 audited numbers, the final share count and the regulatory timetable before giving credit.

6Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Service fees — the stream with over-time recognition — grew 69% YoY to SEK 32.6m (~$3.18m) and have risen every quarter this year (SEK 28.8m → 29.6m → 32.6m); 9M +84% to SEK 90.9m (~$8.89m). This is the durable part of the pivot. structural
  • Underlying EBITDA (ex the SEK 4.9m reversal and prior-year SEK 8.6m redundancies) rose 83% to SEK 18.6m (~$1.82m); margin +1,080bp YoY and +480bp QoQ to 36.1%, on a cost base that grew only ~12% against +28% revenue. structural
  • Customer diversification: top-2 concentration fell to 48% of 9M revenue from 71% a year ago and 54% at H1; rest-of-world service fees quadrupled YoY to SEK 14.7m (~$1.44m) and 'Others' is now 44% of 9M revenue vs 35%. structural
  • Balance sheet repaired: cash SEK 24.0m (~$2.35m) vs SEK 4.6m at Dec-25, no bank debt, equity +97% YoY to SEK 73.7m (~$7.20m); 9M operating cash flow +38% to SEK 35.9m (~$3.51m) and 9M FCF after leases SEK 19.1m (~$1.87m) vs SEK 5.8m. mixed
  • Contract liabilities (prepaid technology access fees) doubled QoQ to SEK 3.5m (~$0.35m) — small, but the only forward indicator the company gives for the lumpy access-fee stream. early
  • MegaBet Plus brings a regulated-EU licence set (Cyprus Class A retail, 200+ shops, and Class B online) at ~3.3x FY24 EBITDA on a 100%-equity basis, settled in shares rather than scarce cash — the first regulated anchor in an otherwise Asia-centred, undisclosed-jurisdiction revenue base. mixed

▼ Lowlights

  • Headline EBITDA growth of +1,360% is manufactured by the base: a SEK 4.9m (~$0.48m) provision reversal is 21% of Q3-26 reported EBITDA and 10% of 9M, while Q3-25 absorbed SEK 8.6m (~$0.84m) of redundancy costs. Reported net margin of 29.3% is not a run-rate. one-off
  • Cash did not follow profit: Q3 operating cash flow SEK 7.5m (~$0.73m), -64% QoQ and 32% of EBITDA, after a SEK 12.5m receivables outflow. Trade receivables SEK 39.3m (~$3.84m) are 76% of quarterly revenue, DSO ~70 days vs ~53 in Q2 and ~8 a year ago — consistent with point-in-time access fees being invoiced but not yet collected. watch
  • Technology access fees (SEK 15.3m, 30% of revenue) came from one new customer and are recognised when access is granted; management itself warns timing 'can influence quarterly performance'. Royalties, the only usage-based recurring stream, are down 74% to SEK 3.8m (~$0.37m) and in run-off. structural
  • Underlying costs are rising again: personnel +13% YoY ex-redundancy on +25% headcount, other opex +11% ex-reversal, and lease liabilities have doubled since December to SEK 33.4m (~$3.26m) with SEK 36.2m of commitments through 2029+ after three new offices. watch
  • Customer concentration still 48% in two names and Asia still 67% of Q3 revenue, with no disclosure of end-market licensing or regulatory status; the business remains exposed to a handful of relationships in opaque jurisdictions. structural
  • MegaBet Plus terms: EUR 4.1m (~SEK 45.6m / ~$4.46m) payable entirely in new shares at a VWAP that is ~40% below the 52-week high — ~77m shares (~21% of enlarged capital) at SEK 0.59, more if the stock falls — plus a EUR 2.67m (~SEK 29.7m) put for another 39% that EMB could not currently fund in cash. The share count and dilution 'cannot be determined at this time', FY25 target accounts are unaudited, and no independent valuation is referenced. governance
  • Disclosure gaps: interim not reviewed by auditors, no guidance, no segment profit, no KPIs beyond revenue type/location, unnamed SEK 10.7m associate, and a 'restructuring program' with no quantified savings target or end-date. Micro-cap liquidity (~130k shares/day) limits institutional relevance. governance

7Forecast & outlook

Guidance bridge. There is no guidance to bridge: EMB has never issued numerical guidance and the Q3 report repeats the same qualitative priorities as Q1 and Q2 (commercialise the proprietary platform, expand technology access agreements, deepen existing relationships, develop AI-enabled capabilities, maintain cost and working-capital discipline). The 'guidance change' this quarter is therefore implicit and comes from the revenue-stream side: the platform streams (technology access + service fees) have moved from 'offsetting' royalty decline in Q2 to 'driving overall revenue growth' in Q3, and the CEO's tone shifted from 'the transition is gaining momentum' to 'greater confidence in our direction'. The moving parts for the rest of the year are (i) whether another point-in-time access-fee event lands in Q4, (ii) whether service fees continue to compound from SEK 32.6m, (iii) collection of the SEK 39.3m receivables book, and (iv) the EUR 300k MegaBet loan and any deal costs leaving cash. Independent view. Revenue: we model Q4-26 at ~SEK 45m (~$4.4m): service fees SEK ~33.5m (+3% QoQ, continuing the Q1–Q3 compounding), royalties ~SEK 3.2m (run-off), and technology access fees ~SEK 8m — deliberately below Q3's SEK 15.3m because that figure was one customer and contract liabilities are only SEK 3.5m. That gives FY26E revenue of ~SEK 181m (~$17.7m), +9% YoY, with a swing range of SEK 41–50m for Q4 depending entirely on access-fee timing. Margin: with personnel running ~SEK 23–24m a quarter and underlying other opex ~SEK 10m, Q4 underlying EBITDA should be ~SEK 13–15m (30–33% margin), below Q3's 36.1% as the access-fee contribution normalises; FY26E underlying EBITDA ~SEK 59m (~$5.8m), reported ~SEK 64m (~$6.3m) including the SEK 4.9m one-off, versus SEK 33.6m in FY25. We estimate FY26E net profit of ~SEK 33m (~$3.3m), EPS ~SEK 0.11 (~$0.011), with D&A ~SEK 7m a quarter and negligible cash tax given SEK 118.6m of retained losses. Cash and leverage: the direction should improve in Q4 if receivables collect — a partial unwind of the SEK 18.5m 9M build would put year-end cash at SEK 28–35m (~$2.7–3.4m) after the EUR 300k loan and capex/lease outflows of ~SEK 4–5m. The group has no bank debt; net debt including IFRS16 leases is ~0.14x LTM EBITDA and will stay there until MegaBet consolidates, at which point goodwill, a non-controlling interest and the target's own balance sheet [undisclosed] arrive. 2027: MegaBet would add ~SEK 20m of annualised EBITDA on 2024 numbers (49% minority) against ~77m+ new shares; on our arithmetic that is roughly EPS-neutral to modestly accretive before integration costs, and accretion depends on the FY25 audit and the closing VWAP. The equity debate is not the P&L trajectory — which is visibly improving — but whether a business with 48% top-two concentration, point-in-time revenue, 70-day DSOs and a pending ~21% dilution deserves a re-rating from ~2.8x LTM EV/EBITDA. We would not model one until Q4 shows the receivables reversing.

What to watch next quarter

  • Q4 operating cash flow and trade receivables: we need the SEK 39.3m balance to fall materially (DSO back below ~50 days) to trust that Q3's access-fee revenue converts to cash.
  • Technology access fee recognition in Q4 and the contract-liability balance at year-end — the single biggest swing factor (SEK 41–50m revenue range) and the test of repeatability.
  • MegaBet Plus closing: Cyprus NBA approval timing, final purchase price, the number of consideration shares and resulting dilution, and publication of the target's audited FY25 accounts.
