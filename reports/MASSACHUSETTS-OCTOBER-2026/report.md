# Massachusetts () — OCTOBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (market reports natively in US dollars; tables in $ millions unless stated) · Results released 20 Nov 2026 (MGC October 2026 Revenue Report; revenue page updated 11.20.2026)**

> Massachusetts is a mature, two-operator-dominated online sportsbook market (DraftKings + FanDuel = 74% of handle, 82% of taxable revenue) where volume has stalled (October handle $874.1m, -2.0% YoY; YTD +1.5%) but hold expansion keeps revenue and tax creeping higher (taxable SWR $73.6m, +3.3%; YTD $685.1m, +5.7%). The land-based base is shrinking (casino GGR $90.3m, -6.8%, strike-hit Encore -11.7%), iGaming is parked until 2027, and the live regulatory risk is binary: a Senate bill proposing a 51% online tax plus prop/in-play bans sits in Ways & Means, while the SJC has yet to rule on whether Kalshi's sports contracts are unlicensed wagering.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **A $2.0bn-revenue regulated market, c.43% sports / 57% casino.** Over the last twelve months (Nov-25 to Oct-26) Massachusetts licensees generated $878.5m of taxable sports wagering revenue (TSWR, +11.7% YoY) on $8.63bn of handle (+3.5%) plus $1,166.7m of casino GGR (-2.9%), for combined taxable gaming revenue of $2,045m (+2.9%) and $510.4m of state tax. All figures are gross of tax, accrual basis, as reported by the Massachusetts Gaming Commission (MGC).
- **October 2026 sports handle fell 2.0% YoY to $874.1m** — the first October decline since launch — but rose 13.1% MoM with the full NFL/NBA/MLB-postseason calendar. Online handle was $866.8m (-1.5% YoY), retail $7.3m (-42.3%). The 2-year CAGR on October handle is still +8.1% (Oct-24: $748.1m).
- **Revenue and tax grew anyway on hold expansion.** Accrual win was $75.8m (hold 8.67% vs 8.28% in Oct-25); after the 0.25% federal excise ($2.1m) TSWR was $73.6m (+3.3% YoY, +3.4% MoM) and tax collected $14.7m (+3.3%). Year-to-date TSWR is $685.1m (+5.7%) on handle of $6.87bn (+1.5%), i.e. revenue is outgrowing stakes by c.4pp as parlay mix lifts structural hold (YTD taxable/handle 9.97% vs 9.58%).
- **Online is effectively the whole sportsbook market: 99.2% of handle and 99.1% of TSWR.** The three casino retail books settled just $7.3m of wagers in October; YTD retail handle is down 22.4% to $74.5m. Retail exists for licence-tethering, not for economics.
- **Duopoly, hardening.** DraftKings took 48.1% of online handle ($417.3m) and 51.4% of online TSWR ($37.5m); FanDuel 26.3% / 31.0% ($228.0m / $22.6m). Top-2 = 74.4% of handle and 82.4% of revenue. Fanatics is the only scaled challenger (13.1% handle, $113.7m, +37.9% YoY) but ran a 3.9% hold and its taxable revenue fell 44%. BetMGM 6.7%, Caesars 2.7%, theScore Bet (ex-ESPN Bet) 2.6%, Bally Bet 0.5%.
- **Land-based casino GGR fell 6.8% YoY to $90.3m**, the weakest October since 2020. Encore Boston Harbor (-11.7% to $50.8m) absorbed a 62-day strike by c.1,300 workers (4 Sep–5 Nov 2026); September was -22.1%. MGM Springfield -3.5% ($23.3m); slots-only Plainridge Park +6.6% ($16.2m). Casino tax: $26.5m (-5.0%). YTD casino GGR $970.1m (-2.9%).
- **Tax regime is moderate by north-east standards and base-friendly only in one respect.** Sports: 20% of adjusted gross sports wagering receipts for online (Category 3), 15% for retail (Cat 1/2); base = gross receipts minus winnings minus federal excise; promotional credits are NOT deductible; negative months carry forward. Casino: 25% of GGR (Cat 1 resort casinos), 49% (Cat 2 slots parlour). DFS: 15% of net adjusted gross fantasy receipts. Blended October sports rate 19.95%; tax = 1.68% of handle.
- **Licensing is capped and mostly vacant.** MGL c.23N allows 16 mobile licences (6 tethered to the 3 casinos, 3 tethered to racetracks/simulcast, 7 untethered); only 7 are in use (4 tethered: BetMGM–MGM Springfield, Caesars–Encore, Fanatics and Penn/theScore–Plainridge; 3 untethered: DraftKings, FanDuel, Bally Bet). Betr and WynnBET exited in 2024. Fees: $200k application, $5m per 5-year licence, $5m renewal; full 5-year licences were awarded through 2025 (DraftKings 25 Jul 2025, BetMGM 12 Dec 2025), so the first renewals fall c.2030.
- **The door has reopened for new entrants.** On 9 Apr 2026 the MGC voted 5-0 to reopen the application window after bet365 asked for an untethered Cat 3 licence; bet365 filed a Letter of Intent on 15 May 2026 (BetMGM also reportedly filed an untethered LOI). Award status as of 25 Nov 2026 is [unverified]; four untethered slots remain.
- **Headline regulatory risk: the Bettor Health Act (S.302).** Reported favourably 5-0 by the Joint Committee on Economic Development on 9 Mar 2026 and referred to Senate Ways & Means, it would lift the online tax from 20% to 51%, ban in-play and proposition wagering, bar sportsbook ads during televised games, and cap bettors at $1,000/day or $10,000/30 days absent an affordability check (wagers ≤15% of bank balance). On the LTM base a static 51% rate would lift sports tax from $175m to c.$445m (our estimate) — before the demand and hold loss a prop/in-play ban would cause.
- **Prediction markets are the unpriced competitor.** AG Campbell sued Kalshi (Sep 2025); Suffolk Superior Court granted a preliminary injunction (20 Jan 2026) holding sports event contracts are unlicensed wagering; the Appeals Court stayed it (Feb 2026); the SJC took direct review, heard argument 4 May 2026 with visible scepticism of Kalshi, and had not ruled as of late October. Kalshi therefore still operates in-state, untaxed, with no 21+/RG regime; 38 AGs filed amicus for Massachusetts; Polymarket has sued pre-emptively.
- **iGaming is off the table until 2027.** H.4431 (Muradian) was sent to study 11-0 on 16 Mar 2026; the companion H.332/S.235 (Cahill/Feeney) casino-tethered model (3 casinos x 2 skins, $5m licences) also lapsed at the March reporting deadline. Public-health opposition and cannibalisation fears carried the day; neighbouring CT and RI remain live iGaming states.
- **Payments: statutory credit-card ban; no published payment-mix data.** c.23N prohibits credit-card funding of wagering accounts; deposits run on debit/ACH/e-wallets (operator-level mix n/a). Minimum age 21; geolocation mandatory; in-state college teams barred except in tournaments; no high-school wagering.
- **Responsible gambling is regulator-led and active.** MGC runs Voluntary Self-Exclusion, GameSense and the PlayMyWay slot budgeting tool at casinos, plus the 'Bet On Respect' and 'Safer Bets' campaigns; 9% of sports tax is earmarked to the Public Health Trust Fund (c.$15.8m on LTM receipts). No statutory ad ban today, but the MGC has been among the most interventionist US regulators on advertising, patron limiting and suitability.
- **Tax allocation (sports):** 45% General Fund, 27.5% Gaming Local Aid Fund, 17.5% Workforce Investment Trust Fund, 9% Public Health Trust Fund, 1% Youth Development and Achievement Fund (per MGC). FY2026 (Jul-25–Jun-26) sports tax was $169.3m on $848.5m TSWR; casino tax $340.6m; combined $509.9m.
- **DFS is small and now taxed.** October 2026 gross fantasy receipts $15.0m, net adjusted $2.3m, tax (15%) $0.34m; DraftKings is c.60% of DFS receipts (Oct-25 operator split). The AG retains DFS consumer-protection rules; operators register with the MGC.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** This is a cash-generative but closed-shop market: 99% online, a two-brand duopoly, hold-driven growth, no iGaming until 2027 at the earliest, and a tax rate that could jump 2.5x by legislative fiat. A greenfield untethered entry costs $5.2m in fees before marketing against DK/FD's 82% revenue share; the realistic angle is B2B supply (pricing, trading, RG tooling) to incumbents and to any new entrant (bet365), or a tethered skin via Raynham/Suffolk Downs if a Cat 2 licence is ever activated. Wait for the SJC Kalshi ruling and the fate of S.302 before committing capital.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online sports betting (Category 3)

*Volume has plateaued at c.$8.5bn LTM handle; growth now comes from hold, and 82% of revenue sits with two brands.*

### Handle and hold

**Volume has plateaued at c.$8.5bn LTM handle; revenue growth is almost entirely a hold story with tax flowing straight to the Commonwealth.**

- October 2026 online handle $866.8m, 1.5% below October 2025 ($879.6m); second consecutive YoY decline after September (-3.0%).
- LTM online handle $8.53bn (+3.5%); LTM online taxable revenue $870.2m (+11.7%).
- Taxable revenue/handle moved from 9.6% (YTD-25) to 10.0% (YTD-26); October accrual hold 8.66% versus 8.28% a year earlier.
- Promotional credits cannot be deducted from the tax base; online tax $14.6m in October (+3.2%), $135.8m YTD (+5.6%).

### DraftKings and FanDuel

**The two brands held 74.4% of handle and 82.4% of revenue, a concentration that has barely moved since launch.**

- DraftKings: $417.3m handle (48.1% share), $37.5m taxable revenue (51.4%); handle -6.7% YoY, revenue share down from 54.4% in October 2025.
- FanDuel: $228.0m handle (-3.9%), 10.2% hold, taxable revenue +30.7% to $22.6m; revenue share 31.0% from 24.5%.
- The marginal customer is already a DK or FD customer; acquisition cost is set by two balance sheets treating Massachusetts as a flagship market.

### Fanatics and mid-tier

**Fanatics is the only operator taking volume share, but at a 3.92% hold its buy-share strategy cost is visible in the tax base.**

- Fanatics handle +37.9% YoY to $113.7m (13.1% share, up from 9.4%) and +51% MoM; taxable revenue only $4.2m (-44.1%).
- Hold far below peers (9–10%) is consistent with aggressive bonus-bet and FanCash funding showing up as payouts.
- BetMGM ($57.7m, 6.7% share) is the most efficient mid-tier book: taxable revenue +49.5% to $5.3m on a 9.5% hold.
- Caesars ($23.6m, 6.3% hold), theScore Bet ($22.6m, -18.5% YoY after ESPN Bet rebrand) and Bally Bet ($4.0m) together hold 5.8% of handle, 4.6% of revenue.

### Year-to-date shares

**YTD shares show the same picture, with DraftKings at 49.8% of handle and 51.5% of revenue.**

- DraftKings 49.8% / 51.5% (revenue +1.5%); FanDuel 25.4% / 28.3% (revenue +7.2%); Fanatics 10.4% / 7.9% (handle +35.1%).
- BetMGM 7.6% / 7.4% (revenue +11.9%); Caesars 3.4% / 2.3% (revenue +33.1%); theScore Bet 2.8% / 2.4% (handle -12.7%); Bally Bet 0.6% / 0.6%.
- MGC January 2026 'Total Online' row ($801.1m / $79.1m TSWR) does not tie to operator rows ($797.1m / $82.1m); MGC totals used for aggregates, operator rows for shares.

### New entrants

**bet365 and possibly BetMGM have filed for untethered licences; a bet365 launch would be the first new brand since 2023.**

- MGC reopened the licence application window on 9 April 2026 (5-0 vote); bet365 filed a Letter of Intent for an untethered Category 3 licence on 15 May 2026.
- Secondary reporting indicates BetMGM filed an untethered LOI, which would free its MGM Springfield tether.
- No award reported as of 25 November 2026 [unverified].
- First credible test of whether a pricing-led book can dislodge DK/FD share in a no-promo-deduction state; MGC chair framed reopening as alternative to unregulated prediction markets.

### Operator arithmetic

**Full untethered licence costs $5.2m up front for five years, tax is 20% of a base including bonus payouts, and the top two own four-fifths of revenue.**

- Hold is the only lever.
- Realistic routes: B2B supply to an incumbent or new entrant, or a tethered skin via a Category 2 racetrack licence (Raynham Park, Suffolk Downs) if activated — none is today.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Land-based: casinos and retail sportsbooks

*Casino GGR is in gentle structural decline (-2.9% YTD) and was knocked a further leg lower by the 62-day Encore strike; retail betting is immaterial.*

### Casino GGR

**Casino GGR is in gentle structural decline (-2.9% YTD); October 2026 was the weakest October since pandemic-affected 2020.**

- Three properties produced $90.3m table and slot GGR in October 2026, -6.8% YoY and 4.2% below October 2024; October 2020 was $68.7m.
- Slots $66.3m, live tables $24.0m.
- YTD GGR $970.1m (-2.9%), casino tax $279.1m (-2.3%); LTM GGR $1,166.7m (-2.9%).
- Casino is the larger tax engine: $26.5m in October versus $14.7m from sports, as Category 1 pays 25% of GGR and Plainridge 49%.

### Encore strike

**The 62-day Encore strike cost c.$19.8m GGR and c.$5.0m state tax, but Encore's YTD GGR is still down 5.3%.**

- Roughly 1,300 unionised workers struck 4 September to 5 November 2026; GGR fell 22.1% YoY in September ($46.1m) and 11.7% in October ($50.8m: tables $19.7m, slots $31.2m).
- Two-month GGR $96.9m against $116.7m a year earlier (our estimate of shortfall).
- New contract ratified, so November should show partial normalisation; YTD GGR $580.1m suggests underlying softness predating the dispute.
- MGM Springfield ($23.3m, -3.5%; YTD +0.6%) flat; Plainridge Park ($16.2m, +6.6%; YTD +1.7%) the only property growing.

### Retail sports wagering

**Retail sports wagering is a rounding error at 0.8% of statewide handle and down 42.3% YoY.**

- Three casino books settled $7.3m in October (Encore $3.5m, Plainridge $3.1m, MGM Springfield $0.7m); $0.68m taxable revenue, $0.10m tax at 15%.
- Retail handle down 22.4% YTD to $74.5m; MGM Springfield's book posted zero taxable revenue in several months of 2025-26 as negative months net off.
- Neither Category 2 facility (Raynham Park, Suffolk Downs) operates a sportsbook; bet365's earlier plan to enter via Raynham was abandoned.

### Tethering value

**Retail has no standalone value; its worth is as a tethering host, with Encore and MGM Springfield each holding a spare tether.**

- Each casino may carry two mobile skins, each racetrack one.
- Encore (Caesars only) and MGM Springfield (BetMGM only, possibly freed if BetMGM goes untethered) each have a spare tether.
- Cheapest regulatory path for a brand avoiding an untethered bid, though still carrying the $5m five-year fee.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*A capped, fee-light licence structure (16 mobile slots, 7 used, $5m/5 years) with a 20%/15% GGR-style tax that disallows promo deductions — and a live proposal to take online to 51%.*

### Licence structure

**A capped structure of 16 possible mobile licences, with seven in use, under MGL c.23N administered by the five-member MGC.**

- H.5164 signed 10 August 2022; retail launch 31 January 2023, mobile 10 March 2023; MGC also regulates three casinos under 2011 Expanded Gaming Act and horse racing.
- Category 1 casinos (Encore, MGM Springfield, Plainridge) up to two tethered platforms each; Category 2 racetracks (Raynham Park, Suffolk Downs) one each; Category 3 up to seven untethered.
- 16 possible mobile licences (6 casino-tethered, 3 track-tethered, 7 untethered); seven in use: BetMGM, Caesars, Fanatics, Penn/theScore Bet tethered; DraftKings, FanDuel, Bally Bet untethered.
- Betr and WynnBET held temporary licences in 2023 and did not renew.

### Fees and renewals

**Fees are modest: $200,000 application fee and $5m per five-year licence, with the renewal cycle beginning c.2030.**

- $5m renewal; MGC clarified in 2023 that every mobile platform, tethered or not, pays the $5m.
- Operators ran on one-year temporary licences from February 2023; full licences awarded after 2025 suitability reviews (DraftKings 25 July 2025, BetMGM 12 December 2025).
- Application window reopened 9 April 2026 for any interested company, triggered by bet365.

### Tax rates

**Tax is 20% online and 15% retail on adjusted gross receipts with no promo deduction, making the effective rate materially higher for promo-heavy books.**

- Base is gross receipts less winnings paid and less the 0.25% federal excise; negative months carried forward.
- Fanatics' 3.9% October hold is the visible symptom of non-deductible promotional credits.
- October blended rate 19.95%; tax equalled 1.68% of handle.
- Casino tax 25% of GGR for Category 1, 49% for Category 2 slots parlour; DFS operators pay 15% of net adjusted gross fantasy receipts ($0.34m in October).

### Tax allocation

**Sports tax allocation gives c.$15.8m a year to problem-gambling and public-health programming on the LTM $175.3m.**

- 45% General Fund, 27.5% Gaming Local Aid Fund, 17.5% Workforce Investment Trust Fund, 9% Public Health Trust Fund, 1% Youth Development and Achievement Fund.
- The public-health allocation is one reason the regulator's RG posture is well resourced.
- FY2026 (July 2025–June 2026): $169.3m sports tax on $848.5m TSWR, $340.6m casino tax, $509.9m combined.

### Bettor Health Act

**S.302 would raise the online rate to 51% and the 5-0 committee vote makes 51% the anchor for any future tax debate.**

- Cleared Joint Committee on Economic Development and Emerging Technologies 5-0 on 9 March 2026; sits in Senate Ways & Means.
- Would prohibit in-play and proposition wagers, ban sportsbook ads during televised sporting events, and impose default limits of $1,000/day and $10,000/30 days.
- Limits lifted only if bettor passes an affordability check showing wagers do not exceed 15% of available bank balances.
- No further floor action as of 25 November 2026; 2025-26 formal session ended, so the bill must be refiled or attached to a 2027 vehicle.

### Licensing read

**Cheap to license, expensive to compete: a $5.2m untethered licence is trivially affordable relative to marketing against DK/FD.**

- Harder constraints are the no-promo-deduction tax base, the 2030 renewal and suitability cycle, and the tail risk that the tax rate 2.5x's.
- Tethered entry through a spare casino or racetrack slot carries the same $5m fee but avoids a competitive bid.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling & advertising

*Credit cards are barred by statute, the age floor is 21, in-state college betting is prohibited, and the MGC is one of the more interventionist US regulators on RG and advertising conduct.*

### Payments

**Credit cards are barred by statute and there are no deposit or stake limits today, which S.302 would change.**

- Deposits flow through debit cards, ACH/online banking, e-wallets and pay-at-cage; no payment-method split published by MGC or operators (n/a).
- S.302's $1,000/day and $10,000/30-day defaults with bank-balance affordability assessments would be the first UK-style construct in a US state.
- Operators would need to source financial data on customers.

### Eligibility and products

**Age floor is 21 with mandatory geolocation; in-state college betting is prohibited and props/in-play drive hold expansion.**

- Wagering on Massachusetts college teams barred unless in a tournament of four or more teams; no high-school or youth events.
- MGC maintains an approved-events-and-wagers catalogue; operators must seek approval for new markets.
- Player-prop and in-play markets are allowed and are the principal driver of hold expansion; their proposed prohibition under S.302 is the single most value-destructive element for operators.

### Responsible gambling

**RG infrastructure is regulator-run, funded by the 9% Public Health Trust Fund allocation (c.$15.8m LTM).**

- Statewide Voluntary Self-Exclusion list covering casinos and sportsbooks, GameSense advisers, PlayMyWay slot budgeting tool, campaigns 'Bet On Respect' and 'Safer Bets'.
- Commission funds research on gambling harm and uses public roundtables (on limiting winning patrons and advertising) to pressure licensees ahead of rulemaking.
- Allocation finances treatment and the state helpline.

### Advertising

**No advertising ban, but MGC rules restrict targeting and compliance cost should be modelled as above average.**

- 2022 Act requires '21+' language on sportsbook signage at Fenway Park, TD Garden and Gillette Stadium.
- MGC regulations restrict targeting of under-21s and college audiences and police inducement language [detail of 205 CMR 256 not re-verified in this review].
- Bettor Health Act would add a ban on sportsbook ads during televised games.
- Regulator will act on conduct (limiting, bonusing, ad placement) before the legislature does.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Legislative, legal & competitive pipeline

*Three binary events frame the next 12 months: the SJC's Kalshi ruling, whether S.302 is revived in 2027, and whether bet365 (and a freed BetMGM) are licensed; iGaming is dead until 2027.*

### Kalshi ruling

**Prediction markets are the most immediate distortion; an SJC affirmance would be the strongest state-court precedent in the country.**

- AG Andrea Campbell sued KalshiEX LLC September 2025; Suffolk Superior Court (Judge Barry-Smith) granted preliminary injunction 20 January 2026, rejecting federal-preemption argument.
- Appeals Court stayed the injunction February 2026; SJC heard argument 4 May 2026 (justices reportedly sceptical of Kalshi); no decision as of 28 October 2026.
- Thirty-eight attorneys general filed amicus support; Polymarket has sued pre-emptively.
- Kalshi takes volume with no licence fee, no 20% tax, no 21+ age gate and no RG regime; a reversal would open the state to every exchange.

### Bettor Health Act

**S.302 is the fiscal and product risk; a static re-rating to 51% lifts sports tax from $175m to c.$445m (our estimate).**

- 51% rate sized on precedent set by New York, New Hampshire and Rhode Island; the number will attract Ways & Means attention in any budget gap.
- Prop and in-play ban, broadcast ad ban and affordability-gated limits would cut hold and handle, so net fiscal gain far smaller than static maths implies.
- Cleared committee 5-0 on 9 March 2026 and has not progressed; expect a refiled version in January 2027 with tax rate as negotiating chip.

### iGaming

**iGaming is dead until 2027; a casino partnership is the only viable entry route.**

- H.4431 (Rep. Muradian) sent to study 11-0 on 16 March 2026; H.332/S.235 (Rep. Cahill/Sen. Feeney) — three casinos, two skins each, $5m licences — lapsed at 18 March deadline.
- Public-health leaders and cannibalisation concerns from land-based interests weighed in.
- Connecticut and Rhode Island live and New Hampshire adjacent; Massachusetts leaks iCasino demand across borders and to offshore/sweepstakes sites.
- 2027-28 session is the earliest realistic window; the casino-tethered model is the template.

### New entrants

**Our base case for a bet365 launch is a low-single-digit handle share within 12 months, mostly from the sub-scale tier.**

- MGC reopened the Category 3 window 9 April 2026 at bet365's request; bet365 filed LOI 15 May 2026; secondary reporting indicates BetMGM also filed for an untethered licence.
- Awards not reported in our sources as of 25 November 2026 [unverified].
- Would test whether a pricing-led, low-promo book can take share in a no-promo-deduction state.

### Land-based outlook

**Land-based direction of travel is flat-to-down, with casino GGR falling in two of the last three Octobers.**

- Encore's labour settlement raises its cost base.
- Expanded Gaming Act's unused Region C (south-east) resort licence remains unawarded with no live process.
- Horse racing confined to harness racing at Plainridge; Race Horse Development Fund continues to be funded from casino tax.

### Independent view

**Massachusetts stays a high-quality, slow-growth sportsbook state with rising compliance intensity; not a market to enter B2C from a standing start.**

- 55% that the SJC affirms the injunction by mid-2027, removing the Kalshi leak and marginally lifting licensed handle.
- 35% that a diluted S.302 — tax 25–35%, ad restrictions, limits with affordability opt-out, props retained — reaches a floor vote in the 2027-28 session.
- <15% that iGaming passes before 2028.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Hold-driven revenue growth without volume growth: YTD taxable sports revenue +5.7% to $685.1m on handle +1.5%, as parlay/prop mix lifts structural hold toward 10% — a market where product and pricing, not promo spend, determine returns. _(structural)_
- Licensing is cheap and capacity is open: $200k application + $5m per five-year licence, four untethered Category 3 slots vacant, two spare casino tethers and three racetrack tethers; the MGC reopened the window on 9 Apr 2026 and is explicitly favouring licensed entrants over prediction markets. _(structural)_
- A credible regulator with a litigating AG: the Kalshi preliminary injunction (Jan 2026) and 38-state amicus coalition signal that Massachusetts will defend the licensed market's perimeter; an SJC affirmance would remove an untaxed competitor. _(early)_
- No-promo-deduction tax base punishes bonus-led share-buying (Fanatics' 3.9% hold) and structurally favours books that win on pricing and product — an opening for a trading-led brand such as bet365, and for B2B pricing/risk suppliers. _(structural)_
- Large, stable fiscal contribution ($510m combined tax LTM; $175m from sports) gives the legislature a reason to protect the licensed channel and fund RG (9% of sports tax, c.$15.8m) rather than restrict it outright. _(structural)_
- Sub-scale tier is contestable: Caesars, theScore Bet and Bally Bet together hold 5.8% of handle and 4.6% of revenue; theScore's handle is -18.5% YoY post-rebrand, leaving share available to a well-executed entrant or a tether swap. _(mixed)_
- Encore's strike settlement (5 Nov 2026) removes a one-off drag worth c.$19.8m of GGR over Sep–Oct; November casino figures should rebound, flattering YoY optics into Q1-27. _(one-off)_

**Lowlights**

- Tax-rate cliff: S.302 would take online sports tax from 20% to 51% (c.+$270m static on the LTM base) and ban props and in-play; it cleared committee 5-0 and will anchor the 2027 debate even if diluted. _(structural)_
- Entrenched duopoly: DraftKings and FanDuel hold 74% of handle and 82% of online revenue, barely changed since 2023; customer acquisition against two flagship balance sheets in a no-promo-deduction state is value-destructive for a B2C entrant. _(structural)_
- Volume has plateaued: October handle -2.0% YoY, September -3.0%, YTD +1.5%; retail handle -42%; Massachusetts has reached its penetration ceiling at c.$1,230 of handle per resident and future growth depends on hold and new products that S.302 would prohibit. _(structural)_
- Unresolved prediction-market leakage: the Kalshi injunction is stayed and the SJC has not ruled six months after argument, so an untaxed, un-age-gated sports product remains live in-state and its volume is invisible to the MGC. _(watch)_
- iGaming shut until at least 2027: all three bills died or were sent to study in March 2026 on public-health opposition; neighbouring CT and RI capture cross-border iCasino demand, and any future model is casino-tethered (three hosts, two skins each). _(structural)_
- Land-based erosion: casino GGR -2.9% YTD and -6.8% in October (weakest October since 2020); Encore -5.3% YTD even before the strike, and the 25%/49% casino tax base is shrinking — tethering hosts are weakening assets. _(mixed)_
- Interventionist conduct regulation: the MGC polices limiting of winning patrons, advertising and bonusing through roundtables and rulemaking, and proposed affordability checks (15% of bank balance) would be the first in the US — compliance cost and data burden are above the national median. _(governance)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

No official guidance exists for a state market; the bridge below re-bases the trailing-twelve-month regulator-reported figures (Nov-25 to Oct-26) and overlays the one live fiscal proposal. Sports taxable revenue grew 11.7% to $878.5m on handle +3.5% — i.e. almost entirely hold — while casino GGR fell 2.9% to $1,166.7m, strike-affected in Sep–Oct. The only scheduled change to the rules is nil; the unscheduled ones (S.302, the SJC Kalshi ruling, a bet365 award) are binary.

- **LTM taxable sports revenue (Nov-25–Oct-26):** $878.5m (+11.7% YoY; handle $8.63bn (+3.5%); hold proxy 10.2%)
- **LTM casino GGR:** $1,166.7m (-2.9% YoY; Encore strike c.-$19.8m (est.))

Our independent view: calendar-2026 sports handle finishes at c.$8.5bn (+1 to +2%) with taxable revenue c.$850–870m (+7 to +9%) as Q4 hold normalises above 9%; 2027 handle **flat to +3%** absent a new entrant, with revenue growth narrowing to mid-single digits as hold plateaus near 10%. Casino GGR recovers to c.-1% YoY in 2027 as Encore normalises but has no structural growth. **Tax rate: base case unchanged through FY2027** (the bill must be refiled in January 2027), but we assign c.35% probability to a diluted package (25–35% online rate, broadcast ad limits, affordability-gated limits, props retained) reaching a floor vote in the 2027-28 session and <10% to the full 51%/prop-ban version. **Kalshi: 55% the SJC affirms** by mid-2027, which would marginally lift licensed handle. **iGaming: not before 2028**. Market structure stays a DK/FD duopoly; a bet365 licence (if awarded) takes low-single-digit share mostly from the sub-scale tier. For B2Tech the direction of travel argues for B2B exposure to the incumbents and any new entrant, not a B2C launch.
