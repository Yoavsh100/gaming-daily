# Lithuania () — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: EUR (tables in € millions unless stated; ~USD at EUR/USD 1.095) · Results released 23 Nov 2026**

> Lithuania's regulated gambling GGR rose a derived 9.3% YoY to €75.4m (~$82.6m) in Q3 2026 (9M: €229.0m/~$250.8m, +14.2%), with online now 78% of the market and online slots alone 57% — but growth has decelerated sharply from Q2's ~27%, betting is shrinking and every land-based product is down. The policy direction is unambiguously restrictive: a 22/29/36% progressive GGR tax from 1 Jul 2027, a complete advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028 and a mandatory cross-operator player card plus cashless venues from 1 Jan 2029. A profitable, slots-led, euro-denominated market whose regulated-growth window is narrowing fast.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Q3 2026 gambling GGR €75.4m (~$82.6m), +9.3% YoY (derived).** The Gaming Control Authority (Lošimų priežiūros tarnyba, LPT) publishes cumulative data; Q3 = 9M €229.0m less H1 €153.6m, against a derived Q3-25 base of €69.0m. QoQ the market fell 14.2% from Q2's €87.9m (~$96.3m), which had benefited from the World Cup group stage and seasonal peak. Including lotteries, Q3 revenue was €94.2m (~$103.1m), +10% (Gaming Intelligence) — that figure adds lottery GGR to LPT's gambling headline.
- **9M 2026 GGR €229.0m (~$250.8m), +14.2%** vs €200.5m (~$219.5m): remote €178.99m (~$196.0m, +21.8% from €146.97m), land-based €50.0m (~$54.8m, −6.5% from €53.5m). Add lottery GGR of €57.3m (~$62.7m; ticket sales €131.5m less prizes €74.2m) and the all-in market is €286.3m (~$313.5m), ~+13%.
- **Growth is decelerating through the year**: Q1 +6% (€65.7m, remote 76%), Q2 ~+27% (derived, World Cup-assisted), Q3 +9.3%. FY2025 GGR was €274.1m (~$300.1m, +13%); our FY2026 estimate is ~€308m ±€4m (~$337m), +12% [estimate].
- **Online penetration is 78% of GGR** in both Q3 and 9M (Q3-25: ~74%; 9M-25: 73%; FY2025: 74%); remote overtook retail in 2020 and has widened the gap every period since. Remote stakes were ~€4.24bn (~$4.64bn) in 9M with €4.06bn paid out — a 4.2% hold typical of a slots-dominated mix; land-based stakes €296m (~$324m), hold 16.9%.
- **Online slots are the market**: remote Category A (unlimited-win) machines generated €131.6m (~$144.1m) in 9M, +30% from €101.2m, equal to 57% of ALL gambling GGR; Q3 alone ~€45.9m (~$50.3m, ~+25%). Remote table games added €17.7m (~$19.4m, +24%). Online casino combined ≈ €149.9m, +28%, ~65% of GGR.
- **Betting is shrinking in absolute and relative terms**: remote betting €29.1m (~$31.9m) 9M, −2% (H1 +0.9%; Q3 ~€7.6m, ~−9% despite the World Cup knockout rounds); retail betting €4.9m (~$5.4m), −26.1%. Total betting = 14.8% of GGR vs 18.1% a year earlier — low by Western European standards.
- **Every land-based product declined in 9M**: B-category halls €22.2m (~$24.3m, −2.4%), A machines €12.1m (~$13.2m, −2.4%), casino tables €10.8m (~$11.8m, −7.8%), betting shops €4.9m (−26.1%). Installed gaming machines fell to 3,736 at 30 Sep 2026 from 3,914 (−4.5%).
- **Tax take is rising faster than GGR**: 9M gambling & lottery taxes €74.19m (~$81.2m), +15% (gambling operators ~€50.0m/~$54.8m; lottery operators €24.2m/~$26.5m). Q3 receipts ≈ €27.8m (~$30.4m), +22% YoY. Receipts lag by one quarter (Q3 gambling receipts €19.3m ≈ 22% × Q2 GGR €87.9m).
- **Tax regime today: 22% of GGR** on machines, tables, bingo, totalisator, betting and remote gambling (raised from 20% on 1 Jan 2025; 20% applied from the 2022 reform). Lotteries pay **18% of the face value of tickets sold (turnover base, not GGR)**. Tax period is the calendar quarter. No operator withholding on player winnings under the tax law; PIT treatment of winnings not verified [n/a].
- **Progressive tax proposal (Finance Ministry draft, 12 Nov 2026)**: keep 22% on the first €3m (~$3.3m) of GGR, 29% on €3–5m, **36% above €5m (~$5.5m)** from 1 Jul 2027, applying initially to Q3/Q4-2027 liabilities; lottery tax 18%→20%. Ministry estimates +€36m (~$39.4m) p.a. on the 2025 base — i.e., an effective rate of ~35% for scale operators (our estimate). It follows the ministry's Sept-2026 rejection of a Farmers & Greens bill for 30%/25% rates and +€100k licence fees; the governing coalition backs the progressive variant.
- **Player card and cashless venues (Gaming Law amendments before the Seimas since 10 Nov 2026)**: a mandatory physical/digital card for ALL online and land-based play, recording stakes, winnings, spend/time limits and restrictions across operators; income verification where a player sets a monthly loss limit above €20,000 (~$21,900); cash eliminated from venues. General provisions 1 May 2027; card and cashless 1 Jan 2029. The Budget & Finance Committee is forming a working group; industry body ALVA warns of leakage to unlicensed sites.
- **Advertising**: gambling advertising is already heavily restricted and a **complete ban is legislated to take effect 1 Jan 2028** (trade-press reporting; implementing scope — sponsorship, affiliates, bonuses — not verified).
- **Licensing is open, indefinite and capital-gated**: since 1 Jul 2022 (Law XIV-581) remote licences no longer require a land-based footprint. One-off licence tax: remote €500k (~$548k); table games/A machines €300k; B machines €300k; betting, totalisator, bingo €100k each; all types €1m (~$1.1m). Minimum paid-up capital €1.158m (~$1.27m) for casinos, remote tables and remote A slots; €289k (~$316k) for remote betting/tote/B/bingo; €144k for remote horse totalisator. A separate operating permit (leidimas) with a state fee is needed per venue/remote operation; statutory processing 30 days.
- **Operator landscape**: 12 licensed gambling companies at 30 Jun 2026 (LPT; one outlet cites 'eight' — discrepancy noted). LPT publishes no operator-level shares. Brands active in the market include TOPsport, Betsafe, Olympic/OlyBet, 7bet, Optibet, Uniclub and Cbet [brand list not verified in this session; shares n/a]. Lotteries are run by a small number of 'major lottery' licensees paying €29.3m (~$32.1m) of tax in 2025.
- **Illegal market is large and rising despite blocking**: LPT's blacklist reached 2,204 domains by early Sep 2026 (2,017 at end-2025); research cited by 15min puts the share of players using illegal sites at 31% in 2026 and the likely unlicensed market at 35–40%. In Oct 2026 LPT blocked its first 'prediction market' site as unlicensed betting.
- **Responsible gambling**: minimum age 21; the national self-exclusion register had taken >101,000 applications by 31 Oct 2026 (~3.5% of a ~2.9m population, cumulative); quarterly requests were 4,515 in Q3-25 (+12.9% YoY). Remote winnings must be paid to the player's gaming account or, on request, a bank account (Gaming Law Art. 20^5).
- **Payments reality**: euro/SEPA market; online deposits via bank transfer and cards (method split not published — n/a); venues remain cash-based until the 2029 cashless mandate. Banks/PSPs face a duty to refuse transfers to blacklisted operators, which the new draft reportedly clarifies [secondary, unverified].
- **Per-capita intensity is high**: FY2025 GGR of €274.1m on ~2.9m residents ≈ €95 (~$104) per head, rising toward ~€105 on the 2026 run-rate — above Latvia and Estonia on secondary estimates — which is precisely what is driving the political response.
- **Read-through for B2Tech**: a slots-led, 78%-online, euro market with clear licensing (≈€1.7m to enter via licence tax + capital) but a fast-closing window — effective tax ~22%→~35% in H2-2027, ad ban 2028, player card 2029 and a 35–40% black market. Value lies in (i) entering before the 2028 ad ban to build brand, (ii) a sharper sportsbook in a betting vertical incumbents are losing, and (iii) compliance-tech (card/limit/KYC, cashless) that will be mandatory for all 12 licensees and their venues.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Online casino (remote Category A slots & table games)

*Remote slots generated €131.6m in 9M (+30%), 57% of all Lithuanian GGR; Q3 growth slowed to ~+25% but the product still out-earns the entire land-based estate nearly 3:1.*

### Remote slots growth

**Remote Category A slots generated €131.6m (~$144.1m) of GGR in 9M 2026, up roughly 30%, now 57% of all Lithuanian gambling GGR.**

- Prior-year 9M figure was €101.2m (~$110.8m); H1 2026 was €85.7m (~$93.8m, +33%).
- Derived Q3 contribution ~€45.9m (~$50.3m) versus ~€36.8m a year earlier (~+25%); deceleration real but modest.
- Nine-month slots increment (~€30m) exceeds the entire market's net gain (€28.5m), so slots growth is funding declines everywhere else.
- Headline: slots still out-earn the entire land-based estate nearly 3:1.

### Tables, Category B, bingo

**Remote table games added €17.7m (~$19.4m), +24%, while combined online casino GGR reached ≈€149.9m in 9M, +28%, ~65% of total GGR.**

- Table games grew +34% in H1 (€12.3m) from €14.3m prior-year 9M; derived Q3 ~€5.4m is only ~+5% YoY.
- Rounding in the regulator's reported totals can swing the quarter by ±€0.3m.
- Remote Category B machines (€0.50 stake, 200× stake max win) fell 67% to €608,708 (~$0.67m) as operators migrate to Category A.
- Online bingo recorded €284.
- Combined online casino Q3 GGR ≈€51.5m (~+21%).

### Stakes, hold, product approval

**Total remote turnover of ~€4.24bn (~$4.64bn) with ~€4.06bn returned gives a 4.2% hold, confirming a slots-led, heavy-recycling mix.**

- Category A machines must return 90–100% to players under the Law (Category B 80–100%).
- Operators' remote-gaming regulations and feature additions (e.g., LPT-approved supplier content, Early Payout, Bet Builder) need regulator clearance before launch.
- Centralised approval lengthens roadmap cycles relative to MGA-style markets.

### Reform exposure

**The vertical is attractive today (22% GGR tax, no turnover levy, euro/SEPA rails) but is the primary target of the reforms.**

- Finance Ministry's 12 Nov 2026 draft taxes GGR above €5m per operator at 36% from 1 Jul 2027.
- An operator earning €60m of annual GGR would pay ~€21m, a 35% effective rate (our estimate).
- From 1 Jan 2029 every session must tie to a cross-operator player card with spend and time limits; income verification once monthly loss limit exceeds €20,000 (~$21,900).
- Norway's state card and Dutch deposit-limit rules suggest an initial regulated-GGR drag concentrated in this high-intensity slots cohort.

### Competitive landscape

**Operator-level revenue is not published (share data n/a); twelve companies held gambling licences at 30 Jun 2026.**

- Online casino understood to be contested by TOPsport, Betsafe, OlyBet, 7bet, Optibet, Uniclub and Cbet-branded licensees [brand list not verified in this session].
- New entrant ticket: €500k one-off remote licence tax plus €1.158m paid-up capital and a permit fee.
- The real barrier is distribution once advertising is banned from 1 Jan 2028.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Sports betting (remote and retail)

*Betting is losing share and now absolute revenue: 9M remote €29.1m (−2%), retail €4.9m (−26%); derived Q3 remote betting fell ~9% even with the World Cup knockout rounds inside the quarter.*

### Remote betting

**Remote betting GGR was €29.1m (~$31.9m) in 9M 2026, down 2% from ~€29.7m, with derived Q3 falling ~9%.**

- H1 was flat at €21.5m (+0.9%); derived Q3 ~€7.6m (~$8.3m) versus ~€8.4m in Q3-25.
- FIFA World Cup round-of-16 to final (1–19 July 2026) fell inside the quarter, making the decline notable.
- Either margins were poor or Lithuanian bettors' wallet share is migrating structurally to slots.

### Retail betting

**Retail betting continued its collapse at €4.9m (~$5.4m) in 9M, −26.1%, with derived Q3 of only ~€1.05m (~−41%).**

- H1 was −20.6% at €3.85m.
- Retail betting shops are in terminal decline and will face cashless and card obligations from 2029; they are not an entry vector.

### Betting share and leakage

**Total betting of ~€34.0m (~$37.2m) in 9M, −6%, equals 14.8% of gambling GGR against 18.1% a year earlier.**

- Mature Western European markets see sportsbook at 25–40% of online GGR; Lithuania's vertical is small for a basketball-mad country.
- Points to pricing/product weakness among incumbents or leakage to unlicensed books; 15min-cited research estimating a 35–40% illegal share supports the latter.
- LPT's October 2026 blacklisting of the first 'prediction market' website as unlicensed betting shows the regulator will police the betting perimeter.

### Tax and licensing

**Betting pays 22% of GGR quarterly, identical to casino, with proposed 29%/36% bands from 1 Jul 2027 applying to combined operator GGR.**

- A betting-led entrant below €5m of GGR would sit in the 22–29% bands.
- Betting-only remote model needs €289k (~$316k) paid-up capital versus €1.158m for casino; remote licence tax (€500k) is the same.
- A land-based betting licence costs €100k.
- Betting regulations, including early payout and bet builders, require LPT approval before launch.

### B2Tech implication

**Betting is the one vertical where incumbents are demonstrably losing, so a sharper pricing/product offer could take share.**

- Only if brand can be built before the 1 Jan 2028 advertising ban.
- Sportsbook must be bundled with the slots product that generates the economics.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Land-based venues & lottery

*Retail GGR fell 6.5% to €50.0m in 9M with every product down and the machine estate shrinking 4.5%; lotteries grew 9% to €131.5m of sales (GGR €57.3m) and face a 20% turnover tax from July 2027.*

### Retail GGR decline

**Land-based GGR fell 6.5% to €50.0m (~$54.8m) in 9M 2026 from €53.5m, with every product down.**

- H1 was €33.7m (−5%); derived Q3 ~€16.3m (~$17.8m, −9.6%).
- Category B halls €22.2m (~$24.3m, −2.4%); casino A machines €12.1m (~$13.2m, −2.4%); casino tables €10.8m (~$11.8m, −7.8%); betting shops €4.9m (−26.1%).
- Venue stakes €296m (~$324m) with €246m paid out, a 16.9% hold.
- Installed machine base fell to 3,736 at 30 Sep 2026 from 3,914 (−4.5%); LPT notes slight contraction in venue numbers.
- Land-based share fell to 22% from 27% in 9M-25.

### Category B and casinos

**Category B is a Lithuanian specificity: maximum €0.50 stake, maximum win 200× stake, minimum three-second cycle, 80–100% RTP, licensed separately.**

- Category B licence tax €300k; capital €289k, or €1.158m if operated inside a casino.
- Casinos (lošimo namai) run A machines and tables and require €1.158m paid-up capital.
- All venue products pay the same 22% GGR tax; the pre-2022 fixed per-device levy has been replaced.

### Lotteries

**Lottery ticket sales reached €131.5m (~$144.0m) in 9M, +9%, with operator GGR of €57.3m (~$62.7m, ~+9%), facing a 20% turnover tax from July 2027.**

- Taxed under a separate statute at 18% of face value of tickets sold; prizes €74.2m (~$81.2m, +9.3%, a 56% payout).
- Lottery operators paid €24.2m (~$26.5m) of tax in the period (FY2025: sales €167.2m, prizes €91.9m, tax €29.3m).
- Finance Ministry's November draft lifts the rate to 20% from 1 Jul 2027; the Sept 2026 alternative restructuring the 18% duty would have cost the budget €11.9m (~$13.0m) a year.
- Lottery GGR is ~20% of the all-in €286.3m market.

### 2029 mandate outlook

**The 2029 cashless mandate and player card are existential for the B-hall and casino estate.**

- Every machine and table needs card-reader and account-based play infrastructure; venue economics already deteriorating at −6.5% will absorb the capex.
- We expect continued closures, consolidation among licence holders and, plausibly, licence sales.
- A possible acquisition route for an operator wanting an incumbent's brand and customer base rather than a greenfield licence.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing & tax regime

*Open, indefinite licences with a €500k remote licence tax and €1.158m capital; 22% GGR tax today, 22/29/36% progressive bands proposed from 1 Jul 2027 (~35% effective for scale operators), lottery 18%→20%.*

### Legal framework

**The 2021 reform (Law XIV-581, in force 1 Jul 2022) replaced the old casino-network-only online model with indefinite per-activity licences issued within 30 days.**

- Rests on the Law on Gaming (No. IX-325, 2001, as amended), the Law on Lottery and Gaming Tax (No. IX-326) and the Law on Lotteries.
- Supervised by the Gaming Control Authority (LPT) under the Ministry of Finance, headed by Virginijus Daukšys.
- Licences per activity: table games & A machines; B machines; bingo; totalisator incl. horse racing; betting; remote gambling; cannot be sold or transferred.
- Separate operating permit (leidimas) with state fee required per venue and for remote operation; LPT must approve gaming regulations.

### Entry costs

**One-off licence taxes range from €100k to €500k (~$548k) for remote gambling, or €1m (~$1.1m) for the full set, with €1.158m capital for casino/remote slots.**

- €300k (~$329k) for table games and A machines, €300k for B machines, €100k (~$110k) each for bingo, totalisator and betting.
- Capital under Art. 13: €1.158m (~$1.27m) for casinos and remote table games/A slots; €289k (~$316k) for B-machine operators and remote betting, totalisator, B machines, bingo; €144k (~$158k) for remote horse totalisator.
- No local-ownership requirement and no mandated local server rule that we could verify [n/a]; operator must be a Lithuanian company (UAB/AB).
- 12 such companies held licences at 30 Jun 2026.

### Current tax

**Gaming tax is 22% of GGR assessed quarterly, up from 20% on 1 Jan 2025; lotteries pay 18% of ticket nominal value, ~41% of lottery GGR.**

- The 2024 bill projected ~€4.4m of extra revenue from the rise to 22%.
- 9M-2026 receipts €74.19m (~$81.2m), +15%: ~€50.0m from gambling operators and €24.2m from lotteries.
- FY2025 was €86.9m (~$95.2m; €57.6m gambling, €29.3m lottery).
- Tax is paid the quarter after accrual; Q3 gambling receipts of ~€19.3m reconcile almost exactly to 22% of Q2 GGR.

### Proposed progressive bands

**The 12 Nov 2026 draft makes gambling tax progressive from 1 Jul 2027: 22% to €3m, 29% on €3–5m, 36% above €5m, lottery to 20%.**

- Initially applied to Q3/Q4-2027 liabilities; ministry estimates +€36m (~$39.4m) a year on 2025 data.
- On 2025 gambling GGR of €274.1m that is a blended rate near 35%, consistent with our operator model (€60m GGR → ~€21m tax, 35.1%).
- Whether bands apply to annual or quarterly GGR is not yet clear [unverified].
- In Aug–Sep 2026 the ministry rejected a Farmers and Greens bill for flat 30% gambling, 25% lottery and +€100k on every licence tax.
- Coalition backed the progressive design; Seimas Budget & Finance Committee set up a working group with industry.

### Operator arithmetic

**At ~34–35% blended, 9M gambling GGR of €229m would yield ~€78–80m of tax versus ~€50m at 22%.**

- On our FY2027 base case (~€335m GGR, est.) the H2-2027 switch alone adds ~€20m of tax.
- Annualised gambling tax rises from ~€73m to ~€110m [estimate].
- Scale operator margins compress by 10–13 points unless the 2028 advertising ban cuts marketing spend commensurately — the one offset the reforms hand to incumbents.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising & enforcement

*Age 21, >101k self-exclusion applications and 2,204 blocked domains already; a cross-operator player card and cashless venues from 1 Jan 2029 and a total ad ban from 1 Jan 2028 will reshape acquisition and retention — while the unlicensed share is estimated at 35–40%.*

### Payments

**Lithuania is a euro, SEPA-integrated market where online deposits run over bank transfers and cards; venues remain cash-based until the 2029 mandate.**

- Gaming Law Art. 20^5 requires remote winnings credited to the gaming account or, on request, a bank account; no retail cash-out for online play.
- LPT publishes no payment-method split (n/a); mobile money is irrelevant.
- Banks and payment institutions expected to refuse transfers to blacklisted operators; November draft reportedly makes this explicit [secondary source; unverified].
- 15min analysis notes the state loses millions of euros of tax to the unlicensed segment, the ministry's rationale for tighter payment blocking.

### Responsible gambling

**Minimum age is 21 and the self-exclusion register had over 101,000 applications by 31 Oct 2026, equivalent to ~3.5% of the population.**

- Age raised from 18 under recent amendments; trade sources date this to 2025 [date unverified].
- Applications are cumulative including renewals; quarterly intake runs 4,500–4,600 (Q3-25: 4,515, +12.9% YoY).
- Draft before the Seimas since 10 Nov 2026 adds a mandatory player card for all online and venue play (general provisions 1 May 2027; card and cashless 1 Jan 2029).
- Income verification where a monthly loss limit above €20,000 (~$21,900) is requested.
- ALVA, the operators' association, argues it needs reconsideration and will push players to unlicensed sites; a parliamentary working group is hearing concerns.

### Advertising ban

**A complete gambling advertising ban is scheduled for 1 Jan 2028, which will freeze brand share and favour the largest incumbents.**

- Reported by multiple trade outlets; precise scope (sponsorship, affiliates, bonus communication, in-venue signage) could not be verified in this session.
- Post-2027 acquisition will depend on organic search, existing databases and product.
- Raises the value of entering, or acquiring, before the ban.

### Enforcement and channelisation

**The LPT blacklist grew from 2,017 domains at end-2025 to 2,204 by early September 2026, but the illegal market is estimated at 35–40%.**

- ISP blocking applies; in October 2026 the first 'prediction market' site was added, treating event contracts as unlicensed betting.
- 15min-cited research shows players using illegal sites rising to 31% in 2026.
- Channelisation of roughly 60–65% is poor for an EU market and the tax and card measures risk worsening it.
- Administrative fines on licensees are published (e.g., the 2024 penalty on UAB Amber Gaming); the LPT's 2025 activity report records supervisory output quarterly.

### B2Tech read-through

**Compliance infrastructure becomes mandatory for all 12 licensees and every venue by 2029, a B2B opportunity as much as a B2C cost.**

- Card-based identity, cross-operator limit synchronisation, income checks above €20k and cashless venue hardware are required.
- A credible, early player-card implementation could be a licensing-relationship asset with a regulator visibly scaling its ambitions.

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Online casino demand is deep and still compounding: remote Category A slots +30% to €131.6m (~$144.1m) in 9M, 57% of all GGR, with 78% online penetration and ~€95–105 (~$104–115) of GGR per capita — among the highest intensities in the Baltics/CEE. _(structural)_
- Licensing is predictable and open: indefinite-term remote licence, 30-day statutory decision, no land-based footprint required since Jul 2022, all-in entry cost ≈€1.7m (~$1.9m) (€500k licence tax + €1.158m capital + permit fee), euro/SEPA payments, no local-ownership rule. _(structural)_
- Betting is a share-available vertical: total betting is only 14.8% of GGR (vs 18.1% a year ago) and remote betting fell ~9% in a World Cup quarter — incumbents are losing it, leaving room for a better-priced, product-led sportsbook bundled with slots. _(structural)_
- Pre-2028 window: the complete advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028 means brand built in 2027 is effectively locked in; an early entrant or an acquisition of one of the 12 licensees before the ban captures a defensible position. _(one-off)_
- Compliance-tech demand is about to be mandated: every licensee and venue must run card-based identity, cross-operator limits, income checks above €20,000 and cashless hardware by 1 Jan 2029 — a B2B revenue line for an operator-technology group. _(structural)_
- Land-based consolidation is accelerating (retail −6.5%, machines −4.5%, closures) and licences are non-transferable but companies are not — distressed licence-holding entities may become inexpensive entry vehicles with existing customer databases. _(mixed)_
- Lotteries are growing 9% on €131.5m (~$144.0m) of sales and the state is signalling a desire to channel rather than prohibit; the tax lift to 20% is modest relative to the 36% top gambling band. _(mixed)_

**Lowlights**

- Tax shock from 1 Jul 2027: 36% on GGR above €5m (~$5.5m) lifts the effective rate for scale operators from 22% to ~35% (est.), adding ~€36m (~$39.4m) a year of state take and compressing operator margins by 10–13 points — one of the highest GGR-tax regimes in the EU. _(structural)_
- Complete advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028 freezes brand share and forecloses conventional customer acquisition for any post-2027 entrant. _(structural)_
- Mandatory cross-operator player card and cashless venues from 1 Jan 2029 add friction, data-sharing obligations and capex; comparable regimes have initially cut regulated GGR by mid-to-high single digits, concentrated in the slots cohort that generates 57% of revenue. _(structural)_
- Channelisation is already weak and deteriorating: 2,204 blocked domains, yet research puts illegal-site usage at 31% and the unlicensed market at 35–40%; higher taxes and card friction risk pushing it higher. _(watch)_
- Scale and concentration risk: a ~2.9m-population market with a single product (online slots) at 57% of GGR, a shrinking betting vertical and a land-based estate in structural decline; growth decelerated from ~+27% in Q2 to +9% in Q3. _(structural)_
- Policy volatility: the tax rate has moved in 2022, 2025 and now 2027; the Farmers & Greens bid for 30%/25% rates and +€100k licence fees was rejected only on timing grounds, and the working-group process could still harden the drafts. _(governance)_
- Data opacity: LPT publishes no operator-level shares, no betting handle/margin and no payment-method split, so competitive benchmarking must rely on secondary sources. _(watch)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The 'bridge' in Lithuania is regulatory rather than guidance-driven. On the revenue side the market moved from +6% (Q1) to ~+27% (Q2, World Cup-assisted) to +9.3% (Q3), with online now 78% and slots 57% of GGR; we expect Q4 at high single digits to low double digits, taking FY2026 gambling GGR to ~€308m ±€4m (~$337m), +12% [estimate]. On the rules side, three dated changes drive the next two years: the Finance Ministry's progressive GGR tax (22/29/36%; lottery 20%) from 1 Jul 2027, the complete advertising ban from 1 Jan 2028, and the Gaming Law amendments introducing general player-protection provisions from 1 May 2027 and the mandatory player card plus cashless venues from 1 Jan 2029. The Seimas began considering the Gaming Law package on 10 Nov 2026 and the tax draft was published 12 Nov 2026; both are coalition-backed and face a parliamentary working group with industry (ALVA) rather than outright opposition, so the direction is set and only calibration is in play.

- **Gambling tax (GGR):** 22% flat → 22% ≤€3m / 29% €3–5m / 36% >€5m (from 1 Jul 2027; ~35% effective for scale operators (est.); +€36m (~$39.4m) p.a.)
- **Advertising:** Restricted → complete ban (1 Jan 2028)
- **Player card & cashless venues:** Voluntary limits → mandatory cross-operator card (general provisions 1 May 2027; card/cashless 1 Jan 2029)
- **FY2026E gambling GGR:** ~€308m (~$337m) vs €274.1m FY25 (+12% (est.); online share ~79%)

Our independent view is that **regulated GGR keeps growing into the tax change but at a slowing pace**: FY2027 ~€330–340m (~$361–372m, +8–10%, est.) with online crossing 80% and land-based contracting 5–8% a year; the H2-2027 switch to progressive bands raises annualised gambling tax from ~€73m to ~€110m (est.) and **compresses scale-operator EBITDA margins by 10–13 points**, partly offset in 2028 by the forced collapse of marketing spend under the ad ban. **2029 is the real inflection**: a mandatory card with cross-operator limits and income checks above €20k should cut regulated slots GGR by mid-to-high single digits in year one [estimate], and with 35–40% of play already unlicensed, channelisation is the swing factor — if blocking and payment interdiction do not improve, the black market absorbs the displaced demand and the state's +€36m tax ambition proves optimistic. Expect **consolidation among the 12 licensees** (retail exits, licence-holding entities changing hands) and incumbents to pre-position brands aggressively in 2027. For B2Tech, the market is a **profitable but late-cycle entry** on current economics and a **compliance-technology opportunity** on the 2029 rules; a greenfield B2C entry only makes sense if executed before the 2028 ad ban and underwritten at a ~35% tax rate.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Seimas timetable and final text of the Gaming Law amendments (player card, cashless, income checks) — whether 1 May 2027 / 1 Jan 2029 dates hold and what the working group concedes to ALVA.
- Final form of the Lottery and Gaming Tax amendments: band thresholds (annual vs quarterly GGR), treatment of multi-licence groups, and whether the 36% top rate survives committee; effective 1 Jul 2027.
- Implementing rules for the 1 Jan 2028 advertising ban — scope on sponsorship, affiliates, bonuses and in-venue promotion.
- LPT FY2026 release (expected ~Mar 2027): Q4 growth, remote share vs 80%, and whether remote betting keeps shrinking.
- Blacklist growth beyond 2,204 domains, bank/PSP payment-blocking enforcement and the next illegal-market prevalence survey (31% usage; 35–40% share).
- Lottery tax design (20% rate vs the €11.9m reallocation alternative) and any widening of LPT's 'prediction market' enforcement to crypto-based event contracts.
- Operator M&A and retail closures among the 12 licensees as the 2029 cashless/card capex approaches.
