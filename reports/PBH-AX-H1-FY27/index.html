H1 FY27 · results · analyst report

PointsBet Holdings

PBH.AX · Released 30 Oct 2026 · Currency AUD (A$m; USD equivalents at AUD/USD 0.703, 30 Oct 2026) · Data confidence: HIGH

Market reaction: Shares closed unchanged at A$0.81 (~US$0.57) on 30 Oct 2026 with negligible volume — MIXI holds 66.4% and the ~34% free float trades a few thousand shares a day — then fell 8.6% to A$0.745 (~US$0.52) on the next session (2 Nov) and sat at A$0.75 by 4 Nov. Market cap ~A$281m (~US$198m) at A$0.81; EV ~A$301m (~US$212m) including the parent loan and leases.

The one-line verdict

A pre-announced miss delivered as pre-announced: H1 FY27 (Apr–Sep 2026) revenue fell 16% to A$112.7m (~US$79.2m) and normalised EBITDA swung from +A$2.9m (~US$2.0m) to -A$6.9m (~-US$4.9m), entirely driven by Australia, where self-imposed VIP compliance cuts took ~A$17m (~US$12m) out of net win and an 8.7% AFL/NRL-season hold in Aug–Sep cost roughly another A$6m (~US$4m). The more important disclosure is below the EBITDA line: corporate cash of A$5.3m (~US$3.7m), A$21m (~US$15m) of a A$23m parent facility drawn, net liabilities of A$34.8m (~US$24.5m) and going-concern language leaning on a MIXI letter of support — the equity story is now a funding story.

1Executive summary

  • Headline in line with the 13-Oct pre-release, but a sharp deterioration YoY. Revenue A$112.7m (~US$79.2m), -16% vs the unaudited PCP (Apr–Sep 2025: A$134.9m / ~US$94.8m) and -13% vs the reviewed statutory comparative (Jul–Dec 2025: A$129.3m / ~US$90.9m). The trading update had guided to ~A$113m, so the 'surprise' was zero; there is no meaningful sell-side consensus to miss (Yahoo shows a thin A$1.08 average target).
  • Normalised EBITDA -A$6.9m (~-US$4.9m), mid-point of the -A$6m to -A$8m guide, versus +A$2.9m (~US$2.0m) in the PCP — a A$9.8m swing and a margin of -6.1% (-830bps). Statutory EBITDA was -A$8.5m (~-US$6.0m) after A$1.6m of significant items (A$2.2m legal/one-offs less A$0.6m AASB 16), versus -A$12.2m in the Dec-25 half, which carried A$5.9m of MIXI transaction costs and A$3.1m of share-based payments.
  • Statutory net loss -A$17.3m (~-US$12.2m), EPS -5.0¢ (~-US$0.035), a 22% narrower loss than the Dec-25 half (-A$22.2m, -6.4¢) — but that optical improvement is one-off cost roll-off; normalised net loss widened 18% to -A$15.7m (~-US$11.0m) from a derived -A$13.3m.
  • Australia drove more than 100% of the decline. Australian revenue fell 19% to A$91.8m (~US$64.5m; -A$20.9m) and segment EBITDA fell 76% to A$2.9m (~US$2.0m; -A$9.1m, margin 3.2% vs 10.6%). Canada revenue fell 6% to A$20.9m (~US$14.7m) but grew 3% in C$ (AUD +10% vs CAD cost A$2.1m of revenue); its segment loss widened to -A$6.8m (~-US$4.8m) from -A$4.1m on World Cup/legacy-sponsorship marketing.
  • Two self-described Australian themes. (1) 'Proactive compliance actions' on the highest-value cohort produced sustained double-digit VIP turnover and revenue declines throughout H1 — management calls these 'important decisions… in an increasingly complex regulatory environment'. (2) A deliberate acceleration of mass-market sports actives, which raises net-win-margin volatility: NW margin ran 10.4% Apr–Jul, then 8.7% in Aug–Sep as AFL/NRL results went the punters' way, landing at 9.8% for the half (PCP 10.4%, target 10%).
  • Volume vs price: mostly volume. Group turnover -16% to A$1,116.0m (~US$784.5m). Our decomposition of the A$22.9m Australian net-win decline: ~A$17m (~US$12m) from the 14% handle fall (compliance-driven) and ~A$6m (~US$4m) from the 60bps NW-margin drop. The Aug–Sep 'luck' alone (1.7pp on ~A$344m of handle) is worth ~A$5.8m — i.e. roughly the entire normalised EBITDA loss.
  • Structural margin is actually improving; promo intensity is not. Australian structural (expected) gross-win margin rose ~50bps YoY and actual GW/NW margins matched structural for the half as a whole (GW margin 13.6%, -10bps). But promotions (GW minus NW, derived A$39.1m / ~US$27.5m) rose to 27.9% of gross win from 24.4% — 32.8% in Aug–Sep — and to ~3.8% of handle from ~3.3%, despite absolute promo spend being 2% lower.
  • Actives up, yield down — the mass-market mix shift is visible. Group 12m-rolling cash actives +6% to 310,058 (Australia +2% to 237,265; Canada +18% to 72,793). Derived Australian H1 net win per active fell ~20% to ~A$427 (~US$300) from ~A$534: the VIP cohort has been cut and replaced by lower-value AFL 'Pull 'Em' and World Cup recruits.
  • Cost base held flat, but marketing is now 29% of revenue. Total opex (incl. marketing) A$63.9m (~US$44.9m), -A$0.3m YoY; marketing +6% to A$33.1m (~US$23.3m), 29.4% of revenue (+620bps); other opex -6% to A$30.8m (~US$21.7m). Versus the Dec-25 half, employee costs fell 19% to A$20.7m (no SBP charge), consulting fell 92% to A$0.5m and D&A fell 13% to A$8.6m. No formal cost-transformation programme with quantified targets has been disclosed.
  • Tax/product-fee leakage improved — one genuine structural positive. Australia paid A$48.0m (~US$33.7m) in GST, point-of-consumption taxes and racing product fees, 47.4% of net win versus 49.1% (-170bps), with better racing gross-profit efficiency partly offset by 'high AFL product fees'. Group gross margin rose 90bps to 50.6%.
  • Canada: iGaming growing, sportsbook VIP churn, marketing front-loaded. In C$, iGaming turnover +21% to C$592.9m and net win +9%; sports turnover -32% to C$83.4m after a small number of high-staking clients churned in Q1, though sports NW margin improved 150bps to 6.8%. Marketing +16% in A$ (+28% in C$) to A$9.1m (~US$6.4m) = 43.5% of revenue, tied to the FIFA World Cup and the final year of legacy sponsorship deals. The iGaming platform was migrated to Bede Gaming.
  • Alberta is the only new TAM. Sportsbook and iGaming launch in Alberta — Canada's second regulated province — is 'expected in 2026', i.e. inside H2 FY27, subject to approvals. Expect launch marketing to keep Canada loss-making through FY27. Separately, AGCO cut a proposed 5-day Ontario site suspension (failure to detect suspicious Jontay Porter bets) to 2 days via settlement in October.
  • Cash and funding are the real story. Cash A$24.8m (~US$17.4m) but only A$5.3m (~US$3.7m) is corporate; A$19.5m is player cash against A$17.9m of player liabilities. Operating cash flow -A$8.1m (~-US$5.7m; -A$9.6m ex player-cash movement), capitalised software A$7.1m (~US$5.0m), FCF (our calc) ~-A$15.6m (~-US$11.0m). The gap was filled by drawing A$18.0m from MIXI Australia: A$21.3m (~US$15.0m) of a A$23m facility is now drawn at 7.19%, maturing within 12 months of each drawdown.
  • Going-concern basis rests on the parent. Net liabilities A$34.8m (~US$24.5m; NTA -16.7¢/share). The Directors state the Group is 'seeking to access working capital funding options from external funding sources' and an increase to the parent facility, and that MIXI Australia has provided a letter of financial support. PwC's review conclusion is unmodified, but minority holders should read this as: the next A$15–20m (~US$11–14m) has to come from MIXI or an external lender.
  • Governance and structure. MIXI, Inc (TYO:2121) holds 66.4% via MIXI Australia (since Sept 2025); the board is five directors including three MIXI nominees; founder Sam Swanell is now listed as Executive Director and Senior Advisor (current Group CEO identity not verified in the filings read); CFO/CoSec is Alister Lui. The financial year-end moved to 31 March (changed 12 Dec 2025), making FY26 a 9-month transition period and the PCP an unaudited management comparative. No dividend.
  • No numeric FY27 guidance; qualitative H2 optimism. The deck's framing is 'positive signals to return to PCP growth in H2', and the 13-Oct update cited early-H2 re-alignment of actual and expected net-win margins. H2 (Oct–Mar) is racing-heavier (Spring Carnival) and AFL/NRL-light, which historically stabilises Australian hold; the VIP compliance cuts, which appear to have begun in H2 FY26, should start to annualise by Q4 FY27.
  • Event engagement not quantified. The FIFA World Cup (June–July 2026) fell inside H1 and drove 'mass market campaigns' in Australia and front-loaded Canadian marketing, but management disclosed no World Cup handle, actives or revenue — a gap versus peer disclosure standards; the only comparative given is +6% group actives.
  • Share price: illiquid non-reaction, then a drift down. Shares closed unchanged at A$0.81 (~US$0.57) on 30 Oct on negligible volume, then fell 8.6% to A$0.745 (~US$0.52) on 2 Nov. Market cap ~A$281m (~US$198m) at A$0.81; EV ~A$301m (~US$212m) including the parent loan and leases.
  • Thesis read-through. PointsBet is a sub-scale challenger (Australian and Ontario market shares not disclosed) that has traded VIP revenue for regulatory robustness and is spending 29% of revenue on marketing to rebuild the base with lower-yield mass-market customers. Structural margin gains and tax-leakage improvement are real, but with FCF of ~-A$31m annualised (~-US$22m) against A$5m of corporate cash, the investment case for the ~34% minority is a function of MIXI's intentions (fund, dilute, or mop up) rather than operating momentum.

3Segment deep-dive: Australia (Australian Trading — online sports & fixed-odds racing)

Compliance-driven VIP cuts took 14% off handle, then an 8.7% AFL/NRL hold in Aug–Sep took 76% off segment EBITDA; structural margin is improving but promo intensity hit 32.8% of gross win.

Revenue and VIP reset

Revenue fell 19% to A$91.8m as compliance-driven VIP cuts took 14% off turnover; we read this as a permanent reset, not timing.

  • Net win down 18% to A$101.2m (~US$71.1m); turnover down 14% to A$1,031.4m (~US$725m); revenue ~US$64.5m.
  • Proactive actions on the highest-value cohort produced double-digit turnover and revenue declines in that cohort throughout the half.
  • 13-Oct update framed these as important decisions in an increasingly complex Australian regulatory environment.
  • Actions appear to have started in H2 FY26; comparison does not ease until they annualise.
  • Market share undisclosed; sub-scale challenger versus Sportsbet, TAB, Entain's Ladbrokes/Neds and bet365, so numbers are company-specific.
“the continued strengthening of the Company's compliance standards”Management

Hold and promotions

Net-win margin fell to 9.8% from 10.4% after an 8.7% Aug–Sep AFL/NRL hold; promotions reached 32.8% of gross win in that period.

  • Gross-win margin 13.6% (-10bps); structural gross-win margin +50bps on PCP, with actual margins matching structural for the half overall.
  • Net-win margin 10.4% April–July, then 8.7% August–September; 10% internal target.
  • Promotions A$39.1m (~US$27.5m), 2% lower, but 27.9% of gross win versus 24.4% and ~3.8% of handle versus ~3.3%.
  • Of the A$22.9m net-win decline, ~A$17m (~US$12m) is volume and ~A$6m (~US$4m) margin; Aug–Sep variance alone ~A$5.8m (~US$4.1m), the whole group normalised EBITDA loss.
  • Bear case: chasing mass-market AFL punters with 'Pull 'Em' and World Cup campaigns means a higher-variance book just as the VIP cushion is gone.

Customer economics and marketing

Actives rose 2% to 237,265 yet net win per active fell ~20% to ~A$427, while marketing rose to 26.0% of revenue.

  • Derived H1 net win per active ~A$427 (~US$300) from ~A$534.
  • Marketing up 2% to A$23.9m (~US$16.8m), 26.0% of revenue versus 20.8% (+520bps), on FIFA World Cup and AFL Pull 'Em campaigns.
  • Gross-profit efficiency improved in racing but softened in sports because of high AFL product fees; the mass-market sports pivot is a lower-gross-margin pivot.

Costs and segment EBITDA

Taxes and fees fell to 47.4% of net win and opex fell 4%, but segment EBITDA still dropped 76% to A$2.9m (3.2% margin).

  • GST, point-of-consumption taxes and product fees A$48.0m (~US$33.7m), 47.4% of net win versus 49.1% (-170bps).
  • Gross profit down 17% to A$46.9m (~US$33.0m); gross margin on revenue up 110bps to 51.1%; other opex down 4% to A$20.1m (~US$14.1m).
  • EBITDA A$2.9m (~US$2.0m), 3.2% margin versus 10.6%, and 63% below the A$7.8m of the Jul–Dec 2025 half.
  • The ~A$9.1m decline reconciles to the ~A$6m margin shortfall plus after-tax, after-promo flow-through of lost VIP volume.

Outlook

Reversion to ~10% net-win margin in H2 is plausible, but returning to PCP revenue growth requires mass-market to outgrow the annualising VIP hole.

  • October update pointed to early-H2 re-alignment of actual and expected net-win margins.
  • H2 (Oct–Mar) is structurally better for hold: Spring Racing Carnival, summer cricket and tennis, no AFL/NRL.
  • On ~A$170m monthly handle, each 1pp of margin is worth ~A$1.7m (~US$1.2m) a month.
  • With promotions near 28–33% of gross win, EBITDA leverage from any recovery will be thinner than the FY25 cost base implied.

4Segment deep-dive: Canada (Canadian Trading — Ontario sportsbook & iGaming; Alberta pending)

Up 3% in C$ with iGaming turnover +21% and actives +18%, but AUD strength, Q1 VIP churn in sports and World Cup/legacy-sponsorship marketing widened the segment loss to -A$6.8m (-32.5% margin).

Revenue and FX

Revenue fell 6% to A$20.9m but rose 3% in local currency, with 73% of revenue now iGaming.

  • Average AUD/CAD moved to 0.9851 from 0.8939, a 10% appreciation estimated to cut reported revenue by A$2.1m (~US$1.5m) and flatter the loss by A$0.7m.
  • Mix: iGaming A$15.2m (~US$10.7m), sportsbook A$5.7m (~US$4.0m); an online-casino business with a sportsbook attached.
  • Ontario is a crowded ~50-operator market; PointsBet is a mid-tier brand with undisclosed share.

iGaming

iGaming turnover grew 21% in C$ to C$592.9m, but net win was flat-to-down 1% in A$ at A$15.2m.

  • Turnover up 10% in A$ to A$602.5m (~US$423.6m); gross-win margin eased 30bps to 2.9%, net-win margin 30bps to 2.5%.
  • Net win grew 9% in C$; derived promotions ~12% of iGaming gross win versus ~11%.
  • Casino product migrated onto Bede Gaming's platform, giving more flexibility on content, engagement and retention to lift hold toward peer levels.
“working well since launch”Management

Sportsbook

Sportsbook turnover fell 32% in C$ on Q1 VIP churn, but higher margins limited the net-win decline to 20% (A$5.7m).

  • Turnover C$83.4m (-38% in A$ to A$84.7m / ~US$59.5m); churn of a few high-staking clients in April–June; mass-market consistent.
  • Gross-win margin up 270bps to 10.6%; net-win margin up 150bps to 6.8%.
  • Derived promotions ~36% of sports gross win versus ~33%; margin gain is being spent on FIFA World Cup and NFL launch, not banked.
“enabling reinvestment”Management

Costs and segment loss

Marketing rose to 43.5% of revenue and segment EBITDA worsened 65% to -A$6.8m, a -32.5% margin.

  • Cash actives up 18% to 72,793; marketing up 16% in A$ (28% in C$) to A$9.1m (~US$6.4m) versus 35.4% of revenue.
  • Spend reflects 'the upfront weighting of FY27 spend tied to the FIFA World Cup and the final year of legacy sponsorship deals'.
  • Other opex up 11% to A$7.8m (~US$5.5m); gross profit down 6% to A$10.1m (~US$7.1m) at a stable 48.3% margin.
  • EBITDA -A$6.8m (~-US$4.8m), margin -32.5% versus -18.4%, but 17% better than -A$8.3m in Jul–Dec 2025.
  • Marketing should step down in H2 as World Cup and legacy sponsorships roll off, but Alberta will absorb much of that saving.

Alberta and regulation

Alberta launch expected in calendar 2026 is the only growth optionality, but unit economics are unproven at a -32.5% margin after four years.

  • Sportsbook and iGaming launch in a second regulated province expected calendar 2026, subject to approvals — effectively inside H2 FY27.
  • AGCO settled its February five-day Ontario suspension proposal (Jontay Porter-linked suspicious wagers) at two days, removing a tail risk.
  • The iGaming engine is genuine, but a launch funded from a A$23m parent facility 91% drawn needs MIXI's cheque-book or a very disciplined budget.

5Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Customer base growing through the reset: 12m-rolling cash actives +6% to 310,058 (Australia +2%, Canada +18%) despite the VIP cull — the mass-market acquisition engine (AFL 'Pull 'Em', World Cup) is working on volume, if not yet on yield. structural
  • Hold quality is improving underneath the luck: Australian structural (expected) gross-win margin +50bps YoY, actual margins matched structural for the half, and Canadian sports NW margin +150bps to 6.8%. The Aug–Sep 8.7% hold (~A$5.8m / ~US$4.1m of net win, our estimate) is variance, not pricing. one-off
  • Tax and product-fee leakage fell: A$48.0m (~US$33.7m) of GST/POCT/product fees = 47.4% of Australian net win vs 49.1% (-170bps), lifting group gross margin 90bps to 50.6% — a rare structural lever in a jurisdiction where taxes usually only go one way. structural
  • Canada is growing in local currency: revenue +3% in C$, iGaming turnover +21% to C$592.9m, iGaming net win +9%, Bede platform migration complete, and Alberta (sportsbook + iGaming) targeted for calendar 2026 — the only incremental TAM in the group. early
  • Cost discipline on the non-marketing base: other opex -6% to A$30.8m (~US$21.7m); versus the Dec-25 half, employee costs -19% to A$20.7m, consulting -92% to A$0.5m, D&A -13% to A$8.6m (~US$6.0m); total opex incl. marketing flat at A$63.9m (~US$44.9m). structural
  • Delivered the pre-release and cleaned up the one-offs: revenue A$112.7m (~US$79.2m) vs ~A$113m guided, normalised EBITDA -A$6.9m (~-US$4.9m) at the mid-point of -A$6m to -A$8m; significant items fell to A$1.6m from A$8.9m, narrowing the statutory loss 22% to -A$17.3m (~-US$12.2m). one-off

▼ Lowlights

  • Revenue -16%, net win -17%, normalised EBITDA swing -A$9.8m (~-US$6.9m): Australia's VIP compliance reset removed ~A$17m (~US$12m) of net win on our decomposition and will not annualise until late FY27; Australian segment EBITDA -76% to A$2.9m (~US$2.0m), margin 3.2% vs 10.6%. structural
  • Promotional intensity is rising, not falling: 27.9% of Australian gross win (32.8% in Aug–Sep) vs 24.4%, ~3.8% of handle vs ~3.3%; Canadian sportsbook promos ~36% of gross win. The margin gains being generated are being reinvested rather than dropped to EBITDA. mixed
  • Marketing at 29.4% of group revenue (+620bps) while revenue fell 16%; Canada at 43.5% of revenue (+810bps). Management attributes the Canadian step-up to World Cup front-loading and the last year of legacy sponsorships, but Alberta launch spend will replace much of that in H2. watch
  • Going-concern language and a parent-dependent balance sheet: corporate cash A$5.3m (~US$3.7m); A$21.3m (~US$15.0m) of a A$23m (~US$16.2m) MIXI Australia facility drawn at 7.19% with ≤12-month maturities; net liabilities A$34.8m (~US$24.5m), NTA -16.7¢; Directors are seeking external working-capital funding and a larger parent facility backed by a MIXI letter of support. governance
  • Cash burn ran at ~A$31m (~US$22m) annualised: OCF -A$8.1m (~-US$5.7m), -A$9.6m ex player-cash movement, plus A$7.1m (~US$5.0m) of capitalised software — FCF ~-A$15.6m (~-US$11.0m) in six months against A$5.3m of unrestricted cash. structural
  • Yield dilution from the mass-market pivot: derived Australian net win per active -20% to ~A$427 (~US$300); high AFL product fees mean the new sports customers also carry lower gross margin than racing. structural
  • Minority governance and liquidity: MIXI holds 66.4% with three of five board seats; the founder-CEO is now 'Executive Director and Senior Advisor'; the stock traded ~zero volume on results day and fell 8.6% the next session. Independent view. Our H2 FY27 (Oct-26–Mar-27) estimate is revenue of ~A$120–128m (~US$84–90m), i.e. FY27 revenue of ~A$233–241m (~US$164–169m) — flat to modestly up on the H2 PCP, consistent with management's 'return to PCP growth' but not more. The arithmetic: Australian handle of ~A$170m/month at a reverted ~10% NW margin adds ~A$2m/month of net win versus the Aug–Sep run-rate (1.3pp × ~A$172m), Spring Carnival racing carries better product-fee economics, and Canada should grow mid-single digits in C$ with iGaming carrying the sportsbook. On margin we model H2 normalised EBITDA of roughly -A$3m to +A$1m (~-US$2m to +US$0.7m), giving FY27 normalised EBITDA of ~-A$6m to -A$10m (~-US$4m to -US$7m) — the Australian margin recovery (worth ~A$6m vs H1 if promos also normalise below 25% of gross win) is largely consumed by Alberta launch marketing, with Canadian legacy-sponsorship roll-off the swing factor. Leverage direction is up: with D&A of ~A$17m/yr non-cash but capitalised software of ~A$14m/yr cash, we expect H2 FCF of -A$8m to -A$12m (~-US$6m to -US$8m), taking parent borrowings above A$25m (~US$18m) by March 2027 unless an external facility or equity is introduced; net debt/EBITDA stays not meaningful.
