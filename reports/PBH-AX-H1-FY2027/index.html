The one-line verdict

PointsBet pre-announced a sharp H1 FY27 miss: revenue ~A$113m (-16% YoY vs A$135m) and a normalised EBITDA loss of A$6–8m, entirely attributable to Australia, where self-imposed VIP compliance tightening has cut highest-value-cohort turnover/revenue by double digits all half and a deliberate mass-market push ran into punter-friendly AFL results and promo/product-fee dilution from the Pull 'Em product. The Canada/iGaming growth leg (FY26 revenue +13%) is intact but, at ~18% of group, is too small to carry a structurally de-rated Australian book; with cash of roughly A$23m and a 66%-owned register, the quarter re-opens the question of how long MIXI tolerates a listed, loss-making minority structure.

1Executive summary

  • Pre-announced miss, not a result. On 13 Oct 2026 PointsBet (ASX: PBH; 66.43% owned by MIXI, Inc.) issued an ASX trading update guiding H1 FY27 (1 Apr–30 Sep 2026) group revenue to ~A$113m, -16% YoY vs A$135m, and normalised EBITDA to a loss of A$6–8m. Full accounts are due 30 Oct 2026; nothing below the revenue/EBITDA lines is yet disclosed.
  • Guidance change is 100% Australia. The company states the decline is 'attributable to the Australian business'; Canada is not cited as a contributor. Two themes flagged at the 25 Aug 2026 AGM did the damage: (i) compliance-driven attrition of the highest-value client cohort, and (ii) a deliberate mass-market sports push that ran into adverse results.
  • VIP attrition is structural, not luck. Management reports a 'sustained trend of double-digit turnover and revenue declines vs PCP throughout H1' from the highest-value cohort, attributed to 'proactive compliance actions'. At the AGM the CEO said this cohort fell only 1–2% across FY26 but went double-digit negative in the final months — i.e. the drag accelerated into FY27 and has now persisted for ~9 months.
  • Mass-market volume up, margin down. Mass-market sports actives, turnover and structural net-win margin were all up vs PCP, but were 'eroded by negative variance in actual Net Win margin throughout the half', principally AFL results in August–September. No handle or margin figures were quantified in the update.
  • Pull 'Em is a self-inflicted margin drag. The product (launched March 2026) was made available on every AFL game and the FIFA World Cup to recruit mass-market actives and push Same Game Multis; management concedes it lifts sports turnover but lowers net-win and gross-profit margins via higher promotional cost and higher AFL product-fee rates on Premium Bet Types.
  • The EBITDA comparator in the press is not like-for-like. Media compare the A$6–8m loss to a A$3.3m loss 'in H1 FY26' — that figure is the six months to 31 Dec 2025 (old June-year basis). On our arithmetic the true Apr–Sep 2025 pcp was likely positive (Q4 FY25 alone contributed +A$10.0m normalised EBITDA: FY25 A$11.2m less 9M-to-Mar-25 A$1.2m), so the YoY swing is plausibly A$11–20m, not ~A$4m [estimate; Q1 FY26 standalone EBITDA not disclosed].
  • Revenue trend was already deteriorating before H1 FY27. Derived quarterly revenue: Q4 FY25 A$73.0m, Q1 FY26 ~A$62.0m, Q2 FY26 ~A$67.3m, Q3 FY26 ~A$57.3m (-10.5% YoY). H1 FY27 at ~A$56.5m/quarter is a continuation and acceleration of the Jan–Mar 2026 run-rate, not a one-quarter shock [estimates from reported period totals].
  • FY26 (9M to 31 Mar 2026) base: flat top line, negative EBITDA. Group revenue A$186.6m (-1%), total net win A$202.0m (-1%), gross profit A$93.6m (-6%, margin 50.2% vs 53.0%), normalised EBITDA -A$0.8m (vs +A$1.2m), statutory EBITDA -A$11.7m, statutory loss A$26.6m (LPS 7.7c vs 5.5c for 12-month FY25). Yogonet reports a A$19.2m loss figure we could not reconcile — we quote the A$26.6m carried in the annual report commentary.
  • Australia FY26: tax take now ~48% of net win. Turnover A$1,690.7m (-1%), gross-win margin flat at 13.3%, net-win margin 9.9% (-20bp), net win A$167.3m (-4%), revenue A$152.0m (-4%), gross profit A$77.0m (-8%), segment statutory EBITDA A$14.2m (-30%, margin 9.3% vs 12.9%). GST, POCT and product fees of A$79.8m absorbed 47.7% of Australian net win — the CEO puts taxes and fees at ~50% of revenue.
  • Canada FY26: iGaming is the only growth engine. Revenue A$34.6m (+13%), net win A$34.7m (+14%), with iGaming net win A$23.6m (+28%, now 68% of Canadian net win) while sports turnover collapsed 39% to A$161.5m on lower VIP play (sports net win -8% to A$11.1m, implied net margin ~6.9% vs ~4.6%). Ontario casino migrated to Bede Gaming on 28 Apr 2026; Alberta pre-registration opened Feb 2026 but the brand still has not launched (guided 'H2 CY2026').
  • No cost-transformation program; opex trimmed only at the margin. FY26 9M opex A$105.2m (56.4% of revenue): S&M A$49.2m (26.4% of revenue, vs A$50.2m), employee A$32.7m, product & tech A$9.5m, admin A$13.8m. Marketing has been cut by exiting NRL club sponsorships (Manly, Cronulla, 2025) and 'materially' reducing free-to-air TV, but no quantified savings target or timeline exists.
  • Capital position is thin and un-levered. Cash was A$26.1m at 31 Dec 2025 (A$8.9m corporate cash) and ~A$23.0m at 31 Mar 2026 per third-party balance-sheet data [unverified]; the same source shows total liabilities (A$72.7m) exceeding total assets (A$55.2m), implying negative book equity after the 2024 capital return. No bank debt is disclosed; net debt/leverage is therefore not meaningful, but an H1 EBITDA loss of A$6–8m plus capex and Alberta launch costs implies material cash burn into a small balance sheet.
  • MIXI overhang cuts both ways. MIXI's A$1.25 cash offer closed 12 Sep 2025 (66.43%; implied equity A$434m); at A$0.815 the equity is worth ~A$283m (-35%) with ~117m free-float shares (~A$95m). The group has been consolidated into MIXI since 1 Oct 2025, the board was reconstituted and the year-end moved to 31 March. A parent-funded balance sheet is a backstop; a low-ball mop-up of minorities is the risk.
  • Leadership and governance. Andrew Catterall (ex-Australia CEO) became Group CEO on 1 Feb 2026; co-founder Sam Swanell remains an executive director/senior adviser. VIP account-manager remuneration has been restructured to remove commissions linked to customer losses; the 2026 AGM drew shareholder questions on VIP inducements following Senate-inquiry evidence.
  • Regulatory backdrop is tightening, and management says offshore is winning. The federal gambling-advertising reform package passed parliament (Aug 2026) with implementation detail pending; the National Self-Exclusion Register (BetStop) is cited for racing-turnover softness. The CEO estimates illegal offshore operators at ~30% of the licensed domestic market and growing faster than licensed operators.
  • Strategic repositioning: 50/50 sport/racing, 'compliance above benchmark'. PointsBet is deliberately moving turnover mix toward 50/50 sport/racing and operating compliance settings above industry norms, accepting the loss of high-staking three-code racing clients. It also bought racing/sports content business Foxcatcher (July 2026; terms not disclosed) as a content/acquisition play.
  • Event read-through. The half contained the entire 2026 FIFA World Cup (June–July, widely reported as punter-friendly early on) plus AFL/NRL finals; PointsBet did not quantify World Cup revenue, handle or actives, and explicitly pinned the margin miss on AFL in Aug–Sep rather than the World Cup.
  • Share price: the damage was done pre-announcement. Shares closed ~A$0.815 on 13 Oct (-0.6% on the day; intraday A$0.805) having already fallen ~9% from A$0.88 over the prior week; A$0.805 on 14 Oct sits near the A$0.755 52-week low (YTD -8%).
  • Thesis read-through. The bull case (Canada iGaming compounding, MIXI backstop, Australian share gains in a shrinking market) is intact only on the first two legs; the Australian franchise is being deliberately shrunk at the top end while the replacement mass-market cohort is lower-margin and more tax-intensive. Until the VIP base annualises (from ~Q3 FY27) and Alberta goes live, group EBITDA is likely to stay negative and the equity story rests on MIXI's intentions rather than operating leverage.

3Segment deep-dive: Australia (Australian Trading)

Compliance-led VIP shrinkage plus a promo-heavy mass-market pivot have turned a -4% FY26 revenue decline into a double-digit H1 FY27 fall; the tax/fee take at ~48% of net win leaves no cushion.

H1 FY27 downgrade

Australia is ~82% of group revenue and the sole source of the H1 FY27 downgrade, implicitly down high-teens to low-20s percent.

  • Trading update gives no Australian revenue number; group revenue guided to ~A$113m against a A$135m pcp, with Canada excluded from the explanation.
  • Pcp Australian revenue ~A$112m if Canada ran at ~A$23m in Apr–Sep 2025, both estimates from reported period totals.
  • Management framed the decline as 'a continuation of two key themes identified at our FY26 AGM': stronger compliance standards and a deliberate mass-market pivot.
  • Compliance produced 'a sustained trend of double-digit turnover and revenue declines vs PCP throughout H1 from our highest value client cohort'.
  • We read the compliance theme as structural and the mass-market pivot as a mix of structural (lower-margin cohort, higher product-fee incidence) and one-off (AFL luck).
“a deliberate choice to accelerate growth in mass market sports actives, which can lead to short term volatility in Net Win margins”Management

VIP compliance shrinkage

The VIP decline is compliance-led and structural; it will not mean-revert and annualises from roughly the March 2027 quarter.

  • FY26 annual report attributed racing-turnover softness to compliance settings 'above industry benchmarks' and the National Self-Exclusion Register.
  • High-staking, high-volume clients with the most at-risk behaviours are concentrated in three-code racing.
  • At the August AGM the CEO said highest-value-cohort turnover and gross profit were down only 1–2% across FY26 but double-digit negative in the final months.
  • VIP account-manager and external BDM remuneration restructured to prioritise compliance and remove direct commissions on customer losses.
  • Right posture for a licensee facing a Senate inquiry into inducements.
“regardless of revenue impact”CEO

Mass-market pivot

Volume growth is not converting: FY26 net win fell 4% to A$167.3m and H1 FY27 net-win margin ran below structural all half.

  • FY26 (9M) sports and racing turnover A$1,690.7m (-1%); gross-win margin held at 13.3% despite customer-friendly Spring Racing Carnival results.
  • Net-win margin slipped 20bp to 9.9%; gap between gross and net margin widened to ~340bp.
  • Jul–Dec 2025 half: turnover A$1.19bn (+4%), gross win A$156.1m (~13.1% margin), revenue -1% to A$115.1m, gross profit -6% to A$52.3m.
  • H1 FY27: mass-market sports actives, turnover and structural net-win margin improved vs PCP, but 'adverse results for key sports in August and September — primarily AFL'.
  • No handle, net-margin or promo-as-%-of-handle figures provided; the 30 October accounts are needed to size luck versus structural.

Pull 'Em and fee leakage

Pull 'Em lifts turnover but leaks margin twice; with tax/fees at 47.7% of net win, each mass-market dollar yields less gross profit than the VIP dollar it replaces.

  • Launched March 2026, then available on every AFL game and the FIFA World Cup to recruit mass-market actives and grow Premium Bet Types such as Same Game Multis.
  • CEO acknowledged at the AGM it lifted sports turnover but weakened net-win and gross-profit margins via higher promotional cost and higher AFL product-fee rates.
  • GST, POCT and product fees were A$79.8m or 47.7% of FY26 Australian net win; the CEO rounds it to ~50% of revenue.
  • Australian gross profit fell 8% to A$77.0m in FY26 (50.7% of revenue); segment statutory EBITDA fell 30% to A$14.2m (9.3% margin vs 12.9%).
  • H1 FY27 will almost certainly show a further step down.

Customers, marketing, regulation

A flat active base against -4% net win implies falling spend per active; advertising reform is the swing factor and will be 'more complicated and more expensive'.

  • FY26 group cash-active clients rose 1% to 298.1k with Australia down ~1%; ARPU not disclosed.
  • NRL club sponsorships (Manly, Cronulla) voluntarily ended in 2025 and free-to-air TV 'materially' cut; stated ambition is a 50/50 sport/racing turnover split.
  • July 2026 Foxcatcher acquisition (racing/sports content and a small wagering book; terms undisclosed) is a content-led acquisition channel rather than consolidation.
  • Federal advertising reform package has passed but definitions are pending.
  • Illegal offshore operators, estimated at ~30% of the licensed market and growing faster, pay no tax.
“more complicated and more expensive”Management

Outlook and watch items

No Australian segment guidance; we expect a smaller YoY decline in Oct–Mar but segment EBITDA well below the FY26 run-rate.

  • Oct–Mar half (Spring Racing Carnival, summer cricket, NRL/AFL off-season) should see AFL luck normalise but a continued double-digit VIP drag.
  • From the 30 October release we want Australian net-win margin versus structural margin and the mass-market actives number.
  • Also wanted: promotional spend as a percentage of handle and whether the VIP decline is stabilising month-on-month.

4Segment deep-dive: Canada (Canadian Trading — Ontario; Alberta pending)

iGaming carries Canada (+28% net win, 68% of segment) while sports handle has been deliberately shrunk; a growth leg, but at ~18% of group it cannot offset Australia, and Alberta is still not live.

iGaming-led growth

Canada grew in FY26 with iGaming net win up 28% to A$23.6m, now 68% of the segment, but at ~18% of group cannot offset Australia.

  • Canada was not blamed for the H1 FY27 shortfall; annual report cited 'strong iGaming momentum and a recovery in margins across both Sports Betting and iGaming'. We assume growth in the half; no number given.
  • FY26 (9M) revenue rose 13% to A$34.6m and total net win 14% to A$34.7m, on higher turnover and better margins in core game categories, particularly slots.
  • Jul–Dec 2025 half: revenue A$24.4m (+35%), iGaming net win A$17.2m (+58%), gross profit A$11.8m (+30%), implying Jan–Mar 2026 revenue of roughly A$10m.
  • Canadian cash actives grew 9% in FY26 against a 1% decline in Australia, so group active-base growth is essentially a Canadian story.

Ontario sports betting

Sports handle has been deliberately shrunk; FY26 turnover fell 39% to A$161.5m but net win fell only 8% to A$11.1m as margin improved.

  • Jul–Dec 2025 sports turnover A$109.2m (-35%), attributed to lower VIP play and higher gross-win margins that reduce reinvestment.
  • Implied net margin rose to ~6.9% from ~4.6% (our derivation); Jul–Dec gross hold was 10.2% on A$11.2m of gross win.
  • Same playbook as Australia, but executed against a growing iGaming base rather than a shrinking one, so segment economics are improving.
  • Market share not disclosed; PointsBet is a sub-scale Ontario operator behind the North American majors with no iGaming-share figure to quote.

Platform and Alberta

Alberta is the delayed catalyst: not live on day one, with roughly ten weeks left to meet 'the second half of calendar year 2026' guidance.

  • Ontario casino migrated to Bede Gaming on 28 April 2026, which management says unlocks enhanced promotional capability.
  • Data-feed costs were called out as a gross-margin headwind in the Jul–Dec half.
  • Alberta pre-registration opened February 2026; PointsBet is in the AGLC registration process.
  • Launch costs land in H2 FY27 regardless, so Canada's EBITDA contribution, not separately disclosed, is unlikely to improve materially until FY28.

Outlook and watch items

No segment guidance; we expect mid-teens FY27 revenue growth driven by iGaming with the segment broadly EBITDA-neutral to modestly positive pre-Alberta marketing.

  • Sports expected flat-to-down on the VIP strategy.
  • On 30 October we want H1 FY27 Canadian revenue and iGaming net win, and Alberta timing with a date rather than a half.
  • Also whether Ontario iGaming actives and ARPU are still compounding post-migration.

5Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Canada iGaming compounding: FY26 net win +28% to A$23.6m (Jul–Dec half +58%), segment revenue +13%, actives +9%, and the Bede Gaming platform migration completed 28 Apr 2026 — the one line of the P&L with visible operating leverage. structural
  • Mass-market KPIs moving the right way: management reports consistent gains in mass-market sports actives, turnover and structural net-win margin vs PCP through H1 FY27; the miss is in actual vs structural margin, part of which (AFL Aug–Sep) should mean-revert. mixed
  • Compliance posture is defensible: deliberately operating above industry benchmark, restructured VIP remuneration (no loss-linked commissions), early exit from NRL sponsorships and FTA TV — lowers regulatory tail risk as the federal ad-reform package is implemented. structural
  • Ontario sports net margin re-based higher (~6.9% vs ~4.6% derived) as low-quality VIP handle was shed; net win fell only 8% on a 39% handle decline — evidence the 'quality over volume' strategy can work where there is a growing core to lean on. structural
  • Pre-announcement reduces 30 Oct event risk and the stock had already de-rated (~-9% in the prior week); MIXI's 66% ownership provides a funding backstop that a standalone A$23m-cash operator would not have. one-off
  • No debt; opex already trimmed (FY26 9M marketing A$49.2m vs A$50.2m, other opex A$45.2m vs A$48.6m) — cost base is not bloated, so the fix is revenue mix rather than restructuring. mixed

▼ Lowlights

  • Revenue -16% YoY to ~A$113m with the decline entirely Australian; derived quarterly run-rate (~A$56.5m) is below every quarter since at least Q3 FY25 and extends a deterioration already visible in Jan–Mar 2026 (-10.5% YoY est.). structural
  • Normalised EBITDA loss of A$6–8m: the widely quoted -A$3.3m comparator is the Jul–Dec 2025 half; the like-for-like Apr–Sep 2025 period was likely profitable (Q4 FY25 +A$10.0m derived), so the true swing is A$11–20m on our arithmetic. structural
  • VIP cohort turnover/revenue down double digits for the entire half and not annualising until ~Q4 FY27; management has told the market it will not chase this revenue back. structural
  • Pull 'Em / Premium Bet Type push dilutes margin twice — higher promotional cost between gross and net win, higher AFL product fees between net win and gross profit — on a base where taxes and fees already take ~48% of net win. structural
  • Adverse AFL results in Aug–Sep drove actual net-win margin below structural all half; unquantified, which makes it impossible to separate luck from mix until 30 Oct. one-off
  • Alberta still not launched despite Feb 2026 pre-registration and 'H2 CY2026' guidance; Canada at ~18% of group cannot offset Australia even on a good day. watch
  • Thin balance sheet and governance overhang: ~A$23m cash [unverified], book equity negative per aggregator data, 66% parent with a reconstituted board, equity 35% below the A$1.25 takeover price — minority holders are exposed to both dilution and a low-priced squeeze-out. governance

Independent view. Our independent take: H2 FY27 (Oct 2026–Mar 2027) revenue of A$108–115m (-8% to -13% YoY vs a derived A$124.6m pcp), assuming the VIP drag persists at double digits through the Spring Racing Carnival, AFL/NRL off-season removes the luck swing, and Canada grows mid-teens. That puts FY27 revenue at ~A$221–228m, roughly -12% to -15% against a derived pro-forma 12-month-to-March-2026 base of ~A$259.6m (Q4 FY25 A$73.0m + FY26 9M A$186.6m). On margin, we model H2 normalised EBITDA between -A$2m and +A$3m — AFL mean-reversion and lower marketing help, but Alberta launch costs and continued product-fee inflation on Premium Bet Types cap the recovery — giving FY27 normalised EBITDA of roughly -A$5m to -A$10m against -A$0.8m for the 9-month FY26. Gross-profit margin should drift below 50% (FY26: 50.2%) as mix shifts toward higher-fee multis and Canadian iGaming. Leverage direction: the group is un-levered but cash-consumptive — an H1 EBITDA loss of A$6–8m plus capex and working capital against ~A$23m of cash [unverified] implies year-end cash well below A$15m absent parent support; we would not be surprised by an intercompany facility from MIXI before FY27 year-end, and we flag that negative book equity (per aggregator data) makes a formal recapitalisation or minority buy-out a live scenario. Swing factors: the H2 luck line (Spring Carnival, NFL/NBA for Canada), the month-on-month trajectory of VIP turnover (whether it has bottomed or is still falling), implementation detail of the federal advertising reforms, and Alberta go-live timing. All figures above are our estimates, not company guidance.
