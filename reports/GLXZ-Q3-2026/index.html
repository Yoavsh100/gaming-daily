Q3 2026 · results · analyst report

Galaxy Gaming

GLXZ · Released 9 Nov 2026 · Currency USD (tables in $ thousands unless stated; company reports in USD so no FX conversion required) · Data confidence: HIGH

Market reaction: Shares closed -3.8% at $1.54 on 9 Nov 2026 (prior close $1.60 on 6 Nov, per Morningstar/StockTitan quotes), on thin OTCQB volume; the stock is down ~41% over twelve months and ~52% below the terminated $3.20 Evolution offer. Market cap ~$39m; EV ~$67m (analyst calc).

The one-line verdict

A clean recurring-revenue quarter wrapped in a messy P&L: recurring revenue net of royalties hit a record $7.96m (+9.7% YoY, 99.9% of sales) and Core recurring accelerated to +11.4%, but adj. EBITDA was flat at $3.22m (margin -380bps YoY to 40.4%) on G2E timing and bonus accruals, and the $5.1m / $0.20 GAAP profit is almost entirely the $5.2m Evolution break fee. Deleveraging (2.75x) and a first $1.7m capital return are real; Digital's -4.2% sequential dip on a 31% royalty take, 32% top-two customer concentration into a 2027 contract roll, and a Jan-2028 debt maturity keep this a show-me microcap now that the $3.20 take-out is gone.

1Executive summary

  • Revenue +8.9% YoY to $7.98m — the fastest growth of 2026 (Q1 -1.6%, Q2 +5.4%, Q3 +8.9%) as the drag from de-emphasised one-time perpetual system sales fades ($12k in Q3 vs $69k). There is no sell-side consensus for this OTCQB name, so the yardstick is the company's own run-rate: recurring revenue net of royalties was a record $7.96m, +9.7% YoY and +1.0% QoQ, and 99.9% of sales.
  • Recurring growth is accelerating, led by Core: recurring revenue YoY went +3.9% (Q1) → +8.5% (Q2) → +9.7% (Q3); Core recurring net of royalties went +3.8% → +7.0% → +11.4%. Digital decelerated the other way (+4.2% → +10.7% → +7.1%) and fell 4.2% sequentially.
  • Adj. EBITDA flat at $3.22m (-0.5% YoY, -9.2% QoQ); margin 40.4%, -380bps YoY and -430bps QoQ. Management attributes ~$0.3m to G2E trade-show costs landing in Q3 (Q4 last year) plus higher incentive-comp accruals against a 'reduced' prior-year accrual. Ex-G2E we estimate ~$3.5m (+9% YoY, ~44% margin) — i.e. in line with revenue growth, so no operating leverage was delivered this quarter despite the 'largely fixed cost base' pitch.
  • The GAAP profit is the break fee: net income $5.13m ($0.20 diluted) vs $1.04m ($0.04) reflects $5.23m of transaction-termination income from Evolution, partly offset by $1.19m of merger-related professional fees (vs $0.31m) and a $0.19m sales/use-tax charge. Income from operations fell 65% to $0.66m. Stripping the fee, merger costs and tax charge, underlying net income was ~$1.28m (our estimate, untaxed), ~12% below the comparable Q3-25 figure.
  • No formal guidance; the one outlook change is a softer line on perpetual sales. FY26 perpetual license sales are now expected to be 'lower' than FY25's $1.18m (Q2 wording: 'slightly lower'); 9M-26 perpetual sales are $0.16m vs $0.83m. Separately, the deck states that partnerships with FanDuel, Caesars, IGT PlayDigital and Delaware North 'have yet to launch product' and are 'expected to produce new revenue in 2027' — nothing in Q4-26.
  • The Evolution merger is dead and the $3.20 floor is gone. Evolution terminated the 18 Jul 2024 merger agreement on 21 Jul 2026 after regulatory approvals failed to arrive (per the 8-K and secondary coverage) and paid the $5,234,678 reverse termination fee in Q3. Secondary sources put the original equity value at ~$85m; at $1.54 the shares sit ~52% below the deal price.
  • First-ever capital return, fee-funded: $4.0m buyback authorised 22 Jul; 330,758 shares repurchased at ~$1.55 ($0.51m, 31 Jul) and all 778,320 Fortress $0.01-strike warrants cancelled for $1.21m (12 Aug, ~$1.56 per warrant). Diluted share count -4.2% (1.11m shares); ~$2.3m remains. Caveat: the BMO First Amendment only permits buybacks funded from the termination fee and requires ≥$5.0m unencumbered liquidity — so this is a one-off pool, not an ongoing programme, unless the lender agrees otherwise.
  • Balance sheet: cash $9.06m (vs $4.8m at Q2-26 and $4.31m at Dec-25); gross term debt $37.69m ($37.37m net of issuance costs, from $39.28m at Dec-25). Net debt ~$28.6m (our calc). Leverage on the company's gross definition 2.75x TTM adj. EBITDA of $13.7m (2.79x Q2-26; 3.22x Q3-25; 4.42x Q4-24); ~2.1x net. Effective interest rate 6.69% vs 7.82% a year ago after the sub-3.0x step-down to SOFR+3.0%.
  • Cash flow: 9M operating cash flow $11.86m including the $5.2m fee (~$6.6m ex-fee, +21% YoY); investing outflow $3.13m (+41%: internally developed software $1.24m, +65%; systems deployed at customer sites $1.41m, +51%); BMO principal repaid $1.94m; $1.73m returned. Q3 FCF (company definition: adj. EBITDA less cash interest, investing and cash taxes) $1.43m, +8.7%; 9M FCF $4.61m, flat YoY; LTM FCF $6.9m = 50% of adj. EBITDA.
  • Digital is the quarter's soft spot: net Digital revenue $3.00m, +7.1% YoY but -4.2% QoQ, on gross revenue flat QoQ (+0.3%) and royalties +12.0% QoQ / +19.5% YoY. The royalty take rose to 31.2% of gross from 28.0% in Q2 and 28.9% a year ago — third-party licensed titles are outgrowing Galaxy-owned content, which dilutes net revenue. Q4-25's record $3.31m Digital quarter is a tough comp.
  • Core is the engine: Core recurring revenue net of royalties $4.96m, +11.4% YoY / +4.4% QoQ, a record, on new U.S. placements and higher participation-based revenue in international markets, driven by the Galaxy Operating System (GOS) progressive platform and the MONOPOLY-branded progressives launched in H1 (named Product Innovation of the Year at the Global Gaming Awards Americas 2026). Progressive-table installs and EZ Baccarat placements are both at records.
  • Geography: EMEA revenue +14.9% YoY to $3.81m (48% of sales; 9M +13.3%); Americas +3.9% to $4.16m (9M -3.0%, reflecting the perpetual-sales wind-down; H1 Americas was -6.2% on our derivation, so Q3 is an inflection). Note all Digital royalty contra-revenue is booked to EMEA.
  • Customer concentration is rising, not falling: two European clients were 18.0% and 14.1% of 9M gross revenue (32% combined; Client B up from 10.6%). The deck identifies Evolution — the jilted acquirer — as the largest Digital customer, and a €6.0m/yr (~$6.8m) minimum-guarantee content agreement (successor to the PGP licence) is in its final contract year to 31 Mar 2027. That renewal is the single biggest 2027 swing factor.
  • Cost base ran ahead of revenue: SG&A +45.6% reported to $5.85m; ex merger costs and the tax charge +21%; ex-G2E as well still ~+12.5% vs +8.9% revenue — incentive comp is the swing (segment note: compensation +19.3% to $2.26m). Stock comp $0.28m vs $0.12m (September option/RSU grants). D&A +11% to $0.90m as capitalised software and deployed systems grow.
  • Sales/use-tax clean-up is ongoing: $0.19m charged in Q3 after $0.51m in Q2 ($0.69m LTM) from self-initiated managed audits and voluntary disclosures across multiple jurisdictions, 'substantially all' pre-2026. BMO's 23 Sep Second Amendment approved a one-time $505,361 covenant EBITDA add-back and lifted the capex cap to 10% of prior-year net revenue — a sign the covenant headroom needed tending.
  • Governance and people: director Meredith Brill stepped down 20 Oct; John M. Boushy appointed Class II director 21 Oct. CTO Anand Singh (ex-Light & Wonder) joined in July. In September the company amended executive employment agreements and granted 270,000 options at $1.656 plus conditional grants of 250,000 restricted shares.
  • Product and commercial pipeline: Galaxy Link all-table linked progressive unveiled at G2E (initial themes MONOPOLY Building Riches and Spotlight Spin in Art Deco); High Card Flush Ultra; Resorts World NYC floor-wide content; MSC Cruises 30 blackjack tables across 7 ships on GOS; Egypt and Germany market entries; Perfect Pairs exclusive global online rights renewed multi-year with Netco. Footprint: 600+ casinos, 30+ countries, 140+ licences, 5,700+ tables; ~3% of U.S. iGaming GGR; 4.5bn+ online wagers a year.
  • Refinancing is the next balance-sheet event: the BMO term loan matures 6 Jan 2028 (the merger-contingent maturity, since the deal did not close by 31 Dec 2025), with $33.75m due in 2028 after $0.56m (Q4-26) and $3.375m (2027) of amortisation. Expect a 2027 refinancing; falling leverage and a 6.7% effective rate help, but the stockholders' deficit is still $(11.5)m.
  • Thesis read-through: at $1.54 (market cap ~$39m; EV ~$67m, our calc) the shares trade on ~4.9x LTM adj. EBITDA and ~9.8x LTM FCF. The 99%-recurring base, 43% LTM margin and 50% FCF conversion are durable; the open questions are Digital's net growth given royalty leakage, the 2027 Evolution contract roll, the 2028 maturity, and whether capital returns continue once the fee-funded $4m is spent.
  • Shares -3.8% to $1.54 on the day (prior close $1.60), on thin OTCQB volume; -41% over twelve months.

3Segment deep-dive: Core (land-based table games, side bets & progressives)

Record Core recurring revenue of $4.96m (+11.4% YoY) on GOS/MONOPOLY progressive placements; perpetual sales are now a rounding error and the Americas has inflected back to growth.

Core revenue

Core recurring revenue net of royalties hit a record $4.96m, +11.4% YoY and +4.4% QoQ, with growth clearly accelerating through the year.

  • Core revenue net of royalties $4.97m, +10.0% YoY and +3.5% QoQ, making Core 62% of group revenue.
  • Recurring license revenue (gross) rose 10.1% to $5.71m; perpetual license sales just $12k (vs $69k a year ago, $51k in Q2).
  • Royalties netted against gross revenue flat at $0.75m, 13.1% of gross vs 14.0%.
  • Recurring net growth trajectory: +3.8% in Q1, +7.0% in Q2, +11.4% in Q3.
  • Growth from new U.S. game placements and higher participation-based revenue abroad, driven by GOS Progressive System and MONOPOLY progressives; MONOPOLY won Product Innovation of the Year at Global Gaming Awards Americas 2026.

Installed base and volume

Volume disclosure is thin; no billable-unit counts, revenue per unit or churn, so price cannot be separated from volume.

  • Last hard datapoint at Q2: recurring-revenue progressive systems up 11% YoY and 40% versus Q2-24; Q3 says only progressive tables and EZ Baccarat placements hit records.
  • Footprint: 600+ casinos in 30+ countries, 5,700+ tables carrying Galaxy content, 140+ gaming licences.
  • Billing is per billable unit: a table can carry a premium game or PRIME licence, up to three side bets, a progressive and a utility unit, each billed monthly.
  • Management says the installed base 'sits well below' multi-unit potential, so growth should come from densifying tables already served.
  • Peers Light & Wonder and AGS give table counts and daily yields; we would push for the same.

Perpetual sales wind-down

The deliberate wind-down of perpetual sales is almost complete and the Americas has inflected back to growth, up 3.9% in Q3 to $4.16m.

  • One-time system sales: $3.5m in FY24, $1.18m in FY25, $0.83m in 9M-25, $0.16m in 9M-26.
  • Management expects FY26 perpetual sales 'lower' than FY25 (Q2 said 'slightly lower').
  • On LTM framing, one-time hardware sales fell from $1.3m to $0.5m; recurring growth 'more than absorbed' the decline.
  • Americas revenue down 6.2% in H1 (our derivation) but up 3.9% in Q3 to $4.16m.
  • Q4-25 carried $0.35m of perpetual sales and will flatter the YoY optics against Q4-26.
“deprioritize”Management

Product pipeline

Galaxy Link and new commercial wins underpin the pipeline, but we would not model Galaxy Link revenue before H2-27.

  • Galaxy Link is an all-table linked progressive connecting every table on the floor to a shared jackpot; launch themes MONOPOLY Building Riches and Spotlight Spin in Art Deco.
  • Pitched as additive billable units on tables already served rather than a swap; High Card Flush Ultra is a new felt variant.
  • Wins: Resorts World NYC floor-wide content 'growing further in the fall', MSC Cruises 30 blackjack tables across seven ships on GOS, Egypt and Germany market entries.
  • Licence applications pending in several new markets; deck caveats pipeline items are subject to regulatory approval and customer timing.
“look forward to converting that interest into placements in the coming quarters”CEO

Core costs and margin

Core is now the growth engine with high-quality growth, but it is also where the cost went, and its margin cannot be verified.

  • Growth is recurring, increasingly participation-based internationally, and brand-led.
  • The ~$0.3m G2E expense moved into Q3 is a Core selling cost.
  • D&A rose 11% to $0.90m as 9M capex on systems deployed at customer sites rose 51% to $1.41m.
  • Tariffs were nil vs $95k in Q3-25; cost of ancillary products and assembled components fell 74% to $63k.
  • No separate segment profit disclosure (CODM manages on consolidated net income), a structural limitation of single-segment reporting.

4Segment deep-dive: Digital (iGaming content licensing — RNG & live dealer)

Net Digital revenue $3.00m, +7.1% YoY but -4.2% QoQ: gross play was flat sequentially while the royalty take jumped to 31% as licensed third-party titles outgrew owned content.

Digital revenue

Net Digital revenue was $3.00m, +7.1% YoY but -4.2% QoQ, as the royalty take jumped to 31.2% of gross.

  • Gross Digital revenue +10.7% YoY to $4.37m but flat QoQ (+0.3%); Digital is 38% of group revenue.
  • Royalties rose 19.5% YoY and 12.0% QoQ to $1.37m; take rate 31.2% vs 28.0% in Q2 and 28.9% in Q3-25.
  • Three months earlier royalties had fallen to 28% from 30%, so owned-versus-licensed mix swings quarter to quarter.
  • All Digital revenue is recurring; no one-time sales.
“increased 20%, growing faster than gross revenue”Management

Sequential trend

In the current quarter Core (+11.4%) outgrew Digital (+7.1%), and net growth lags gross by ~360bps from royalty leakage.

  • Digital net: record $3.31m in Q4-25 (gross $4.52m), $2.89m in Q1-26, $3.14m in Q2, $3.00m in Q3.
  • Deck's claim Digital is 'growing about 1.6 times as fast as Core' rests on the Q3-24 base (+19% vs +12% over two years).
  • 9M net +7.4% to $9.03m on gross +9.5%; royalty rate 30.2% vs 28.8%.
  • The direction of the take rate is the wrong one for net margin.

Market position

Market position is described rather than measured; the only geographic read-through is EMEA at +14.9% YoY.

  • Galaxy content generates roughly 3% of U.S. iGaming GGR; 4.5bn+ wagers a year on Galaxy iGames; live on 'thousands' of sites.
  • Two routes: direct to B2C operators (larger Galaxy share) and via B2B aggregators and live-dealer providers.
  • Revenue is a monthly licence per placement plus performance billing as a share of GGR on every wager.
  • No disclosure of GGR on content, actives, or per-market growth; EMEA carries all Digital royalty contra-revenue.
“majority of partners saw amounts played, GGR and royalties improve in Q3 2026”Management

Customer concentration

Concentration is acknowledged but not quantified; a €6.0m (~$6.8m) guaranteed content agreement enters its final year through 31 Mar 2027.

  • Two European clients at 18.0% and 14.1% of 9M gross revenue (vs 19.2% and 10.6%).
  • Deck names Evolution, which walked away from the acquisition in July, as the largest customer 'under a license agreement'.
  • Progressive Games Partners agreement guarantees €6.0m per contract year, billed at €500k/month minimums.
  • Deferred revenue under that agreement €58,576 at 30 Sep 2026 vs €9,637 a year earlier; billed minimums exceeded usage more than last year.
  • Nothing has been said about renewal terms.

2027 pipeline

The 2027 pipeline is real but prospective; we model net Digital growth at or below gross growth until new distribution is live.

  • IGT PlayDigital (five-year agreement signed 2025), FanDuel, Caesars and Delaware North/Ruby Seven 'have yet to launch product', 'expected to produce new revenue in 2027'.
  • Perfect Pairs exclusive global online rights renewed multi-year with Netco; EZ Baccarat going online; RNG development aligned to land-based titles.
  • Management leans on new U.S. iGaming states where Galaxy content 'is typically first to market' via partners.
  • Economics attractive (no incremental content cost per market), but high-single-digit net growth, rising royalty take and guarantee expiring March 2027 temper the outlook.

5Segment deep-dive: Group cost base, geography, customers & capital structure

SG&A +46% reported (+12.5% ex merger costs, tax charge and G2E) outran +8.9% revenue; interest expense -16%; BMO covenants were amended twice in the quarter; $33.75m matures January 2028.

Operating costs

Costs are where the quarter went wrong: total costs rose 34.8% to $7.31m against revenue growth of 8.9%.

  • SG&A $5.85m, +45.6% YoY; ex $1.19m merger fees (vs $0.31m) and $0.19m sales/use-tax charge +20.6%; ex ~$0.3m G2E timing ~+12.5%.
  • Compensation +19.3% to $2.26m (9M +11.7%) on incentive accruals vs 'reduced accrual level' last year; SG&A ex-compensation +62% to $3.79m.
  • R&D -6% to $0.22m (9M -40%) as development is capitalised: internally developed software spend +65% to $1.24m in 9M.
  • D&A +11% to $0.90m; stock-based compensation $0.28m vs $0.12m on September grants of 270,000 options at $1.656 and conditional 250,000 restricted shares.
  • Cost of ancillary products -74% to $63k; tariffs nil vs $95k.

Geography and FX

EMEA revenue rose 14.9% YoY to $3.81m (47.8% of group) while the Americas rose 3.9% to $4.16m.

  • 9M: EMEA +13.3%, Americas -3.0%; Americas H1 decline (-6.2% on our derivation) explained by perpetual-sales wind-down.
  • All Digital royalty contra-revenue allocated to EMEA, so the regional split understates EMEA gross activity.
  • Euro exposure via the €6.0m guarantee and European top-two clients.
  • P&L FX loss trivial ($1.6k); OCI translation adjustment $(48)k for the quarter and $(201)k for 9M.

Interest and tax

Interest expense fell 16% to $0.73m on a lower effective rate of 6.69% vs 7.82% and $2.5m less principal.

  • 9M interest -17% to $2.32m; cash interest paid, net, $0.69m vs $0.84m.
  • Tax provision $56k on $5.19m pre-tax income (~1% effective rate), consistent with NOL utilisation; deferred tax liability down to $638.
  • 9M-25 base included a $2.97m loss on extinguishment of Fortress debt when the BMO facility was put in place in January 2025, hence 9M-25 net loss of $32k.

BMO facility and covenants

The BMO facility now matures 6 Jan 2028 with $33.75m due that year; two amendments show a supportive lender managing headroom carefully.

  • $45.0m term loan plus $2.0m revolver, Term SOFR + 3.0–3.5% on a leverage grid, interest paid monthly at one-month SOFR; maturity extended because Evolution merger did not complete by 31 Dec 2025.
  • Scheduled maturities: $0.56m (Q4-26), $3.375m (2027), $33.75m (2028).
  • Covenants: maximum total funded debt/EBITDA, minimum fixed-charge coverage, minimum EBITDA, maximum capex.
  • First Amendment (24 Jul) permits up to $4.0m share/warrant repurchases funded solely from the termination fee, with ≥$5.0m unencumbered liquid assets and covenant compliance; reset fixed-charge coverage definition.
  • Second Amendment (23 Sep) raised capex cap to 10% of prior-year net revenue and approved a one-time $505,361 EBITDA add-back for the Q2 sales/use-tax charge.

Balance sheet

6Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Recurring revenue growth is accelerating — +3.9% (Q1) → +8.5% (Q2) → +9.7% (Q3) YoY — to a record $7.96m, now 99.9% of sales; the model has genuinely converted from hardware sales to monthly licence/participation billing. structural
  • Core recurring revenue +11.4% YoY / +4.4% QoQ to a record $4.96m on GOS and MONOPOLY-branded progressives (Product Innovation of the Year), with progressive-table and EZ Baccarat installs at records and the Americas back to growth (+3.9%) after an H1 decline. structural
  • Interest expense -16% to $0.73m; effective rate 6.69% (from 7.82%); leverage 2.75x gross / ~2.1x net, down from 4.42x at Q4-24; LTM cash interest $2.9m vs $7.0m in FY23 — the refinancing dividend is flowing to FCF. structural
  • First capital return: $1.7m deployed to retire 330,758 shares at ~$1.55 and all 778,320 penny warrants at ~$1.56 each, cutting the diluted count 4.2% and removing the Fortress overhang; ~$2.3m of the $4.0m authorisation remains. one-off
  • Cash $9.06m (up $4.8m YTD) and 9M operating cash flow of $11.9m ($6.6m ex-fee, +21%) funded a 41% step-up in growth capex, $1.9m of debt paydown and the buyback simultaneously; LTM FCF $6.9m = 50% of adj. EBITDA. mixed
  • 2027 distribution pipeline — IGT PlayDigital (5-yr), FanDuel, Caesars, Delaware North social, Perfect Pairs exclusive online renewal — plus Galaxy Link and new-market entries (Egypt, Germany, MSC Cruises, Resorts World NYC) give visible, if unquantified, growth levers beyond the current base. early

▼ Lowlights

  • Adj. EBITDA flat at $3.22m with margin -380bps YoY / -430bps QoQ to 40.4%; even adding back ~$0.3m of G2E timing, EBITDA grew only in line with revenue (~+9%) because incentive-comp accruals absorbed the operating leverage the deck advertises. mixed
  • Digital net revenue -4.2% QoQ to $3.00m: gross flat sequentially while royalties jumped 12% QoQ, lifting the royalty take to 31.2% (28.0% in Q2) — licensed titles are outgrowing owned content and net growth (+7.1%) lags gross (+10.7%). watch
  • Customer concentration rose: top-two European clients are 32% of 9M gross revenue (18.0% / 14.1%, the latter up from 10.6%); the largest Digital customer is Evolution, the party that just walked from the merger, and the €6.0m/yr minimum-guarantee agreement is in its final contract year to 31 Mar 2027. structural
  • SG&A +46% reported and ~+12.5% even after stripping merger fees, the tax charge and G2E, versus +8.9% revenue; stock comp more than doubled after September grants (270k options, 250k conditional RSUs) and amended executive employment agreements. watch
  • Sales/use-tax exposure keeps surfacing — $0.19m in Q3 after $0.51m in Q2 ($0.69m LTM) from a self-initiated multi-jurisdiction review that is still 'ongoing'; the lender had to grant a one-time covenant add-back for the Q2 charge. governance
  • Buybacks are lender-gated: only termination-fee proceeds may be used, with ≥$5.0m unencumbered liquidity, so capital returns stop at $4.0m absent a further amendment; meanwhile $33.75m of the BMO term loan matures 6 Jan 2028 and must be refinanced in 2027 from a $(11.5)m stockholders'-deficit position. governance
  • With the $3.20 Evolution take-out gone, the shares (-3.8% to $1.54 on the day, -41% over 12 months) have no strategic floor, no sell-side coverage and thin OTCQB liquidity; the only outlook change this quarter was a downgrade of FY26 perpetual sales from 'slightly lower' to 'lower'. structural

7Forecast & outlook

Guidance bridge. Galaxy does not issue formal revenue or EBITDA guidance, so there is no numeric bridge to reconcile. The qualitative outlook moved in two places: (1) FY26 perpetual license sales of progressive systems are now expected to be 'lower' than FY25's $1.18m (Q2: 'slightly lower'), which is a Core-stream statement and mechanically trims total-revenue growth while leaving recurring revenue untouched; and (2) the deck newly states that the FanDuel, Caesars, IGT PlayDigital and Delaware North partnerships have yet to launch and are expected to produce revenue in 2027, i.e. no Digital contribution in Q4-26. Mechanically, Q4-26 benefits from ~$0.3m of G2E cost that was in Q4-25 and is now in Q3-26, while Q4-25 is a tough top-line comp ($8.24m total including $0.35m perpetual sales and a record $3.31m Digital quarter). Scheduled term-loan amortisation is $0.56m in Q4-26 and $3.375m in 2027; the remaining ~$2.3m buyback capacity is fee-funded and lender-conditioned.

Margin direction into 2027 is the real debate: the fixed-cost story requires incentive comp and D&A (running +11–19%) to grow slower than the ~7–9% recurring revenue line, and Digital's net margin is being squeezed by a rising royalty take; we would model adj. EBITDA margin flat-to-slightly-up at ~43–44% rather than the expansion the deck implies. The 2027 swing factors are, in order of size, the renewal terms of the €6.0m minimum-guarantee content agreement expiring 31 Mar 2027 (and the broader Evolution relationship post-termination), the timing and scale of the IGT/FanDuel/Caesars/Delaware North launches, the refinancing of the $33.75m 2028 bullet (which, done at current leverage, should be achievable but will consume management bandwidth), and whether the board can secure lender consent to continue buybacks from operating cash once the fee-funded $4.0m is exhausted. Downside risks: further sales/use-tax assessments, a Digital royalty take drifting above 30% structurally, and any slippage in Galaxy Link placements. Upside: Core recurring sustaining double-digit growth on MONOPOLY/Galaxy Link densification and a 2027 Digital step-up from new distribution.
