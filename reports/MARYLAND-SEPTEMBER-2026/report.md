# Maryland () — SEPTEMBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: USD (market reports in USD; tables in $m unless stated) · Results released 13 Oct 2026**

> September 2026 was a volume-not-value month for Maryland: handle was flat at $652.2m (+0.2% YoY, the third-largest month on record) despite one fewer NFL week, but hold compressed to 9.0% from 11.2%, cutting GGR 20% to $58.5m, and with deductible promo play tripling to $11.0m the state's tax take fell 32.5% to $9.1m even at the higher 20% mobile rate. Structurally the market is a FanDuel/DraftKings duopoly (71.6% of mobile handle), 98% online, tax-efficient for operators (15.6% effective on GGR) and increasingly exposed to fiscal pressure as Annapolis sees FY27 YTD receipts running 17.5% below FY26.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Regulator and framework.** Sports wagering and the six commercial casinos are regulated by the Maryland Lottery and Gaming Control Agency/Commission (MLGCA/MLGCC) under State Government Article §9-1E (2021 Sports Wagering Law, following the Nov-2020 referendum) and COMAR Title 36; a separate Sports Wagering Application Review Commission (SWARC) awards licences. Retail launched Dec 2021, mobile 23 Nov 2022. Online casino is not authorised.
- **Headline: flat handle, sharply lower value.** September 2026 handle was $652,200,900 (+0.2% YoY vs $650,971,388; +40.0% MoM from August's $465.9m) — the third-biggest month in market history — but hold fell to 9.0% from 11.2%, so GGR (hold) dropped 20.0% to $58.45m from $73.06m. September 2026 contained one fewer NFL regular-season week than September 2025.
- **Tax take fell faster than GGR.** Taxable win was $45.92m (−32.4% YoY) and state contributions $9.10m (−32.5%): $6.89m to the Blueprint for Maryland's Future Fund and $2.21m to the General Fund. The effective state take was 15.6% of GGR versus 18.4% a year earlier, because deductible promotional play tripled to $10.96m (1.68% of handle; 18.7% of GGR) from $3.66m (0.56%; 5.0%).
- **Online penetration is near-total.** Mobile accounted for $641.15m of handle (98.3%) and $56.65m of GGR (96.9%); retail handle was $11.05m (−22.8% YoY) with $1.81m GGR at a 16.3% hold. Maryland is effectively a pure-digital sports-betting market with retail as a casino amenity.
- **Fiscal year to date: more volume, less money.** FY2027 YTD (Jul–Sep 2026) handle is $1,614.5m (+11.4% vs $1,449.3m), but GGR is $159.2m (−6.4%) at a 9.9% hold (vs 11.7%), and state tax $24.57m (−17.5% vs $29.77m). FY2026 full-year contributions were $132.3m (+48.8%), flattered by the July-2025 rate rise; the FY27 run-rate is tracking below that.
- **Calendar 2026 is still growing on volume.** Nine-month 2026 handle reached $4.98bn (+9% YoY per Gaming Intelligence/SI, vs $4.55bn in 9M 2025 per InGame), implying H1-2026 handle of ~$3.37bn. Record months remain Oct-2025 (mobile $723.3m) and Nov-2025 (mobile $707.3m).
- **Operator landscape is a duopoly with a thin tail.** Of 11 live mobile books, FanDuel (Live! Casino licence) took $265.9m (41.5% of mobile handle; 42.9% of mobile GGR at 9.1% hold) and DraftKings $193.2m (30.1%; 28.4% of GGR at 8.3%). Fanatics $50.6m (7.9%), BetMGM $44.9m (7.0%), bet365 $44.8m (7.0%) and Caesars $20.7m (3.2%) follow; theScore Bet, BetRivers, Bally Bet, betPARX and LetsBetMD share the remaining 3.3%. Top-5 = 93.5% of mobile handle.
- **Tail consolidation is under way.** Crab Sports (Maryland's only locally owned book) paused operations on 12 Jul 2026 (FY27 YTD handle $58.8k); Betfred (Long Shot's) and SuperBook licences show zero activity after both brands withdrew; PointsBet became Fanatics. Twelve mobile licences remain issued against a statutory ceiling of 60.
- **Product mix is parlay-led and that drives hold.** Parlays/combinations were $246.3m (37.8% of handle) at a 14.9% hold, generating $36.7m — 62.7% of all GGR. Straight-market holds were thin: NFL $99.6m at 4.3%, NBA $66.1m at 7.4%, MLB $62.4m at 5.3%, tennis $43.4m at 4.9%, NCAA football $34.9m at 4.7%, soccer $32.0m at 4.5%.
- **Tax regime: taxable-win base, differentiated by channel.** Mobile operators pay 20% of taxable win (15% Blueprint + 5% General Fund) since 1 Jul 2025 (previously 15%; the Governor's January-2025 proposal was 30%); retail pays 15%. Taxable win = handle − prizes − deductible promotional play − other deductions (incl. the 0.25% federal excise); losses can be carried forward three months; there is no promo-deduction cap.
- **Licensing: fixed fees, five-year terms, percentage renewals.** Non-refundable application fees are $2.0m (Class A-1: casinos ≥1,000 VLTs, major stadiums), $1.0m (A-2), $250k (B-1), $50k (B-2) and $500k (mobile). Renewal = 1% of the licensee's average annual sports-wagering proceeds over the preceding three years. A $500k cash reserve is mandatory; a mobile licensee may hold one licence and contract one online operator at a time. SWARC may competitively award up to 60 mobile and 30 Class B licences; 17 Class A/B licences are designated in statute (14 entities currently hold them).
- **The licence window is shut unless HB 1431 revives it.** HB 1431 (2026 session, Del. Buckel) would have obliged SWARC to accept applications until 1 Jan 2028; it reached a Ways & Means hearing on 5 Mar 2026 with no recorded further action, so new mobile entry currently depends on acquiring/partnering with an existing licensee (e.g., the dormant Riverboat/P, Long Shot's or SuperBook slots).
- **Land-based casinos are flat-to-down.** Six casinos generated $151.4m in September (−0.1% YoY; −5.4% MoM): MGM National Harbor $63.9m (+1.1%), Live! $54.2m (−1.4%), Horseshoe $13.1m (−6.8%), Ocean Downs $8.9m (+6.0%), Hollywood Perryville $6.7m (+6.3%), Rocky Gap $4.6m (−0.2%). July was $157.9m (−4.7%) and August ~$160m (−6%), putting Q3-2026 at ~$469m (~−4%). State contributions exceeded $65m (+1.8%), including ~$47.6m to the Education Trust Fund — an effective ~43% blended take.
- **Public-benefit flows are large and politically salient.** Since December 2021 sports wagering has contributed $295.6m to the Blueprint fund, $38.6m to the General Fund and $5.8m of expired prizes to the Problem Gambling Fund; casinos contributed $606.2m to the Education Trust Fund in FY2025 alone. Education funding dependency is why FY27's 17.5% YTD shortfall will be noticed in Annapolis.
- **Responsible gambling and advertising.** Minimum age is 21; a statewide Voluntary Exclusion Program and 1-800-GAMBLER/mdgamblinghelp.org are mandated; unclaimed prizes after 182 days fund problem-gambling services. COMAR 36.10 advertising rules bar targeting under-21s and college campuses and MLGCA barred 'risk-free' terminology in 2023 [regulatory detail from analyst records; not re-verified this session].
- **Enforcement direction: hostile to unlicensed verticals.** MLGCA issued cease-and-desist letters to Kalshi, Robinhood and Crypto.com over sports event contracts in April 2025 and Kalshi sued; the 2026 session carried SB 652/HB 1226 (Maryland Illegal Online Gambling Enforcement) and HB 295 targeting unlicensed/sweepstakes online gaming (hearings Feb 2026). Outcomes of the 2026 bills and the current litigation posture could not be verified in-session.
- **Read-through for an operator.** Maryland is a large (~$7bn annualised handle), 98%-online, promo-deductible, 20%-tax market with no supply cap binding — but the window for a fresh licence is closed, the top two hold 72% of volume, challengers are sub-8% each after three years, and September shows the state's tax base is highly hold-sensitive, which raises the probability of further fiscal tightening (rate or promo-deduction cap) in the 2027 session.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Mobile sports betting — operator landscape and economics

*FanDuel and DraftKings take 71.6% of mobile handle and 71.3% of mobile GGR; the challenger tier is stuck below 8% each and the local tail is exiting.*

### Mobile market totals

**Mobile handle of $641.15m in September 2026 rose just 0.7% YoY, but hold fell to 8.8% and mobile GGR dropped 20.1%.**

- Handle compares with $636.67m in September 2025, with one fewer NFL week in the comparison base.
- Operators held 8.8% ($56.65m) against 11.1% ($70.90m) a year ago.
- Deductible promotional play was $10.94m versus $3.64m; mobile taxable win fell 32.9% to $44.16m and mobile tax to $8.83m.
- Books gave back almost one promo dollar per five gross-win dollars, up from one in twenty a year earlier.
- Whether this is structural NFL-season reinvestment or a timing artefact will show in October–November data.

### FanDuel and DraftKings

**FanDuel and DraftKings take 71.6% of mobile handle and 71.3% of mobile GGR, generating $40.4m GGR and $6.07m of tax together.**

- FanDuel (Live! licence) wagered $265.90m (41.5% share, +4.6% YoY vs $254.1m); hold fell to 9.1% from 12.7%, GGR dropped roughly 25% to $24.32m.
- FanDuel still delivered 42.9% of mobile GGR and $3.61m of the state's $8.83m mobile tax.
- DraftKings took $193.18m (30.1%, flat on $193.4m) at 8.3% hold for $16.08m GGR (+6.5% YoY, as September-2025 hold was a weak 7.8%).
- FY27 YTD: FanDuel $635.2m handle (40.0%), DraftKings $485.1m (30.5%).

### Challenger tier

**The second tier is stuck below 8% each, with Fanatics, BetMGM and bet365 within $6m of handle of each other.**

- Fanatics (Maryland Stadium Sub licence) $50.63m at market-low 7.6% hold ($3.83m GGR; 2.3% promo intensity).
- BetMGM $44.95m at 10.5% ($4.71m GGR; zero deductible promo); bet365 (Riverboat 'B' licence) $44.77m at 7.8% ($3.48m; zero deductible promo).
- bet365 shows hold volatility: a year ago 24% hold and $11.2m GGR on $46.7m handle; this September GGR down ~69% on handle down 4%.
- Caesars (American Wagering/Horseshoe) is a distant sixth at $20.66m (3.2%), 9.5% hold, $1.97m GGR.

### Sub-scale tail

**Three years after launch, no operator outside the top five has reached 2% share, and the local tail is exiting.**

- theScore Bet (Penn, Hollywood Casino licence; rebranded from ESPN BET) $8.98m handle at the market's tightest 12.9% hold.
- BetRivers (Bingo World) $7.81m; Bally Bet $2.38m; betPARX (Greenmount) $1.15m; LetsBetMD (Veterans Services Corp/Bee-Fee) $0.75m.
- Crab Sports, the only Maryland-owned brand, announced on 17 Jun 2026 it would pause wagering from 12 Jul 2026; zero September volume.
- Betfred (Long Shot's) and SuperBook licences are dormant after withdrawal; the second Riverboat slot ('P') is unused.
- 2% share is the empirical ceiling for a late, undifferentiated entrant in this market.

### Tax economics and entry

**Maryland's 20% promo-deductible rate yields an effective 15–16% of GGR, favourable to incumbents; entry now runs via dormant licence partnerships.**

- Effective take is well below Pennsylvania (36%, promo-deductible with limits), Illinois' tiered 20–40% plus per-wager surcharge, or New York's 51%.
- Practical route for an entrant is partnering with a dormant holder (Riverboat/P, Long Shot's, SuperBook) rather than a new SWARC award.
- The competitive application window is closed absent legislation such as HB 1431.

### Regulator hold guidance

**MLGCA computes hold on wagers placed, not settled, so monthly hold is revised as futures settle.**

- The September NBA line ($66.1m handle in a month with no regular-season games) is almost certainly futures/preseason-driven.
- That line will distort hold in later months.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Sports betting — product mix, retail channel and seasonality

*Parlays are 38% of handle but 63% of GGR; straight NFL/NBA/MLB markets hold 4–7%, so monthly tax receipts swing on parlay results.*

### Product mix

**Parlays are 38% of handle but 63% of GGR; straight NFL/NBA/MLB markets hold only 4–7%.**

- Parlays/combinations were $246.3m of handle (37.8%) at 14.9% hold, producing $36.7m — 62.7% of statewide GGR.
- NFL $99.6m at 4.3% ($4.3m), NBA $66.1m at 7.4% ($4.9m), MLB $62.4m at 5.3% ($3.3m), tennis $43.4m at 4.9% ($2.1m).
- NCAA football $34.9m at 4.7% ($1.6m), soccer $32.0m at 4.5% ($1.4m), 'other' $63.8m at 5.7% ($3.7m); motor sports and NCAA basketball negative hold on negligible volume.
- FYTD parlays $586.3m (36.3% of handle) at 16.9% hold = $99.3m, or 62.4% of the $159.2m GGR.

### Parlay implications

**Pricing and product in parlay/SGP is the entire margin story in Maryland, making state revenue unusually sensitive to a handful of weekends.**

- A book without a competitive same-game-parlay engine is structurally a 4–6% hold business paying 20% on taxable win plus 0.25% federal excise on handle (deductible).
- Annapolis's own analysts have noted that forecasts rest on a hold the state cannot control.

### Retail channel

**Retail is now an amenity, not a channel: September handle $11.05m (1.7% of market), down 22.8% YoY.**

- Retail handle was 54.8% above August's $7.14m; hold 16.3% ($1.81m), tax $0.26m.
- MGM National Harbor ($4.36m, 39% of retail) and Live! ($3.12m, 28%) dominate; Horseshoe, Ocean Downs and Hollywood Perryville each took $0.5–0.75m.
- Six smaller sites (Whitman Gaming, Bingo World, Long Shot's/Caesars, Riverboat, Greenmount OTB, Maryland Stadium Authority kiosk) shared $1.73m; three Canton Gaming OTBs and Long Shot's/Betfred reported no handle.
- Retail hold is structurally higher (FY27 YTD 11.9% vs 9.8% mobile) from parlay-heavy walk-up mix, but the base is too small to matter for tax.

### Seasonality

**FY27 handle is +11.4% but GGR −6.4% and tax −17.5%: volume growth is intact, monetisation is not.**

- August ($465.9m) to September ($652.2m) is the normal NFL step-up (+40%).
- October and November 2025 were all-time peaks (mobile $723.3m and $707.3m).
- 'Nearly $5bn in nine months' ($4.98bn, +9%) points to calendar-2026 handle of $7.0–7.2bn (estimate) if Q4 repeats 2025's ~$2.1bn.

### Hold accounting artefact

**Hold is booked on wagers placed, not settled, so large futures positions depress reported hold now and inflate it later.**

- Example: the $66m NBA line in a month without regular-season games.
- The regulator flags this as a reporting artefact.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Licensing and tax regime

*Fixed-fee, five-year licences with 1%-of-proceeds renewals and a promo-deductible 20%/15% tax on taxable win — operator-friendly on paper, but the application window is closed and the rate has already moved once.*

### Licence structure

**Fixed-fee, five-year licences renew at 1% of average annual proceeds; 17 facility licences are designated in statute, 14 currently held.**

- SG §9-1E-06(a) designates licences for six casinos, three major stadiums, racetracks, OTBs and bingo halls; SWARC may add up to 30 Class B and 60 mobile licences.
- Statutory diversity mandate and an Assistance Fund financed by 5% of Class A fees.
- Application fees: $2.0m (A-1), $1.0m (A-2), $250k (B-1), $50k (B-2), $500k (mobile); renewal at 1% of three-year average proceeds implies a seven-figure cheque for FanDuel's Live! licence.
- Every operator holds a $500k cash reserve; one mobile licence and one online operator per licensee, so brand changes (PointsBet→Fanatics, ESPN BET→theScore Bet) run through the holder.

### Entry closed

**Entry is practically closed: SWARC's window has lapsed and HB 1431 stalled at its 5 Mar 2026 hearing.**

- HB 1431 would have required applications before 1 Jan 2028; its 2024 predecessor HB 526 also failed.
- New entrants must acquire or partner with dormant/sub-scale licensees — Riverboat's second slot, Long Shot's, SuperBook, Bally's, Greenmount, Veterans Services — or await legislation.
- The DLS fiscal note anticipated only 'minimal' incremental revenue from new entrants, signalling supply is not seen as the lever.

### Tax mechanics

**Tax on promo-deductible taxable win is 20% mobile and 15% retail, giving an effective state take of 15.6% of GGR in September.**

- Taxable win is handle less prizes, deductible promo and 'other deductions' (adjustments and 0.25% federal excise); negative months floored at zero, losses carried forward three months.
- Retail pays 15% to Blueprint; mobile has paid 20% since 1 Jul 2025 (15% Blueprint, 5% General Fund), up from 15%, after the Governor sought 30%.
- Mobile licensees retain 80% of proceeds, all others 85%.
- Effective take was 15.6% in September (FY27 YTD 15.4%) versus 18.4% in September 2025 when promo was lighter.

### Political outlook

**FY27 YTD contributions of $24.6m are −17.5% on handle +11.4%, the kind of print that prompts rate or promo-deduction changes in 2027.**

- FY2026 contributions were $132.3m (+48.8%, partly the rate step-up).
- Blueprint's sports-betting line is now visibly hold- and promo-dependent.
- Available levers: higher headline rate, promo-deduction cap or phase-out (Pennsylvania, Virginia, Colorado), or per-wager fee (Illinois).
- Since December 2021 sports wagering has paid $295.6m to Blueprint, $38.6m to the General Fund and $5.8m to the Problem Gambling Fund.

### Casino and DFS tax

**Casino taxation is separate and heavier at a ~43% effective rate; DFS operators retain 85% of proceeds.**

- Table games 20% (15% ETF, 5% local); VLTs on property-specific state shares.
- September's >$65m contributions on $151.4m GGR.
- Daily fantasy regulated by MLGCA as 'fantasy competitions'.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling, advertising and enforcement (prediction markets, sweepstakes, iGaming)

*A 21+ market with standard US digital payments and mandated RG infrastructure; the regulatory energy in 2025–26 is directed at unlicensed verticals (prediction markets, sweepstakes) rather than new licensed ones (iGaming).*

### Payments

**Payments follow the standard US regulated template with no mobile-money dimension and no published deposit-method statistics.**

- Methods: online banking/ACH, debit cards, PayPal and e-wallets, prepaid Play+ cards and cash at casino cages.
- Credit-card funding under COMAR 36.10 could not be re-verified and is [unverified]; assume card-network and bank-level frictions.
- Withdrawals governed by MLGCA operational requirements and the $500k reserve rule rather than a statutory speed standard.

### Responsible gambling

**RG architecture is statutory in a 21+ market, but there are no deposit limits, affordability checks or advertising volume caps.**

- Unclaimed prizes after 182 days and prizes withheld from excluded persons go to the Problem Gambling Fund ($5.82m cumulative; $65k in September).
- Voluntary Exclusion Program spans casinos and sportsbooks; mandated 1-800-GAMBLER/mdgamblinghelp.org messaging; investigative summaries published.
- COMAR 36.10 prohibits targeting under-21s and college campuses; 'risk-free' promotional language barred in 2023 [not re-verified].

### Enforcement

**Enforcement is the live front, targeting prediction markets and sweepstakes rather than new licensed verticals.**

- April 2025 cease-and-desist letters to Kalshi, Robinhood and Crypto.com; Kalshi sued in federal court; 2026 status unverified and should be checked.
- Litigation determines whether CFTC-regulated exchanges can keep siphoning NFL/NBA volume from 20%-taxed licensed books.
- 2026 'Illegal Online Gambling Enforcement' package (SB 652/HB 1226) and HB 295 heard in February 2026; outcome [unverified].
- Direction of travel toward criminalising or penalising unlicensed online gaming is clear even where statute is not.

### iGaming

**Online casino remains unauthorised, leaving Maryland's ~$1.8bn annual casino GGR with no legal digital channel.**

- iGaming requires enabling legislation and a constitutional referendum; a House-passed bill died in the Senate in 2024.
- No iGaming question is known to be on the November 2026 ballot [unverified].
- Sweepstakes/social-casino products face rising enforcement risk rather than a licensing path.

### Daily fantasy

**DFS is regulated by MLGCA under 'fantasy competitions' with operators retaining 85% of proceeds.**

- Pick'em-style DFS has not, to our knowledge, been separately restricted in Maryland [unverified].

## 8. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Scale and digital penetration: ~$7bn annualised handle (9M-2026 $4.98bn, +9%) with 98.3% of handle and 96.9% of GGR online — one of the most purely digital sports-betting markets in the US, so no retail footprint is needed to compete. _(structural)_
- Operator-friendly tax base: 20% mobile / 15% retail on taxable win with uncapped promo deductibility, a 0.25% federal-excise deduction and three-month loss carry-forward produced an effective 15.6% of GGR in September (15.4% FY27 YTD) — far below PA (36%), IL (20–40% + per-bet fee) or NY (51%). _(structural)_
- Volume is still growing: FY27 YTD handle +11.4% to $1.61bn; September was the third-largest month ever despite one fewer NFL week; Q4 seasonality (Oct/Nov-2025 mobile records of $723m/$707m) lies ahead. _(mixed)_
- Dormant licence capacity creates an M&A/partnership entry route: Riverboat on the Potomac's unused second mobile slot, Long Shot's (ex-Betfred), SuperBook and sub-scale holders (Bally's, Greenmount, Veterans Services) are all potential vehicles for a B2B platform or new brand without waiting for SWARC. _(structural)_
- Parlay economics reward product: parlays/SGPs are 37.8% of handle at a 14.9% hold and 62.7% of GGR — an operator with superior SGP pricing and content can out-earn its handle share, as theScore Bet's 12.9% and BetMGM's 10.5% holds on small bases illustrate. _(structural)_
- Fixed, modest licence costs: $500k mobile application, 1%-of-proceeds five-year renewal and a $500k reserve are low relative to the fee stacks in NY, PA or IL; renewal cycles for the casino-anchored licences began in August 2026 with staff recommending approval. _(structural)_
- Regulatory hostility to unlicensed competitors (C&D letters to prediction markets in 2025; 2026 enforcement bills against sweepstakes/illegal online gaming) protects the licensed channel if sustained. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- Duopoly lock-in: FanDuel (41.5%) and DraftKings (30.1%) hold 71.6% of mobile handle and the top five 93.5%; no operator outside the top five has exceeded 2% share after three years and Crab Sports has already paused. A new B2C brand faces a sub-2% realistic share. _(structural)_
- Hold-driven revenue volatility: GGR fell 20% and tax 32.5% on flat handle in September; FY27 YTD GGR is −6.4% and tax −17.5% while handle is +11.4%. Monthly margin is hostage to parlay results and futures accounting (hold booked on wagers placed, not settled). _(mixed)_
- Fiscal-tightening risk: the mobile rate already rose from 15% to 20% on 1 Jul 2025 (after a 30% proposal), and a Blueprint-funded education plan is now watching sports-betting receipts run 17.5% below last year. A higher rate, a promo-deduction cap or a per-wager fee in the 2027 session are plausible responses. _(watch)_
- Promo intensity has stepped up: deductible promotional play tripled YoY to $11.0m (18.7% of GGR, 1.68% of handle) in September and is 19.8% of GGR FY27 YTD — a sign the leaders are spending to defend NFL-season share, raising the cost of entry for challengers. _(mixed)_
- No licensed online-casino channel and no referendum path in sight: iGaming requires a constitutional amendment; bills have failed since 2024 and 2026 status is unverified. Land-based GGR (~$1.8bn/yr) is flat-to-down and cannot be migrated online. _(structural)_
- The new-licence window is closed: HB 1431 (reopen SWARC applications to Jan 2028) stalled at its March 2026 hearing; entry depends on partnering with incumbents on their terms. _(one-off)_
- Unlicensed substitution risk: CFTC-regulated prediction markets (Kalshi, Robinhood, Crypto.com) continue to contest Maryland's cease-and-desist letters; if they prevail, sports-outcome volume could migrate to an untaxed, unlicensed channel. Litigation status in 2026 could not be verified in-session. _(governance)_

## 9. Forecast & Outlook

The regulatory bridge from September 2025 to September 2026 is unchanged in rate (20% mobile / 15% retail since 1 Jul 2025) but very different in yield: on essentially identical handle ($651.0m → $652.2m) the state's take fell from $13.5m to $9.1m. Two moving parts explain all of it. First, hold compressed 2.2pp (11.2% → 9.0%), removing $14.6m of GGR — a function of one fewer NFL week, thin straight-market margins (NFL 4.3%, NCAAF 4.7%) and futures accounting. Second, deductible promotional play tripled ($3.66m → $10.96m), removing a further $7.3m of taxable win. Together taxable win fell $22.0m (−32.4%) and tax $4.4m. The same dynamics are visible in the regulator's FYTD table (handle +11.4%, GGR −6.4%, tax −17.5%), which is the table the General Assembly's budget analysts will read.

- **Sep-25 state tax (base):** $13.5m (GGR $73.1m at 11.2% hold; promo $3.7m)
- **Hold compression −2.2pp on flat handle:** −$14.6m GGR (≈ −$2.9m tax at 20%)
- **Deductible promo tripled:** −$7.3m taxable win (≈ −$1.5m tax)
- **Sep-26 state tax:** $9.1m (−32.5% YoY; effective 15.6% of GGR)

**Volume growth continues, monetisation normalises at a lower hold.** We expect calendar-2026 handle of ~$7.0–7.2bn (estimate: 9M $4.98bn plus a Q4 in line with 2025's ~$2.1bn) and GGR of ~$650–720m (estimate) at a 9–10% blended hold, i.e. mid-single-digit GGR growth on high-single-digit handle growth. FY2027 state contributions are likely to land **flat-to-down versus FY2026's $132.3m (estimate $120–135m)** unless Q2 (Oct–Dec) holds recover toward the 11% of autumn 2025. **The tax direction of travel is up, not down:** with Blueprint receipts under pressure and a 2027 session approaching, we assign a meaningful probability to a further fiscal move — most likely a cap or phase-out of promo deductibility (the cheapest politically) ahead of a headline-rate increase toward the 25–30% band the Governor originally floated. **Market structure will concentrate further:** expect the tail (Bally Bet, betPARX, LetsBetMD, Crab) to exit or consolidate, leaving 7–8 live brands, while FanDuel/DraftKings defend ~70% share with higher promo. **Licensing stays closed** absent a revived HB 1431-type bill; **iGaming is not a 2026–27 event**; and enforcement against prediction markets and sweepstakes intensifies, with the Kalshi litigation the single biggest swing factor for licensed-channel volume. For B2Tech the actionable path is B2B supply or a licence partnership with a dormant holder, underwritten at a 15–16% effective GGR tax today and a stress case of 20–25% effective after a promo-cap.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- October and November 2026 MLGCA releases (due ~10 Nov and ~10 Dec): whether hold recovers to ≥10.5% and whether promo stays near 19% of GGR — the test of structural vs. one-off margin compression.
- Governor's FY2028 budget (January 2027) and the Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act: any change to the 20% mobile rate, introduction of a promo-deduction cap, or a per-wager fee.
- Kalshi/prediction-market litigation status and any Fourth Circuit ruling on Maryland's cease-and-desist authority; CFTC rulemaking on sports event contracts.
- Outcome of the 2026 enforcement bills (SB 652/HB 1226, HB 295) on unlicensed/sweepstakes online gaming and any 2027 reintroduction; iGaming bill activity ahead of a potential 2028 referendum.
- SWARC/legislative action to reopen the licence window (HB 1431 successor) and disposition of dormant licences (Riverboat/P, Long Shot's, SuperBook) and Crab Sports' licence.
- Five-year licence renewals for the 2021–22 cohort (retail hearings began 27 Aug 2026; mobile renewals follow) and any new conditions attached.
- Casino GGR trend (five consecutive YoY declines to September) and MGM/Cordish responses, given these properties anchor the Class A-1 licences and the Education Trust Fund.
