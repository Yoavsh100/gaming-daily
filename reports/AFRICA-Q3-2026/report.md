# Africa () — Q3 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: Local currencies (ZAR, KES, NGN, TZS, ETB) with ~USD alongside; tables in bn/m as labelled · Results released 20 Oct 2026 (Blask Q3-2026 Africa rankings via Findmore Africa); NGB South Africa FY2024/25 report published Apr 2026**

> Africa has no pan-continental GGR print; the Q3-26 "release" is a demand index (Blask, 42 markets) showing the continent down 1.9% QoQ with West Africa (Nigeria +15.6%, Ghana +21.5%) rebounding and the tail (Mali −80.8%, Rwanda −56.1%) collapsing. The only audited benchmark, South Africa, is a R74.5bn (~$4.07bn) GGR market growing 25.6% on online betting alone, while the rules elsewhere are moving toward higher fixed licence costs (Kenya KES 50m/~$388k online licence, 1-year term), broader stake/deposit-based excises (Kenya 5% of deposits, Tanzania 5% of stakes), player withholding (Kenya 20%) and payment-rail enforcement. Entry economics are deteriorating faster than demand is growing in most jurisdictions except South Africa and Nigeria.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **There is no regulator-published pan-African GGR; the Q3-26 "release" is a demand proxy.** Blask's Q3-2026 ranking (published 20 Oct 2026 via Findmore Africa) sums a search/visibility-based "Blask Index" across 42 African markets for Jul–Sep 2026: aggregate demand −1.9% QoQ after a steeper Q2 slide, with 20 markets up and 22 down. It is not GGR, NGR or stakes and must be read as direction of travel only.
- **West Africa led the partial recovery; the frontier tail collapsed.** Nigeria +15.6% and Ghana +21.5% QoQ grew together for the first time since the Q2 downturn; Kenya +5.5% after a flat Q2; Ethiopia +40.9% off a licence-revoked base; South Africa −0.8%. Fallers: Mali −80.8%, Rwanda −56.1%, Tunisia −30.6%, Angola −15.6%, Mozambique −5.8%.
- **Brand demand is concentrated and the top two are flat.** betPawa (Index 252.6m, 24 markets, −2.2% QoQ) and Betway (215.7m, 30 markets, +0.2%) account for 51.3% of the top-20 Index (52.1% in Q2); the top five take ~74% (our calculation). The betPawa–Betway gap narrowed from 20.1% to 17.1%. SportyBet (+13.9%, #3), Bet9ja (+14.9%) and SportPesa (+26.2%, Kenya +33.1%) captured the Nigerian/Kenyan rebound; Premier Bet −33.3% on a 77% Mali collapse.
- **South Africa is the only audited, regulator-published market and it is large.** NGB's FY2024/25 summary (1 Apr 2024–31 Mar 2025, published Apr 2026): turnover R1.50tn (~$82bn) +30.7%; GGR R74.5bn (~$4.07bn) +25.6%; taxes/levies R5.81bn (~$0.32bn) +20.1%. Post-pandemic four-year average GGR growth is 34.1% vs 5.9% pre-pandemic.
- **SA is now a betting market, not a casino market.** Betting GGR R52.0bn (~$2.84bn) +44.7% = 70% of GGR (60% a year earlier); bookmakers' sports betting R47.9bn (~$2.62bn) +49.3%; bookmakers' horse racing R3.5bn (~$0.19bn) +12.3%; totalisator GGR R0.57bn and shrinking. Casinos R16.6bn (~$0.91bn) −4.1%, LPMs R4.1bn (~$0.23bn) −0.04%, bingo R1.7bn (~$0.09bn) −8.6%.
- **SA online penetration is already mature-market level.** In the six provinces that disaggregate, online betting GGR was R44.5bn (~$2.43bn) = 86% of betting GGR; NGB estimates ~60% of all SA gambling GGR is online, and notes this is understated because non-reporting provinces are booked as retail.
- **SA tax is provincial, GGR-based and low.** Total taxes/levies R5.8bn = 7.8% of GGR; betting R3.42bn (~$0.19bn, +41.8%) = 6.6% of betting GGR; casinos R1.72bn (~$0.09bn) = 10.3%; LPMs 12.7%. Mpumalanga (41%) and Western Cape (37%) host most online bookmaker GGR — a provincial licensing/tax arbitrage. National Treasury's 2025 discussion paper on a national online gambling tax [seen at snippet level only] is the structural swing factor.
- **SA licence counts are consolidating.** Operational bookmakers fell 7.1% to 315 (outlets up 11.9% to 594); 36 casinos operate against a 41-licence cap; 15,743 LPMs and 13,452 EBTs. Bookmaker licences are unlimited in several provinces subject to market demand/viability, so entry is possible but the demand curve is flattening (Blask SA −0.8% QoQ; Hollywoodbets absorbing local demand; Betway SA −2.9%).
- **Kenya has rebuilt its regime and repriced entry.** The Gambling Control Act 2025 created the Gambling Regulatory Authority (GRA, replacing BCLB). The Gambling Control (Licensing) Regulations 2026 (LN 111, gazetted 29 Jun 2026) set the online bookmaker and online casino licence fee at KES 50m (~$388k) versus KES 8m/6m in the March draft, plus KES 5m (~$39k) application, KES 5m annual operating fee and KES 12.5m (~$97k) renewal; online lottery KES 20m (~$155k). Term cut from 3 years (draft) to 1 year; gambling capital for an online bookmaker KES 100m (~$775k); a 6% levy on advertising spend (draft 10%); the hybrid multi-product licence was removed.
- **Kenya's fees are sub judice.** JR E251/2026 (Owuor & Brance v Prime CS, GRA, AG; AGOK and Safaricom interested parties): full stay 20 Jul; narrowed 7 Aug to the fee (Second) and capital (Third) Schedules; 24 Aug the court allowed GRA to collect fees (refundable if struck down) after 246 applications stalled; legacy licences expired on the 60-day transition deadline (28 Aug); judgment due 2 Oct did not land and the matter was listed for mention on 12 Oct — unresolved at 21 Oct 2026.
- **Kenya's tax base widened on 1 Jul 2026.** Finance Act 2026 keeps betting and gaming excise at 5% but moves the base from wallet-based charging to all amounts deposited for betting/gaming (horse racing exclusion removed); reintroduces 20% withholding tax on winnings (resident and non-resident) and redefines 'withdrawals' so WHT bites when funds are credited to the player account, closing the re-bet deferral loophole. The standing 15%-of-GGR betting/gaming tax was not re-verified this cycle. KRA betting taxes FY2025/26: KSh16.53bn (~$128m) +24.9%, 115.9% of the KSh14.26bn (~$110m) target; WHT on betting/gaming +59.2%.
- **Kenya enforcement is wired through payments and data.** LN 112 (Conduct of Gambling Operations) requires GRA approval for any payment service provider to process gambling payments (revocable), pre-trading integration with KRA's system and GRA's central monitoring system, declaration of every paybill, and player data stored/processed on servers in Kenya unless exempted. Safaricom's M-Pesa paybill switch (58 paybills cut in Apr 2025) is the de facto enforcement tool.
- **Nigeria has gone sub-national.** The Supreme Court (22 Nov 2024, AG Lagos v AG Federation) struck down the National Lottery Act 2005; NLRC is confined to the FCT (FCTA-LRO); the President declined assent to the Central Gaming Bill 2025. The Federation of State Gaming Regulators (FSGRN, 20+ states) adopted the Universal Reciprocity Certificate on 7 May 2025 covering online sports betting, online casino, public online lottery and promotional competitions; 2025 fees were waived for transitioning NLRC licensees and normal fees resumed 1 Jan 2026. Non-member states remain a legal gap; no regulator-published GGR, fee schedule or tax take could be verified this cycle (n/a).
- **Ghana is the fastest-growing large market on demand but fiscal creep continues.** Blask Ghana +21.5% QoQ (Betway +24.2%, SportyBet +28.1% in-market). The 10% withholding tax on winnings was repealed in 2025 and GGR is taxed at 20% [both snippet-level, unverified]; trade press on 20 Oct 2026 flagged a 20% VAT on digital services affecting online betting [unverified].
- **Tanzania moved to a stake-based excise.** A 5% excise duty on betting stakes was introduced in the FY2026/27 budget from July 2026, targeted to raise TZS74.5bn (~$29m) [trade-press reports of the budget; not verified against the Finance Act]. betPawa's Tanzania demand fell in Q3; SportPesa added a small lift.
- **Ethiopia is a regulatory vacuum, not a market.** The Ethiopian Lottery Service revoked all sports-betting licences in Dec 2025 and signalled in Sep 2026 a stricter relaunch of sports betting and digital lotteries with no published timetable; an audit cited a Birr 27.5bn revenue gap [snippet-level]. Q3 demand +40.9% reflects grey-market and public-debate activity, not licensed turnover.
- **Payments reality: mobile money is both the rail and the regulator's lever.** Kenya (M-Pesa paybills), Tanzania and Uganda run on mobile money; South Africa runs on cards/EFT/vouchers under provincial rules. Where the regulator can switch off a paybill, licence compliance is enforced in hours, not months — a key design consideration for any PSP stack.
- **Responsible-gambling and advertising rules are tightening in step.** Kenya now levies 6% of advertising spend and has separate 2026 regulations on advertising, conduct, foreign-based operators and an appeals tribunal; NGB flags rising problem gambling and intensified awareness/enforcement in SA; Ethiopia's relaunch is framed around stricter controls.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Three tiers: (i) South Africa — large, measurable, low effective tax (6.6% of betting GGR), 86% online, but growth decelerating (betting GGR growth 53%→51%→45% over three years) and a national online tax under discussion; (ii) Kenya — ~$0.5m of year-one fixed fees for an online book, 5%-of-deposits excise, 20% WHT and 15% GGR tax, with fee legality unresolved — a consolidation market; (iii) Nigeria/Ghana — strongest demand recovery but state-by-state licensing (Nigeria) and VAT/levy creep (Ghana). Frontier markets (Mali, Rwanda, Ethiopia) demonstrate single-event regulatory risk that can erase 50–80% of demand in a quarter.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Demand & operator landscape (Blask Q3-26, 42 markets)

*Continent −1.9% QoQ; West Africa rebounds, frontier tail collapses; betPawa and Betway hold 51% of top-20 demand but are not growing.*

### Aggregate demand

**Aggregate demand across the 42 markets fell 1.9% QoQ, a marked deceleration from the Q2 slide; 20 markets grew and 22 fell.**

- Q3-2026 edition (July–September, published 20 Oct 2026) uses the same 42-market set and data pull for both quarters, so QoQ comparison is internally consistent.
- The index is a demand proxy built from open-source signals rather than reported GGR.
- Gainers led by small bases: Sudan +52.3%, Ethiopia +40.9%, Sierra Leone +36.1%.
- Absolute weight sits with Ghana +21.5% and Nigeria +15.6%, growing for the first time since the Q2 downturn.
- Kenya +5.5% and Uganda +1.3% stabilised; DR Congo +4.2%; Botswana +11% and Zimbabwe +9.9% extended a Southern African recovery South Africa did not share (−0.8%).

### betPawa and Betway

**The two broadest footprints hold 51.3% of the top-20 Index (52.1% in Q2) but have stopped compounding; growth is going to country specialists.**

- betPawa Index 252.6m across 24 countries (−2.2% QoQ); declines in Tanzania, Uganda and Cameroon partly offset by DR Congo and Zambia.
- Betway 215.7m across 30 countries (+0.2%), rotating out of South Africa (−2.9%) into Ghana (+24.2%) and Mozambique (+5%).
- Gap between the two narrowed from 20.1% to 17.1%.
- Our top-20 aggregation gives a combined Index of 912.2m, +0.3% QoQ — essentially flat — with the top five at roughly 74%.

### Nigerian and Kenyan growers

**The growth cohort is Nigerian and Kenyan single-country specialists, effectively bets on Nigerian and Kenyan regulatory stability.**

- SportyBet +13.9% to 91.7m (22 markets) on Nigeria +16.9% and Ghana +28.1%; Bet9ja +14.9% to 33.9m on Nigeria +14.8%.
- SportPesa +26.2% to 18.6m almost entirely on Kenya +33.1% with a smaller Tanzanian lift.
- Fortebet +14.4% on Uganda +14.7% in a market only +1.3% overall; Betika +5.7%, recovering modestly in Kenya (+2.5%) after a sharp Q2 drop.
- None has a second market large enough to offset its home market.

### Decliners and movers

**A single jurisdiction can swing a multi-market operator: Premier Bet fell 33.3% to 22.0m as Mali dropped 80.8%.**

- Premier Bet (22 countries) lost 77% of its Mali line; Elephant Bet −10.2% shed demand in Mozambique and Angola, its two largest markets.
- Rwanda's 56.1% market decline hit Fortebet's non-Ugandan lines.
- South Africa: YesPlay −8.8% and Lotto Star −6% gave back Q2 gains as the market flattened; Hollywoodbets +1.1% (76.4m) continued absorbing local demand.
- MWOS and Betclic entered the top 20 as bet223 and 888bet dropped out.
- 1xBet is present in all 42 markets but ranks only 12th (16.5m): a thin, broad grey-to-licensed footprint rather than depth anywhere.

### Entry implications

**The continent is not growing in aggregate on a demand basis in 2026, so share must be taken rather than ridden.**

- Nigeria and Ghana are where incremental demand is appearing; leaders there (SportyBet, Bet9ja, Betway) are local-content and local-payment specialists.
- Exposure to small francophone and East African frontier markets carries a demonstrated 50–80% single-quarter demand drawdown risk when licensing or payment access changes.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — South Africa — the audited benchmark (NGB FY2024/25)

*R74.5bn (~$4.07bn) GGR +25.6%, driven entirely by online sports betting (+49.3%); land-based in decline; effective tax 7.8% of GGR with a national online tax under discussion.*

### Headline figures

**GGR reached R74.5bn (~$4.07bn), +25.6%, in the only regulator-audited GGR series on the continent, with signs of a plateau by Q4.**

- FY2024/25 (1 April 2024–31 March 2025) compiled from audited provincial licensing authority submissions under s.65 of the National Gambling Act 2004, published April 2026.
- Turnover R1,500.7bn (~$82.0bn), +30.7%; taxes and levies R5.81bn (~$0.32bn), +20.1%.
- Quarterly GGR R17.3bn, R18.1bn, R20.1bn and R19.1bn (vs R13.8bn–R15.7bn prior year); Q4 FY24/25 (Jan–Mar 2025) was already below Q3.
- Blask's flat Q3-26 SA demand reading corroborates the plateau.
- NGB describes a structural post-COVID shift: average GGR growth of 34.1% in the four post-pandemic years against 5.9% before.

### Betting drives growth

**Growth is wholly a betting story: betting GGR R52.0bn (~$2.84bn), +44.7%, lifting its share of GGR from 60% to 70%.**

- Betting turnover R1,125.6bn (~$61.5bn), +47.9%, implying a blended hold of 4.6%.
- Bookmakers' sports betting GGR R47.9bn (~$2.62bn), +49.3% (after +55.3%, +61% and +57% in prior years — decelerating but still compounding); horse racing R3.5bn (~$0.19bn), +12.3%.
- Totalisator horse racing R0.34bn (−10.6%) and totalisator sports R0.23bn (−9.8%) continue to shrink.
- Casinos fell 4.1% to R16.6bn (~$0.91bn), LPMs flat at R4.1bn (~$0.23bn), bingo fell 8.6% to R1.7bn (~$0.09bn); casino share of GGR fell from 81.9% in FY2010/11 to 22.3%.
- In six disaggregating provinces (WC, MP, LP, NW, EC, NC) online betting GGR was R44.5bn (~$2.43bn), 86% of betting GGR; NGB estimates ~60% of all SA GGR online, explicitly understated.

### Licensing map

**Provincial licensing drives the skew: Mpumalanga generated 41% of betting GGR (R21.3bn), Western Cape 37% (R19.5bn), Gauteng only 9%.**

- Bookmaker licences unlimited in Western Cape, Mpumalanga, Limpopo, Northern Cape and Free State subject to demand and viability; capped at 131 in Gauteng, 48 in KZN, 19 in North West.
- Mpumalanga took 54% of betting turnover.
- Operational bookmakers fell 7.1% to 315 (Gauteng 140, Mpumalanga 52, Western Cape 47); outlets rose 11.9% to 594 and totalisator outlets to 366.
- 36 of a maximum 41 casino licences operating after Mayfair Casino (Eastern Cape) closed in January 2025.
- Consolidation at the licence level alongside 45% GGR growth shows scale economics in marketing and payments are already biting.

### Tax and fiscal risk

**Taxes/levies of R5.8bn equal 7.8% of GGR; a national online gambling tax is the single most important fiscal risk.**

- Betting contributed R3.42bn (~$0.19bn), +41.8%, or 6.6% of betting GGR (59% of all gambling tax); casinos R1.72bn (~$0.09bn, 10.3%, −2.9%); LPMs R0.53bn (12.7%); bingo R0.15bn (8.4%).
- Mpumalanga (R1.40bn) and Western Cape (R1.23bn) collected 77% of betting tax.
- National Treasury's 2025 discussion paper on a national online gambling tax [seen at snippet level only; not read] would move SA from the lowest effective betting tax here toward Kenyan levels.
- NGB flags rising problem-gambling consequences and intensified enforcement against illegal operators and uncertified equipment.

### Commercial read

**South Africa is the one African market where an entrant can underwrite a plan on audited numbers, but demand is flat and incumbents entrenched.**

- ~$2.4bn online betting pool growing (but decelerating) at 40%+, 6.6% effective GGR tax, card/EFT/voucher payments, provincial licence obtainable in Western Cape or Mpumalanga.
- Counterweights: flat Q3-26 demand reading; Hollywoodbets (Blask 76.4m) and Betway (largest Betway market) entrenchment.
- Remote Gambling policy vacuum leaves online casino technically outside the national framework; bookmakers offer casino-style 'betting on' products under provincial rules.
- The national tax debate remains a counterweight.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Kenya — Gambling Control Act regime: licensing, tax and enforcement

*Entry repriced to ~KES 60m (~$465k) year-one for an online book on a 1-year term, 5%-of-deposits excise plus 20% winnings WHT from 1 Jul 2026; fee legality unresolved after the 2 Oct judgment slipped.*

### New licensing regime

**Gazetted regulations on 29 June 2026 raised the online bookmaker licence fee from KES 8m to KES 50m (~$388k) on a one-year term.**

- Gambling Control Act 2025 created the GRA in place of the BCLB; regulations include Licensing (LN 111), Conduct of Gambling Operations (LN 112), Advertising, Foreign-Based Operators and an Appeals Tribunal.
- Online casino rose from KES 6m to KES 50m, online lottery KES 6m to KES 20m (~$155k); annual operating fee KES 3m to KES 5m (~$39k); renewal KES 12.5m (~$97k).
- Gambling capital for an online bookmaker doubled to KES 100m (~$775k), public lottery KES 150m (~$1.16m), online bingo KES 50m; hybrid four-product licence deleted.
- Advertising levy set at 6% of spend against a 10% proposal the Association of Gaming Operators Kenya opposed.
- Legacy BLGA licences stayed valid 60 days from gazettement, to 28 August 2026.

### Judicial review

**Fee and capital schedules are under judicial review; judgment set for 2 October did not land, so operators have paid KES 50m-scale fees under an undertaking.**

- JR E251/2026, Owuor & Brance v Prime Cabinet Secretary, GRA and AG; AGOK and Safaricom interested parties.
- Justice Musyoka granted leave on 20 July as a stay of the whole regulations; on 7 August narrowed it to the Second Schedule (fees) and Third Schedule (capital).
- On 24 August GRA was allowed to collect disputed fees so 246 stalled applications could proceed, with refunds undertaken if schedules are struck down.
- Applicants argue the wrong Cabinet Secretary signed; the judge flagged applicants' standing (neither holds a licence).
- Listed for mention 12 October; as of 21 October the outcome is unknown.

### Tax stack

**From 1 July 2026 excise of 5% applies to all deposits and 20% withholding tax on winnings is reintroduced, with receipts already surging.**

- Excise base shifts from wallet-based mechanism to all amounts deposited regardless of whether they sit in a wallet; horse-racing exclusion deleted.
- WHT 20% for residents and non-residents; 'withdrawals' redefined so the tax point is when money is paid to the player's account, closing the re-bet deferral.
- The standing 15%-of-GGR betting/gaming tax was not re-verified in this cycle.
- KRA FY2025/26: betting taxes KSh16.53bn (~$128m), +24.9%, 115.9% of a KSh14.26bn (~$110m) target; betting tax +20.3%, WHT on betting and gaming +59.2%.
- Betting outgrew corporation tax (+14.0%), VAT (+8.5%) and PAYE (+6.7%).

### Payments enforcement

**Licence suspension equals immediate loss of M-Pesa collections; hosting, data and tax-API architecture must be Kenya-resident from day one.**

- LN 112 bars payment service providers from processing gambling payments without GRA approval, which GRA can revoke.
- Licensees must confirm integration with KRA's tax system and GRA's central monitoring system and declare every paybill before trading.
- Player data encrypted in transit and at rest and stored on servers in Kenya unless GRA grants a written exemption.
- Precedent: April 2025, when the previous regulator ordered Safaricom to cut off paybills for 58 platforms.

### Entrant economics

**This is a consolidation regime designed around incumbents; attractive only with an existing Kenyan customer base or a B2B route into a licensed incumbent.**

- Demand recovering modestly: Blask Kenya +5.5% QoQ in Q3 after a flat Q2; SportPesa +33.1% in-market, Betika +2.5%.
- Year-one cost ~KES 60m (~$465k) of fees, KES 100m (~$775k) ring-fenced capital, 6% of advertising spend, 5% of every deposit, 15% of GGR, 20% of player winnings.
- One-year licence whose fee may or may not survive judicial review; sub-scale brands squeezed out in favour of SportPesa, Betika and the pan-African books.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Nigeria — state-led licensing after the Supreme Court

*Largest demand rebound (+15.6% QoQ) inside the least settled legal framework: NLRC confined to the FCT, 20+ states licensing via FSGRN's Universal Reciprocity Certificate, fees live since 1 Jan 2026, non-member states a gap.*

### Supreme Court ruling

**The 22 November 2024 ruling made betting an exclusive state matter, confining NLRC to the FCT after the Central Gaming Bill 2025 was refused assent.**

- Seven-judge panel in Attorney-General of Lagos State & 21 Ors v Attorney-General of the Federation & 15 Ors declared the National Lottery Act 2005 unconstitutional in its entirety.
- Perpetual injunction issued against federal implementation in any state.
- Central Gaming Bill 2025 passed both chambers but presidential assent was refused: the medium does not change the constitutional character of the activity.
- FCTA Lottery Regulatory Office now licenses retail lottery, sports betting, casino, interactive gaming and telco-facilitated gaming in the FCT, described as second-largest market after Lagos.

### Sub-national framework

**FSGRN's Universal Reciprocity Certificate covers online products across 20+ member states, with fees live since 1 January 2026, but non-member states remain a gap.**

- Lagos State's LSLGA (2021 law) is the benchmark regulator across land-based and online channels; Rivers, Ogun and Plateau had parallel regimes before the judgment.
- On 7 May 2025 FSGRN adopted a Subnational Reciprocity Licensing Framework; the URC authorises online sports betting, online casino, public online lottery and promotional competitions.
- Transitioning NLRC licensees meeting criteria received a full licence-fee waiver for the 2025 licensing year.
- URC does not cover land-based categories and has no effect in non-FSGRN states; enforcement risk there takes the form of ad hoc executive or revenue action.

### Data gaps

**No state regulator or FSGRN publishes market GGR, stakes or active-player data; demand is the only quantified signal.**

- URC fee schedule and state-level tax rates (lottery/gaming levies, withholding on winnings under the 2025 federal tax reform) could not be verified and are shown as n/a.
- Blask has Nigeria +15.6% QoQ in Q3-26 after a Q2 contraction; SportyBet +16.9%, Bet9ja +14.8% in-market, BetKing +4.3% Africa-wide.
- All three are Nigeria-centric brands with no second market of comparable weight.

### B2Tech implications

**Nigeria must be approached as a federation of licences; demand recovery is the strongest of the large markets but regulatory cost scales with states.**

- Required: a URC for covered online products, an LSLGA licence for Lagos, FCTA-LRO for Abuja, and a risk-tiered map of FSGRN states, independent-regime states and no-framework states.
- Due diligence on any acquisition must treat legacy NLRC licences as insufficient.
- Upside: incumbents are local brands rather than pan-African books, leaving room for a differentiated product.
- Downside: regulatory cost scaling with the number of states and no audited market data to size the opportunity.

## 8. Segment deep-dive — Payments, responsible gambling and advertising

*Mobile money is both the rail and the regulator's switch; data residency and tax-API integration are now licence conditions in Kenya; advertising levies and RG obligations are rising everywhere.*

### East Africa payments

**In East Africa M-Pesa access, not the licence certificate, is the binding constraint; expect similar architectures to spread to Tanzania and Uganda.**

- Kenya's LN 112 makes GRA approval a revocable precondition for any PSP to process gambling payments; licensees must declare every paybill and integrate with KRA taxation-at-source and GRA monitoring before trading.
- Safaricom is an interested party in the licensing litigation after affidavits raised channel deactivation; GRA assured licensed operators' paybills would stay on.
- KRA's +59.2% growth in withholding on betting and gaming in FY2025/26 is the fiscal dividend of that integration.

### South Africa

**South Africa is the exception: a card/EFT/voucher market with 86% of betting GGR online and no central monitoring system for betting.**

- NCEMS covers LPMs only.
- FY2024/25 report commits to intensified awareness campaigns and enforcement to confine consumers to compliant operators and certified equipment.
- NGB explicitly links the 25.6% growth to more pronounced social consequences — political groundwork for the Treasury's national online tax discussion and tighter advertising rules.

### Kenya compliance stack

**Kenya's regime is designed to make the licensed channel fully observable to the state, requiring full infrastructure before the first bet.**

- Stand-alone advertising regulations and a 6% levy on advertising spend (draft proposed 10%); data encrypted and held on servers in Kenya unless exempted.
- KES 100m (~$775k) ring-fenced gambling capital, one-year licence term and 20% WHT on winnings credited to accounts.
- A B2B/B2C stack needs in-country hosting or an exemption, a tax API, a monitoring-system feed, and PSP contracts that survive regulatory suspension.

### Continental direction

**More of the value chain is being brought inside the licence; compliance cost, not the licence fee, is becoming the main barrier to entry.**

- Ethiopia's relaunch is framed around stricter controls; Nigeria's state regulators (LSLGA in particular) attach technical and consumer-protection standards to online licences.
- Ghana removed a player-side tax but is reportedly adding VAT.
- Payments, data and marketing are converging inside the licence perimeter across the continent.

## 9. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- South Africa offers an audited, ~$2.4bn online betting pool (R44.5bn, 86% of betting GGR) growing 45–49% with an effective betting tax of only 6.6% of GGR and unlimited bookmaker licences in the Western Cape/Mpumalanga — the only African market an entrant can underwrite on regulator numbers. _(structural)_
- Nigeria's demand rebound (+15.6% QoQ; SportyBet +16.9%, Bet9ja +14.8%) coincides with a now-settled constitutional position and a single multi-state instrument (URC) for online sports betting, casino and lottery across 20+ FSGRN states — the first workable multi-state licence since 2005. _(structural)_
- Ghana is the fastest-growing large market on demand (+21.5% QoQ) and removed the 10% player winnings tax in 2025 [unverified], improving price competitiveness versus the grey market. _(mixed)_
- Kenya's KES 50m (~$388k) annual online licence, KES 100m (~$775k) capital requirement and 6% ad levy will force out sub-scale brands; an operator with scale or a B2B route into SportPesa/Betika-tier incumbents can gain share in a market whose tax take grew 24.9% to KSh16.5bn (~$128m). _(structural)_
- The pan-African leaders are flat (betPawa −2.2%, Betway +0.2%; combined share of top-20 demand down to 51.3%), so share is contestable by country specialists with local content and payments. _(early)_
- Southern Africa ex-SA (Botswana +11%, Zimbabwe +9.9%) and DR Congo (+4.2%) are growing quietly with low brand concentration (NgeNge, Congo Bet single-market), offering adjacency entries from a South African base. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- Kenya's entire fee and capital schedule is sub judice (JR E251/2026): fees are being paid under a refund undertaking, the 2 Oct judgment slipped to a 12 Oct mention, and the wrong-signatory argument could void LN 111 — budgeting certainty is nil for FY2026/27. _(governance)_
- Stake/deposit-based taxes are spreading: Kenya 5% of all deposits (horse racing now included) plus 20% WHT on winnings credited to accounts; Tanzania 5% of stakes [unverified] — at a 4.6% blended sports hold such levies can exceed GGR and force odds compression. _(structural)_
- Nigeria has no audited market data, no verified fee/tax schedule, and a material number of states outside FSGRN where the URC has no legal effect — regulatory cost scales with state count and enforcement is ad hoc. _(structural)_
- South Africa's growth is decelerating (Q4 FY24/25 GGR R19.1bn below Q3's R20.1bn; Blask SA −0.8% QoQ; bookmaker count −7.1%) while National Treasury's national online gambling tax proposal [unverified] threatens the 6.6% effective rate that underpins the market's economics. _(watch)_
- Frontier markets can lose half or more of demand in a quarter — Mali −80.8%, Rwanda −56.1%, Tunisia −30.6%, Ethiopia closed since Dec 2025 — and Premier Bet's −33.3% shows how one geography can swing a 22-country operator. _(one-off)_
- Payment-rail enforcement (Kenya PSP approval, paybill cut-offs, data residency, tax-API and monitoring-system integration) makes licence suspension instantaneous and raises pre-launch fixed cost; expect replication in Tanzania and Uganda. _(structural)_
- The continent is not growing in aggregate on a demand basis (−1.9% QoQ after a steeper Q2), so entry plans must assume share-taking against entrenched local leaders (Hollywoodbets, SportyBet, Bet9ja, SportPesa) rather than market lift. _(watch)_

## 10. Forecast & Outlook

The Q2→Q3 bridge is a demand bridge, not a GGR bridge. Blask's 42-market index moved from a broad Q2 contraction to −1.9% QoQ in Q3-26: the West African rebound (Nigeria +15.6%, Ghana +21.5%) and small-base gains (Ethiopia, Sudan, Sierra Leone) were almost fully offset by the francophone/East African tail (Mali −80.8%, Rwanda −56.1%, Tunisia −30.6%, Angola −15.6%) and a flat South Africa (−0.8%). On the rules, the quarter's moving parts were Kenya's gazetted fee schedule (KES 50m online licence, 1-year term) and its litigation, the Finance Act 2026 tax base changes effective 1 July, Tanzania's reported 5% stake excise from July, Nigeria's first full year of URC fees, and Ethiopia's September relaunch signal.

- **Q2-26 Africa demand:** Broad contraction (base)
- **West Africa rebound:** Nigeria +15.6%, Ghana +21.5% (+)
- **Frontier drawdowns:** Mali −80.8%, Rwanda −56.1%, Tunisia −30.6% (−)
- **Q3-26 Africa demand:** −1.9% QoQ (20 mkts up / 22 down)

Our independent view: **South Africa's FY2025/26 GGR (to 31 Mar 2026, due from NGB around Apr 2027) will show betting growth decelerating into the mid-teens-to-20% range from +44.7%** given the Q4 FY24/25 plateau and flat 2026 demand readings [estimate]; total GGR likely R85–90bn (~$4.6–4.9bn) [estimate]. **Kenya's licensed market will consolidate** — expect the number of licensed online operators to fall materially in the 2026/27 cycle as the KES 50m annual fee and KES 100m capital bite, while KRA betting receipts keep growing double-digit on the wider deposit base and account-credit WHT; the judicial review is more likely to produce a procedural remedy (re-gazettement) than a return to draft-level fees. **Nigeria's URC will become the de facto national licence** as more states join, but non-member-state enforcement and the absence of market data will persist through 2027. **Tax direction is unambiguously up and base-shifting (stakes/deposits, VAT, ad levies)**; effective tax on GGR for an online sports book will rise in Kenya, Tanzania and Ghana while South Africa's remains low until a national online tax is legislated — the single biggest swing factor for continental margins. Cost of compliance (data residency, monitoring integration, PSP gating) rather than licence fees will be the main barrier to entry by 2027.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- Tanzania: Finance Act 2026 text and Gaming Board implementation of the 5% excise on stakes; operator odds/promotion response and any shift of demand to the grey market.
- Ghana: confirmation of the 20% digital-services VAT scope for online betting and Gaming Commission licence-fee changes for 2027.
- Ethiopia: ELS relaunch framework, licence caps, fees and timetable; treatment of operators whose licences were revoked in Dec 2025.
- Blask Q4-26 (Jan 2027): whether Nigeria/Ghana growth holds through the African football calendar and whether betPawa/Betway resume growth or continue to cede share.
