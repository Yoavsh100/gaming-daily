# Brazil () — JANUARY-SEPTEMBER 2026 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: BRL (R$) primary; ~USD alongside at USD/BRL 5.37. Tables in R$ bn unless stated. · Results released 22 Oct 2026 (Receita Federal Sept-2026 collection data); framed by MP 1.394 of 25 Sep 2026**

> Brazil's regulated fixed-odds market was running at a record ~R$3.3–3.5bn (~$0.62–0.65bn) of monthly GGR and had already delivered R$11.1bn (~$2.07bn) of federal betting-and-lottery receipts in nine months when the Lula government switched it off: MP 1.394/2026 banned all sports betting and online gaming from 25 Sep 2026, forced platforms dark on 6 Oct and extinguished every authorisation on 25 Oct with no refund of the R$30m (~$5.6m) fee. January–September 2026 is therefore the high-water mark of a 21-month regulated experiment; whether a legal market returns — and in what shape — now rests on STF Justice Fux and a Congress that must act before the measure lapses around March 2027.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **The market was switched off at its peak.** Provisional Measure (MP) 1.394/2026, signed 25 Sep 2026 — nine days before the first-round election — prohibits the operation, offer, intermediation and advertising of all fixed-odds betting (sports and online games) nationwide, including offshore supply to persons in Brazil. Deposits stopped on publication, sites and apps went dark at 00:00 on 6 Oct, and every federal, state and district authorisation was extinguished 30 days after publication (25 Oct) with no refund of the outorga and no indemnity (MP art. 4).
- **Size: a ~R$40bn-a-year market.** SPA data obtained under the access-to-information law (LAI) put regulated fixed-odds GGR at R$20.07bn (~$3.74bn) in H1-26, +15.3% on H1-25 (R$17.4bn / ~$3.24bn), after R$36.96bn (~$6.88bn) in FY-25, the first regulated year. Monthly average was R$3.35bn (~$624m); January peaked at R$4.29bn (~$799m), Feb–Mar fell to ~R$2.8bn (~$521m), and June — 19 World Cup days, 72 matches — was only R$3.34bn (~$622m): no tournament spike.
- **Stakes and payout.** Prizes returned to bettors in H1-26 were R$377.86bn (~$70.4bn), implying stakes of ≈R$398bn (~$74bn) and a payout ratio of ≈95% [estimate from SPA figures]. A separate SPA-sourced report puts H1-26 deposits at R$88.48bn (~$16.5bn) and GGR at R$20.28bn (~$3.78bn) — a 1% gap to the BNLData figure; GGR equated to ~23% of deposits.
- **Online-only market; product split not disclosed.** Regulated fixed-odds in Brazil is in practice 100% digital (there is no meaningful licensed retail sportsbook estate); retail gaming is the Caixa lottery monopoly plus state lotteries. SPA does not publish a verified sports-vs-online-games GGR split — n/a.
- **Player base: 30.9m CPFs, modest growth.** 30,895,934 unique CPFs had bet on licensed sites by end-H1-26 (cumulative since Jan-25), +9.8% on 28.1m; average spend per bettor rose 5.0% to R$649.60 (~$121) per semester (R$108.27 / ~$20 a month). 68.5% male; 72% aged 40 or under; only 2.8% over 60.
- **>5m Brazilians were already barred.** Minister Durigan (13 Aug 2026): ~3m Bolsa Família/BPC beneficiaries (banned since Oct-25), 1.2m self-excluded and ~0.8m Novo Desenrola debt-renegotiation participants could not bet — a structural brake on volume growth.
- **Licensing regime (as it stood).** Law 14.790/2023: federal authorisation at R$30m (~$5.6m) for five years and up to three brands, Brazilian-incorporated entity, SIGAP reporting, monthly fiscalisation fee (aggregate ≈R$115.2m/~$21m a year per the government's own impact note). Operators grew from 78 companies/182 brands (H1-25) to 87/188 (H1-26, +11.5%/+3.3%). All pending applications are voided (MP art. 6).
- **Tax regime: GGR-based, ratcheting.** Operators paid a 12% contribution on GGR (destinations: sport, tourism, public security, health, education), raised by Complementary Law 224/2025 (26 Dec 2025) to 13% in 2026, 14% in 2027 and 15% from 2028 — the extra 1–3pp earmarked for social security — plus ordinary IRPJ, CSLL, PIS and COFINS. Bettors pay 15% income tax on net prizes above the first IRPF exemption band. H1-26 legal allocations of R$2.49bn (~$464m) equal 12.4% of GGR.
- **Tax take: 9M-26 already beat FY-25.** Receita Federal (22 Oct 2026): betting and Caixa-lottery federal receipts were R$11.116bn (~$2.07bn) Jan–Sep 2026 vs R$9.95bn (~$1.85bn) for all of 2025. September was R$1.206bn (~$225m), +3.7% m/m but -7.9% y/y (R$1.310bn / ~$244m Sep-25). Fixed-odds alone generated R$6.86bn (~$1.28bn): R$3.72bn (~$693m) in statutory allocations and R$3.14bn (~$585m) in federal taxes. [Press-relayed; Receita report not opened directly.]
- **Who got the money.** Of H1-26 allocations, sport took R$870.15m (~$162m) via the Ministry of Sport and state secretariats; health R$24.19m (~$4.5m) funding the SUS gambling-harm programme (telehealth from March 2026, Sírio-Libanês contract R$2.94m / ~$548k). Receita's specific codes also show lotteries contributing R$4.96bn (~$924m) in H1 vs R$2.48bn (~$462m) from fixed-odds, Mega-Sena-type numerical lotteries being 77.2% of the lottery block.
- **Government's own fiscal cost of the ban is R$12bn over three years.** EM 2114/2026 books lost tax revenue of R$1.541bn (~$287m) in 2026, R$5.150bn (~$959m) in 2027 and R$5.330bn (~$993m) in 2028, plus R$268.8m (~$50m) of lost fiscalisation fees; it claims no outorga loss because fees were pre-paid. BNLData's counter-estimate at H1-26 run-rates is ≈R$18.2bn (~$3.39bn) foregone Oct-26–Dec-27 vs the government's R$6.8bn (~$1.27bn).
- **Wind-down mechanics are punitive and payments-led.** Operators faced R$200k (~$37k) daily fines for refund failures; banks/PSPs had seven days to return balances to the CPF-matched account of origin, with residuals swept to a Caixa escrow from 15 Oct; banks, payment institutions and Pix arrangement owners are barred from processing any fixed-odds transaction (art. 14) and the BCB must build a real-time rejection/return protocol (art. 15). App stores, OS vendors and platforms carry a duty of care, with fines up to 10% of Brazilian group revenue capped at R$50m (~$9.3m) per infraction.
- **Advertising goes from restricted to prohibited.** Pre-ban rules (Law 14.790 bonus ban; SPA Portaria 1.231/2024 on minors, 'easy money' claims and warnings) are replaced by a blanket ban on all communication, marketing and sponsorship (art. 16), with a 10-day removal deadline; breaches are 'abusive advertising' under the Consumer Code with counter-advertising sanctions.
- **Politics: the ban survived the election.** Lula was re-elected on 25 Oct 2026 against Flávio Bolsonaro, whose camp criticised the shutdown but favours a sports-only regime with online casino banned. The government (SRI minister José Guimarães, 27–28 Oct) lists the MP among its three legislative priorities and wants a vote before year-end, before a more conservative Congress sits in Feb-27. As of 27 Oct no joint committee had been installed and 99 amendments were on file.
- **Judiciary: no relief in force.** ADIs 8.024, 8.027 and 8.028 sit with Justice Fux, who deferred until after the runoff and has now convened a public hearing with 26 participants (government, sector, football clubs). Of 19 federal-court actions, none has an injunction in force; a TRF-3 panel dissolved the one favourable first-instance ruling within 24 hours (9 Oct).
- **Black market was already large and is now the only market.** LCA/Locomotiva for IBJR estimated 38–44% of online betting in H1-26 was on illegal sites despite SPA blocking >25,000 domains in year one (and >16,000 cited by Vixio). ANJL's Legitbet counts >34,000 clandestine betting sites since the MP. Channelisation, not demand, is the variable the ban has broken.
- **Industry damage is already structural.** ANJL: ~51% of the ~15,000 people employed by licensed operators have been laid off or furloughed; football sponsorship income has been severed; player balances have been emptied and the statutory welcome-bonus ban makes re-acquisition expensive. Vixio expects heavy consolidation toward tier-one balance sheets if the market reopens.
- **Timeline to clarity runs to Q2-27.** 9 Nov 2026: MP enters 'urgency' (locks the docket); December: possible expedited vote or deferral to the new legislature; ≈4 Mar 2027: 120-legislative-day expiry, which would automatically restore Law 14.790 (Congress would then need a legislative decree to re-validate licences); April 2027: possible vetoes/overrides.
- **Read-through for B2Tech.** Brazil's legal TAM is zero until at least Q1-27 and the modal outcome is a narrower sports-only regime at 14% GGR plus corporate tax and heavier payments/advertising controls. Do not commit capital to market access now; keep optionality via B2B/supplier readiness and watch the STF window (Nov) and the December vote. The pre-ban numbers — ~R$40bn (~$7.5bn) annualised GGR, 30.9m players, 95% payout — remain the best evidence of what a restored market is worth.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Fixed-odds market: sports betting & online games (Jan–Sep 2026)

*A ~R$40bn-a-year online market growing mid-teens, with no World Cup spike, abruptly halted after nine months.*

### H1-26 GGR and monthly profile

**Regulated fixed-odds GGR was R$20.07bn (~$3.74bn) in H1-26, +15.3% on H1-25, with no World Cup lift.**

- H1-25 GGR was R$17.4bn (~$3.24bn); FY-25 was R$36.96bn (~$6.88bn).
- January peaked at R$4.29bn (~$799m) as state and Brasileirão calendars were pulled forward ahead of the World Cup.
- February and March fell to roughly R$2.8bn (~$521m); April–June recovered to ~R$3.3–3.4bn (~$615–635m).
- June, with 19 World Cup days and 72 matches, produced R$3.34bn (~$622m), slightly below the semester average of R$3.35bn (~$624m).
- SPA/MF publishes no regular bulletin; data come from access-to-information releases reported by BNLData. The tournament did not lift GGR.

### Stakes, payout and deposits

**Prizes of R$377.86bn (~$70.4bn) imply stakes of ≈R$398bn (~$74bn) and a ~95% payout, consistent with a casino-heavy mix.**

- Hold of ~5%; sportsbook alone would not run that thin.
- Second SPA-sourced report (Claudio Dantas, 25 Sep 2026): H1-26 deposits R$88.48bn (~$16.5bn), GGR R$20.28bn (~$3.78bn), 84 companies/193 brands.
- The 1% GGR gap and differing operator counts versus BNLData are unexplained; the LAI figure is quoted as primary.
- GGR at ~23% of deposits points to high recycling of winnings into further play.

### Player base

**30,895,934 unique CPFs were registered by end-H1-26, up 2.77m or 9.8% on 28.1m a year earlier; growth no longer fast.**

- BNLData Q1-26: ~15.2m CPFs actually bet in the quarter, R$7.6bn (~$1.42bn) gross revenue attributed to 'sports betting', active accounts +11.7%.
- Whether that R$7.6bn is sports-only or total cannot be verified; product split treated as n/a.
- Average spend per bettor rose 5.0% to R$649.60 (~$121) per semester, i.e. ~R$108 (~$20) a month.
- Demographics: 68.5% men; 21.3% aged ≤24, 22.0% aged 25–30, 29.0% aged 31–40, 17.7% aged 41–50, under 3% over 60.

### Exclusion policy

**More than 5m Brazilians were barred from licensed platforms by August 2026, deliberately throttling growth.**

- ~3m Bolsa Família and BPC beneficiaries prohibited from October 2025.
- 1.2m voluntary self-exclusions.
- ~0.8m Novo Desenrola debt-programme participants, with a minimum six-month betting block.
- Regulated addressable base was already ~15% smaller than the registered base and skewed away from the most price-sensitive cohort.

### Q3-26 estimate and run-rate

**Q3-26 GGR is unpublished; Receita allocations imply 9M GGR of ≈R$29–31bn and a ~R$40bn annualised pre-ban market [estimate].**

- R$3.72bn (~$693m) of statutory allocations for Jan–Sep at a 12–13% contribution rate implies 9M GGR of ≈R$29–31bn (~$5.4–5.8bn).
- Q3 run-rate ≈R$3.2–3.5bn (~$0.6–0.65bn) a month, in line with H1; September receipts only mildly dented by the 25 Sep deposit freeze.
- Annualised ~R$40bn (~$7.5bn) GGR would place Brazil among the three or four largest regulated online markets globally.
- From 6 Oct 2026 regulated GGR is zero; the only 'market' is offshore.

### Market maturity

**H1 data show a market at steady state: mid-teens GGR growth, high-single-digit player growth, 87 licensees sharing ~R$3.3bn a month.**

- 95% payout indicates heavy casino and promotional intensity despite the welcome-bonus ban.
- Roughly R$38m (~$7m) of GGR per company per month on average, with the long tail far below that.
- The ban hit before the inevitable consolidation; it will now happen in the dark.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Licensing regime and the MP 1.394 wind-down

*A R$30m, five-year federal authorisation regime built since 2024 was extinguished in 30 days, with no refund, no indemnity and pending applications voided.*

### Pre-ban authorisation regime

**Until 25 Sep 2026 Brazil ran a federal authorisation model: R$30m (~$5.6m) outorga for five years and up to three brands.**

- Law 14.790/2023, administered by SPA/MF; Brazilian-incorporated company, .bet.br domain, SIGAP reporting, technical certification and AML requirements.
- Monthly fiscalisation fee valued by the government's impact statement at ≈R$115.2m (~$21m) a year across the sector.
- States and the Federal District could also authorise fixed-odds within their territory.
- Licensees rose from 78 companies/182 brands in H1-25 to 87/188 in H1-26, with more applications queued; ~15,000 direct employees (ANJL).

### MP 1.394 provisions

**MP 1.394/2026 (25 Sep 2026) bans fixed-odds operation, offer, intermediation and advertising, extinguishing all authorisations after 30 days without refund or compensation.**

- Art. 1 covers physical and virtual forms, including agents abroad offering to persons in Brazil, real sporting events and virtual online games; other lottery modalities untouched.
- Art. 2 extends the ban to state and district concessions; art. 6 bars new authorisations and voids pending applications.
- Art. 4 extinguishes authorisations on 25 Oct 2026 'for reasons of public interest' with no right to full or partial refund of the outorga.
- Repeals Law 14.790 arts. 1–40, 43–48, 54, Chapter V of Law 13.756/2018 and art. 68 of Law 15.421/2026, preserving SPA's sanctioning powers over past conduct.

### Transition schedule

**The wind-down was compressed and payments-centric, with deposits banned from 25 Sep and sites down by 6 Oct.**

- Bettors could withdraw voluntarily until 23:59 on 5 Oct; sites, apps and store listings taken down by 6 Oct on pain of blocking.
- Within two days operators had to prove liquidity for full refunds and send CPF-level balance files to banks and SPA (art. 8); R$200,000 (~$37k) daily fine for non-compliance.
- Banks had seven days (9–14 Oct) to return funds to the account of origin; unreturnable balances swept to a Caixa account from 15 Oct (art. 9).
- Operators remain liable for shortfalls, keep records five years and keep transmitting SIGAP data (arts. 10–11).
- Pending sanction proceedings are suspended and archived if operators comply fully (art. 13) — an amnesty-for-cooperation lever.

### Legal status and timeline

**The MP is temporary, lapsing unless converted within 60 + 60 days; Vixio puts expiry around 4 Mar 2027.**

- 'Urgency' locks the congressional docket from 9 Nov 2026.
- If the MP lapses or is rejected, Law 14.790 revives and Congress must issue a decree on interim acts, including whether revocations stand and re-payment of R$30m.
- 99 amendments filed by late October; conversion with amendments would create a new statute.
- Industry argues before the STF there was no constitutional 'urgency' (market regulated for 21 months) and that revocation without compensation breaches acquired rights; AGU argues plenary Union competence.

### Implications for entrants

**Brazilian licence value has been repriced to near zero for now; credibility of future authorisation is impaired until courts or Congress protect prepaid fees.**

- Operators who paid R$30m in late 2024/2025 have had 21 of 60 months.
- Re-entry should assume re-application and possibly a second fee unless Congress waives it.
- Tighter conditions likely; sports-only is the modal compromise.
- Far smaller competitor set — consolidation among the 87 is now inevitable.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Tax & fiscal: GGR contribution, LC 224/2025 ratchet and the 2026 take

*Receipts were running ~R$1.2bn a month and had beaten FY-25 by September; the Treasury itself books a R$12bn three-year hole from the ban.*

### Tax base and ratchet

**Operators pay a GGR contribution of 13% in 2026, rising to 14% in 2027 and 15% from 2028 under LC 224/2025.**

- Law 14.790/2023 set 12% on GGR with destinations across sport, tourism, public security, education and health, plus IRPJ, CSLL, PIS, COFINS.
- Effective burden on GGR reaches well into the 20s in percentage terms depending on margin structure.
- LC 224/2025 (26 Dec 2025): 12% public policies + 1% social security in 2026, +2% in 2027, +3% from 2028.
- MP 1.303/2025's attempt to go straight to 18% lapsed; the Senate CAE had voted 15%/15%/18% before 13/14/15 prevailed.
- Bettors pay 15% income tax on net annual prizes above the first IRPF exemption band; ComprovaBet statement under IN 2.299/2025.

### 2026 receipts series

**Receita's Jan–Apr 2026 betting-house series showed R$4.586bn (~$854m), +99.1%, but appears to include Caixa lotteries.**

- Monthly: January R$1.49bn (~$277m), February R$1.04bn (~$194m), March R$859m (~$160m), April R$1.189bn (~$221m); prior year R$2.283bn (~$425m).
- Series too large to reconcile with R$6.86bn (~$1.28bn) of fixed-odds-only receipts for nine months.
- Fixed-odds codes (BNLData) rose from R$368.4m (~$69m) in January to R$513m (~$96m) in June, totalling R$2.48bn (~$462m) in H1.
- That matches R$2.49bn (~$464m) of statutory allocations from SPA GGR data (12.4% of GGR); the datasets should not be summed.
- Lottery codes contributed R$4.96bn (~$924m) in H1; numerical-lottery code jumped from R$11.6m in January to R$251.1m in June, attributed to a mid-year rule change (unverified).

### Nine-month total

**Federal betting and lottery receipts reached R$11.116bn (~$2.07bn) for 9M-26, surpassing FY-25's R$9.95bn (~$1.85bn) with a quarter to spare.**

- September R$1.206bn (~$225m), +3.7% on August's R$1.163bn (~$217m) but -7.9% on September 2025's R$1.310bn (~$244m); last normal month.
- Fixed-odds operators generated R$3.72bn (~$693m) in statutory allocations and R$3.14bn (~$585m) in federal taxes, per Receita's Marcelo Gomide.
- BNLData's FY-26 forecast of R$14.6–15.5bn (~$2.7–2.9bn) is now unattainable.
- Receita's Claudemir Malaquias said only the non-fixed-odds lottery stream will continue to report.

### Government fiscal arithmetic

**EM 2114/2026 books tax losses of R$1.541bn, R$5.150bn and R$5.330bn for 2026–28, which we view as understated.**

- Implied run-rate ~R$430m (~$80m) a month versus observed fixed-odds receipts of ~R$760m (~$142m) a month in 9M-26.
- Fiscalisation-fee losses of R$38.4m (~$7.2m) for Sep–Dec 2026 and R$115.2m (~$21m) a year thereafter (R$268.8m / ~$50m over the triennium).
- EM claims no loss on outorgas because fees were paid in full — precisely the point operators contest.
- BNLData estimates ≈R$18.2bn (~$3.39bn) foregone Oct-26 to Dec-27 versus the government's R$6.8bn (~$1.27bn); transfers to beneficiaries will be cut, with 2027–28 adjustments in PLOA 2027.

### Operator read-through

**Any restored regime inherits the LC 224 ratchet plus probably a political premium; 18% remains the government's revealed preference.**

- Fiscal dependency built in 21 months — R$870m (~$162m) to sport in one half-year plus club sponsorships — is the strongest restoration argument for the Centrão and football lobby.
- This is why Vixio and others rate a permanent, total ban the least likely outcome.

## 6. Segment deep-dive — Payments, player protection & advertising

*A Pix-only, CPF-bound, bonus-free regime that was already the strictest in Latin America has been converted into a payments blockade and a total advertising ban.*

### Payments rail

**Brazil's Pix-only, CPF-bound payments architecture made the shutdown executable in 10 days and is now turned against offshore supply.**

- Deposits and withdrawals could only move between a CPF-verified bettor account and an operator account at a BCB-authorised institution via Pix, TED or debit; cards, cash, boletos and crypto prohibited.
- Refund mechanism reversed Pix flows to the CPF-matched account of origin, with Caixa as backstop.
- Art. 14 bars banks, payment institutions and arrangement owners, including instant payments, from processing any fixed-odds transaction and requires rulebook prohibitions.
- Art. 15 directs the BCB to build a real-time rejection and interbank return system for illegal betting funds.
- Art. 28 allows forfeiture of frozen funds of irregular operators to the Union administratively, without prior judicial decision, with proceeds to the National Public Security Fund.

### Player protection

**Exclusions covered ≈3m Bolsa Família/BPC beneficiaries, 1.2m self-excluded and ~0.8m Novo Desenrola participants before the ban.**

- Telehealth gambling-harm service launched March 2026 at 600 consultations a month, expanded via Proadi-SUS with Hospital Sírio-Libanês (contract R$2.94m / ~$548k).
- Funded from the R$24.19m (~$4.5m) health allocation in H1-26.
- Government rationale (EM paras. 3–7): instruments 'have not been capable' of protecting health, consumers, minors and household finances.
- Cites 24/7 availability, instant settlement and targeted digital marketing as drivers.

> “may have been tens of billions of reais” — Government (EM 2114/2026)

### Advertising ban

**MP 1.394 art. 16 bans all fixed-odds communication, publicity, marketing and sponsorship in any medium, with a 10-day removal deadline (5 Oct).**

- Pre-ban framework: SPA Portaria 1.231/2024 restrictions, Conar self-regulation, Law 14.790's welcome-bonus ban, and the STF's November 2024 confirmation of Fux's injunction on child-directed ads.
- Art. 17 grandfathers only incidental pre-existing content.
- Breach is 'abusive advertising' under art. 37 §2 of the Consumer Code, including counter-advertising orders.
- Internet application providers carry a duty of care (art. 19) with notice-and-takedown on request of consumer authorities or the Justice Ministry (art. 20).

### Operator implications

**Implications are structural: payments controls and forfeiture power will remain the enforcement spine even under restoration.**

- Any grey-market strategy carries acute counterparty and asset-seizure risk.
- Bonus ban makes re-acquisition of a player base with zeroed balances expensive and slow.
- Advertising, if it returns, is likely closer to Italy's 'Dignity Decree' than the pre-2026 Brazilian regime.

## 7. Segment deep-dive — Enforcement, illegal market and the political–judicial path

*Offshore supply already held 38–44% before the ban; with Lula re-elected and no injunction in force, the decisive windows are the STF hearing in November and a congressional vote before the March 2027 expiry.*

### Illegal market and enforcement

**Offshore platforms held 38–44% of online betting in H1-26 despite over 25,000 sites blocked; enforcement now targets 100% of demand.**

- SPA reported 25,000+ illegal sites blocked in the first year via Anatel and CGI.br; Vixio cites over 16,000 domains.
- LCA Consultores/Instituto Locomotiva study for IBJR: 38–44% unlicensed share in H1-26.
- ANJL's Legitbet monitor claims 34,000+ clandestine sites since the MP; demand of ~R$3.3bn (~$620m) GGR a month.
- Choke-point moves from DNS to payments and platforms, with fines up to 10% of Brazilian group revenue capped at R$50m (~$9.3m) per infraction.
- New Casa Civil-chaired Interinstitutional Committee with mandatory data-sharing among Federal Police, Receita and COAF.

### Judicial track

**No injunction is in force; ADIs 8.024, 8.027 and 8.028 sit with Justice Fux, who convened a 26-participant hearing instead of ruling.**

- Fux signalled he would rule only after the 25 Oct runoff.
- AGU defends on Union competence over lotteries; sector argues no constitutional urgency and breach of acquired rights.
- 26 Oct survey: 19 actions in TRF-1 and TRF-3, three denied, one granted then suspended within 24 hours (Stake, 9 Oct) to avoid 'market distortion'.
- Any reopening before 2027 requires a market-wide STF injunction, ideally ex tunc to unwind the 25 Oct revocations.

### Political track

**Lula's runoff win over Flávio Bolsonaro on 25 Oct makes the MP's author no lame duck; the track is less favourable than Vixio assumed.**

- SRI minister José Guimarães named the MP a top-three priority on 27–28 Oct, aiming for a vote before a more conservative Congress sits in February 2027.
- No joint committee installed a month after publication; 99 amendments tabled.
- Centrão, clubs and states face losses: R$870m (~$162m) to sport in H1-26, ~R$760m (~$142m) monthly federal receipts, Série A sponsorships.
- Bolsonaro camp's position — restore sports betting, keep online casino banned — offers a ready compromise text.

### Scenario weighting

**Sports-only conversion is the single most likely outcome at roughly 40–45% (judgement, not modelled).**

- Lapse or rejection with Law 14.790 reviving and a reinstatement decree: 25–30%.
- Permanent total ban: 15–20%; STF injunction restoring the full market before Congress acts: 10–15%.
- In every scenario except the injunction, no licensed revenue is possible before Q1-27.
- Sports-only TAM would be materially smaller than the ~R$40bn (~$7.5bn) run-rate because the ~95% payout indicates online games carried most GGR.
- Regulated operator count will be far below 87 on the other side.

## 8. Segment deep-dive — Lotteries, retail & land-based

*Caixa's lottery monopoly is untouched and is now the only reporting gambling tax stream; land-based casinos remain unlegislated.*

### Caixa lotteries

**Caixa's lottery monopoly is untouched by the MP and becomes the only reporting gambling tax stream.**

- Art. 1 §2 excludes 'other lottery modalities authorised by law'; Mega-Sena, Lotofácil, Quina, instant tickets and sports pools continue, as do authorised state lotteries.
- State fixed-odds concessions are extinguished under art. 2.
- Lotteries contributed R$4.96bn (~$924m) in H1-26, two-thirds of the R$7.44bn (~$1.39bn) captured by betting-and-lottery codes; numerical lotteries 77.2% of the block.
- Caixa's 2026 sales and prize data were not retrieved and are n/a.
- From October 2026 the 'apostas e jogos de azar' line will be a lottery series and should not be compared with 2025–26 totals.

### Retail and land-based

**Retail is Caixa's ~13,000 lottery agencies with no fixed-odds product; the land-based casino bill PL 2.234/2022 saw no 2026 vote.**

- Agency count not re-verified; no licensed sportsbook estate, so the regulated market was effectively online-only and could be shut via payments and domain blocking.
- The MP's political climate makes passage of PL 2.234/2022 less likely near term.
- The ban has revived the Paulo Guedes-era argument for integrated-resort casinos as a 'controlled' alternative.

### B2Tech implications

**The lottery segment is closed to B2Tech and land-based is a 2028+ question at the earliest.**

- Federal monopoly; state lotteries procure through concessions, largely as iLottery/instant products.
- Brazil has no omnichannel hedge, so a Brazilian strategy is entirely a bet on the online framework being restored.

## 9. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Proven demand at scale: ~R$40bn (~$7.5bn) annualised GGR, 30.9m registered bettors, ≈R$398bn (~$74bn) of H1 stakes and 87 licensees prove Brazil is a top-tier online market the moment legal supply returns — demand is not the risk, channel is. _(structural)_
- Automatic reversion path: if Congress neither converts nor rejects MP 1.394 by ≈4 Mar 2027, Law 14.790 revives by operation of the Constitution; a reinstatement decree waiving re-application and the R$30m (~$5.6m) fee is the industry's stated ask and has precedent in Brazilian MP practice. _(timing)_
- Sports-only compromise is the modal outcome and would hand surviving sportsbook operators a far less crowded market: the ~95% blended payout implies online games carried most GGR, so a sports-only regime shrinks the TAM but also removes casino-led competitors and lowers regulatory temperature. _(structural)_
- Fiscal dependency created in 21 months is the restoration lobby's best weapon: R$11.1bn (~$2.07bn) of 9M receipts, R$870m (~$162m) to sport in one half, club sponsorships and the Treasury's own R$12bn (~$2.2bn) three-year loss estimate all argue against a permanent total ban. _(structural)_
- Forced consolidation creates entry-by-acquisition options: ~51% of sector jobs already cut, balances zeroed and 87 licence-holders with 39 months of pre-paid term in limbo mean brands, Brazilian corporate shells and local teams can be acquired at distressed prices ahead of any reopening. _(one-off)_
- B2B positioning is lower-risk than B2C: suppliers (e.g., Evolution signalling readiness to reopen its Brazil studio) carry no outorga risk and can re-enter on day one of restoration; a platform/content partnership with a tier-one survivor preserves optionality without capital at risk. _(early)_

**Lowlights**

- Expropriation without compensation is now precedent: MP art. 4 revokes five-year authorisations after 21 months 'in the public interest' with no refund of the R$30m (~$5.6m) fee and no indemnity; until the STF rules otherwise, Brazilian licence value carries sovereign-risk discounting no regulated peer market requires. _(governance)_
- The author of the ban won: Lula's re-election on 25 Oct and the government's decision to prioritise conversion of the MP before Congress turns more conservative in Feb-27 remove the 'lame-duck lapse' scenario; a permanent ban on online games — Bolsonaro's position too — is a live, cross-party outcome. _(structural)_
- No judicial relief in force: 19 federal actions, zero injunctions, one granted and dissolved within 24 hours; Fux has opted for a public hearing rather than a ruling. Legal clarity realistically arrives Q1–Q2 2027, implying 5–9 months of zero regulated revenue. _(timing)_
- Tax ratchet is locked in regardless of outcome: LC 224/2025 takes the GGR contribution to 14% in 2027 and 15% in 2028 on top of IRPJ/CSLL/PIS/COFINS, and the government's revealed preference (MP 1.303/2025) was 18%; a restored market would face a higher effective rate than the one that produced 2026's numbers. _(structural)_
- Black market is now the whole market: 38–44% of online betting was already offshore in H1-26 despite >25,000 blocked domains; Legitbet counts >34,000 clandestine sites since the MP. Re-channelising players who have migrated, with the welcome-bonus ban intact and balances emptied, will be slower and costlier than the 2025 launch. _(structural)_
- Payments-rail weaponisation and administrative forfeiture: banks, PSPs and Pix arrangement owners are barred from processing any fixed-odds flow, the BCB must build real-time rejection, and funds in blocked accounts can be forfeited to the Union without a prior court order — grey-market operation in Brazil now carries asset-seizure risk, not just blocking risk. _(governance)_
- Data opacity: SPA publishes no regular statistics; market size relies on LAI requests relayed by trade press, two SPA-sourced H1 figures differ by 1% and the product split is unpublished — any Brazilian business case rests on second-hand numbers. _(watch)_

## 10. Forecast & Outlook

The bridge from a pre-ban forecast to today runs through a single legislative act. Entering 2026 the regulated market was tracking FY-25 GGR of R$36.96bn (~$6.88bn) toward ~R$40bn (~$7.5bn) on H1-26's +15.3% growth, with BNLData projecting R$14.6–15.5bn (~$2.7–2.9bn) of FY-26 betting-and-lottery receipts and LC 224/2025 lifting the GGR contribution to 13%. MP 1.394/2026 drove regulated GGR to zero from 6 Oct, extinguished all 87 authorisations on 25 Oct and, by the government's own account, removes R$1.541bn (~$287m) of 2026 and R$5.150bn (~$959m) of 2027 tax revenue — figures we and BNLData regard as understating the observed ~R$760m (~$142m) monthly fixed-odds take by roughly half. The moving parts for the next six months are the STF (Fux hearing, November), the congressional calendar (urgency 9 Nov; government push for a December vote; expiry ≈4 Mar 2027) and the content of the 99 amendments, of which the sports-only variant is the pivot.

- **FY-25 regulated GGR (actual):** R$36.96bn (~$6.88bn) (First regulated year)
- **H1-26 GGR / pre-ban FY-26 run-rate:** R$20.07bn (~$3.74bn) / ≈R$40bn (~$7.5bn) [est.] (+15.3% YoY)
- **Post-MP Q4-26 regulated GGR:** ≈R$0 (Official tax loss -R$1.54bn (~$287m) 2026)
- **2027 regulated GGR (scenario range) [est.]:** R$0 – R$40bn+; modal sports-only case ≈R$12–18bn (~$2.2–3.4bn) (vs official 2027 tax loss -R$5.15bn (~$959m))

Our base case is that Brazil does **not** return to its pre-ban shape within the next 12–18 months. We assign roughly 40–45% to Congress converting MP 1.394 with amendments that **restore sports betting and permanently prohibit online games**, most likely voted in the December 2026 window or carried into the new legislature with a similar text; 25–30% to the measure lapsing or being rejected around March 2027 with Law 14.790 reviving and a reinstatement decree; 15–20% to a permanent total ban; and 10–15% to an STF injunction restoring the full market first. In the modal case, 2027 regulated GGR would be an estimated **R$12–18bn (~$2.2–3.4bn)** — our judgement that sports carried roughly 30–45% of pre-ban GGR given the ~95% blended payout, with a 2027 restart lag and re-acquisition friction — taxed at **14% of GGR plus corporate taxes**, with advertising closer to a near-total ban than to the 2025 regime and the payments-rail enforcement machinery kept in place against offshore supply. Operator numbers would fall from 87 toward 20–30 well-capitalised sportsbooks. Leverage on the sector is unambiguously negative: zero revenue, continuing SIGAP and refund obligations, severed sponsorships and litigation costs through at least Q1-27 favour tier-one balance sheets. For B2Tech the recommendation is **no B2C capital commitment before the STF ruling and the congressional text are known**, active monitoring of distressed-asset opportunities, and a B2B/supplier-side readiness plan that can be switched on within one quarter of any reopening. The swing factors are Fux (a market-wide ex tunc injunction would reset everything), whether the Centrão trades casino for sportsbook, and whether the government's December push succeeds before the Congress elected on 4 Oct 2026 takes its seats.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- STF: outcome of Justice Fux's public hearing (26 participants) and any plenary ruling on ADIs 8.024/8.027/8.028 — specifically whether relief is market-wide and ex tunc (unwinding the 25 Oct revocations) or prospective only.
- Congress: installation of the joint committee, the rapporteur's text among 99 amendments (watch for a sports-only carve-out and a licence-reinstatement/fee-waiver clause), 9 Nov urgency lock, December floor vote vs deferral to February 2027, and the ≈4 Mar 2027 expiry.
- BCB regulation under MP arts. 14–15 (Pix rejection/return protocol) and the Casa Civil Interinstitutional Committee's first enforcement actions — the template for post-restoration offshore suppression.
- Receita Federal monthly 'apostas e jogos de azar' line from October 2026: how far it falls toward a lottery-only ~R$0.5bn (~$93m) a month, and the PLOA 2027 revisions to beneficiary transfers — the fiscal pain gauge for Congress.
- Black-market indicators: Legitbet clandestine-site count, IBJR/LCA channelisation updates, and app-store/social-platform enforcement cases under arts. 19–22.
- Industry structure: licensee insolvencies, brand/shell disposals, ANJL/IBJR employment data, and whether tier-one operators (and suppliers such as Evolution) maintain Brazilian entities and staff through the blackout.
- Tax trajectory: LC 224/2025 step to 14% on 1 Jan 2027 and any revival of the 18% proposal in the conversion text or the 2027 budget.
