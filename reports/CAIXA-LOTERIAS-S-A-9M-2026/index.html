9M 2026 · results · analyst report

CAIXA Loterias S.A.

Released 12 Nov 2026 (Caixa Econômica Federal 3T26 financial statements / Relatório da Administração; trade-press pick-up 13–17 Nov 2026) · Currency BRL (R$) — tables in R$ millions unless stated; USD equivalents in parentheses at ~5.78 BRL/USD · Data confidence: MEDIUM

Market reaction: Not applicable — CAIXA Loterias S.A. is an unlisted, closed-capital wholly-owned subsidiary of Caixa Econômica Federal, which is itself 100% owned by the Federal Union; no traded equity or bonds of the subsidiary were identified. The listed sister company Caixa Seguridade (B3: CXSE3, insurance) does not consolidate the lottery line and is unaffected.

The one-line verdict

A record but visibly decelerating nine months: federal lottery sales of R$18.71bn (~$3.24bn), +3.9% YoY, with Q3 up only +1.3% to R$6.51bn (~$1.13bn) as the July-2025 average ticket repricing (+21.7%) lapped — nominal growth is now below any plausible inflation print, the operating-cost ratio rose 240bps to 21.1% of sales, and the retail network shrank again. Social transfers of R$7.10bn (~$1.23bn, +3.4%) and a 48% public take of R$8.97bn (~$1.55bn) are the structural positives; the parent quietly dropped lotteries from its headline results package this quarter, which tells you how management reads the trend.

1Executive summary

  • Entity and listing status clarified. CAIXA Loterias S.A. is an unlisted, closed-capital (capital fechado) sociedade anônima and a wholly-owned subsidiary of Caixa Econômica Federal (itself 100% federally owned); it has no traded equity, so there is no share-price reaction, EPS or consensus to benchmark against. Fiscal year is the calendar year (3T26 = Jul–Sep 2026); 9M = Jan–Sep 2026. Caixa's 3T25 management report describes its object as operating lotteries, bets, games and similar services under the federal concession.
  • Headline: record 9M sales, but the slowest quarter of the year. Lottery sales (arrecadação) reached R$18,706m (~$3,236m) in 9M-26, +3.9% YoY vs R$17,998m (~$3,114m); Q3-26 was R$6,514m (~$1,127m), +1.3% YoY vs R$6,429m (~$1,112m) and +4.6% QoQ vs R$6,225m (~$1,077m). All four outlets we read (Gaming Intelligence, BNLData, Sigma World, Focus Gaming News) relay the same figures from Caixa's 3T26 Relatório da Administração, and Q1+Q2+Q3 (5,967+6,225+6,514) reconciles to the 9M total within rounding.
  • Growth has decelerated every quarter: +8.4% (Q1) → +2.6% (Q2) → +1.3% (Q3). The pattern maps exactly onto the July-2025 repricing, when the 12 games took an average ticket-price increase of 21.7%. Q1/Q2-26 were still comparing against pre-hike quarters; Q3-26 is the first clean comparison and shows that underlying growth ex-price is roughly nil in nominal terms. BNLData explicitly notes the variations are nominal and not inflation-adjusted — in real terms Q3 volumes are contracting.
  • Price, not volume, carried 2026. With sales up +8.4% in Q1 against a +21.7% price lift, implied bet volumes fell double-digit in H1; the Q3 +1.3% print therefore reflects a lower-volume, higher-ticket base. Mega-Sena rollover sequences (two consecutive draws in late Feb/early Mar alone generated ~R$247.6m, ~$43m) flattered Q1 — jackpot luck, not structural demand, is the swing factor each quarter.
  • Statutory public take rose ~100bps to ~48% of sales. Transfers to legal beneficiaries were R$7,096m (~$1,228m) in 9M-26 (+3.4%; 37.9% of sales) and R$2,481m (~$429m) in Q3 (+1.6% YoY, +5.0% QoQ). Add income tax withheld on prizes of R$1,874m (~$324m, +17.4%) and the federal lotteries directed R$8,970m (~$1,552m) to taxes and public purposes in 9M-26 — c.48.0% of sales vs an estimated 47.0% a year earlier, with the delta entirely from prize tax.
  • Where the Q3 money went. Social security (Seguridade Social) R$1,100m (~$190m, ~44% of transfers), public security (FNSP) R$628m (~$109m), sport R$471m (~$81m, −0.8% YoY), culture R$185m (~$32m), education R$92m (~$16m). For 9M, sport received R$1,361m (~$235m, +4.9%) — the line Caixa chose to highlight — while education fell 11.0% to R$260m (~$45m) from R$292m (~$51m), the largest decline of any beneficiary.
  • Prizes: 9M R$6,331m (~$1,095m), +4.0%, a stable 33.8% of sales. In Q3 the net prize line fell 5.7% QoQ and 3.3% YoY; Caixa cautions the figure follows payment date and can include prior-quarter bets, so the quarterly move is timing noise rather than a payout-ratio change.
  • Cost creep is the lowlight management is not talking about. Custeio e manutenção — which Caixa says includes the Lottery Development Fund (FDL) and remuneration of the lotérica retail network — rose 22.8% QoQ and 14.2% YoY in Q3, lifting its share of sales from 18.7% to 21.1% (we estimate ~R$1,374m, ~$238m, vs ~R$1,202m, ~$208m, in 3T25). On a +1.3% sales print, a +14% cost line compresses whatever residual accrues to Caixa/the subsidiary.
  • Distribution footprint keeps contracting. Lotéricas numbered 12,917 at end-September, −21 QoQ and −137 YoY (−1.0%); non-betting banking transactions through the network fell 9.8% YoY to 387m in Q3. The lottery business depends on this channel's economics; a shrinking, less-trafficked network is a structural headwind unless digital mix (not disclosed in the material we could access) is filling the gap.
  • Management is burying the line. BNLData notes that, unlike 3T25 when lotteries were a highlighted result, Caixa's 3T26 press release, results presentation and performance-analysis report make no mention of them; the data appear only in the Relatório da Administração. The parent's own recurring net income was R$3,486m (~$603m), −7.4% YoY and −10.6% QoQ, with NPLs at 4.01% — attention is elsewhere.
  • No guidance, no cost-transformation programme, no capital-return framework. Neither Caixa Loterias nor Caixa issues lottery sales guidance; no restructuring or efficiency programme with amounts/target years is disclosed; as a wholly-owned statutory operator there are no buybacks or dividends to third parties. The only 'programme' is the July-2025 repricing, whose stated aims (3T25 report) were higher sales/social transfers and product sustainability — the volume response says the elasticity was higher than hoped.
  • The 'cannibalisation' thesis is not supported by Caixa's own numbers. With the regulated online betting market in full operation since Jan-2025, federal lottery sales hit a record R$26.61bn (~$4.60bn) in FY25 (vs R$25.91bn, ~$4.48bn, in 2024 and R$23.4bn, ~$4.05bn, in 2023) and are +3.9% in 9M-26, while federal bets tax receipts reached R$11.12bn (~$1.92bn) in Jan–Sep 2026, already 11.7% above all of 2025. That said, real-terms lottery stagnation post-repricing leaves room for both narratives.
  • Regulatory overhang is political, not statutory. BNLData reports a bill criminalising bets is blocking the Chamber of Deputies' agenda amid government pressure, with Caixa joining the public commentary. A rollback of regulated betting would be a relative positive for the lottery monopoly; the more immediate uncertainty is Caixa's own stated intent (President Carlos Vieira, earlier in 2026) to enter sports betting/online gaming with a ~R$7bn (~$1.2bn) 2026 ambition — status [unverified] in the material we could access.
  • Q1 seasonality framed the year. Q1-26 sales of R$5,967m (~$1,032m) were −30.7% vs Q4-25 (implied R$8,612m, ~$1,490m), underscoring how Mega da Virada makes Q4 the swing quarter; FY26 will be decided by the December jackpot cycle, not by Q3.
  • Product concentration remains extreme. Mega-Sena (~40%) and Lotofácil (~35%) accounted for 74.8% of Q1-26 sales (72.2% in 3T25, when the pair grew +25.3% on the price hike); no 9M-26 product split was available in the sources we could read. Two games with rollover-driven volatility define the P&L.
  • Responsible-gaming credentials maintained. Caixa Loterias holds WLA Responsible Gaming Level 3 certification and submitted its recertification application during Q3-26 — relevant for any future digital/betting expansion under the federal framework.
  • Disclosure gap: the subsidiary's own P&L is not in the public narrative. Caixa Loterias publishes quarterly financial statements (e.g. 30-Jun-2025 DFs) but its 3T26 revenue, net income, cash and debt could not be verified here; the arrecadação figures are gross consumer stakes, not the subsidiary's revenue. Readers should treat 'sales' as GGR-equivalent volume, not operator revenue.
  • Thesis read-through. For sector investors this is a clean data point on (i) lottery price elasticity in an emerging market — a +21.7% price move bought roughly one year of nominal growth and is now behind the business; (ii) the absence of measurable cannibalisation from regulated bets at the aggregate level; and (iii) rising statutory/channel cost share, which matters for anyone modelling a future Caixa betting entry or a Lotex/instant-lottery relaunch on the same retail backbone.

3Segment deep-dive: Federal lotteries — sales & product mix (sole operating segment)

Record 9M of R$18.71bn (~$3.24bn), +3.9%, but Q3 +1.3% as the July-2025 price hike laps; underlying volume is flat-to-down in nominal terms and negative in real terms.

Q3 and 9M sales

Record 9M stakes of R$18,706m (~$3,236m), +3.9%, but Q3-26 grew only 1.3% YoY to R$6,514m (~$1,127m) as the July-2025 price hike laps.

  • Q3-26 stakes R$6,514m (~$1,127m), 4.6% above Q2's R$6,225m (~$1,077m) and 1.3% above 3T25's R$6,429m (~$1,112m).
  • 9M-26 total R$18,706m (~$3,236m) versus R$17,998m (~$3,114m) a year earlier, a third consecutive record nine months.
  • Extends FY25 record of R$26.61bn (~$4.60bn), after R$25.91bn (~$4.48bn) in 2024 and R$23.4bn (~$4.05bn) in 2023.

Price versus volume

A 21.7% average ticket-price increase in July 2025 implies underlying bet volumes down well into double digits; Q3 confirms the base has reset lower.

  • Q1-26 +8.4% to R$5,967m (~$1,032m) and Q2-26 +2.6% were still comparing against unrepriced quarters.
  • Q3-26 is the first clean comparison at +1.3% nominal, a decline in real terms at any plausible IPCA print.
  • BNLData notes none of these variations is inflation-adjusted.

Product mix

Mega-Sena (~40%) and Lotofácil (~35%) took 74.8% of Q1-26 sales; no Q3 or 9M product split was disclosed, and tail games appear to be leaking volume.

  • Flagship pair share rose from 72.2% in 3T25, when they grew a combined 25.3% on repricing.
  • Tail games (Quina, Lotomania, Dupla Sena, Timemania, Dia de Sorte, Super Sete, +Milionária, Loteca, Loteria Federal) split the remaining quarter; Dupla Sena and Timemania cited at R$620.7m (~$107m) in 3T25 (period basis unclear).
  • Caixa attributed the 'positive' Q1 result to accumulated draws; concursos 2978 and 2979 alone took R$123.6m and R$124m (~R$247.6m, ~$43m).
  • Sport transfers, keyed to specific games, fell 0.8% YoY in Q3, suggesting the tail games are where volume is leaking.

Seasonality and disclosure

Price-driven growth is exhausted, volume soft, with jackpot cycles providing ±3–5 percentage points of quarterly noise; no customer-economics disclosure exists.

  • Q1-26 was 30.7% below Q4-25 (implied R$8,612m, ~$1,490m) because the Mega da Virada makes Q4 the structural peak.
  • Q3's +4.6% sequential gain is the normal winter uplift plus rollover timing, not re-acceleration.
  • No disclosure of actives, ARPU, online penetration or marketing spend, a gap worth noting for any future Caixa betting vehicle.

Guidance and outlook

No segment guidance; we estimate FY26 sales of roughly R$27.3–27.6bn (~$4.72–4.77bn, +2.6% to +3.7%), entirely dependent on the December jackpot cycle.

  • Caixa dropped lotteries from the 3T26 press release, results presentation and performance-analysis report, leaving numbers only in the Relatório da Administração.
  • 2025 repricing objectives of raising stakes and social transfers were met in nominal terms; protecting 'sustainability, continuity and attractiveness' arguably failed.
  • A weak Mega da Virada and further step-down in tail games would push FY growth below 2%.

4Segment deep-dive: Statutory proceeds waterfall — social transfers, prize tax and operating cost

Public take ~48% of 9M sales (R$8.97bn, ~$1.55bn), up ~100bps on prize tax; custeio & manutenção jumped to 21.1% of Q3 sales (+240bps), the quarter's real negative.

Social transfers

Transfers of R$2,481m (~$429m) in Q3-26 and R$7,096m (~$1,228m) for 9M (+3.4%) track sales at a statutory ratio of ~38%.

  • Q3 transfers +5.0% QoQ and +1.6% YoY; ratio 38.1% of stakes in Q3 and 37.9% for 9M, within 20bps of prior year.
  • Q3 split: social security R$1,100m (~$190m, ~44%), public security R$628m (~$109m), sport R$471m (~$81m), culture R$185m (~$32m), education R$92m (~$16m).
  • Caixa highlighted sport's 9M receipt of R$1,361m (~$235m), +4.9%, but not sport's Q3 decline of 0.8% YoY.
  • Education saw the year's largest decline, −11.0% to R$260m (~$45m) from R$292m (~$51m).

Prize tax

Prize income tax reached R$1,874m (~$324m) in 9M-26, +17.4%, but Q3's R$504m (~$87m) implies a ~9.5% YoY decline.

  • H1 prize tax was R$1,370m (~$237m, +31.8%); we estimate 3T25 at ~R$557m (~$96m).
  • Q3 decline is consistent with the −3.3% drop in net prizes paid and Caixa's caveat that prizes are booked on payment date.
  • Transfers plus prize tax totalled R$8,970m (~$1,552m) in 9M-26, 48.0% of stakes versus an estimated 47.0% (R$8,459m, ~$1,463m); the ~100bps increase came entirely from tax.
  • Focus Gaming News' 'R$10bn to social programmes (+45.7%)' is irreconcilable with BNLData and Sigma World's R$7,096m, so we use the lower number.

Custeio e manutenção

Custeio e manutenção rose 22.8% QoQ and 14.2% YoY in Q3, lifting its share of stakes from 18.7% to 21.1%, the quarter's real negative.

  • Roughly R$1,374m (~$238m) in Q3 versus ~R$1,202m (~$208m) in 3T25 and ~R$1,119m (~$194m, ~18.0% of sales) in Q2-26.
  • Line includes the Lottery Development Fund (FDL) and remuneration of the lotérica network; no explanation was offered.
  • Candidates: higher retailer commission or incentives, higher FDL contributions, or a reclassification.
  • A cost line growing 14% against sales growing 1% is incompatible with a stable operator margin, before any betting-entry revenue.
  • Net prizes of R$6,331m (~$1,095m) in 9M were a stable 33.8% of stakes, so the prize ratio is not the offset.

Residual margin

Stacking 9M lines leaves a residual of roughly R$3.4bn (~$0.59bn, ~18.2% of stakes, estimate) versus ~19.2% a year earlier, moving the wrong way.

  • 9M lines: prizes 33.8%, transfers 37.9%, prize tax 10.0%; residual covers custeio, FDL and whatever accrues to Caixa Loterias and parent.
  • Given the Q3 custeio ratio of 21.1%, the exit-rate residual is thinner still.
  • No P&L disclosed; no cost-transformation programme, target ratio or guidance.
  • Only visible management action is a WLA Responsible Gaming Level 3 recertification application in Q3, with no economic bearing.

5Segment deep-dive: Distribution network — lotéricas, channels and responsible gaming

12,917 lotéricas at Sep-26 (−137 YoY, −21 QoQ) and non-betting footfall −9.8%: the physical backbone is eroding while channel-cost share rises.

Network size

12,917 lotéricas at Sep-26, −21 QoQ and −137 YoY (−1.0% from 13,054), with non-betting footfall down 9.8%.

  • Non-betting banking transactions through lotéricas fell 9.8% YoY to 387m in Q3; agents' economics rest on both revenue streams.
  • Stakes per lotérica (annualised Q3, estimate) ~R$2.02m (~$349k), up ~2.4% YoY.
  • Productivity per door improved only because doors closed, not because demand rose.

Channel cost and digital

The operator is paying more per real of stakes to hold a shrinking footprint, with no disclosed digital offset.

  • Lotérica remuneration sits inside custeio e manutenção, which rose 14.2% YoY against a 1.3% sales increase.
  • Whether commission rates rose to arrest attrition or FDL spending on terminals/systems drove the increase is not disclosed.
  • No online share, actives or app metrics for Loterias Online / Loterias CAIXA app appeared in any source.
  • Listed European lottery operators typically report online shares of 30–50% of draw-based sales; the absence here is informative.

Elections and responsible gaming

The 25 October 2026 presidential second round shifts a handful of concursos but should not move Q4 materially; WLA Level 3 recertification was submitted in Q3.

  • Altered draw schedule shifts concursos between weeks only.
  • WLA Responsible Gaming Level 3 is table stakes and a prerequisite for any Caixa-branded online betting or instant-win expansion.

Risk and watchpoints

The network is a slow-burn structural negative that repricing temporarily masked, risking a feedback loop of closures and rising custeio.

  • Real-terms stakes declining, non-betting footfall falling at a high-single-digit rate, and no disclosed digital offset.
  • Weaker agent economics could drive closures that weigh on stakes, keeping the custeio ratio rising.
  • Watch the lotérica count in the FY26 report and any online-share disclosure; a figure above ~15–20% would change our read.

6Highlights & lowlights

▲ Highlights

  • Record 9M stakes of R$18,706m (~$3,236m), +3.9% YoY, and a third straight record nine months — delivered with Brazil's regulated online betting market fully operational, undermining the cannibalisation thesis at the aggregate level. structural
  • Statutory public take rose to ~48.0% of stakes: R$8,970m (~$1,552m) to transfers and prize tax in 9M-26, with transfers to legal beneficiaries R$7,096m (~$1,228m, +3.4%) and sport funding R$1,361m (~$235m, +4.9%). structural

Our central case (+1.5% Q4, FY26 ≈ R$27.4bn, ~$4.74bn) assumes volumes stay flat in real terms; a weak jackpot cycle pushes FY growth below 2% and would make 2026 the first real-terms decline since the 2025 Q1 trough (−10%). Margin/cost: the key unknown is whether the Q3 custeio ratio of 21.1% is a step-change (commission or FDL reset) or a timing spike; we assume partial normalisation to ~19.5–20% in Q4 but flag that a sustained 21% ratio removes ~R$150–200m (~$26–35m) per quarter from the operator residual versus the 18.7% of 3T25. Prize ratio ~33.8% and transfer ratio ~38% are statutory and will not move. Leverage/capital: not applicable — no third-party debt or capital-return framework identified; the subsidiary is funded within the Caixa group [unverified]. Swing factors: (i) December jackpot luck; (ii) any further ticket repricing, which we would now view as volume-destructive; (iii) the bets-criminalisation bill — a ban would be a relative positive for the lottery monopoly but signals policy instability; (iv) a formal launch of Caixa's own betting product, which would re-rate the strategic value of the lotérica/app distribution but also compete for the same wallet; (v) lotérica attrition accelerating beyond ~1%/yr. Net: modest nominal growth, flat-to-negative real growth, rising channel cost — a mature cash franchise for the Treasury, not a growth story.
