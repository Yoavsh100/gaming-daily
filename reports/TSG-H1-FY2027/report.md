# Tsogo Sun Limited (TSG) — H1 FY2027 Analyst Report
** · Reporting currency: ZAR (South African rand); tables in R millions with ~USD equivalents at USD/ZAR 17.55 · Results released 25 Nov 2026 (SENS 07:05 SAST)**
**Market reaction:** Shares were up roughly 1% by late afternoon on 25 Nov 2026 (News24); the exact closing level could not be verified — the stock traded in a ~R6.95–R7.25 range through October–November and the company's post-period buybacks were executed at R6.70. Year-to-date the shares are -8%, underperforming Sun International (+22%) and Southern Sun (+8%).

> A buyback-flattered print: HEPS +7% to 79c (~4.5 USc) masks headline earnings -2% to R757m (~$43m), income -2% to R5.43bn (~$309m) and adj. EBITDA -4% to R1.65bn (~$94m) as slot-machine GGR keeps leaking to online casino-style betting and the Gold Reef City strike cost ~R59m (~$3.4m). The balance sheet is the good news — net debt -12% YoY to R5.99bn (~$341m), 1.81x covenant leverage hits the <1.8x target — and online betting (NGR +43%, 26% margin) is finally real, but management's warning that October and November were "particularly tough" tells you H2 will not rescue the year.

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Soft, and softer than the headline suggests.** Six months to 30 Sept 2026 (H1 FY27; FYE 31 March; JSE: TSG): total income -2% to R5.43bn (~$309m), adj. EBITDA -4% to R1.65bn (~$94m) at a 30.3% margin (-60bp YoY, -110bp vs H2 FY26's 31.4%). There is no meaningful sell-side consensus for this small cap; domestic press characterised the result as poor in a tough market, with HEPS growth only possible because of buybacks (News24, quoting JustOne Lap's Simon Brown).
- **HEPS +7% to 79c (~4.5 USc) is a denominator story.** Headline earnings fell 2% to R757m (~$43m) and IFRS attributable profit fell 7% to R630m (~$36m); the weighted share count dropped 7.6% to 961m after 62m shares were cancelled in FY26 and a further 10m (R67m/~$3.8m at R6.70) in H1 FY27. Basic/diluted IFRS EPS was 66c (~3.8 USc) vs 65c.
- **R70m (~$4.0m) of 'specific events' at EBITDA level, R67m at headline level.** Management's own bridge: industrial action/intimidation at Gold Reef City -R43m, once-off labour cost -R16m, lower dividends received -R8m (City Lodge stake sold), historical VAT/interest -R3m; plus -R11m at headline level from the casino sale. Adding the R70m back gives ~R1.72bn (~$98m), i.e. flat YoY (our estimate) — the underlying business is treading water, not growing.
- **The division driving the decline is Casinos & Hotels (73% of income, 86% of EBITDA).** Divisional income -3% to R3.99bn (~$227m), adj. EBITDA -7% to R1.41bn (~$80m), margin -150bp to 35.4%. Management presents it as -6% 'excluding the Theme Park, which was down 37%'. The root cause is slot-machine win: group net gaming win -3.4% to R4.45bn (~$253m) while rooms revenue grew 11% to R312m (~$18m) and F&B was flat at R331m (~$19m).
- **Gauteng is where it hurt.** Gauteng income -3% to R2.33bn (~$133m), EBITDA -10% to R835m (~$48m), margin -270bp to 35.9%. The THORN-union strike at Gold Reef City Casino & Theme Park (from late April 2026, into a second month by late May; theme park closed, casino partially affected) cost an estimated R43m directly plus R16m once-off labour; even adding both back, Gauteng EBITDA is still ~-3% (estimate). The GRC casino-floor upgrade completed in November 2026; the 5D cinema lands ~June 2027.
- **Impairments R158m (~$9m), of which R150m (~$8.5m) is the Gold Reef City casino licence** (recoverable amount R2.4bn/~$137m) — the second consecutive GRC write-down after R144m at March 2026. Sensitivity disclosure: a 1pp higher discount rate would trigger a further R273m (~$16m) GRC impairment; a 1pp lower trading assumption, R174m. Blackrock goodwill took another R10m (R73m in FY26).
- **KwaZulu-Natal (Suncoast) was the only region that grew**: income +1.6% to R1.08bn (~$62m), EBITDA +3.7% to R423m (~$24m), margin +80bp to 39.0% — a recovery after FY26's -9% EBITDA. Western Cape was the worst: income -8% to R258m (~$15m), EBITDA -17% to R81m (~$4.6m), margin -360bp.
- **Online betting (playTSOGO, bet.co.za) is now a genuine contributor**: NGR +43% to R193m (~$11m), adj. EBITDA R50m (~$2.8m) at a 26% margin vs R13m at 10%; the business has been profitable every month since August 2025 and now runs ~R8m/month. Sequentially, though, NGR grew only +8% vs the derived H2 FY26 R178m (~$10m) after +32% the prior half — momentum has decelerated, and no handle, actives, ARPU or marketing data are disclosed.
- **LPMs (VSlots) held flat in a shrinking market**: income R989m (~$56m, -0.4%), EBITDA R282m (~$16m, -0.4%), margin steady at 28.5%, 17.1% of group EBITDA (16.5%). Peer Sun Slots' EBITDA fell 8.1% in H1 CY26, so Tsogo's route business is outperforming. Growth hinges on the NGB finally adjusting the R5 max bet / R500 max payout unchanged since 1996 — a new National Gambling Board was appointed in October 2026, the first in over a decade.
- **No formal earnings guidance; the quantified guideposts moved as follows.** FY27 capex trimmed to ~R1.1bn (~$63m) from R1.2bn in May; the medium-term leverage target (<1.8x) is 'effectively achieved' at 1.81x; the Somerset West relocation timeline compressed to 'approximately a year and a half' from 'approximately two years'; and the qualitative outlook deteriorated — 'October and November have been particularly tough trading months'.
- **Balance sheet: the genuine positive.** Net debt (NIBD & guarantees) -R810m YoY and -R495m since March to R5.99bn (~$341m); covenant leverage 1.81x vs 1.92x (Mar-26) and 2.01x (Sep-25), covenant <3.0x. Net finance costs (ex-IFRS 16) -14% to R253m (~$14m), though SARB's +50bp of hikes (April and September 2026) partly offset. R4.45bn (~$254m) of calendar-2027 maturities (R500m Feb, R1.0bn May, R900m Aug, plus R650m and R1.5bn RCFs) are 'in advanced stages' of refinancing; current liabilities now exceed current assets (R4.49bn vs R1.02bn) pending that refinancing.
- **Cash generation intact.** Cash generated from operations R1.68bn (~$96m), flat YoY; capex R344m (~$20m), also flat; our estimated pre-dividend free cash flow ~R790m (~$45m) vs ~R750m. Net cash from operating activities (Tsogo's definition, after interest, tax and dividends) rose 10% to R824m (~$47m). R358m (~$20m) was returned to shareholders (R291m dividends + R67m buybacks); a further 4.7m shares (R32m/~$1.8m) were bought at R6.70 post period, taking shares in issue to 965.6m.
- **Interim dividend held at 15c (~0.9 USc), R145m (~$8.3m) aggregate**, payable 21 Dec 2026 (LDT 14 Dec). Controlling shareholder HCI received R157m (~$8.9m) of dividends in the half and its indirect stake has crept to 54% (50% a year ago) as buybacks shrink the register.
- **Portfolio pruning and asset sales continue**: the two smallest casinos were sold (R97m proceeds; 13 casinos now vs 15), vacant land is being sold for R48m (~$2.7m) net and R65m (~$3.7m) of listed shares are held for sale — '>R100m' of further realisations earmarked for debt/buybacks. Group capital commitments stand at R1bn (~$57m) for the next 12 months.
- **Somerset West is the growth project — and a legal fight.** The Caledon licence relocation to the Helderberg (~R1.29bn/~$74m estimated cost, per the Nov-25 booklet) has regulatory approval, but the sole metropole operator (~82% Western Cape casino market share, i.e. Sun International's GrandWest, in which Tsogo itself holds a 20% FVOCI stake valued at R511m) has taken the decision on High Court review; hearing April 2027. Tsogo is proceeding regardless.
- **Regulatory overhang is dense**: proposed ban on dedicated casino smoking areas; the National Gambling Amendment Bill (costly system requirements) held over by Parliament; unresolved online-betting taxation and casinos' inability to offer products online; provincial attempts to raise casino taxes while online 'betting' operators offering casino-style slots pay less; Eastern Cape LPM route licence expires March 2027 and goes to formal rebid.
- **Peer read-through is unflattering.** Sun International (six months to June 2026) grew land-based casino income +1.5% to R3.42bn (~$195m) and SunBet online income +35% to R1.18bn (~$67m) — Tsogo's casinos shrank 3% and its online business is roughly one-sixth of SunBet's scale. The bear case is share loss in both channels; the bull case is that Tsogo's cash yield and deleveraging do not require growth.
- **Share price**: +~1% by late afternoon on results day (News24); the stock is -8% YTD vs Sun International +22% and Southern Sun +8%, trading at roughly R7 (~$0.40) — ~4.5x trailing HEPS of 158c and a ~6.4% dividend yield on 45c. Exact close on 25 Nov not verified.
- **What management emphasises vs buries**: the booklet leads with 'HEPS up 7%' and debt reduction, and quotes casino EBITDA 'excluding the Theme Park'; the slots decline, the Oct/Nov trading warning, the second GRC impairment and its R273m sensitivity sit in the body. There is still no disclosure of hotel occupancy/ADR, online actives/handle or marketing spend, and no public results call.
- **Thesis read-through**: a cheap, cash-generative, now-deleveraged land-based operator with a structural slot problem and a sub-scale but profitable online hedge. The equity case rests on capital returns (dividends + buybacks ~R0.9bn/yr), online optionality and regulatory relief (LPM limits, online rights for casinos), not on a land-based recovery — and the H2 trading warning plus 2027 refinancing at higher rates are the near-term swing factors.

## 3. Segment deep-dive — Casinos & Hotels (Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, Western Cape, Other casinos)

*Slot-win erosion plus the Gold Reef City strike drove income -3% to R3.99bn (~$227m) and EBITDA -7% to R1.41bn (~$80m); KZN grew, Gauteng and the Western Cape fell hard, and management says Oct/Nov got worse.*

### Divisional overview

**Income fell 2.7% to R3.99bn (~$227m) and adjusted EBITDA 6.9% to R1.41bn (~$80m); margin down 150bp to 35.4% and compression is accelerating.**

- Division is 73% of group income and 86% of adjusted EBITDA; income -2.4% and EBITDA -6.7% on preceding half (37.0% margin in H2 FY26).
- Management says EBITDA fell 6% 'excluding the Theme Park, which was down 37%'; Theme Park went from 'more than R50m' (~$2.8m) to roughly R32m (~$1.8m), exclusion worth about a point.
- Driver is slot-machine revenue decline; impairment note cites pressured discretionary spend and casino-styled online betting games continuing to grow to significant levels.
- Net gaming win -3.4% to R4.45bn (~$253m); contract revenue +4.7% to R827m (~$47m): rooms +11.4% to R312m, F&B +2.3% to R315m, other flat at R200m. Implied gaming-and-other income ~-4.5% to ~R3.16bn (~$180m).
- Levies and VAT -4.4% to R659m (~$38m, 16.5% of income); employee costs R756m (~$43m, -0.9%), so operating leverage comes almost entirely through gaming win.

### Gauteng

**Gauteng (58% of divisional income) income fell 3.0% to R2.33bn (~$133m) and EBITDA 9.7% to R835m (~$48m), margin down 270bp to 35.9%.**

- EBITDA down 7.6% on preceding half; bridge attributes R43m (~$2.5m) to Gold Reef City strike and intimidation plus R16m (~$0.9m) once-off labour cost.
- THORN-affiliated strike began late April 2026, closed theme park, partially disrupted casino, into second month by late May on 4% offer versus 12% demand.
- Adding both items back, Gauteng EBITDA still down roughly 3% (our estimate), consistent with underlying slot trend at Montecasino and Silverstar, not disclosed separately.
- Gaming-floor upgrade completed November 2026; redeveloped 5D cinema at Theme Park should follow by about June 2027.
- GRC licence impaired further R150m (~$8.5m) after R144m at March 2026, to recoverable R2.4bn (~$137m); 1pp discount-rate rise costs R273m (~$16m), 1pp trading cut R174m (~$10m). Discount rates lowered 40–60bp (16.4% for Montecasino, Suncoast, GRC), so impairment taken despite valuation tailwind.

> “is difficult” — Management

### KwaZulu-Natal

**KZN was the only region to grow: income +1.6% to R1.08bn (~$62m), EBITDA +3.7% to R423m (~$24m), margin up 80bp to 39.0%, highest in portfolio.**

- Income also 2.2% higher than preceding half; a creditable reversal after FY26 when KZN EBITDA fell 9% and management called performance 'under pressure'.
- Gaming levies rose (R221m vs R217m) in step with win, implying genuine volume rather than cost cutting.
- Blackrock remains the weak link: another R10m goodwill impairment (recoverable amount R127m) after R73m in FY26.
- At regional level KZN is now the stabiliser in the land-based book.

### Western Cape

**Worst performer on every line: income -7.9% to R258m (~$15m), EBITDA -17.3% to R81m (~$4.6m), margin collapsed 360bp to 31.4%.**

- Margin down 620bp against seasonally stronger H2 FY26, when Cape summer trading produced R114m.
- The Caledon, flagged for double-digit declines and R40m goodwill write-off at March, is the drag; licence to relocate to Helderberg at ~R1.29bn (~$74m) over 'approximately a year and a half'.
- Regulator approved, but sole Cape Town metropole operator (~82% Western Cape share, GrandWest, in which Tsogo holds 20% FVOCI stake at R511m/~$29m) has instituted High Court review and interdict proceedings, hearing April 2027.
- Tsogo remains committed and is spending on site, so FY27 capex envelope ~R1.1bn (~$63m) and FY28 commitment carry litigation risk acknowledged but not quantified.

### Other casinos

**Other casinos income fell 9.8% to R322m (~$18m) and EBITDA 14.3% to R72m (~$4.1m), partly from selling the two smallest casinos.**

- Disposals brought R97m proceeds; FY26 EBITDA contribution R11m; estate reduced from 15 to 13 casinos.
- Hemingway's took a R1m PPE impairment.

### Hotels, IT and outlook

**Hotels delivered rooms revenue +11.4% to R312m with no occupancy, ADR or RevPAR disclosed; management says October and November were particularly tough.**

- Pivot hotel fifth-floor upgrade among period projects; F&B flat. IT programme (one-card, gaming-system upgrades, single sign-on, booking engine, Rewards app, AI, resort wallet) absorbs non-gaming capex.
- Divisional additions to non-current assets R268m (~$15m) versus R284m.
- H2 normally slightly stronger (H2 FY26 income R4.09bn, EBITDA R1.51bn); continued -3% income / -7% EBITDA run-rate puts FY27 divisional EBITDA ~R2.8bn (~$160m) versus R3.03bn in FY26.
- GRC refurbishment and festive season are the only identified offsets.

> “subsequent to this period, October and November have been particularly tough trading months” — Management

### Regulatory risks

**Tax arbitrage versus online operators is not closing, and the proposed tobacco smoking-area ban is the single largest unpriced regulatory risk.**

- Casinos pay levies and VAT of 16.5% of divisional income while online 'betting' operators offering casino-style slots are taxed significantly less; provincial authorities attempting to tax casinos higher.
- Proposed tobacco legislation banning dedicated smoking areas in casinos remains live; booklet says only that it is being 'monitored'.

## 4. Segment deep-dive — Limited Payout Machines (VSlots)

*Flat income (R989m/~$56m) and EBITDA (R282m/~$16m) at a steady 28.5% margin — resilient against a peer whose LPM EBITDA fell 8% — but growth is hostage to 1996-era bet limits.*

### Results and costs

**Income R989m (~$56m, -0.4%) and adjusted EBITDA R282m (~$16m, -0.4%) held margin at 28.5%, but growth has stalled.**

- Income -0.8% on preceding half; EBITDA +1.1% half on half (28.0% margin in H2 FY26). Management says performing 'relatively well'.
- LPMs now 17.1% of group EBITDA versus 16.5% a year ago, partly because casinos are shrinking faster.
- Division grew income 4% and EBITDA 5% in H1 FY26 and 3%/3% in FY26.
- Gaming levies and VAT R232m (~$13m), 23.5% of income, well above casinos' 16.5%; employee costs +4% to R47m (~$2.7m). Flat EBITDA reflects flat machine win.

### Peer comparison

**VSlots defended margin far better than Sun Slots (income -0.4% to R698m/~$40m, EBITDA -8.1% to R148m/~$8.4m) and is the larger operator.**

- Sun Slots six months to June 2026 cited protests and weak Western Cape execution.
- We would not read 'flat' as a floor: same slot product losing share to online casino-style betting, though convenience-led venues see slower substitution.

### Regulatory upside and risk

**The R5 maximum bet and R500 maximum payout, unchanged since 1996, are the upside case; an Eastern Cape licence rebid is the near-term risk.**

- Group lobbying National Gambling Board and DTIC for inflationary adjustment that would mechanically lift win per machine.
- New National Gambling Board appointed October 2026, first in over ten years; second-phase roll-outs in KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, Mpumalanga and North West await NGB approval.
- Eastern Cape route-operator licence expires March 2027 and goes to formal bidding; mentioned in May but not repeated in this booklet.

### Capex and forecast

**Additions to non-current assets fell 44% to R50m (~$2.8m) from R89m; we assume FY27 LPM EBITDA ~R560m (~$32m), flat on R562m.**

- Division is being run for cash on a wait-and-see stance.
- Bet-limit adjustment treated as unmodelled optionality worth, on our rough estimate, a high-single-digit uplift to divisional win if granted.

## 5. Segment deep-dive — Other gaming & betting (online betting — playTSOGO & bet.co.za; Galaxy Bingo; HHR associates)

*Online NGR +43% to R193m (~$11m) with EBITDA R50m (~$2.8m) at 26% lifted segment EBITDA +52% to R73m, but sequential online growth slowed to +8%, bingo is shrinking ~20%, and the disclosure is too thin to judge customer economics.*

### Segment and online results

**Segment income fell 1.8% to R429m (~$24m) but EBITDA rose 52% to R73m (~$4.2m), margin 17.0% from 11.0%, entirely on online.**

- Income -8.1% on preceding half; EBITDA +35% half on half.
- Online NGR R193m (~$11m), +43% from R135m (prior booklet said R136m); adjusted EBITDA R50m (~$2.8m) at 26% margin versus R13m at 10%. Turned around from August 2025.
- Against derived H2 FY26 (NGR R178m/~$10m, EBITDA R37m), half-on-half growth +8% NGR and +35% EBITDA, margin +510bp.
- Monthly EBITDA run-rate about R8.3m versus 'more than R7m per month' cited for August–October 2025.

### Sceptical observations

**Sequential NGR growth slowed from +32% to +8%, scale is one-sixth of SunBet, and disclosure is too thin to judge customer economics.**

- A year ago management said it would 'grow more aggressively', 'may need to sacrifice a portion of its profits generated in the short term', and was exploring a new platform; FY27 language has softened.
- 26% margin at R193m NGR implies marketing intensity well below scaled South African operators; segment employee costs +23% to R106m (~$6.0m) is the only visible investment line.
- SunBet online income R1.18bn (~$67m) in six months to June 2026, +35%; Tsogo far smaller than private bookmaker leaders.
- No handle, actives, ARPU, mix, deposit or marketing data; we treat valuation contribution as option value rather than a modellable growth line.

### Regulatory arbitrage

**Casinos cannot offer products online while bookmaker-licensed online operators offer casino-style games at significantly lower tax; neither convergence outcome is in our numbers.**

- This arbitrage explains both falling slot win and rising online NGR; taxation and casinos' online rights are 'subjects of discussion' with no resolution.
- Tax convergence would be negative for online margin but potentially positive for casinos; online casino rights for land-based licensees would be clearly positive.

## 6. Highlights & Lowlights

**Highlights**

- Online betting is now a profit centre: NGR +43% to R193m (~$11m), adj. EBITDA R50m (~$2.8m) at a 26% margin (10% a year ago), profitable every month since August 2025 and running ~R8m/month — 3% of group EBITDA from a division that lost R15m in FY25. _(structural)_
- Deleveraging target hit: net debt -R810m (~$46m) YoY and -R495m since March to R5.99bn (~$341m); covenant leverage 1.81x vs 2.01x a year ago against a <1.8x medium-term target and a 3.0x covenant; net finance costs -14% to R253m (~$14m). _(structural)_
- Cash returns continue: R358m (~$20m) returned in the half (R291m dividends + R67m buying 10m shares at R6.70), interim DPS held at 15c (~0.9 USc), a further 4.7m shares bought post-period; shares in issue down to 965.6m from 1.04bn eighteen months ago, driving HEPS +7% to 79c (~4.5 USc). _(mixed)_
- KwaZulu-Natal reversed FY26's decline: EBITDA +3.7% to R423m (~$24m), margin +80bp to 39.0%, the portfolio's best — evidence the slot problem is not uniform across precincts. _(early)_
- LPMs defended margin at 28.5% (EBITDA R282m/~$16m, flat) while peer Sun Slots' EBITDA fell 8.1%; a newly constituted National Gambling Board (Oct 2026) is the first realistic path in a decade to lifting the R5/R500 limits set in 1996. _(structural)_
- Hotels and non-gaming grew: rooms revenue +11.4% to R312m (~$18m), divisional revenue from contracts with customers +4.7% to R827m (~$47m), partially cushioning the gaming-win decline. _(structural)_
- R70m (~$4.0m) of identified one-offs at EBITDA (strike R43m, once-off labour R16m, dividends R8m, VAT R3m): excluding them adj. EBITDA was ~R1.72bn (~$98m), flat YoY on our estimate. _(one-off)_

**Lowlights**

- The core franchise is shrinking: net gaming win -3.4% to R4.45bn (~$253m), Casinos & Hotels EBITDA -6.9% to R1.41bn (~$80m) with margin -150bp to 35.4% (-160bp HoH); management and the impairment note both attribute it to slot win lost to online casino-style betting — a structural, tax-arbitrage-driven channel shift that is not reversing. _(structural)_
- Gold Reef City is a running sore: Gauteng EBITDA -9.7% to R835m (~$48m); the strike cost ~R59m (~$3.4m) including once-off labour; Theme Park EBITDA -37%; the GRC licence was impaired R150m (~$8.5m) for the second consecutive period (R294m cumulative), with a further R273m (~$16m) at risk from a 1pp discount-rate move. Management says recovery 'is difficult'. _(mixed)_
- Post-period trading warning: 'October and November have been particularly tough trading months' — H2 is seasonally the stronger half, so the run-rate implies FY27 adj. EBITDA down mid-single digits and no earnings recovery before FY28. _(watch)_
- Western Cape EBITDA -17% to R81m (~$4.6m), margin -360bp, and the ~R1.29bn (~$74m) Somerset West relocation that is supposed to fix it faces a High Court review (April 2027) brought by the ~82%-share incumbent — Tsogo is committing capital ahead of the ruling. _(structural)_
- Earnings quality: headline earnings -2% to R757m (~$43m), attributable profit -7% to R630m (~$36m), IFRS operating profit -9% to R1.15bn (~$66m); all per-share growth is buyback-driven, and 54%-holder HCI's stake creeps higher with every repurchase. _(mixed)_
- Online disclosure is inadequate for the valuation weight being placed on it: no handle, actives, ARPU, promo intensity, product mix or marketing spend; sequential NGR growth slowed to +8% from +32%; at R193m of half-year NGR the business is ~one-sixth of SunBet and the earlier 'invest aggressively / new platform' language has been dropped. _(watch)_
- Refinancing and rate risk: R4.45bn (~$254m) matures in calendar 2027 (Feb/May/Aug/Nov) after +50bp of SARB hikes; current liabilities (R4.49bn) exceed current assets (R1.02bn) pending completion; employee costs rose 2.6% to R1.0bn (~$57m, 18.4% of income, +90bp) on a -2% top line. _(structural)_
- Regulatory overhang widening, not narrowing: proposed ban on casino smoking areas, provincial attempts to raise casino taxes, unresolved online tax regime and online rights for casinos, the Gambling Amendment Bill's system-cost burden, and the Eastern Cape LPM licence rebid (March 2027). _(governance)_

## 7. Forecast & Outlook

Tsogo Sun issues no earnings guidance, so the 'bridge' is between the quantified guideposts in the May 2026 FY26 release and this booklet. Three moved: FY27 capex was trimmed to ~R1.1bn (~$63m) from R1.2bn (the GRC refurbishment, Theme Park 5D cinema, IT, gaming equipment and Somerset West all sit inside it, with R1bn contracted); the medium-term leverage target of <1.8x was 'effectively achieved' at 1.81x versus 1.92x in March; and the Somerset West relocation timeline compressed to ~18 months from ~24, with the cost estimate (~R1.29bn/~$74m from the Nov-25 booklet) not updated. The fourth change is qualitative and negative: the conclusion still promises 'resilient headline earnings per share' but the Casinos & Hotels commentary adds that October and November were 'particularly tough', which is the division — slots in Gauteng and the Western Cape — that drove the H1 shortfall. Online and LPMs are not the problem; the refinancing of R4.45bn (~$254m) of 2027 maturities is 'in advanced stages' and the 2027 RCF can be called on 13 months' notice.

- **FY27 capex envelope:** ~R1.1bn (~$63m) (from R1.2bn (May-26): -R100m; H1 spent R344m, implying ~R750m in H2)
- **Leverage target (net debt / adj. EBITDA, covenant basis):** 1.81x achieved (from 1.92x (Mar-26) and 2.01x (Sep-25); target <1.8x 'effectively' met; covenant <3.0x)
- **Somerset West relocation (Caledon licence):** ~R1.29bn (~$74m), ~18 months (from ~24 months; High Court review hearing April 2027 is the gating item)
- **Trading outlook (Casinos & Hotels):** Oct/Nov 'particularly tough' (from 'resilient cash flows'; slots decline persisting into H2)

Our model (estimates, not company guidance) takes management at its word on October/November and assumes no festive-season rescue. For H2 FY27 we pencil income of ~R5.43bn (~$310m), -2.5% YoY against H2 FY26's R5.57bn, and adj. EBITDA of ~R1.63bn (~$93m) at a ~30.0% margin (-140bp YoY; H2 FY26 was 31.4%), which gives **FY27 income ~R10.86bn (~$619m), -2.4%, and adj. EBITDA ~R3.28bn (~$187m), -5.4%, at a 30.2% margin (-90bp)**. By division that is Casinos & Hotels ~R2.80bn (-7%), LPMs ~R0.56bn (flat), Other gaming & betting ~R0.15bn (+45%, online ~R105m), Corporate ~-R0.23bn. Below the line, net finance costs ex-leases of ~R500m (~$28m) for the year (R253m in H1, ~R250m in H2 as lower debt is offset by the +50bp SARB hikes and any refinancing spread), D&A ~R660m and a ~28% tax rate give **FY27 headline earnings of ~R1.50–1.53bn (~$86–87m), -3% to -4%, and HEPS of ~157–160c (~9.0–9.1 USc), +3% to +5%** on ~958m weighted shares — i.e. the buyback continues to do the work, and the FY26 booklet's '8% HEPS growth' cadence is not repeatable without further repurchases. **Leverage keeps falling**: H2 pre-dividend operating cash flow of ~R1.1bn less ~R750m capex, ~R165m of dividends (interim + NCI), ~R50m of buybacks, plus ~R110m of land and listed-share proceeds, takes net debt to ~R5.7–5.8bn (~$325–330m) at March 2027 and covenant leverage to ~1.75x — comfortably inside target, before Somerset West spend lifts FY28 capex back toward R1.2–1.3bn. We assume the final dividend is held at 30c for a **45c (~2.6 USc) FY27 total, ~R435m (~$25m)**, and a similar ~R0.1–0.2bn of buybacks. Risks to the EBITDA line are skewed to the downside (December trading, GRC recovery, smoking-area legislation, provincial tax); the upside swing factors — LPM limit adjustment, online rights for casinos, a favourable court outcome in April — are regulatory, binary and not in our numbers. The equity remains a cash-yield-plus-deleveraging story priced at ~4.5x trailing HEPS; the H2 trading warning and the 2027 refinancing terms are what will move it near term.

**What to watch next quarter:**

- December/January festive trading at Montecasino, Gold Reef City (post-refurbishment) and Suncoast: does the Oct/Nov slot run-rate (our assumption: net gaming win -3% to -4%) persist into Q4 FY27, and is the GRC licence impaired again at March 2027 (R273m sensitivity to +1pp discount rate)?
- Refinancing terms for the R4.45bn (~$254m) of 2027 maturities — spread, tenor and whether the group terms out the R1.5bn November 2027 RCF; the going-concern note relies on completion.
- Western Cape High Court review of the Somerset West relocation (hearing April 2027): an adverse ruling strands early spend and leaves the Caledon problem unsolved; a favourable one commits ~R1.29bn (~$74m) of capex into FY28–29.
- Online betting: whether NGR growth re-accelerates from +8% HoH, any first disclosure of actives/handle/marketing, and the direction of online-betting tax reform and casino online rights — both of which cut across Tsogo's land-based and online economics in opposite directions.
- Regulatory calendar: tobacco/smoking-area legislation; provincial casino tax proposals; the new NGB's stance on LPM bet/payout limits and second-phase roll-outs; Eastern Cape LPM licence rebid (expires March 2027); the parked National Gambling Amendment Bill.
- Capital allocation and control: buyback cadence (4.7m shares post-period at R6.70) versus HCI's 54% and rising indirect stake; proceeds from the R48m land sale and R65m listed shares; final dividend decision in May 2027.
