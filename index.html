Analyst report · FY26 · Confidence medium

Betfred

Betfred's last set of accounts before the UK duty shock shows a group that is growing online (+20% on a 52-week pro-rata basis, now 45% of turnover) but whose profitability has already compressed sharply: EBITDA of £140.6m is ~28% below the pro-rata run-rate of the prior 78-week period and the margin is down ~600bps to 14.1%, before a single pound of the 40% RGD hits. With retail shrinking ~9% pro-rata, 132 further shop closures under way, a 76% dividend payout and management now withholding geographic disclosure, this is a business entering the FY27 tax regime with far less cushion than the £996m turnover headline suggests.

Analyst report · FY26 · Confidence medium

Singapore Pools

A third straight record year — turnover +6.2% to S$13.48bn with growth re-accelerating from +4% and no mega sporting event in the period — but the incremental dollar went almost entirely to prizes, opex and the Exchequer: betting duties & taxes rose 9% to S$2.49bn while the Tote Board contribution (the only "earnings" line the entity reports) was flat at S$583m (+1.4%). For sector investors the read-through is a still-growing Singapore gambling wallet and a monopoly lottery/tote model where the state captures ~88% of implied gross win before costs; this is a fiscal instrument first and an operating business second, and it discloses accordingly.

PointsBet Holdings

PointsBet pre-announced a sharp H1 FY27 miss: revenue ~A$113m (-16% YoY vs A$135m) and a normalised EBITDA loss of A$6–8m, entirely attributable to Australia, where self-imposed VIP compliance tightening has cut highest-value-cohort turnover/revenue by double digits all half and a deliberate mass-market push ran into punter-friendly AFL results and promo/product-fee dilution from the Pull 'Em product. The Canada/iGaming growth leg (FY26 revenue +13%) is intact but, at ~18% of group, is too small to carry a structurally de-rated Australian book; with cash of roughly A$23m and a 66%-owned register, the quarter re-opens the question of how long MIXI tolerates a listed, loss-making minority structure.

Analyst report · Q2 2026 · Confidence high

Evolution AB

An in-line, marginally light quarter that confirms the new shape of Evolution: a ~66%-margin cash machine whose growth now comes from the Americas and RNG, while Europe stabilises and Asia (37% of end-player revenue) stays hostage to content piracy. Net revenue fell 1.2% to EUR 517.8m (+2.4% constant currency) and EBITDA fell 1.2% to EUR 341.0m at a flat 65.9% margin; EPS still rose 4.7% because the EUR 2bn buyback and a financial-items swing did the work. Until Asia and Europe both grow, the equity case is capital return plus product optionality rather than compounding growth.
